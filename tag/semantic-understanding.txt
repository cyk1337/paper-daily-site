19:T5a8,Two-stage pipeline: generation then validation.
1) Generation: Starting from ATOMIC triples <A, R, B>, they prompt Llama 3.1 70B to insert an explicit negation cue (“not”) into the event text, targeting the main verb when present, otherwise negating a modifier. They create three variants per triple: <¬A, R, B>, <A, R, ¬B>, and <¬A, R, ¬B>. For Anion triples (already <¬A, R, B’> with human-written B’), they only negate the then-event to form <¬A, R, ¬B’>. A small manual check reports high syntactic correctness.
2) Validation: They argue frontier LLMs used as judges (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4) are unreliable at deciding whether a negated triple is commonsense-consistent. Instead, they train a dedicated 3-way classifier (Valid/Invalid/Ambiguous) via SFT on noisy automatically-constructed data: (i) Valid from original commonsense corpora, (ii) Ambiguous from random recombinations of (if, relation, then), (iii) Invalid from GPT-4o-generated conflicting then-events given an if-event. They evaluate on a human-labeled set built by sampling relations and adding the three negated variants.
3) Use in training: The filtered negation triples (2M+) are used for continued pretraining, and improvements are reported on multiple negation-focused benchmarks.
