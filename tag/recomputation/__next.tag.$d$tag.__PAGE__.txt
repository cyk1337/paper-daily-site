9:T5b0,Key insight: selection outputs a discrete set of indices; top-k is non-differentiable, and backward only needs the k selected scores. Therefore, you can (i) avoid materializing the full score table and (ii) avoid rerunning selection during recomputation.

HyScale implements this via:
1) HyTopK: an on-the-fly, exact top-k operator that maintains top-k while scoring, eliminating the full score table. It is deterministic and CP-compatible. It uses a threshold-filtering idea with an “optimistic threshold” and a rare/cheap recovery path to keep exactness without slowing down.
2) Selection replay: record forward-pass selection results and, during activation recomputation, replay only the k selected entries needed for the indexer loss—rather than recomputing selection over all prior tokens. This moves recomputation granularity below the operator level to “only what the indexer loss needs.”
3) End-to-end integration on Megatron-LM: once selection is faster, CP communication and sparse kernels become visible bottlenecks. HyScale overlaps CP collectives for sparse attention behind selection (decoupled CP overlap) and introduces locality-aware sparse kernels that process adjacent queries together to reuse loads/updates for shared entries.

A notable design choice is insisting on exact top-k (vs sampling/approximation) for determinism and training stability, while using replay to avoid paying the recomputation cost of exact selection.a:T43f,Deep-read plan (1.5–2 hours):
