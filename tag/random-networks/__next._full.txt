19:T454,The estimator propagates approximate activation distributions through the network layer by layer. Instead of evaluating many input samples, it represents each layer’s activation distribution using low-order cumulants, with Hermite expansions used to handle nonlinear transformations. Cumulants encode deviations from Gaussianity: mean and variance are the lowest-order terms, while higher-order cumulants capture skewness and higher correlations. The estimator is quenched: the network weights are fixed, and the expectation is over inputs. This differs from the more common annealed analysis of random networks, where one also averages over weights. The paper introduces several variants, including factorized and augmented factorized estimators. Factorization exploits the approximate structure that appears in wide networks, avoiding the cost of maintaining full high-order tensors. The augmented version keeps additional statistics to improve accuracy.
