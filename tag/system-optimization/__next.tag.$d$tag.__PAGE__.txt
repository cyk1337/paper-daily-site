9:T459,The paper proposes a Model-Algorithm-System (MAS) taxonomy. At the model level, it covers architectural mechanisms such as modality-specific versus unified encoders, sparse expert routing, structural decoding, and modular adaptation. This layer addresses what should not be computed in the first place. At the algorithm level, it reviews ways to reduce effective computation under a fixed architecture, including token compression, pruning, quantization, distillation, speculative decoding, and cache reuse. At the system level, it focuses on execution orchestration: cache management, edge-cloud collaboration, latency-aware scheduling, and hardware-software co-design. A useful part of the survey is its emphasis on vertical synergies rather than isolated tricks. For example, reducing visual tokenization load at the model level changes the payoff of compression at the algorithm level and the bandwidth bottlenecks at the system level. Likewise, autoregressive multimodal generation pushes KV/cache design and scheduling into the foreground. The contribution is organizational and analytical, not algorithmic.a:T542,The method has three parts. First, the authors build a black-box calibrated cost model for the deployed expert kernel. Per-expert latency is modeled with a two-regime structure: a fixed activation floor for instantiating an expert replica, plus a token-dependent term that becomes relevant beyond an inflection point. They also explicitly model grouped-GEMM M-tile padding, which explains why splitting one expert across multiple replicas can create extra work instead of merely redistributing it. Second, they formulate per-batch dispatch as a fixed-charge makespan minimization problem over routed tokens and replica placements. The paper proves the problem is already NP-hard in a small setting, while showing that prior token-balancing and activation-balancing methods emerge as degenerate limits when certain terms vanish. This is a clean unification of earlier heuristics. Third, they propose tempo_fast, a practical near-optimal solver. It uses cost-aware seeding, augmenting-chain activation rebalancing for the flat regime, bottleneck-focused local search with partial migrations for the linear regime, and an ensemble that scores candidate solutions such as token-LP and round-robin under the same calibrated model. The strength of the algorithm is that each move is tied to a measured hardware effect rather than a surrogate objective.b:T449,The most informative results are the measurement studies. On two generations of datacenter GPUs, expert FFN latency stays nearly flat up to about 156–168 tokens per expert, then transitions to a more linear region. The fitted activation floor b ranges from 1.74 microseconds to 14.8 microseconds depending on expert shape, an 8.5x spread, with mean fit error around 4–8%. In real decode traces, 92–100% of batches contain both regimes simultaneously: a few hot experts carry roughly 91% of tokens, while many activated experts still sit in the flat regime. On the optimization side, tempo_fast gets within about 1.02% of optimal or near-optimal references, which is strong given the combinatorial objective.
