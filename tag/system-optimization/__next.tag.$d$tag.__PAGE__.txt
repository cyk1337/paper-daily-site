a:T542,The method has three parts. First, the authors build a black-box calibrated cost model for the deployed expert kernel. Per-expert latency is modeled with a two-regime structure: a fixed activation floor for instantiating an expert replica, plus a token-dependent term that becomes relevant beyond an inflection point. They also explicitly model grouped-GEMM M-tile padding, which explains why splitting one expert across multiple replicas can create extra work instead of merely redistributing it. Second, they formulate per-batch dispatch as a fixed-charge makespan minimization problem over routed tokens and replica placements. The paper proves the problem is already NP-hard in a small setting, while showing that prior token-balancing and activation-balancing methods emerge as degenerate limits when certain terms vanish. This is a clean unification of earlier heuristics. Third, they propose tempo_fast, a practical near-optimal solver. It uses cost-aware seeding, augmenting-chain activation rebalancing for the flat regime, bottleneck-focused local search with partial migrations for the linear regime, and an ensemble that scores candidate solutions such as token-LP and round-robin under the same calibrated model.
