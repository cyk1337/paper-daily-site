19:Ta12,方法设计的优点是控制变量做得很干净。作者把 self-play 形式化成 proposer-solver 双角色系统，但实现上沿用常见做法，用同一个模型配两个 prompt template。每个 outer step 中，proposer 先生成任务 q=(program, input)，环境 E 执行得到 ground-truth output o*(q)，然后数据 gate F 决定这个任务是否进入训练池 D_t。进入池子的任务再交给 solver 作答，并根据某种 reward 更新策略。

论文的关键不是提出一个新 reward，而是把 gate 和 reward 当成两条独立轴来系统扫描。任务环境有两个：一个是真实 Python output prediction，另一个是作者专门构造的 deterministic DSL twin task，包含 15 个操作符，刻意去掉三类混杂因素：预训练先验、输出歧义、执行器噪声。这样如果在 DSL 上仍然复现塌缩，就能说明问题来自优化动力学，而不是 Python 语义、代码执行噪声或模型已有知识。

作者测试了多种 reward 设定，其中最关键的是 self-consistency reward 与 grounded reward 的对比。self-consistency reward 不看真实答案，只看 solver 多次采样之间是否一致；grounded reward 则依赖执行真值。直觉上很多人会以为，只要 reward grounded enough，坏数据自然会被低奖励压下去。但论文证明这不成立：当训练池里混入歧义任务或可被投机的任务时，self-consistency 会把“容易形成错误共识”的样本推成高奖励样本，坏数据不是被 down-weight，而是被主动放大。

全文有三个机制性发现。第一，intrinsic-grounded gap：作者定义 solver 的 intrinsic reward 与 grounded accuracy 之间的差值，用来量化“模型内部自洽”和“真实正确”之间的脱钩。一旦 gap 持续扩大，就说明系统正在向错误但稳定的 attractor 收敛。第二，Grounded Proposer Paradox：如果 solver 用的是 self-consistency reward，那么一个能访问 ground truth 的 proposer 反而会更快把系统推向塌缩，因为它更擅长生成结构干净、格式统一、最容易形成错误共识的任务，等于给 spurious attractor 铺路。第三，连续 gate strictness ε 分析：作者不只比较开/关两种 gate，而是把 gate 放松程度参数化，观察到 sharp phase transition，而且是两阶段结构——训练侧指标先在较低 ε 处开始解耦，但下游验证精度要到更高 ε 才明显崩。这说明只看训练 reward 或自一致性曲线，会晚于真实病灶，或者说会看错病灶。1a:T88c,实验分两条主线。第一条是在 Python output-prediction 任务上做完整 self-play，第二条是在 deterministic DSL twin 上复现实验。DSL 的作用很关键：它去除了预训练偏置、输出歧义和 executor noise，因此如果 collapse 仍出现，就说明塌缩不是环境偶然性，而是训练闭环自身的动力学结果。作者报告的总体结论很强：有 strict gate 时，测试过的 reward 变体都能稳定训练，甚至包括完全不访问 ground truth 的 self-consistency reward；没有 gate 时，所有 reward 变体都不够，哪怕 solver reward grounded in execution truth，也会在含歧义输出的任务上塌缩。

第二条主线是 collapse 机制分析。作者展示了 intrinsic-grounded gap 随训练扩大的现象：solver 在 self-consistency reward 上越来越高，但 grounded accuracy 下降或脱钩，说明模型学到的是“群内一致”而不是真实求解。这个现象在 Python 和 DSL 两个环境都复现，支撑了“优化动力学而非环境噪声”的判断。

Grounded Proposer Paradox 的实验也很有意思。直觉上 proposer 越 grounded 越安全，但结果相反：当 solver 依赖 self-consistency 时，grounded proposer 会更快 collapse。原因不是 proposer 变差，而是它更高效地把训练分布集中到那些最容易形成错误共识的 clean tasks 上。这个结果对很多 proposer-solver 共训练系统是个提醒：提升 proposer 质量，不一定提升系统稳定性，取决于 solver reward 的形状。

连续 gate strictness ε 的实验给出了更细的相图。随着 ε 放松，训练指标先出现异常分叉，但验证准确率在一段区间内还能维持，直到 ε 更高时才突然掉下去。这个两阶段结构说明，如果只用训练 reward 监控 self-play，很可能在系统已经进入危险区后仍误判为“训练正常”。

从摘要和正文可见，论文更强调机制和相变，而不是堆很多 benchmark 数字。对读者来说，最重要的不是某个绝对分数，而是“strict gate suffices, reward alone does not”这个因果结论，以及它在两个环境中的复现。1b:Ta4b,The strongest part of the paper is its experimental control. Self-play is formalized as a proposer-solver system, though implemented in the common way with one model and two prompt templates. At each outer step, the proposer samples a task q=(program, input), the environment executes it to obtain the ground-truth output o*(q), and a gate F decides whether the task enters the training pool D_t. Only then does the solver answer the task and receive reward.

The paper’s contribution is not a new reward but a clean factorization of gate and reward as two independent axes. The authors study two environments. One is Python output prediction. The other is a deterministic DSL twin task with 15 operators, deliberately constructed to remove three confounders: pretraining priors, output ambiguity, and executor noise. If collapse still appears in the DSL setting, it is much harder to blame the environment; the failure must come from the optimization dynamics of the self-play loop itself.

Several reward variants are tested, with the most important contrast being self-consistency reward versus grounded reward. Self-consistency does not use the true answer; it rewards agreement across multiple solver samples. Grounded reward uses execution truth. A common intuition is that sufficiently grounded rewards should naturally suppress bad data. The paper shows that this intuition fails. Once ambiguous or exploitable tasks enter the pool, self-consistency can assign higher reward to corrupted data than to clean data, because ambiguous tasks are exactly where spurious consensus is easiest to maximize.

Three mechanistic findings stand out. First, the intrinsic-grounded gap measures the difference between the solver’s intrinsic reward and grounded accuracy. When this gap widens, the system is converging toward an attractor that is internally self-consistent but externally wrong. Second, the Grounded Proposer Paradox shows that when the solver is trained with self-consistency reward, a proposer with access to ground truth can accelerate collapse rather than prevent it. The reason is subtle: a grounded proposer concentrates training on clean, well-structured tasks that provide the lowest-resistance path to spurious consensus. Third, the gate is relaxed continuously through a strictness parameter epsilon rather than only toggled on or off. This reveals a sharp phase transition with a two-stage structure: training-side metrics decouple early, while downstream validation remains stable until epsilon is much larger. That matters because many systems monitor only training reward and would miss the onset of failure.1c:T8c8,The experiments follow two main tracks. The first is full self-play on Python output prediction. The second reproduces the same phenomena on the deterministic DSL twin task. The DSL control is crucial because it removes pretraining bias, ambiguity, and execution noise. Collapse persisting there strongly supports the claim that the failure is in the learning dynamics, not in Python semantics or tooling artifacts.

The headline result is simple and strong. With a strict gate, every tested reward variant remains stable, including self-consistency reward with no access to ground truth. Without the gate, none of the tested reward variants is sufficient. Even rewards grounded in execution truth collapse once ambiguous-output tasks are admitted. That is the paper’s main causal result.

The collapse analysis centers on the intrinsic-grounded gap. During failure, self-consistency reward rises while grounded accuracy falls or decouples, showing that the solver is learning agreement rather than correctness. The same pattern appears in both Python and DSL environments, which makes the argument much more convincing than a single-environment anecdote.

The Grounded Proposer Paradox is another important empirical result. Intuition suggests that a more grounded proposer should be safer. The paper finds the opposite under self-consistency-trained solvers: grounded proposers can drive faster collapse because they generate cleaner, more homogeneous tasks that make spurious consensus easier to optimize. This is a useful warning for co-training systems: improving proposer quality does not guarantee system-level stability if the solver reward is misaligned.

Finally, the continuous epsilon sweep produces a phase diagram rather than a binary ablation. As epsilon is relaxed, training metrics begin to separate at relatively low values, but validation accuracy remains stable for a while before dropping sharply. This two-stage transition suggests that training-side reward curves are not reliable early-warning signals for collapse.

The paper is more about mechanism than leaderboard numbers. The key empirical takeaway is not a single score, but the replicated conclusion that strict gating suffices whereas reward design alone does not.1d:T5cd,This one deserves a careful read. Start with Section 2 and make sure the boundaries between proposer, solver, environment, gate, and reward are clear. Many self-play papers blur data generation and policy optimization into one loop; this paper is useful precisely because it separates them.

Then spend time on Section 3. The two concepts to track are the intrinsic-grounded gap and the Grounded Proposer Paradox. The former is the best diagnostic for collapse. The latter explains why a seemingly better proposer can make the whole system less stable under certain solver rewards. When reading the figures, compare reward curves against grounded accuracy rather than looking only at final outcomes.

Section 4 is also worth close attention because the epsilon sweep gives a more dynamical view of stability than a standard ablation table. Anyone building self-play or synthetic curriculum systems could reuse this style of phase-transition analysis.

That should take around 35 to 50 minutes and is enough to capture the main argument. For deeper follow-up, two questions matter.
