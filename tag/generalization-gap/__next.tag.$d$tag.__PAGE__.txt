c:Tbbc,方法上，作者没有再提一个新算法，而是把多种 SE 策略统一成 generator–verifier 框架下的离线偏好学习问题。基础流程是：给定未标注 prompt，基座模型先生成候选解；再由同一个模型或其派生流程产生验证、反馈或修订信号；最后把这些内部信号转成 preference data，对模型做离线训练。全文重点分析四类策略。

第一类是 single-round verification。模型对同一题生成多个候选答案，再用内部 verifier 对答案进行打分或二元判断，构造 chosen/rejected 对。这对应最直接的 generate-then-rank 范式，优点是便宜、实现简单，缺点是 verifier 噪声直接决定训练上限。

第二类是 multi-turn revision with feedback。这里不是只给一个“对/错”标签，而是让模型先批改，再给自然语言反馈，再据此修订答案。作者发现这种 richer feedback 比单轮验证更有效，因为它把稀疏偏好信号变成了带方向性的误差描述，训练时模型学到的不只是“哪个答案更好”，还有“为什么错、该往哪里改”。这点对 reasoning 很关键，因为推理错误往往不是表面格式问题，而是中间约束传播出错。

第三类是 iterative training。作者把离线 SE 做成多轮：先用当前模型生成监督并训练，再用更新后的模型重新生成数据继续训练。这个设定接近很多 self-training / self-rewarding 工作的实际流程。全文的一个重要观察是，迭代确实能继续抬升性能，但收益递减明显，而且如果 verifier 质量没有同步提升，误差会被反复固化，难以逼近 oracle。

第四类是 curriculum learning。Knights-and-Knaves 任务天然有难度参数，作者按人数或复杂度从易到难组织训练。课程学习的作用不是简单“先学简单题”，而是减少早期内部监督的噪声：在简单样本上，模型自验证和自修订更可靠，因此先在高置信度区域建立推理模板，再迁移到更难样本。作者把它视为提升内部监督有效性的一个间接手段，而不是独立于监督质量之外的技巧。

训练目标上，全文核心比较对象是 oracle preference optimization。也就是说，作者并不是拿 SE 去和原始 base model 比就结束，而是始终问：如果同样做偏好优化，只是把偏好标签从“模型自己给”换成“真值导出的 oracle 给”，差距还有多大。这个设计很重要，因为它把问题从“SE 是否有用”转成“内部信号损失了多少可学习信息”。

任务选择上，Knights-and-Knaves 很合适。它有确定性唯一解，难度可控，污染风险低，而且能自然测 easy-to-hard generalization。相比 GSM8K 这类可能被训练数据覆盖的 benchmark，KK 更像一个干净的机制实验台。作者还补充了 OpenThoughts3 及其他下游 reasoning benchmark，用来检查结论是否只局限于逻辑谜题。d:T40a,For pretraining and post-training interface research, the main contribution is a cleaner boundary on what pure self-bootstrapping can and cannot do. Recent work often mixes self-rewarding, self-verification, self-revision, and intrinsic-reward RL under one optimistic narrative. This paper separates them within a unified offline framework and keeps oracle preference optimization as the reference point throughout. The result is clear: internally generated supervision does improve reasoning, and more structured internal signals help more, but a persistent gap remains even in a clean, controllable, uniquely verifiable reasoning environment. On Knights-and-Knaves, that gap is roughly 8–13 accuracy points relative to oracle supervision. This matters because it suggests the bottleneck is not merely whether one uses RL or self-training, but the fidelity of the learning signal. If one wants self-improvement to feed back into pretraining or continual training, verifier quality and feedback structure remain central constraints.e:Tb6e,The paper does not introduce a new training algorithm. Instead, it casts several representative self-evolution strategies into a common generator–verifier pipeline with offline preference learning. Starting from unlabeled prompts, the base model generates candidate solutions; the same model, or a procedure built from it, then produces verification judgments, feedback, or revisions; these internal signals are converted into preference data for training.

The first strategy is single-round verification. The model samples multiple candidate answers and uses an internal verifier to score or classify them, producing chosen/rejected pairs. This is the simplest generate-then-rank setup. It is cheap and easy to implement, but its ceiling is tightly coupled to verifier noise.

The second strategy is multi-turn revision with feedback. Instead of only assigning a scalar or binary judgment, the model critiques an answer, provides natural-language feedback, and then revises the solution. This richer signal matters because it turns sparse preference supervision into directional error information. For reasoning tasks, that is often more useful than a bare preference label, since many failures come from incorrect intermediate constraint propagation rather than surface-level formatting issues.

The third strategy is iterative training. The authors repeat the offline self-evolution cycle: generate internal supervision with the current model, train on it, then regenerate data with the updated model and continue. This mirrors practical self-training and self-rewarding loops. The paper shows that iteration helps, but with diminishing returns, and that errors can be repeatedly reinforced if verifier quality does not improve in parallel.

The fourth strategy is curriculum learning. Knights-and-Knaves provides a natural difficulty axis, so the authors train from easier to harder instances. The role of curriculum here is not just standard optimization smoothing. It improves the effective quality of internal supervision, because self-verification and self-revision are more reliable on easier examples. The model can first stabilize reasoning patterns in a higher-confidence regime before moving to harder cases.

The central comparison is against oracle preference optimization. This is an important design choice. The question is not whether self-evolution beats the base model, but how much learnable information is lost when preference labels come from the model itself rather than from ground-truth-derived oracle signals.

The task choice is also deliberate. Knights-and-Knaves offers deterministic unique solutions, controllable difficulty, and low contamination risk, making it a good testbed for studying generalization rather than benchmark memorization. The paper also includes experiments on OpenThoughts3 and downstream reasoning benchmarks to test whether the conclusions extend beyond logic puzzles.f:T7d7,The main numbers are straightforward and informative. Two experimental patterns matter. First, performance improves roughly monotonically as the internal supervision becomes more structured: single-round verification < multi-turn feedback < iterative training < curriculum. This suggests the issue is not that self-generated supervision is useless, but that its information content and reliability are limited. Second, larger models benefit more from richer feedback. The paper notes that a 12B model gains especially strongly from multi-turn feedback, which is consistent with the idea that stronger base models can better exploit natural-language critiques. Even then, the oracle gap remains.

The OpenThoughts3 experiments add another useful point. Offline preference learning performs on par with more expensive online methods, but the gains are modest and can even become negative in some settings. This weakens the common assumption that moving self-evolution into online RL automatically solves the problem. If the reward or preference signal is still generated by the same model and remains noisy, the online format alone does not remove the supervision bottleneck.

The most informative ablation theme is supervision quality. Multi-turn feedback beats simple verification because it exposes error structure. Curriculum helps because it increases label reliability early in training.
