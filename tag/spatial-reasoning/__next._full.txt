18:T74c,The core contribution is GUI-Primitives, a 994-item benchmark organized as 497 contrastive instruction pairs over seven GUI spatial primitives: left/right, above/below, containment, alignment, proximity, list ordinal, and occlusion. In each pair, the screenshot and anchor element stay fixed while the relation expression changes, so the correct target flips between two designated candidates. This minimal-pair construction is the key idea. It suppresses screenshot salience, centrality bias, and fixed-answer shortcuts: a model that always clicks the more salient element can only get one item in the pair correct.

The dataset also includes lexical variation beyond the relation term in 253 of the 497 pairs, changing verbs, head nouns, or anchor quoting, which reduces template memorization. A 196-item subset is validated by five annotators, with Fleiss kappa = 0.94 for well-formedness and kappa = 0.79 for target selection, giving the benchmark a reasonably strong annotation foundation.

Methodologically, the paper goes beyond strict point-in-box accuracy and performs candidate-level analysis. Errors are split into two types: selecting the contrastive wrong candidate versus predicting a point outside both candidate regions. This distinction matters because it changes the diagnosis. A low score does not necessarily mean the model misunderstood the relation word; it may simply have failed to localize either candidate reliably.

The paper also evaluates three training-free interventions: Set-of-Mark prompting, primitive-aware chain-of-thought, and activation steering. SoM explicitly marks the two candidates and therefore acts as an oracle upper bound on candidate discrimination rather than a deployable solution. CoT tests whether explicit reasoning helps. Activation steering tests whether internal representations can be nudged without retraining.19:T84b,The empirical picture is consistent and fairly stark.

First, absolute performance is poor. Across 19 VLMs, the best model, Claude Opus 4.7, reaches only 31% strict point-in-box accuracy on the human-clean subset; the abstract also states that no model exceeds 32% overall. So even on tightly controlled GUI spatial primitives, current models remain far from human-level grounding.

Second, all tested models fall below the two-candidate reference level of 0.50 on four primitives: containment, occlusion, alignment, and proximity. Candidate-level analysis explains why: the dominant error is not choosing the opposite candidate, but predicting outside both candidate boxes. Depending on the model, off-candidate predictions account for 60% to 92% of errors, and on the real desktop screenshot subset this rises to 96.2%. The main bottleneck therefore looks more like candidate localization failure than relation-word confusion.

Third, the benchmark tracks downstream grounding. For the ten models also evaluated on ScreenSpot-Pro, GUI-Primitives accuracy correlates with downstream grounding accuracy at Spearman rho = +0.74 with p = 0.015. The paper also reports item-level logistic regression over n = 15,810 model-item pairs with pseudo-R^2 = 0.40, which supports the claim that the benchmark is diagnostically meaningful rather than synthetic in a disconnected way.

Fourth, only Set-of-Mark helps substantially. Marking the two candidates improves selection accuracy by 35 to 57 points, raising GPT-5 from 30% to 87% and OS-Atlas from 10% to 52%. Primitive-aware CoT and activation steering do not produce significant gains. This is a useful diagnosis: once the candidate set is made explicit, models can often discriminate between candidates much better than their raw screenshot-grounding scores suggest.

Fifth, shortcut controls behave mostly as expected. Blank canvas, shuffled instructions, and heavy blur reduce performance, indicating that the benchmark is not solved by pure template matching. The blur result is significant but not catastrophic, which suggests some models still rely on coarse visual cues.1a:T7f5,The setup is deliberately narrow and controlled. The authors use the camera-relative direction subset of ViewSpatial-Bench and decompose items into pure horizontal, vertical, and depth axes, which avoids mixing different spatial factors. They evaluate both the native 18-way task accuracy and a within-axis binary accuracy. On top of this task decomposition, they run a standard probe-and-steer pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. A five-fold cross-validated logistic probe is trained on PCA-50 residual-stream features at the decision token, layer by layer, to decode each axis. Steering directions are then extracted either from class mean differences or from back-projected probe weights, and injected as a training-free activation edit in the L20-L24 band, scaled by residual-stream standard deviations and concentrated on top-k neurons with a single global coefficient. Up to this point, the setup mirrors the usual latent-knowledge recovery recipe. The key addition is the vision-ablation arbiter. The image is replaced by a gray blank while keeping the prompt, answer options, and decoding unchanged. The signed gap between real-image and blank-image performance is then used to classify each axis into one of three regimes: grounded-correct if real-image performance is higher and blank performance collapses toward chance; grounded-inverted if real-image performance is below chance but blank performance returns toward chance; prior-driven if real and blank conditions are statistically indistinguishable, including under steering. The paper also includes robustness checks against the objection that a gray blank is an unnatural input, using multiple ablations and mismatched but in-distribution real images. In later sections, the authors analyze the geometry of depth inversion and test minimal corrective edits. Some models can be fixed by a training-free rotation in representation space, while others require a trained low-rank edit, suggesting a spectrum from localized geometric misalignment to more distributed errors.1b:T80a,The headline result comes from the three-axis analysis on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Behaviorally, horizontal is strong, vertical is near chance, and depth is below chance. Probing tells a very different story: all three axes are decodable from the residual stream, with vertical being the most decodable. The reported within-axis probe accuracies span roughly 73% to 97%, with vertical around 94% and horizontal around 85%. The training-free projection result looks even more convincing under the standard interpretation: vertical task accuracy rises from about 59% to 79%, while horizontal remains largely unchanged, exactly the pattern one would describe as unlocking latent knowledge. The gray-image arbiter overturns that interpretation. Horizontal drops from about 82% with the real image to 49% with the blank, so it is genuinely vision-grounded. Depth rises from 31% with the real image to 45% with the blank, showing that the error requires looking at the image and is therefore grounded but inverted. Vertical stays essentially unchanged, about 59% versus 60%, and the projection gain is almost identical with and without vision, roughly +21 versus +19, indicating that the decoded and amplified signal is a prior rather than visual grounding.
