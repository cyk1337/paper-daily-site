9:T643,The method, FlowTracer, models a generated sequence as an attention-induced directed acyclic graph. Tokens are nodes, edges go from earlier to later tokens, and edge capacities come from attention weights aggregated across heads and layers. A naive propagation over this graph fails because many branches never contribute to the answer, and long paths or large fan-out dilute the influence of early but decisive tokens. To fix this, the paper introduces answer-conditioned graph reweighting. It first defines an answer region, such as the final answer span, and computes a reachability potential for each node, measuring how much downstream influence can eventually reach the answer. Then it applies a Doob-h-like transform to reweight outgoing edges so that only answer-relevant influence is preserved and local flow conservation holds. This removes dead branches and avoids structural bias from path length or branching. On the cleaned graph, the method injects unit flow from a super-source attached to prompt tokens and collects it at a super-sink attached to answer tokens. The resulting token throughput identifies the dominant prompt-to-answer backbone, with high-throughput tokens interpreted as transit hubs or aggregation checkpoints. These throughput scores are then used in RLVR for token-level reward shaping and loss reweighting, amplifying updates on structurally important tokens and suppressing filler.
