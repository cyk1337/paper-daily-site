18:T8b0,方法叫 Reward-based Velocity Matching，简称 RVM。核心做法很直接：先用当前模型生成 endpoint 样本 x0，根据 reward model 或任务奖励给每个样本一个标量回报；然后把 x0 前向加噪到某个中间时刻 xt，并构造该时刻对应的 velocity target v；最后不再估计 policy ratio，也不再回放整条反向去噪轨迹，而是直接用 reward 变换得到的权重，对 velocity prediction 做带符号的回归更新。

直观上，正奖励样本会强化与该样本对应的 velocity 方向，负奖励样本会抑制它。作者还引入了 anchor velocity v_anc，作为独立于 reward 的稳定器。anchor 可以来自参考模型、原模型或其他保守目标，用来约束模型不要因为奖励优化而快速漂移，避免画质、语义对齐或运动先验被破坏。这样，RVM 的设计空间被压缩成三个真正重要的维度：reward 如何映射成权重；anchor 如何选；样本如何 rollout 生成。

论文的一个理论贡献是统一视角。作者指出，近期一些看似不同的方法，如 RAM 和 DiffusionNFT，在特定 reward 设计和 anchor 选择下都可以写成 RVM 的特例。这个统一很有价值，因为它说明争论焦点不该放在损失公式表面长什么样，而应放在更实质的地方：奖励信号质量、权重变换是否稳定、anchor 强度如何影响保真与偏移、以及 rollout sampler 的成本与偏差。

另一个关键点是 trajectory-free。传统 trajectory-based 方法需要存储随机去噪路径上的中间状态，并把最终奖励分配回这些随机转移，训练成本高且方差大。RVM 只需要 endpoint 样本，再独立采样一个噪声时刻做单步 velocity matching，因此可以和 deterministic ODE sampling 结合，显著降低 rollout 成本。作者还强调不需要 classifier-free guidance，这进一步减少了训练和采样开销。

从方法定位看，这不是 diffusion pretraining，而是 post-training。但它和 pretrain 的接口很紧：更新目标直接沿用 velocity matching 这一原生训练表征，而不是强行套用 AR policy gradient 语言。这种接口设计是全文最有启发的部分。19:T82c,The proposed method is Reward-based Velocity Matching (RVM). The procedure is simple. First, generate endpoint samples x0 from the current model and score them with a reward function. Then forward-noise each x0 to an intermediate state xt, construct the corresponding velocity target v, and update the model by regressing its predicted velocity toward or away from that target using reward-derived signed weights. Positive rewards reinforce the velocity direction associated with a sample; negative rewards suppress it.

A key ingredient is the anchor velocity, v_anc. This acts as an independent stabilizer and can be derived from a reference model or another conservative target. The anchor is meant to control drift and preserve desirable properties such as visual quality, semantic alignment, or motion priors while still allowing reward-driven adaptation. Under this view, the real design choices are not hidden in a policy-ratio derivation. They are explicit: how to transform rewards into weights, how to choose the anchor, and how to generate samples.

The paper also provides a unifying lens. It shows that recent methods such as RAM and DiffusionNFT can be recovered as special cases of RVM under particular reward and anchor choices. That is useful because it shifts attention away from superficial differences in loss algebra and toward the substantive issues: reward quality, stability of weighting schemes, anchoring strength, and rollout efficiency.

Another important property is that RVM is trajectory-free. It does not require storing the full reverse denoising path or assigning terminal rewards back through stochastic transitions. Instead, it works from endpoint samples plus independently noised intermediate states. This makes it compatible with deterministic ODE sampling and avoids some of the variance and systems overhead of trajectory-based approaches. The method is post-training rather than pretraining, but its strongest idea is precisely at the pretrain/post-train interface: adapt the model in the same representation space in which it was originally trained.1a:T6e9,The experiments cover three settings: text-to-image with Stable Diffusion 3.5, text-to-video with Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, and image-to-video with SkyReels-I2V. The main empirical claim is that trajectory-independent, velocity-based methods consistently match or outperform trajectory-based baselines while being substantially cheaper to train.

The clearest efficiency result is on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B. The RVM recipe uses a 16-step deterministic ODE rollout without classifier-free guidance, yet achieves better performance at much lower training cost than trajectory-based methods such as DanceGRPO and FlowGRPO. The exact table values are not fully visible in the provided text, but the systems implication is clear: in video generation, removing trajectory storage and stochastic reverse-path dependence is a major practical gain.

A particularly important finding concerns reward design. Standard preference and visual-quality rewards can improve benchmark scores while pushing the model toward nearly static videos. The paper identifies this as a failure mode of the reward, not necessarily of the optimization rule. To address it, the authors introduce a dynamic-tracking (DT) reward based on optical flow, explicitly encouraging meaningful motion. Adding DT substantially improves VBench Dynamic Degree and also raises the overall VBench score. This is one of the most useful takeaways in the paper: reward specification may matter more than increasingly complicated policy-loss design.

When reading the experiments, pay special attention to three comparisons: trajectory-based versus ELBO-based versus RVM in cost and quality; ablations over reward-to-weight mappings and anchor choices; and the motion-quality trade-off before and after adding DT reward in video tasks.1b:T4a4,Start with the Introduction and the first part of the method section. Then read the RVM formulation carefully and track three objects: the reward-derived weight, the velocity target, and the anchor velocity. Two questions are worth keeping in mind while reading: why a single forward-noising step is sufficient for the update, and how the anchor compares to more familiar KL-style reference constraints.

In the experiments, prioritize the video results. That is where rollout cost is highest and where the systems advantage of trajectory-free training is easiest to see.
