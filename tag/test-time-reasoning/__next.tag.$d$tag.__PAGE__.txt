b:T4ac,The main contribution is benchmark design plus validation. The dataset contains 1,505 questions across 10 categories and 36 subcategories, spanning perceptual, structural, and conceptual reasoning. Samples are collected from online visual reasoning sources and civil-service exam repositories, then rewritten into a standardized VQA format with four answer types: multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, open-ended, and 2D bounding-box selection. Each item is reviewed by multiple annotators for correctness and consistency. The most important methodological choice is the caption-substitution control: the authors explicitly test whether a benchmark remains solvable when the image is replaced by a generated caption. VisReason shows a much larger degradation than prior benchmarks, which supports the claim that it is more vision-centric. The paper also studies inference-time scaling and explicit chain-of-thought prompting, and finds that both help only modestly when visual evidence extraction itself is weak. That is a useful message for multimodal pretraining: the bottleneck may lie earlier, in visual representations or cross-modal interfaces, rather than in longer textual reasoning traces.c:T426,There are four takeaways.
