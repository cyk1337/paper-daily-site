18:T497,All methods are built on a shared primitive, solve(x, c), which makes one model call with optional prior context and extracts an answer. The baseline is a single solve call. Grow performs additive recursion by extending one reasoning path; Prune performs reductive recursion by decomposing the problem, solving subproblems, and recomposing; Branch samples multiple alternative traces and selects or aggregates among them. A practically important contribution is the explicit finalization step inside solve. Some modern reasoning models produce hidden deliberation before visible output; if the token budget is exhausted during hidden reasoning, the API may return successfully but with empty visible content and a length stop. Treating that as a wrong answer unfairly penalizes methods that make more calls. The paper therefore adds a finalization mechanism to salvage budget-exhausted traces into explicit answers when possible. On evaluation, the authors use paired scoring: within each model × benchmark cell, operators are compared only on the exact set of items completed by all operators, and unrecoverable transport failures are excluded rather than marked incorrect.19:T4ac,The main contribution is benchmark design plus validation. The dataset contains 1,505 questions across 10 categories and 36 subcategories, spanning perceptual, structural, and conceptual reasoning. Samples are collected from online visual reasoning sources and civil-service exam repositories, then rewritten into a standardized VQA format with four answer types: multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, open-ended, and 2D bounding-box selection. Each item is reviewed by multiple annotators for correctness and consistency. The most important methodological choice is the caption-substitution control: the authors explicitly test whether a benchmark remains solvable when the image is replaced by a generated caption. VisReason shows a much larger degradation than prior benchmarks, which supports the claim that it is more vision-centric. The paper also studies inference-time scaling and explicit chain-of-thought prompting, and finds that both help only modestly when visual evidence extraction itself is weak. That is a useful message for multimodal pretraining: the bottleneck may lie earlier, in visual representations or cross-modal interfaces, rather than in longer textual reasoning traces.1a:T426,There are four takeaways. First, the strongest diagnostic result is the caption-replacement test: performance drops by less than 6.35% on MMMU, MMMU-Pro, and MathVista, but by 48.12% on VisReason, indicating much heavier reliance on the original image. Second, the benchmark exposes a large human-model gap while still separating models across a broad range, so it is neither trivial nor uniformly saturated. Third, increasing inference-time thinking budget yields steady but slow and saturating gains; extra compute does not compensate for weak visual grounding. Fourth, explicit CoT prompting gives only marginal benefit for non-thinking models, with GPT-4o improving by about +1.1% on average and not consistently on perception-heavy subsets. The paper also reports a clear model-size trend: larger models do better, but even the strongest ones remain well below human performance.
