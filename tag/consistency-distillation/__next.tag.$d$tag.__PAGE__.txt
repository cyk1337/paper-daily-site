a:Taa1,方法核心是 Consistency Forcing（CForce）。训练起点是一个预训练好的 dLLM。作者先用该模型在自己的解码策略下生成 self-rollout trajectories，而不是使用 teacher rollout 或 oracle trajectory。这样做的原因是，学生在推理时真正会遇到的状态分布，来自它自己在 threshold decoding 下逐步 reveal token 的轨迹；如果训练时看的是别的轨迹，尤其是信息条件更优的 teacher 轨迹，蒸馏目标就会偏离真实推理状态。

接着，作者不按 native denoising step 逐步对齐，而是把轨迹按“每揭示固定数量的新 token”切成若干 stage。这样做有两个考虑：一是存储 native step 全轨迹成本高；二是 reveal-count 更直接对应高并行解码下的承诺强度。对于每一对相邻 stage，训练目标是让早期 stage 在 still-masked positions 上的预测分布，去匹配同一模型在后期 stage 的 stop-gradient 预测。这里没有冻结 teacher，teacher 就是同一个模型在更后状态下的输出。直观上，后期状态拥有更多上下文，尤其 edit-capable 模型还可能经过 T2T refinement，因此它对某些位置的判断更可信，可以反过来监督早期 M2T 预测。

关键损失设计有两部分。第一部分是 Confidence Adaptive KL Divergence（CAD），用于 still-masked positions 的分布对齐。作者没有固定用 forward KL 或 reverse KL，而是根据 later-stage prediction confidence 动态插值。原因在于两者偏好不同：forward KL 更覆盖 teacher 支持集，reverse KL 更偏 mode-seeking。后期预测若置信度高，更适合强调 mode-seeking；若置信度低，则需要更保守地保留分布质量。CAD 试图把这两种偏好按置信度自适应结合。第二部分是 cross-entropy anchor，用于 reveal-boundary token，也就是那些在当前阶段即将被提交或刚被揭示的 token。这个 CE 项的作用是稳定 token commitment，防止纯分布对齐把模型推向“看起来和后期一致，但不敢明确提交”的状态。

此外，作者加入了 reveal difficulty curriculum。因为越早期、越高并行的阶段越难，直接在最激进的 reveal 设置上做相邻阶段蒸馏容易不稳定。课程学习让模型先在较容易的 reveal 条件下学会一致性，再逐步过渡到更难的高并行情形。理论部分则给出一个上界分析：早期阶段预测误差可由相邻阶段分布漂移和 reveal-boundary token error 两项控制，这正好对应 CAD 和 CE anchor。这个理论不是完整收敛证明，但它把损失设计和误差来源一一对应起来，解释了为什么不是只用 KL，也不是只用 CE。b:T436,This is a meaningful contribution to alternative language modeling paradigms. That distinction says something substantive about where the extra supervision signal comes from.c:T9e3,The method, Consistency Forcing (CForce), starts from a pretrained dLLM and collects self-rollout trajectories under the model’s own decoding policy. This is a deliberate choice. The states that matter are the ones the student will actually encounter under threshold decoding, not cleaner or more informative states produced by a separate teacher. Using self-rollouts therefore reduces the mismatch between training supervision and inference-time state distribution.

The collected trajectory is then partitioned into stages, not at every native denoising step, but after a fixed number of newly revealed tokens. This stage definition is important for two reasons: it lowers storage overhead, and it ties supervision directly to reveal intensity, which is what controls practical parallelism. For each adjacent stage pair, the model is trained so that the earlier stage matches a stop-gradient prediction from the same model at the later stage on positions that remain masked. There is no frozen teacher. The later stage acts as a stronger target because it has more observed context, and in edit-capable models it may also incorporate token-to-token refinements that correct earlier drafts.

The loss has two main components. The first is Confidence Adaptive KL Divergence (CAD), used to align distributions on still-masked positions. Instead of choosing either forward KL or reverse KL globally, CAD interpolates between them based on the confidence of the later-stage prediction. The intuition is straightforward: high-confidence later predictions justify a more mode-seeking target, while lower-confidence predictions call for a more conservative distributional match. The second component is a cross-entropy anchor on reveal-boundary tokens, which stabilizes token commitment. Without this anchor, pure distribution matching could improve consistency while weakening the model’s ability to make decisive commitments at the reveal boundary.

The paper also introduces a curriculum over reveal difficulty. Early-stage, high-parallelism supervision is the hardest regime, so training directly on the most aggressive reveal settings can be unstable. The curriculum gradually increases difficulty. The theoretical section supports the design by showing that early-stage prediction error can be bounded by adjacent-stage distribution drift and reveal-boundary token error, which map directly onto the CAD and CE terms. It is not a full convergence theory, but it does provide a clean justification for why both terms are needed.d:T78b,The headline results are on two LLaDA variants. The full paper also appears to include several important analyses. One is performance under different decoding budgets or threshold aggressiveness, which should show that the gains concentrate in the high-parallelism regime rather than uniformly across all settings. Another is loss ablation: CAD versus plain forward KL or reverse KL, with and without the CE anchor. That ablation is important because the method’s credibility depends on showing that confidence-adaptive interpolation is not just cosmetic. A third likely analysis is the effect of the reveal-difficulty curriculum on stability and final few-step accuracy. Finally, the comparison between edit-capable and non-edit models is conceptually important. It helps separate gains from simply having more context later in the trajectory versus gains from having genuinely improved later states due to token-level refinement.
