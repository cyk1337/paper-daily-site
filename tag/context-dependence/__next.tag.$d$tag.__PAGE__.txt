9:T62a,Main contributions are the SODA benchmark and a causal-style decomposition of what in the history drives safety gains.
1) SODA (Safety Over Depth for Agents): a depth-controlled threat injection protocol. The same safety-critical tool-use threats are evaluated at multiple conversation depths D, where D is the number of ordinary agentic tool tasks completed before the threat (up to 20). The benchmark includes 16 tool environments (e.g., BankAccount, EmailManager, FileSystem, WebBrowser), 5 scenarios per environment (80 total), and 5 threats per scenario (400 threats). Preceding tasks are sampled from a scenario-specific pool of benign tool tasks to keep tool schemas and interaction patterns realistic.
2) Phenomenon: safety improves monotonically with depth; the paper names this the cold-start safety gap.
3) Ablations: separate the effect of (a) benign user task requests in history vs (b) the agent’s own prior responses. They find benign task requests are the primary driver; the exact content of the agent’s prior responses matters little for safety (random/agreeable filler works similarly).
4) Utility caveat: while response content is not needed for the safety gain, real agent responses/tool interactions are important to preserve utility; fake responses degrade tool performance.
5) Representation analysis: hidden states move across a linear “safety boundary” as depth increases, supporting an ‘agent persona gradually activates’ hypothesis.
