9:T73d,The framework has three stages. First is Discover. Starting from pairwise human preference data (prompt, preferred response, dispreferred response), the system does not directly ask for a final rubric. Instead, for each pair it extracts 5 to 15 salient behavioral criteria that distinguish the two responses, and scores both responses on those criteria. This produces a large pool of raw candidate criteria, on the order of 10^3 to 10^4. These candidates are then consolidated through an embed-cluster-subgroup-describe pipeline to remove redundancy, reduce instance-specific phrasing, and produce a compact reusable rubric set. The goal is not to reproduce every local rationale, but to recover reusable behavioral dimensions that repeatedly explain human preferences. Second is Audit. The induced rubric-conditioned judge is evaluated along four axes: structural adequacy, reliability, preference fit, and adversarial robustness. Structural adequacy checks whether criteria are atomic, non-overlapping, and behaviorally covering. Reliability measures agreement across judges or prompt templates. Preference fit asks whether the rubric adds predictive value for human preferences beyond a no-rubric prior. Adversarial robustness tests whether high scores can be obtained while violating the intended construct. This decomposition is one of the strongest parts of the paper because it separates agreement from validity and ordinary preference prediction from exploit resistance. Third is Repair. The paper proposes two targeted edits: preference-rank selection, which keeps criteria that materially help preference prediction, and reliability-constrained refinement, which improves robustness while controlling degradation in agreement. The key idea throughout is to optimize the rubric as an editable specification rather than retrain the judge itself.a:T472,The main empirical finding is that no rubric source dominates all four audit axes. Handwritten rubrics, published policy-style specifications, and automatically discovered rubrics each have different failure modes. Some look reasonable structurally but do not fit human preferences well; others predict preferences on ordinary examples but remain easy to exploit adversarially. The discovered PReMISE rubrics are reported as the only source that scores non-trivially on per-prompt applicability, source-distribution specificity, and effective dimensionality at the same time, suggesting that the mined criteria are more than generic evaluation buzzwords. In the repair stage, preference-rank selection improves paired-response judge accuracy from 65.0% to 68.6%, averaged across three judges and two prompt templates. Reliability-constrained refinement reduces Verified Fool Rate from 46.4% to 36.0% with only a small reliability tradeoff, reported as Δα = -0.012.
