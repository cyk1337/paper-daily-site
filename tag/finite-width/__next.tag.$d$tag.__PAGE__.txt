b:T7b2,The analysis focuses on two minimal settings: a linear RNN without sequential inputs, and a linear recurrent unit with input injection. In both cases, the same random complex matrix W is applied at every recurrent step, with i.i.d. proper complex Gaussian entries of variance 1/n. The quantity of interest is the normalized expected squared hidden-state norm, called the signal energy.

The first technical contribution is an exact finite-width treatment. Instead of relying on infinite-width mean-field recursions, the paper derives exact finite-n formulas for hidden-state energies at arbitrary time t. The complex Gaussian ensemble makes the analysis tractable and sharp. This is important because the finite-width correction is not left as an asymptotic remainder; one can see explicitly how it scales jointly with t and n.

The second contribution is the identification of depth-width scaling regimes. The paper establishes a subcritical regime t=o(√n), where the infinite-width approximation remains valid; a critical regime t=Θ(√n), where finite-width effects become order-one and a crossover appears; and a supercritical regime t≫√n, where repeated application of the same matrix amplifies dominant spectral directions and the signal energy departs strongly from the infinite-width prediction, eventually exhibiting explosion. This differs sharply from feedforward networks, where the known critical depth is typically L=Θ(n). The reason is structural: feedforward layers use fresh random matrices, while recurrence reuses the same one.

The paper also explains why complex weights are a natural choice. They match modern recurrent architectures that use complex eigenvalues to model oscillatory dynamics, and they allow cleaner analysis. At the same time, the authors provide a sufficient condition showing that real Gaussian recurrences also deviate from infinite-width predictions, so the phenomenon is not merely an artifact of the complex setting.c:T5a4,The empirical section is mainly a validation of the theory rather than a performance benchmark. The key findings are:

1. In the subcritical regime t=o(√n), measured hidden-state energies closely track the infinite-width prediction, and the discrepancy shrinks with width.
2. Around the critical window t≈c√n, the curves begin to deviate systematically from mean-field behavior, but the deviation follows the predicted crossover rather than appearing as an abrupt failure.
3. In the supercritical regime t≫√n, the energy grows much faster than the infinite-width theory predicts, consistent with amplification along a few dominant spectral directions.
4. The same qualitative scaling law appears in both the simple linear RNN and the LRU-style recurrence, suggesting that the shared recurrent kernel—not the presence or absence of input injection—is the main driver.
5. The paper also discusses the real-valued Gaussian case, indicating that the breakdown of infinite-width predictions persists beyond the complex ensemble, even though the sharpest results are proved in the complex setting.
