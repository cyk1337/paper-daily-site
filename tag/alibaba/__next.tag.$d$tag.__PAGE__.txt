9:T6b6,The main idea is to decompose a standard MoE expert into composable internal sub-transformations and execute path compositions instead of whole experts. The paper builds on the common SwiGLU-style FFN expert used in models such as Mixtral and Qwen-MoE. In a vanilla expert, the input goes through parallel gate and up projections on the expansion side, then through element-wise gating, and finally through a down projection back to the hidden dimension. Standard MoE routing treats this entire computation as one atomic unit. PCoMoE instead turns it into a path-level execution space.

That creates a combinatorial problem: once internal components can be recombined, the number of possible paths can explode, which would hurt both quality and runtime. To control this, the paper introduces compatibility-aware gating and layer-wise pruning. Compatibility-aware gating suppresses low-value off-diagonal combinations, keeping only structurally plausible or useful path compositions. Layer-wise pruning further reduces the path space according to layer sensitivity, preserving more flexibility where the model is fragile and pruning more aggressively where redundancy is higher. Importantly, vanilla expert paths remain available, so the method augments rather than replaces the original execution graph.

The runtime design is a central part of the contribution. The authors propose a hardware-friendly execution engine that groups active paths by source and shares expansion-side operations, aiming to convert path-level flexibility into actual speedups instead of scheduling chaos. This system co-design is essential.
