Second is the dense-to-language interface, which is the central idea of the paper. Given object boxes or referring prompts, the model pools predicted depths and decoder features over each referenced region, producing object-aligned continuous geometry tokens tied to explicit object identifiers. Third is geometry preservation. The authors argue that answer supervision alone is too weak: once geometry tokens interact with the language context, their metric content can degrade. To prevent this, they add auxiliary depth objectives that require object-level depth to remain recoverable both before and after contextualization, plus a ranking loss that supervises relative depth ordering across objects. This effectively imposes an information-preservation constraint on the multimodal bottleneck.

Fourth is instance-level instruction tuning and cached continuation.
