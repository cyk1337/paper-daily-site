9:T53f,The authors first diagnose the representation geometry. They find that image and text embeddings are not unrelated distributions: their covariance spectra have similar long-tail decay, and their principal subspaces overlap much more than random baselines. This suggests that contrastive pretraining has already built a shared dominant semantic backbone. The residual modality gap is not explained by a global centroid shift. After moving text embeddings to the image centroid, most of the discrepancy remains. Spectral analysis further shows that the residual is anisotropic, concentrated along a small number of dominant directions rather than spread as isotropic noise. AnisoAlign follows from this diagnosis. It decomposes the shared space into a dominant subspace and its orthogonal complement, then uses a blockwise polar parameterization inside the dominant subspace to model radius and phase structure. Training is staged: first, a periodic phase prior is learned from target-modality samples only; second, source-modality embeddings are corrected through bounded residual updates so that they move toward target-modality support while preserving instance-level semantics.
