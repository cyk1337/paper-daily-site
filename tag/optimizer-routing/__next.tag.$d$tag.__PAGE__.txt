5:T5f5,MoLF (Mixture of LoRA and Full) maintains two experts during fine-tuning: an FFT expert (all weights trainable) and a LoRA expert (low-rank adapters). Unlike classic MoE, both experts participate in every forward/backward pass so their parameters and optimizer states remain present and continuously accumulate gradient statistics. After backprop, MoLF applies *sparse optimizer updates at the expert level* based on a momentum-based, capacity-aware scoring function—effectively routing the update budget between experts without changing parameter shapes.
A key design choice is avoiding adaptive-rank methods that “promote” ranks mid-training, which creates AdamW cold-start moments for newly introduced parameters (missing moment estimates), often destabilizing training. By keeping parameter space fixed and only sparsifying updates, MoLF aims for smoother dynamics.
MoLF-E is the memory-constrained variant: it drops the FFT expert and routes updates between two LoRA experts, preserving the fixed-state benefit while sacrificing full-parameter expressivity.
The paper also frames LoRA-vs-FFT regimes via the singular value spectrum of the optimal update ΔW*: for heavy-tailed spectra (knowledge injection), low-rank truncation has a large unavoidable tail-energy error (Eckart–Young–Mirsky), implying a structural capacity bottleneck for LoRA; for other tasks, the low-rank constraint can act as beneficial spectral regularization under finite data,
