18:T60e,The method has three main components. First, for a fixed trained decoder-only pre-norm transformer, a layer ℓ, and a target position t, the paper defines a loss-to-go from the intermediate hidden state to the terminal prediction loss. Second, it performs a local second-order analysis around low-loss validation trajectories. The key result is that the dominant curvature of this loss-to-go is approximated by a pullback Fisher operator: start with the Fisher geometry of the output distribution over logits, then pull it back through the Jacobian of the downstream computation from layer ℓ to the output. Intuitively, this operator measures how strongly perturbations in a hidden-state direction affect the final predictive distribution. Third, the paper turns this geometry into measurable signals. Its eigenspectrum identifies output-sensitive directions and approximately prediction-null directions in residual space. For causal transformers, the curvature can be decomposed over token fibers, yielding a tokenwise Fisher-Jacobian saliency score κ_{ℓ,s}. This score is supported only on causal ancestors of the target token and is controlled by downstream Jacobian couplings, making it more loss-faithful than raw attention weights. The same geometry is then used to guide matched-budget rank allocation, structured token pruning, and low-rank student distillation. The novelty is less about a new compression algorithm and more about deriving a common criterion for compression and pruning directly from the terminal prediction objective.19:T4c7,The experiments are designed to validate the geometry rather than to maximize benchmark scores. Four empirical themes stand out. (1) Perturbation sensitivity: perturbing hidden states along high-eigenvalue directions of the pullback Fisher hurts prediction much more than perturbing along low-curvature or near-null directions, supporting the observable/null decomposition. (2) Layerwise rank allocation: under a fixed total rank budget, allocating rank according to the spectrum of the operator preserves performance better than uniform allocation, suggesting that prediction-relevant dimensionality varies substantially across layers. (3) Structured token pruning: using κ_{ℓ,s} to select tokens is more principled than attention-based heuristics because it directly measures contribution to target-logit curvature. (4) Low-rank student distillation: when student capacity is limited, preserving high-curvature directions helps recover teacher behavior more effectively.
