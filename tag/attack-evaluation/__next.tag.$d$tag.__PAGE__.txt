9:T517,The main technical idea is an agentic attacker equipped with a search harness. Instead of explicitly enumerating all possible payloads and injection sites, the paper treats the attack surface as a latent space induced jointly by the environment, the benign user task, and the malicious injection task. To search this space, the attacker is given reconnaissance tools of two kinds: tools for understanding the environment and task context, and tools for understanding candidate injection vectors and their constraints.

The attacker then performs structured reasoning over possible strategies, explores promising subspaces, and uses victim-agent feedback plus environment-state changes to adapt future attempts. A key design choice is explicit strategy management. The paper argues that without tracking explored directions, pruning unproductive branches, and maintaining search state, larger compute budgets lead to redundant exploration and weaker scaling. In that sense, the search harness is what turns prompt injection from one-shot payload generation into an iterative attack process. The study is conducted in a white-box environment setting, which makes the attack surface visible to the attacker and isolates the role of test-time search more cleanly; black-box transfer is left for future work.a:T451,From the available full text, the experiments are organized to show two main findings rather than to optimize a single benchmark number. First, increasing attacker test-time compute improves both vulnerability discovery and exploitation across heterogeneous tasks. This is the central empirical claim and supports the paper’s argument that fixed-budget evaluations can underestimate real-world risk. Second, ablations indicate that explicit strategy management matters: when search tracking and redundancy control are removed, gains from larger budgets degrade because the attacker wastes compute revisiting similar attack directions.
