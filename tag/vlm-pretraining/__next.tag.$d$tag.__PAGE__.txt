The real contribution is the analysis stack.

First, they study behavior. Low-rank alignment consistently shifts the model from a more hallucinatory decision style to a more conservative one, reducing overconfident mistakes while preserving calibrated uncertainty. This is not just a benchmark win; the paper explicitly analyzes behavioral tendencies on perception and hallucination-oriented evaluations.

Second, they analyze token-level features. The paper identifies what it calls the LS-curse: full-parameter alignment disrupts the linear separability of some visual tokens, whereas low-rank alignment better preserves the token-wise knowledge structure inherited from the pretrained vision tower. The interpretation is that full alignment encourages premature entity-level mixing, while low-rank adaptation enforces a more progressive fusion process.

Third, they provide geometric evidence. Using fine-grained and coarse-grained probes, angular coverage analysis, and manifold visualization, they show that full alignment produces more diverse and more widely spread token features, consistent with aggressive fusion. Low-rank alignment keeps the representation space more homogeneous and structurally conservative.

Fourth, they add a theoretical account. Theorem 3.1 shows that low-rank gradient flow induces a noise-weighted smoothing effect, favoring updates along directions that are robust to stochastic variation and therefore biased toward flatter regions of the loss landscape. Theorem 3.2 shows that the steady-state distribution of low-rank parameters concentrates on flat-gradient, noise-robust subspaces. The first is architectural. It introduces Turing Linear Attention (TLA) as the dominant attention operator, replacing most softmax-attention layers with a linear-complexity alternative. TLA is not just a generic linearized attention block. It adds sequence-length-aware normalization and an input-dependent output gate, aiming to remain stable when the number of visual tokens varies widely while preserving local and high-frequency visual responses. The model does not remove full attention entirely; instead, it inserts a small number of standard softmax MHA layers periodically for explicit token-token interaction and global routing. This hybrid design is pragmatic: linear attention handles most of the cost, while sparse full attention recovers some of the expressivity.

The second component is data: VISTA-Curation. For image-language data, the pipeline filters low-quality images, generates multiple caption candidates, and then selects the best one using relative image-text scoring plus textual informativeness ranking. For video-language data, it decomposes raw videos into compact clips, samples representative frames, filters clips by semantic and motion consistency, and fuses local frame details with global temporal semantics into better captions. The key idea is not just “more cleaning,” but converting noisy image-text and video-text corpora into more grounded and discriminative supervision.

The third component is training. Instead of pretraining at a fixed resolution and later adapting to dynamic resolution, TuringViT is trained with variable resolutions and aspect ratios from the start. To keep this affordable, the first 80% of training caps the maximum image size at 512, and the later stage removes that cap and introduces larger images and video data. The logic is straightforward: learn robust generic visual representations early, then spend the expensive part of the budget on high-resolution and temporal modeling later. The linear-attention backbone makes this schedule feasible.11:T5c2,The experiments are designed to support three claims.
