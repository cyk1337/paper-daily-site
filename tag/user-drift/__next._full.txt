文章的一个关键贡献是把这种 drift 变成可诊断对象。作者引入 negative control outcomes：这些变量在真实实验中应当不受 intervention 影响，而且在时间上、因果上先于 intervention，例如 race、出生背景等固定属性。如果在 synthetic experiment 中，不同 intervention 下这些 negative controls 的分布发生系统性变化，就说明 intervention 改变了模型内部模拟出来的用户群体，而不是只改变了用户对 intervention 的反应。这个诊断思路很干净，因为它不要求知道真实 treatment effect，只要求找到理论上不应被 treatment 改变的属性。

在缓解方面，作者测试了 confounder adjustment during persona specification。直观上，就是在 persona prompt 里补充更多与 treatment 和 outcome 都相关的属性，减少模型自由补全的空间。论文的结论不是“属性越多越好”，而是要补那些与实验机制直接相关的 confounders。若加入与 treatment/outcome 关系弱的属性，收益不稳定，甚至可能引入新的偏移。这一点很重要，因为它说明 drift 不是简单靠 prompt 变长就能解决，而是要做因果上有针对性的 persona 设计。

从 LLM 机制角度看，这篇文章抓住了一个生成模型常见现象：当 prompt 不充分时，模型会用训练语料中的相关性结构去补全世界状态。用于创作时这是能力，用于因果实验时却会破坏识别条件。作者把这个现象从直觉层面推进到了 formal bias analysis，这部分是全文最有价值的地方。19:T8b6,The paper casts synthetic user experiments in a causal inference framework. Let A be the intervention, Y the primary outcome, X the full user persona, and L the observed subset of persona attributes explicitly specified in the prompt. The remaining attributes X\L are latent. In a valid randomized experiment, treatment assignment should not alter pre-treatment user attributes. But in LLM simulations, the model may infer different latent personas under different interventions, so the conditional distribution P(X\L | A, L) changes with A. That creates selection bias: even if the visible persona is the same, the effective simulated user is not.

The main diagnostic tool is the use of negative control outcomes. These are user attributes that should remain invariant under intervention and are causally upstream of it, such as race or other fixed background characteristics. If their distribution changes across treatment conditions in the synthetic experiment, that is evidence that the intervention has shifted the latent user population rather than merely changing the user’s response. This is a clean idea because it does not require access to ground-truth treatment effects; it only requires attributes that should not be affected by treatment.

For mitigation, the paper studies confounder adjustment at the persona-specification stage. The intuition is to reduce the model’s freedom to invent latent traits by explicitly including attributes that are relevant to both treatment and outcome. An important result is that more persona detail is not automatically better. Adjustment helps when the added attributes are causally relevant to the experimental mechanism. Adding weakly related attributes gives inconsistent benefits and can even worsen bias. That is a useful practical point: the issue is not prompt length, but targeted control of latent confounders.

At a deeper level, the paper is about a general property of generative models. When prompts are underspecified, the model fills in missing world state using correlations learned from observational data. That behavior is often desirable in generation, but it breaks identification assumptions in causal experiments. The paper makes that intuition formal.1a:T640,The experiments are designed to establish three things: user drift exists, negative controls can detect it, and targeted confounder adjustment can reduce it. The authors report evidence of confounding across multiple major open-source and proprietary LLM families, which suggests the problem is not model-specific but tied to the generative interface itself.

The empirical setup appears to have three main components. First, synthetic intervention experiments where the same or similar initial persona is exposed to different agents or treatments, while both the primary outcome and one or more negative control attributes are measured. If the negative controls differ systematically across treatments, that indicates drift. Second, bias-direction analysis showing that intervention-dependent drift can either inflate or attenuate the observed treatment effect. This matters because the issue is not just extra variance; it can change the sign or magnitude of the estimated effect. Third, mitigation experiments where additional confounders are included in the persona prompt. Relevant confounders reduce selection bias, while less relevant ones help inconsistently and may even make things worse.

The paper is more about effect patterns than a single headline metric. The most informative details are likely the size of negative-control shifts, consistency across model families, and which confounders actually help.
