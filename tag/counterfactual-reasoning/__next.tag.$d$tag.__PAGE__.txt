9:T62d,The method, CLWM, augments a standard latent world model with a contrastive counterfactual objective over action-conditioned futures. The base architecture remains familiar: a recurrent belief encoder summarizes history, stochastic latent dynamics model future evolution, and planning is done by imagined rollouts in latent space. The key change is in the training signal. Instead of only optimizing observation, reward, and continuation prediction, the model is explicitly trained to separate imagined futures when different interventions lead to different control-relevant outcomes.

The central object is an action-conditioned latent summary, Φ(h_t, α), obtained by rolling out a candidate action sequence α from belief state h_t. Positive and negative pairs are defined by intervention outcome rather than pixel similarity. If two interventions are behaviorally different, their latent futures should be pushed apart even when their near-term observations are visually similar. If two trajectories are outcome-equivalent, their latent summaries should align despite nuisance visual variation. The paper highlights hard counterfactual negatives as the critical ingredient: these are exactly the cases where standard predictive objectives tend to collapse distinct futures.

The authors also introduce diagnostics beyond task success: counterfactual separability, intervention-outcome prediction, and model-exploitation rate.
