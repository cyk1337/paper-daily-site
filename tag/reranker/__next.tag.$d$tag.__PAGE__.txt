9:T636,The method has two stages. Stage one builds structured compositional supervision. Instead of crude object swapping, the pipeline starts from real images sampled from LAION-400M. Qwen3-VL-32B extracts a structured scene graph-like description with objects, attributes, and relations. A sampled schema decides how many objects and attributes to keep. Conditioned on this schema and a five-level matching taxonomy, the MLLM generates one retrieval query and five captions. The five levels are: full match, partial presence, attribute error, object error, and full mismatch. Z-Image-Turbo then synthesizes one candidate image per caption, yielding a graded candidate list rather than a flat positive/negative set.

Stage two performs distillation. The synthesized data first fine-tunes a reranker, producing Core-Reranker. Then the reranker’s listwise compositional preferences are distilled into the embedding model, producing Core-Embed. The paper compares standard contrastive learning, pairwise CoSENT, and listwise Rank-KL. The main conclusion is that listwise supervision is the right fit for this setting because the target is not just “correct vs incorrect” but an ordered similarity spectrum. Rank-KL works by matching the embedding-induced ranking distribution to the teacher reranker’s distribution over the whole candidate list. Another useful detail is the diagnostic on Matryoshka dimensions: increasing representation dimension helps binding-sensitive subsets much more than simple lexical substitutions, which motivates distillation beyond merely widening the embedding.a:T59f,There are three main experimental messages. First, the paper validates the capability gap between reranking and embedding. A representative example shows that both the backbone and the reranker rank fine-grained compositional candidates correctly, while embedding similarity fails. The Matryoshka sweep supports the same story: on two SugarCrepe++ subsets, increasing the representation dimension from 512 to 1536 barely changes Replace Attribute, from 0.773 to 0.777, but improves the more binding-sensitive Swap Attribute from 0.589 to 0.623.

Second, the objective comparison is important. CoSENT and Rank-KL both outperform standard contrastive learning under the five-level supervision, and Rank-KL is the strongest overall. This suggests the gain is not only from better hard negatives, but also from using an objective aligned with ordered supervision.

On MCMR, Core-Embed-8B improves R@1 from 0.375 to 0.412 over its backbone, while preserving performance on standard retrieval sets such as COCO and Flickr30K.
