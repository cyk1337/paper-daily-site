19:T970,方法核心是ZPPO，建立在GRPO之上，并吸收了DAPO和REINFORCE++里对训练稳定性有帮助的若干recipe。它有三个关键部件。

第一是BCQ，Binary Candidate-included Question。作者先定义hard question：学生在该题上的平均rollout准确率低于0.5。对这类题，构造一个改写后的prompt，在原题后附上两个匿名候选答案，一个来自教师且正确，一个来自学生且错误，顺序打乱，然后要求学生“参考候选但重新作答”。关键点是学生最终输出的答案仍然是自己生成的，不是复制候选，因此policy gradient仍然作用在学生自己的response上，而不是教师response上。和直接把teacher completion当作正样本做RL或SFT不同，这里教师只提供局部支架，不进入梯度路径。

第二是NCQ，Negative Candidate-included Question。作者把同一题上学生此前所有错误回答显式列出来，并告诉模型这些都错了，再要求重新作答。它利用了一个很有意思的训练信号：在普通group rollout里，不同采样彼此不可见，错误只是独立失败；而NCQ把这些失败聚合成共享负信号，让模型第一次“看到自己都错在了哪里”。这不是教师监督，而是把学生自己的失败变成可利用上下文。

第三是prompt replay buffer。作者只缓存难题prompt，不缓存response。难题会被反复采样训练，直到该题rollout准确率升到0.5以上才“毕业”，否则在buffer满时按FIFO淘汰。这个设计对应文中的zone of proximal development：只反复练那些学生暂时不会、但在少量帮助下有机会学会的题。只存prompt而不存答案，也是在避免把训练退化成离线模仿。

训练底座方面，作者使用GRPO，并加入DAPO的clip-higher和token-level policy gradient loss。同时采用REINFORCE++里两个看似简单但对小模型很敏感的设置：每步更多iterations，以及batch-level advantage normalization，并且把zero-advantage group排除在归一化统计之外。文中强调，这些recipe如果处理不好，会带来相当明显的性能回落。方法上的区别也很清楚：它不同于KD，因为不拟合teacher distribution；不同于on-policy distillation，因为teacher不直接参与student action的梯度；也不同于纯self-improvement，因为hard case上额外引入了教师支架。1a:T979,ZPPO is built on top of GRPO, with additional training choices borrowed from DAPO and REINFORCE++. The method has three main components.

First, BCQ, or Binary Candidate-included Question. A question is marked hard when the student’s mean rollout accuracy is below 0.5. For such questions, the prompt is reformulated by appending two anonymized candidate answers: one correct teacher answer and one incorrect student answer, shuffled in order. The student is then asked to solve the problem again while consulting the candidates. The key point is that the student still generates its own final response. The policy gradient is applied only to the student’s generated answer, not to the teacher answer. This is the central mechanism behind “teacher in prompts, not gradients.”

Second, NCQ, or Negative Candidate-included Question. The prompt includes all wrong student responses collected for the same question, explicitly marks them as incorrect, and asks the student to answer again. This turns independent failures into a shared negative signal. In ordinary grouped rollouts, failed attempts do not see one another. NCQ is the first place where the model can condition on its own collective mistakes.

Third, a prompt replay buffer. Only hard prompts are stored, not responses. These prompts are repeatedly sampled until they “graduate,” meaning rollout accuracy reaches at least 0.5, or until they are evicted by FIFO when the buffer is full. This operationalizes the “zone of proximal development” idea: repeatedly practice questions that are currently unsolved but potentially learnable with limited guidance. Storing prompts without responses is also a deliberate choice to avoid drifting into offline imitation.

On the optimization side, the method uses GRPO with DAPO’s clip-higher and token-level policy gradient loss, plus two recipe choices highlighted from REINFORCE++: more iterations per step and batch-level advantage normalization, with zero-advantage groups excluded from the normalization statistics. The paper argues that these details matter materially in the small-model regime. Relative to prior work, ZPPO differs from distillation because it does not fit teacher distributions; from on-policy distillation because the teacher response is not part of the student gradient target; and from pure self-improvement because hard cases still receive external scaffolding from the teacher.1b:T85f,The experimental focus is not frontier-scale performance but knowledge transfer and generalization for small students. The method is instantiated on small Qwen3.5-family models, with particular emphasis on capacity-limited students such as 0.8B and 2B. The tasks are reasoning-heavy, especially math-style settings, and the evaluation separates in-distribution gains from generalization to benchmark families beyond the training corpus.

Three result patterns appear central. First, ZPPO improves out-of-distribution generalization more reliably than distillation-style baselines and standard RL. The paper’s argument is that distillation can fit teacher behavior near the training distribution but becomes brittle beyond it, whereas ZPPO lets the student solve with prompt-level support rather than distribution-level imitation.

Second, the hard-question machinery is doing real work. Ablations removing BCQ, NCQ, or the prompt replay buffer all hurt performance, with BCQ and replay typically carrying most of the gain and NCQ adding complementary signal. The paper also analyzes zero-advantage groups directly: under standard RL they contribute almost nothing, while ZPPO converts many of them into learnable training cases through prompt reformulation.

Third, training recipe details matter a lot for small models. Iterations per step, batch-level advantage normalization, and excluding zero-advantage groups from normalization statistics account for a meaningful fraction of the total gain. That is an important practical point: the improvement is not only from adding teacher hints, but also from stabilizing the RL dynamics of small students.

Start with the introduction and the overview figures. The paper is easiest to understand once two rejected options are clear: logit imitation is problematic for small students, and directly using teacher responses inside the policy gradient creates off-policy drift.

Then go to the formal definitions of BCQ, NCQ, and the prompt replay buffer. The most important implementation question is what exactly receives gradient and what remains context only. That is where the paper’s main claim lives. Many methods in this area sound like RL but quietly rely on offline supervision; this paper is trying hard not to do that.

Next, read the ablations carefully. Prioritize the effect of removing BCQ, NCQ, and replay; the sensitivity to the hard-question threshold at 0.5; the role of replay capacity and eviction; and whether zero-advantage groups are included in normalization statistics. In small-model training, these details often matter more than the algorithm label.

Finally, focus on the generalization results rather than only in-distribution metrics. The paper’s main argument is not just higher reward, but less brittleness than distillation beyond the training corpus.
