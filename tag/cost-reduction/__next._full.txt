19:T68f,The pipeline has three stages. First, CoT trajectory generation: for each question, a teacher LLM produces a zero-shot chain-of-thought trajectory. The key premise is that questions with different wording but similar logical structure become closer once their reasoning paths are made explicit. Second, contextualized trajectory embedding: the question and its generated reasoning trace are embedded into a dense space intended to reflect the latent reasoning manifold better than plain question embeddings. Third, isomorphic clustering and selection: clustering or geometric covering is performed in that space, and representative items near cluster centers are selected under a fixed budget.

A useful design choice is the decoupling between subset selection and score estimation. CoT-Core only constructs the subset. The selected coreset can be used with simple standard aggregation in a fully training-free setup, or combined with stronger estimators such as GP-IRT when historical logs are available. This is more flexible than IRT-style methods, which typically tie item calibration and score reconstruction together.

Compared with lexical embedding plus k-center selection, the novelty is not the sampler itself but the representation space: neighborhood structure is induced by generated reasoning traces rather than surface wording. Compared with IRT-based approaches, it avoids the cold-start dependency on historical correctness matrices. The trade-off is also clear: it is training-free, but not cost-free, because CoT trajectories must be generated for the benchmark upfront. The method assumes that this one-time cost is amortized over many future evaluation runs.1a:T58c,The experimental section is organized around three questions: whether CoT-based representations are better than plain text embeddings for coreset construction, whether the selected subsets remain faithful in cold-start score estimation, and when the gains disappear.

The main result is that in cold-start settings, CoT-Core consistently outperforms text-only selection, especially on benchmarks with nontrivial multi-step reasoning. The selected subsets better preserve full-benchmark score estimates and model ranking. The paper also states a boundary condition: when tasks are dominated by factual recall, template matching, or shallow judgments, the benefit of reasoning-aware pruning becomes smaller because the underlying reasoning manifold is weak to begin with.
