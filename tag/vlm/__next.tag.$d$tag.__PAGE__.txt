For data, Video-HopChain does not simply concatenate single-hop video questions. Each example is a dependency chain of 3 to 6 hops. Every hop is a yes/no question about one or two moments in the same video, and each answer maps to one of two integers. The final answer is the sum across hops, which makes the task exactly verifiable by exact match. Two design choices matter. First, every question includes at least one temporal-order hop, so a single frame cannot contain all required evidence. Second, some hops are selectors: the answer to an earlier hop determines which later moment should be inspected next, creating real inter-hop dependence rather than a bag of independent subquestions. The generation pipeline first produces shot-level captions with a VLM, then uses a text-only LLM to synthesize the multi-hop question from those captions. This is a deliberate trade-off: direct end-to-end VLM question generation is harder to control and harder to verify, while operating in caption space gives tighter control over logic structure and answer form.

For training, the paper introduces Confidence-Gated Exploration on top of GRPO. Standard GRPO derives its signal from reward variance within a group of rollouts for the same prompt. If all rewards in the group are identical, the group yields no relative advantage. CGE addresses this without drawing extra rollouts. With group size G=8, the first 4 rollouts are sampled normally. If those 4 are all correct or all incorrect, the remaining 4 are sampled with a top-token mask inside the reasoning span. Whenever the model assigns more than tau=0.95 probability mass to a single token, that token is temporarily removed and sampling is renormalized over the rest. This is not generic noise injection; it is targeted exploration only when the model is highly confident and the group is likely to collapse. Masked positions are excluded from the token-level loss, but all 8 rollouts still participate in the group advantage computation.
