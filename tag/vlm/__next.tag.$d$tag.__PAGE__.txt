9:T745,The paper has two methodological layers. The first is task construction. The authors build a synthetic shape-counting task where a scene of rectangles and trapeziums can be specified either as text or as an image, with exact semantic equivalence between the two forms. The text form is Python-like code describing edges, coordinates, positions, and rotations; executing it renders the image. A key design choice is to shuffle the order of edges in the code, which prevents the text from being locally readable and forces the model to compose distant pieces into shapes. This gives a controlled setting where the same underlying problem is much easier in visual space than in textual form.

The second layer is representation diagnosis and intervention. They use model families that provide clean minimal pairs: the same decoder before and after VL adaptation, namely Qwen 2.5 vs Qwen 2.5-VL, and Mistral Nemo vs Pixtral. They first compare behavior. The VL models are much stronger on the image version, but show essentially no gain on the text version relative to their text-only counterparts. They then probe decoder hidden states with linear and contrastive probes to test whether text inputs induce more recoverable visual structure after VL alignment. The answer is again no: the decoder adapted for vision does not produce more vision-aligned text representations.

To show that stronger multimodality is at least learnable, they introduce a self-distillation objective. Instead of supervising the final answer, the model is trained on paired text/image examples so that, from text alone, it predicts the embedding it would have produced if given the image. This is the key shift: the target is an internal representation from the image pathway, not a task label. In effect, the method is representation distillation aimed directly at text-side alignment.a:T64a,The experimental story is clean. First, the task itself is validated: the same VLM performs much better on the image version than on the text version, showing that visual input genuinely makes the problem easier and that the synthetic setup is not simply out of distribution. Second, in minimal-pair comparisons, Qwen2.5-VL provides almost no improvement over the corresponding Qwen2.5 text model on the text-only shape-counting task; Pixtral shows the same pattern relative to Mistral Nemo 12B. So the gain from VL alignment stays confined to image-conditioned use.

Representation analysis reinforces the behavioral result. Both linear probes and contrastive probes indicate that visual structure remains poorly recoverable from decoder states when the input is text, and the gap between the text-only LM and the VL-adapted decoder is small. This rules out the easy interpretation that the model may have learned better visual representations internally but failed to express them behaviorally.

The final experiment is a proof of concept for the proposed self-distillation objective. Without explicit task supervision, the method yields substantial gains on the text version across difficulty levels. The important point is less the absolute score and more the mechanism: once the training signal directly targets text-side representations, the desired capability can be induced.
