9:T44a,MM-IFEval-Pro defines 4 task categories with 24 subcategories, and 8 constraint categories with 52 subcategories. Each example contains 3.0 constraints on average. A key design choice is native bilingual construction instead of translating English prompts. Each constraint has an explicit Language Support field: bilingual, English-only, or Chinese-only. English-specific constraints include case and title-case checks, while Chinese-specific constraints include character- and punctuation-oriented checks. The generation pipeline preserves one language identity from the seed instruction to the final composed query, and samples only constraints compatible with the target response language. Evaluation uses deterministic rule-based verifiers. The benchmark also treats visual instruction hijacking as a dedicated dimension, testing whether a VLM follows the user’s intended constraints when visual content or adversarial cues conflict with the instruction. The authors also build a 13,547-example RL training set covering Chinese, English, and adversarial instructions, and apply GRPO training.a:T6b4,The method, FactoSR, follows a two-stage recipe: supervised spatial fine-tuning first, then factorized reinforcement learning. In the SFT stage, the model is taught to produce structured outputs that separate observation, localization, reasoning, and final answer. In the RL stage, the authors build on RLVR with GRPO-style optimization, but replace the usual single correctness reward with a multi-objective reward design.

There are five reward components. A format reward keeps outputs structurally valid during RL. An accuracy reward preserves standard final-answer supervision. The key novelty lies in three geometry-aware rewards. The XY reward enforces cross-view planar consistency and correspondence, encouraging the model to identify the correct region before answering. The Z reward targets 3D localization and relative depth ordering, which is critical for front/back, near/far, and occlusion reasoning. The T reward enforces temporal cycle consistency and reversible camera-motion reasoning, pushing the model to remain coherent over sequences rather than treating frames independently.

This is not an architectural change to the backbone. It is a change in task decomposition and training signal design. Relative to prior spatial RLVR work, the differences are clear: it moves from final-answer-only rewards to factorized rewards, from mostly single-view reasoning to multi-view and temporal settings, and from implicit language-space reasoning to explicitly geometry-constrained optimization. The trade-off is also clear: the approach depends on datasets and annotations that support verifiable XY/Z/T rewards, so scalability and transfer will depend on how broadly those rewards can be instantiated.b:T59b,The headline results reported in the paper are gains of 5.9% on VSI-Bench and 4.5% on All-Angles-Bench, while maintaining decent general multimodal capability. The experimental story is less about one benchmark and more about whether the factorized rewards produce separable gains on separable spatial skills.

The key ablations compare SFT only, SFT plus standard RLVR, and SFT plus FactoSR-RL. The paper’s main claim is that answer-only RL helps somewhat, but remains unstable on depth, cross-view correspondence, and temporal consistency. Adding XY/Z/T rewards yields larger improvements on tasks that require explicit geometric reasoning chains. Reward ablations further support the decomposition: removing XY hurts cross-view localization and correspondence; removing Z weakens relative depth and occlusion judgments; removing T degrades camera-motion and temporal reverse-reasoning tasks. The authors also emphasize stronger generalization than pure SFT, arguing that RL encourages strategy learning rather than memorization of static patterns.

What is clear is that this is an SFT-plus-RL enhancement on top of an existing VLM, with the main contribution in reward design and spatial-temporal data construction rather than large-scale pretraining from scratch.c:T7db,方法核心是 GLANCE。它不是再训练一个小型自回归 drafter，而是在冻结的目标 VLM 隐状态上加一个 one-pass block drafter。具体做法有三步。

第一步，使用 block-diffusion head 对一个长度为 B 的未来块同时建模。这里的关键不是把整个模型改成 diffusion LM，而是把 diffusion 式并行去噪/填充能力用作 speculative drafting head：给定当前已提交前缀和目标模型已经融合好的 vision-language hidden states，head 在一次前向里输出整个 block 各位置的分布。这样 depth 不再对应 k 次 sequential draft pass，而是一次 draft pass 就覆盖 k 个未来位置。

第二步，从整块分布中构造宽候选树。作者强调，在 one-pass drafting 的设定下，宽度比深度更便宜，因为整块已经算出来了，额外扩展多个高概率前缀主要把成本放在 verify 阶段，而不是重复 draft 阶段。于是 GLANCE 不是像传统 AR drafter 那样靠多轮逐步展开树，而是直接从 block 位置分布中提取高分前缀，形成 wide candidate tree。

第三步，用一次 target pass 验证整棵树，并证明最终提交路径与目标模型 greedy decoding 完全一致。这里的 lossless 条件很严格：不是近似一致，而是 bitwise equality with greedy decoding。作者还做了运行时 gating，不是假设等价，而是在每轮验证中检查提交路径是否满足精确一致条件。

The design has three main pieces.

First, a block-diffusion head predicts a whole future block of length B in parallel. The key point is that the target model itself remains unchanged and autoregressive; the diffusion-style component is only the drafting head. Given the committed prefix and the target’s already fused vision-language state, the head outputs token distributions for all positions in the block in a single forward pass. This removes the usual depth cost of k sequential draft passes for k drafted tokens.

Second, GLANCE constructs a wide candidate tree from the block-level distributions. In this setup, width becomes the cheap axis because the block has already been drafted in one shot. Expanding multiple high-probability prefixes does not require repeated draft passes; most of the extra cost is pushed into verification. This is a major departure from standard autoregressive drafters, where both depth and width are expensive because they are built sequentially.

Third, the target verifies the whole tree in one pass, and the paper proves that the committed path is exactly identical to the target model’s own greedy decoding. The lossless claim is therefore strict: not approximate quality preservation, but exact equality to greedy output. The authors also add runtime gating so that equality is checked rather than assumed.

A particularly strong part of the paper is the mechanism analysis. The authors argue that accepted block length is governed by the target model’s next-token entropy, and they fit a single law across tasks. They then use a mismatched-image probe to show that the image helps through this entropy channel: replacing the image with a mismatched one raises uncertainty and shortens accepted blocks; replacing fused visual state with text-only hidden state hurts most on grounded tasks where acceptance should otherwise be longest.e:T5ea,Three result groups matter most. First, end-to-end speedup: on grounded workloads such as document QA, infographic QA, and chart QA, GLANCE reaches up to 2.93× speedup over plain autoregressive decoding while preserving exact greedy outputs. One practical detail stands out: GLANCE uses one draft pass per round, whereas the production-style VLM EAGLE-3 head requires eight. Second, comparison against a strong baseline under matched training conditions: trained on the same corpus and schedule as an EAGLE-3 head, GLANCE achieves 2.7× longer accepted blocks. That suggests the gain comes from the one-pass block-drafting structure rather than from extra training budget. Third, mechanism validation: accepted length follows a common relationship with next-token entropy across tasks; grounded tasks sit at the low-entropy end and therefore accept the longest blocks, while open-ended description sits at the high-entropy end and accepts the shortest.
