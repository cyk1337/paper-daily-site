9:T4a2,PreviewDiff is a training-free inference-time method for pretrained image and video diffusion models. It treats intermediate denoised latent estimates as search states. At selected denoising checkpoints, the latent is decoded into a preview and passed to a multimodal judge. The judge produces a prompt-satisfaction estimate and natural-language corrective feedback. The feedback may identify missing objects, wrong attributes, incorrect counts, violated spatial relations, or temporal inconsistencies. These critiques are converted into semantic search actions through alternative correction prompts, local re-noising restarts, and latent continuations. Candidate branches are scored, pruned, and rolled forward with a beam-style tree search. Compared with Best-of-N, the method can intervene before generation is complete and reuse partial computation. Compared with scalar reward-guided diffusion, it uses the judge as both a value estimator and a policy-like proposer of semantic fixes. Compared with video-specific tree methods such as Video-T1, it searches over corrected diffusion latent continuations rather than only over final candidates or autoregressive frame continuations.a:T517,The main technical idea is an agentic attacker equipped with a search harness. Instead of explicitly enumerating all possible payloads and injection sites, the paper treats the attack surface as a latent space induced jointly by the environment, the benign user task, and the malicious injection task. To search this space, the attacker is given reconnaissance tools of two kinds: tools for understanding the environment and task context, and tools for understanding candidate injection vectors and their constraints.

The attacker then performs structured reasoning over possible strategies, explores promising subspaces, and uses victim-agent feedback plus environment-state changes to adapt future attempts. A key design choice is explicit strategy management. The paper argues that without tracking explored directions, pruning unproductive branches, and maintaining search state, larger compute budgets lead to redundant exploration and weaker scaling. In that sense, the search harness is what turns prompt injection from one-shot payload generation into an iterative attack process.
