9:T4e1,The model follows the standard LoopLM decomposition: a Prelude P, a shared recurrent core F, and a Coda C, written as C ∘ F^K ∘ P during training. At inference, the number of recurrent applications can be changed to r. The study first uses minimalist LoopLMs under controlled pretraining settings, fixing effective training depth while varying the number of physical layers and recurrent loops. This isolates the effect of recurrence from total training depth. The paper then studies two design axes. The first is where non-recurrent computation should be placed. Output-side non-shared blocks help preserve knowledge behavior, especially when the model is under-unrolled, while input-side non-shared computation tends to help reasoning extrapolation. The second is how the recurrent core should be conditioned. Initial-state injection can hurt knowledge robustness when over-parameterized. The proposed history-state injection instead conditions on relative changes from intermediate states, giving the recurrent core access to trajectory information. Timestep conditioning lets the shared core behave differently at different loop indices. A lightweight channel-wise parameterization combines history-state injection and timestep conditioning.a:T42a,The experiments compare inference budgets below, at, and beyond the training horizon on both knowledge and reasoning tasks. A simple LoopLM can continue improving beyond its training horizon, and some configurations outperform non-recurrent counterparts under comparable training compute. The behavior is task-dependent: reasoning tasks can benefit from extra loops, while knowledge tasks may degrade. Harder reasoning examples do not consistently gain more from additional recurrence, which weakens the view that recurrence automatically allocates computation to difficult cases. Models with the same effective training depth but different physical-depth and loop-count configurations show different scaling curves. Ablations show that output-side non-recurrent blocks improve knowledge robustness under shallow unrolling, input-side blocks are more useful for reasoning extrapolation, and the joint conditioning design outperforms BaseLoop and the individual conditioning variants under extended inference budgets, with Fig. 7(c) serving as the central comparison.b:T423,LoopLM reuses a stack of L transformer layers for t recurrent steps (shared weights), effectively deepening the computation graph without increasing parameter count. A prediction head is attached at each step, enabling multi-step supervision.

Adaptive compute is implemented via a learned early-exit (or equivalently, a learned weighting over step-wise losses). The key training issue is collapse: without constraints, the model either exits too early (cheap but weak) or always runs the maximum steps (strong but no adaptivity). The proposed fix is an entropy-regularized objective with a uniform prior over exit steps to force unbiased exploration of depths early in training, followed by a focused stage that tunes the compute–quality trade-off and lets the model allocate steps based on input difficulty.

For interpretation, the paper positions recurrence as iterative refinement in latent space, and backs it with controlled studies that aim to disentangle “knowledge storage” from “knowledge manipulation” (composition, multi-hop reasoning).c:T52e,Scale: pretraining up to 7.7T tokens; open-sourced Ouro models at 1.4B and 2.6B parameters.

Efficiency claim: 1.4B/2.6B LoopLMs reportedly match roughly 4B/8B standard (non-looped) transformers on many benchmarks, implying ~2–3× parameter efficiency under their evaluation protocol.

Ablations: varying loop steps shows generally increasing capability with more steps, but training can be unstable due to exit-policy collapse; entropy regularization plus the two-stage schedule stabilizes depth usage and preserves peak performance while enabling early exit on easier inputs.

Mechanistic results: the paper reports similar “knowledge storage density” between looped and non-looped models (on the order of ~2 bits/parameter), while showing larger gains on tasks requiring fact composition and multi-hop reasoning—supporting the “manipulation over storage” explanation.

Safety/faithfulness: lower harmfulness on HEx-PHI, improving with more recurrent steps (including extrapolated steps). It also argues latent iterative traces align better with final answers than explicit CoT, suggesting less post-hoc rationalization.
