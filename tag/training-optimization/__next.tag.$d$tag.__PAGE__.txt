5:T484,The paper first formalizes KV-compressibility as the existence of a compression policy that maps a long KV cache to a shorter one while preserving the next-token distribution up to small error. The theoretical section shows that compressibility is not determined by the task or the input sequence alone: nearly any sequence-to-vector function can be implemented by transformers whose caches are either highly compressible or fundamentally hard to compress. This motivates making compressibility an explicit training target.

The proposed method, KV-CAT, is a continued pretraining procedure. During training, the model is exposed to a KV sparsification policy that masks out a constant fraction of KV slots, creating an information bottleneck. The objective combines two losses: a self-distillation loss that makes the masked model match the dense model’s output distribution, and a standard next-token prediction loss on the unmasked forward pass to preserve the original model’s dense-cache quality. The goal is not to replace post-hoc compression algorithms, but to reshape the learned representations so that those algorithms work better
