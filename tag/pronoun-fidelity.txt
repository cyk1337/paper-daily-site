17:T8ba,方法核心是用 Boundless Distributed Alignment Search 去找三类候选机制在 residual stream 中的因果子空间。作者把先前行为研究里的三种解释，重写成 mechanistic hypotheses：G 表示 group entity binding，即模型是否把“实体+其 pronoun”作为一个绑定单元来检索；R 表示 recency bias，即是否更容易复制最近出现的 pronoun；S 表示 stereotypical bias，即是否受职业-性别统计关联驱动。

DAS 的基本思想是：在某一层 residual representation 上学习一个正交旋转，把高层概念对应的信息压到某个可识别子空间里；然后做 interchange intervention，把 source 输入中的该子空间替换进 base 输入，观察输出是否按预期改变。如果替换后输出跟高层因果假设一致，就说明这个子空间在因果上承载了该机制。Boundless DAS 进一步把子空间维度 k 也做成可学习的软边界，而不是手工枚举。

这篇工作的价值在于它不是只找“有没有一个 pronoun neuron/head”，而是把机制视为 distributed subspace，并且允许多个机制同时存在。作者发现三种机制都能在不同深度找到因果子空间，没有任何一种单独解释全部行为。进一步，他们把这些机制组合成 mixture model，可以解释绝大多数模型行为。

除了 DAS，作者还做了 attention head 分析，发现 pronoun 信息到达答案位置主要有两条竞争路径：一条复制 occupation-pronoun pair 这样的语义绑定单元，另一条更像表面形式复制，直接沿最近或显著的 pronoun token 传播。这个分析把“为什么模型会在某些例子里忠实、在另一些例子里失真”讲得更细：不是单一模块失效，而是多条回路竞争，哪条回路占优取决于上下文结构和模型偏置。

实验对象覆盖十个 instruction-tuned decoder-only 模型，来自 Llama-3.1、Gemma-2、Qwen2.5、OLMo-2 四个家族，规模从 1B 到 72B。评测时不是直接看自由生成，而是比较 pronoun 选项的 log-prob，避免无关 token 抢占概率质量。这个设计更适合做机制分析，因为它把输出空间限制在真正关心的 pronoun 决策上。18:T8a3,The main tool is Boundless Distributed Alignment Search (DAS). The authors formalize three candidate mechanisms as mechanistic hypotheses: G for group entity binding, where the model retrieves an entity together with its associated pronoun; R for recency bias, where the model prefers the most recently seen pronoun; and S for stereotypical bias, where occupation-linked gender associations influence the prediction.

DAS works by learning an orthogonal rotation of the residual stream at a chosen layer so that a high-level concept becomes localized in a subspace. The method then performs interchange interventions: it takes a base input, swaps in the learned subspace from a source input, rotates back, and checks whether the output changes in the way predicted by the causal hypothesis. If it does, that subspace is treated as causally carrying the mechanism. Boundless DAS extends this by learning the effective subspace size with differentiable soft boundaries instead of manually searching over dimensions.

A strong point of the paper is that it does not look for a single neuron or a single head. It treats mechanisms as distributed subspaces and allows multiple mechanisms to coexist. The results support exactly that picture: all three mechanisms are present, distributed across depth, and none alone explains the full behavior.

The paper also adds an attention-head analysis. It finds two competing routes by which pronoun information reaches the answer position. One route copies an occupation-pronoun pair as a semantic unit, which is closer to correct referent tracking. The other route copies the surface form more directly, which makes the model vulnerable to recency effects and stereotypical interference. This gives a more concrete circuit-level story for why the same model can be faithful in some contexts and fail badly in others.

The experiments cover ten instruction-tuned decoder-only models from four families: Llama-3.1, Gemma-2, Qwen2.5, and OLMo-2, ranging from 1B to 72B. Outputs are scored by comparing log-probabilities over pronoun options rather than unrestricted generation, which is a sensible choice for mechanism analysis because it isolates the pronoun decision itself.19:T69e,There are three main findings. First, all three mechanisms—group entity binding, recency bias, and stereotypical bias—can be recovered as causal subspaces with Boundless DAS, and these subspaces are distributed across model depth rather than localized to a single layer. Second, no single mechanism explains model behavior on its own, but a mixture model combining them predicts 91% to 99.5% of behavior. That is the central empirical result: pronoun fidelity is not governed by one dominant heuristic, but by multiple simultaneously active mechanisms. Third, the attention analysis identifies two competing copying pathways, one semantic and one surface-form based, which helps explain when models stay faithful and when they drift.

The study builds diagnostic counterfactual datasets from RUFF, a pronoun-fidelity dataset with more than 5 million instances. Earlier behavioral work had already shown that adding a single distractor entity with a different pronoun can reduce accuracy sharply, with a cited drop of up to 34%. This paper uses that structured setup not to improve task accuracy, but to train and validate causal interventions.

For a foundation-model reader, the most useful pattern is not which model family scores highest. It is that the same three mechanisms appear across families and scales, suggesting that these are not isolated quirks of one architecture. Another useful point is that the mechanisms are distributed, which implies that patching one head or one layer is unlikely to fully fix the problem. The appendix is likely where the most informative details live: layerwise IIA, learned subspace sizes, and how the mixture weights differ across model families.1a:T51b,This is a skim paper and can be read in about 30 to 45 minutes. Start with the task setup. The important point is that pronoun fidelity is a deliberately minimal setting designed to separate entity binding, recency copying, and stereotype effects, not just another coreference benchmark.

In the method section, focus on DAS and Boundless DAS. Three things matter: how the high-level causal hypotheses are converted into base/source/counterfactual examples, how the rotation and soft boundaries are learned, and how to interpret Interchange Intervention Accuracy. Once those are clear, the rest of the paper becomes much easier to follow.

In the experiments, prioritize the layerwise IIA results for the three mechanisms, the mixture-model explanation rate, and the attention-head evidence for the two copying routes. If time is short, you can skip many of the per-model details and just verify the main claim that multiple separable mechanisms coexist and jointly explain behavior.
