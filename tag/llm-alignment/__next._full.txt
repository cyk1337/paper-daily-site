19:T8d9,方法叫 Recoverability-Aware Intervention Learning，简称 RAIL。核心概念是 recoverability。按作者的定义，recoverability 衡量的是：在某个任务或轨迹状态上施加 intervention 后，reward-distribution signal 能提升多少。这里的重点不是最终 reward 本身，而是干预是否让训练看到更有区分度、更有学习价值的回报分布。

围绕这个概念，RAIL 做了三层设计。

第一，它把 rollout intervention 建模成一个 online contextual bandit。context 是当前任务或轨迹状态，action 是一个结构化 intervention 选择，observed utility 是实际观测到的 recoverability gain。这样做的意义是，controller 学到的是“在当前 policy、当前状态分布下，什么干预最能带来额外学习信号”，而不是依赖固定规则。作者明确把这一步用来弥补 non-stationary gap：policy 在变，干预策略也应跟着学。

第二，action space 不是单一的 rollout 数量，而是 structured intervention space。虽然给出的全文片段没有展开全部细节，但从引言和方法定义看，至少包括在哪里 branch、采用哪种 intervention strategy 等耦合决策。作者把这一步用来弥补 non-scalar gap：很多有价值的干预不是“多采样几个”这么简单，而是要在正确的状态、以正确的方式扩展轨迹。

第三，部署方式采用 shadow-to-live procedure。先用一个短 shadow phase 收集 intervention traces 和 supervision，不直接让 learned controller 影响主训练；等 controller 对 utility 有一定把握后，再进入 utility-gated live phase，选择性地把它接入 policy optimization。这个设计比较务实，因为在线学 intervention policy 容易把训练搞偏，先 shadow 再 live 相当于给 controller 一个冷启动缓冲区，也降低了早期错误干预的代价。

从整体上看，RAIL 不是改 GRPO 的 policy gradient 公式，而是改 rollout generation process。它把 rollout 生成从固定程序变成可学习、可适应的训练环节。和基于 entropy、variance 的启发式 branching 方法相比，差别在于：RAIL 关心的是 intervention 的 realized utility，而不是某个代理指标是否高。1a:T7cf,RAIL stands for Recoverability-Aware Intervention Learning. Its central concept is recoverability, defined as how much an intervention improves the reward-distribution signal at a task or trajectory state. The emphasis is not just on final reward, but on whether the intervention exposes more informative reward contrast for learning.

The method has three main layers.

First, rollout intervention is formulated as an online contextual bandit. The context is the current task or trajectory state, the action is a structured intervention choice, and the observed utility is the realized recoverability gain. This directly addresses the non-stationary gap: as the policy evolves, the intervention policy should also adapt from observed outcomes rather than remain a fixed heuristic.

Second, the action space is structured rather than scalar. From the paper’s framing, it includes coupled decisions such as where to branch and which intervention strategy to apply, not merely how many additional rollouts to generate. This addresses the non-scalar gap: useful intervention often depends on acting at the right state with the right branching pattern, not just increasing sample count.

Third, deployment uses a shadow-to-live procedure. A short shadow phase collects intervention traces and supervision without directly affecting the main optimization loop. Once the controller has enough signal, a utility-gated live phase selectively applies it during policy optimization. This is a practical design choice because online intervention learning can destabilize training if deployed too early; the shadow phase acts as a cold-start buffer.

Overall, RAIL does not modify the GRPO gradient formula itself. It modifies the rollout-generation process, turning it from a fixed procedure into an adaptive, learned component of training. Relative to entropy- or variance-based branching heuristics, the key difference is that RAIL optimizes for realized intervention utility rather than a proxy statistic.1b:T4cf,The paper states that it evaluates RAIL along four axes: effectiveness, adaptivity, expressiveness, and efficiency, and that it outperforms heuristic intervention baselines across multiple benchmarks. What can be inferred from the text is still useful. First, the learned recoverability controller appears to adapt better than static heuristics as the policy changes, which supports the claim that the non-stationary gap is real. Second, structured intervention outperforms scalar budget control, supporting the non-scalar argument. For implementation, you would need the appendix details on the intervention space, the utility definition, and the online update rule.1d:T481,The framework treats per-problem failure probability of the policy as a random variable over sampled problem instances. By the Hausdorff moment theorem, a distribution supported on [0,1] is uniquely determined by its full sequence of moments, so single-moment objectives capture only partial information about policy behavior. Multi-Moment Policy Optimization (MMPO) jointly minimizes multiple moments of the failure distribution, an objective that maps operationally to minimizing the expected truncated number of rollouts needed to obtain a first correct response, naturally balancing average performance and attention to high-failure hard problems. A generalized moment transformation framework further unifies a broader family of policy optimization objectives—including MaxRL, which is interpreted as optimizing moments of a transformed rather than raw failure distribution—and proves that this family of objectives is strictly Schur-convex, meaning optimization explicitly pushes for more balanced success rates across problems. The practical training surrogate is compatible with existing on-policy RLVR pipelines without major modifications.1e:T42b,The paper starts from a probability-allocation view: given a prompt and a set of responses sampled from the current policy, the directional gain from shifting probability mass from one response to another equals the evaluator score gap between them. This motivates response-set-conditioned evaluation rather than prompt-only evaluation. DynamicRubric implements this principle with two components. DR-Generator takes a prompt and a set of sampled responses and produces weighted binary rubric items tailored to the actual differences within that set. DR-Verifier applies each rubric item to every response, producing yes/no judgments that are aggregated into response-level scores. Training alternates between adapting the generator to response sets from the current policy and optimizing the policy using the resulting scores. Compared with static rubrics or fixed reward models, the dynamically generated rubrics can focus on instance-specific distinctions that matter for the current update, such as missed constraints, a flawed reasoning step, or intent mismatch.1f:T460,They use the standard abliteration pipeline: build paired harmful/harmless prompts, extract a per-layer refusal direction from last-token hidden states (mean difference between harmful vs harmless), then apply orthogonal projection to remove that direction from selected layers.
