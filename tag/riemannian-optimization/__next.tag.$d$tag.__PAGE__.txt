Each head’s query and key projection is constrained to St(d,r), the space of matrices with orthonormal columns. The optimizer projects the Euclidean gradient onto the Stiefel tangent space, accumulates a scalar second moment per frame using the Frobenius norm of the tangent gradient, applies a trust-region cap to the tangent step, retracts back to the manifold with a polar retraction, and transports momentum by projection after retraction. The scalar second moment removes per-coordinate adaptivity but preserves O(d) equivariance, so the update does not depend on an arbitrary coordinate system. The paper proves several properties of this update: steepest descent in the embedded metric, scale independence, better conditioning, and exact O(d) equivariance. A key mechanism result is that weight decay has zero Riemannian gradient on the Stiefel manifold because W lies in the normal space. This explains why Euclidean weight decay can damage attention geometry while the constrained update does not move along the manifold in response to that term.
