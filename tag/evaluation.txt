18:T5f0,Setup: continuous dLLMs operate entirely in embedding space during the diffusion/flow trajectory. A denoising head is trained with regression (MSE) to predict clean embeddings (x-prediction), and a decoding head maps embeddings to vocabulary only at the final step.

Mechanism argument:
1) Measure tail collapse by comparing generated word rates to held-out human text from the same distribution, with rarity defined by corpus frequency (not model probability).
2) Explain collapse via mean-seeking regression: when the conditional distribution over tokens is multi-modal (rare token vs common competitor), regression pulls the state toward the conditional mean, effectively favoring the common mode. By the time the final decoding head is applied, each position has only ~1.02–1.05 “effective candidates,” so sampler knobs cannot recover the tail.

GRAFT (training-free) restores a discrete sampling interface mid-generation:
- During denoising, it periodically reads the decoding head to obtain a token distribution.
- It identifies rare tokens that still have substantial probability but are being overtaken by more common competitors.
- It restarts generation from noise while hard-fixing (committing) those rare tokens from the first step, letting the rest of the sequence adapt around them.
- Repeating this read-and-restart accumulates committed rare words; a final generation produces the output.
Subword-level constraints and an anti-repetition correction reduce artifacts introduced by hard token fixing.19:T50d,The paper combines community-authored data with a layered evaluation pipeline.
- Dataset: 6,191 original riddles written by community members in their native languages, spanning 62 communities, 51 languages, 45 countries, explicitly supporting many-to-many language–locale mappings.
- Validation: each riddle is verified by native speakers other than the author to ensure cultural grounding and appropriate difficulty.
- Task: open-ended answer generation (not multiple-choice), intentionally stressing compositional reasoning (combining clues) and abductive reasoning (selecting the best explanation among plausible candidates).
- Scoring protocol (core engineering contribution): for 170,520 model responses across 28 model configurations, they use (1) deterministic rules for trivial cases (exact match / empty / “I don’t know”), (2) large-scale native-speaker labeling of unique answers with a fine-grained error taxonomy, and (3) a calibrated two-LLM “jury” validated against human labels to score most remaining answers; unresolved cases are excluded from accuracy.
The design choice is pragmatic: open-ended cultural tasks need human anchors, but full human scoring is too expensive, so they amortize human effort over unique answers and use validated LLM judging for scale.1a:T548,Engineering-heavy but carefully scoped:
- Task: from 76 real policy documents, recover the requirements that the author visually singled out. Labels come from the document’s own OOXML run properties (not human subjective judgment), scored by a deterministic matcher.
- Formatting-aware parser: parse .docx via python-docx into a flat sequence of blocks; split into FormattingSpan units where formatting is constant; each span carries 12 formatting attributes.
- SemanticRole hints: apply only high-confidence formatting→semantics rules (e.g., red text/red highlight→critical_warning; other visible highlight→warning; strikethrough→deprecated). They explicitly treat these as hints and avoid medium-confidence mappings to prevent misleading the model.
- Serialization: hybrid Markdown for familiar cues plus custom tags for non-Markdown cues ([COLOR:#FF0000], [HIGHLIGHT:YELLOW]) and structural tags for document-level metadata.
- Attribution: span-level leave-one-out over cue classes to identify which cues drive the effect.
- Cue recovery vs cue meaning: a preregistered test checks whether merely preserving cues is enough or whether the agent must be told what the cues mean.
The key design choice is prioritization: preserve the cues that are both common and systematically lost (color/highlight), and validate via deterministic scoring.1b:T40f,Main empirical claims (as described in the full text; some details are qualitative in the provided excerpt):
- Prevalence: about one-third of real documents contain such visually marked requirements/exceptions.
- Downstream impact: formatting-blind ingestion increases the false-positive rate on unflagged requirements by ~4×, producing confident wrong answers rather than abstentions.
- Robustness: the effect holds across six answering models from two vendors.
- Cue attribution: nearly all measurable benefit comes from color and highlighting; bold/italic/monospace/strikethrough contribute little in their setting.
- Preregistered comparison: providing cue semantics (not just preserving cues) yields a modest gain.
- Negative results: progressive disclosure doesn’t reduce token cost; a typed layer shows no benefit for two models that only sparsely attempt the task.
For replication, the most important artifacts are the OOXML-to-label mapping, the deterministic matcher, and the leave-one-out protocol with uncertainty estimates.1c:T65b,Melange is built as a visual extension of BBQ, but the construction pipeline is more careful than simply generating images from prompts. First, the authors select seven categories with visually representable attributes: age, gender, appearance, socioeconomic status, race, religion, and disability. They exclude categories such as sexual orientation and nationality because these often lack reliable visual cues. Second, they manually filter BBQ contexts to keep only scenarios where the relevant demographic distinction can be meaningfully depicted in images.

Third, they generate images with diffusion models and apply two filtering stages. An automatic VQA-based filter checks prompt-image alignment, and a human validation stage removes artifacts, semantic mismatches, and cases where image generation may have introduced confounds. The paper explicitly tries to reduce the risk that the benchmark merely reflects the text-to-image model’s own bias. Fourth, for each unique prompt, they curate ten visually diverse but semantically equivalent images, which makes the benchmark less sensitive to a single image realization.

The evaluation protocol is one of the stronger parts of the paper. It uses three matched settings—text-only, image-only, and image-with-context—to isolate modality effects. It also includes a perception control to test whether models can correctly identify the depicted demographic attributes. Finally, the paper introduces polarity-aware stereotype alignment, arguing that earlier analyses can miss important structure if they ignore question polarity when measuring stereotypical responses.1d:T469,The benchmark contains 12,640 bias scenarios across seven categories and 126,400 released evaluation images, with ten images per scenario. The evaluation covers 23 open-source MLLMs from nine model families, ranging from 1B to 72B parameters. Three findings stand out. First, switching from text to vision reduces fairness by an average of 0.291 in FaiR.
