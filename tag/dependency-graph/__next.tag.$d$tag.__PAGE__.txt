9:T482,The core abstraction is the evidence graph. Nodes are source-grounded claims, and edges represent necessary inference steps needed to reach the answer. Graph depth is used as the target difficulty variable. GRADS then runs a graph-regulated synthesis loop with seed selection, evidence expansion, QA construction, and verification, but difficulty is not left to emerge from exploration. Instead, the accepted sample must satisfy structural constraints on the evidence graph. This explicitly decouples exploration from difficulty control: agents can search broadly over large corpora, but only examples with the intended necessary-inference depth are retained. To handle large real-world corpora, the pipeline cleans and clusters raw records and exposes unified local and online search tools. To control hallucination, each answer is paired with a rubric of atomic claims, each tied to verbatim source spans, and verification subagents check every item before acceptance. Relative to prior graph-sampling or task-extension methods, the key difference is that the graph is used as a regulator of realized difficulty, not merely as a source of raw material.a:T4e0,The evaluation spans three heterogeneous domains: GitHub issue tracking, experimental particle physics, and security.
