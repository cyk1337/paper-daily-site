9:T5be,The paper develops a “Mixture-Gated” local linear theory of self-attention by splitting the output change induced by a small hidden-state perturbation into:
- Value transport: output change when attention weights are held fixed and only the values being aggregated change; typically contraction-like due to averaging/transport.
- Query gating: perturbations affect Q, which changes attention weights and thus reroutes which tokens contribute.
- Key gating: perturbations affect K, also changing attention weights.
A key geometric point is that changing weights does not necessarily change the output: if the attended token representations are nearly identical in some subspace, redistributing weight within that set has little effect. This constrains which perturbation directions can express themselves through gating.
Building on this, the paper formalizes “gating-induced regime switching” where gating counteracts (and can dominate) transport-induced contraction. It provides spectral conditions for switching, characterizes switching directions and the largest subspace in which every nonzero direction switches, and introduces two strength measures:
- Relative switching strength (gating vs transport suppression, normalized).
- Absolute reversal severity (how strongly transport can suppress while gating still amplifies).
It also analyzes robustness of identified directions under operator-estimation error (finite-sample/finite-perturbation estimation).a:T426,Empirical validation is done by applying small perturbations to hidden representations in pretrained Transformers and comparing observed amplification/contraction with theoretical predictions. Key reported takeaways from the provided full-text content:
- Random unguided search often fails to find directions where transport strongly suppresses but full attention amplifies; spectral/theory-guided analysis can identify such directions more directly, especially when many directions must be screened.
- High relative switching strength does not necessarily imply gating overcomes strong transport suppression; absolute severity better separates “easy-to-switch because transport is weak” from “switching despite strong suppression.”
- The paper also checks how reliable switching directions are when the underlying local operators are estimated with noise.

Their central result states that, for i.i.d. Gaussian weights and calibration activations with sufficiently large effective rank, the normalized RTN reconstruction loss converges uniformly over scales to a deterministic scalar objective: the mean-squared error of uniformly quantizing a standard Gaussian variable. They then analyze this limiting objective. It has a unique nondegenerate optimal scale, and the optimal scale decreases as quantizer resolution increases. They also study a scale-invariant curvature that captures local sensitivity around the optimum and observe that it decays roughly exponentially with bit-width. The paper connects this analysis to Hadamard incoherence processing, which spreads weight magnitudes through a random orthogonal transform and makes transformed weights closer to Gaussian. It also introduces Proxy-static, a computationally cheaper GPTQ-aware proxy objective for choosing scales.c:T863,The method has two main components. The first derives Muon’s update direction from a constrained response model. The weight update is posed as a response of the parameter matrix to an applied force, with an output-side safety budget limiting how much the update may perturb the model’s outputs. Among all admissible responses, the preferred one is the maximally dissipative direction. Under the paper’s assumptions, this leads to the orthogonal polar factor of the momentum matrix, which is exactly the semi-orthogonalized direction used by Muon. This is a matrix-level geometric argument, not a coordinate-wise adaptive scaling argument. That is the key distinction from AdamW: AdamW relies on per-parameter second-moment preconditioning, while Muon chooses an update shape that is better behaved in spectral terms and less dominated by a few singular directions.

The second component derives a two-timescale momentum from stress relaxation. Standard momentum assumes a single exponential decay, which corresponds to a single relaxation mode. The paper argues that this is too crude for real training dynamics. Borrowing from Maxwell-type viscoelastic models, it decomposes internal stress into a fast and a slow component, each tracked by its own EMA coefficient, and combines them into the momentum that is later semi-orthogonalized. This yields the Bi-Maxwell optimizer. Importantly, the rest of Muon is kept unchanged: the orthogonalization step stays, and the benchmark recipe is otherwise held fixed. That makes the causal claim cleaner, because the observed gains can be attributed mainly to the memory structure rather than to a bundle of recipe changes.

A central conceptual point is that the optimal memory length should not be constant over training. Early on, gradient directions change quickly, so long memory mixes in stale directions and hurts responsiveness. Later, directions evolve more slowly, so longer memory improves denoising and accumulates consistent signal. Bi-Maxwell does not explicitly schedule beta over time; instead, it uses two fixed timescales to approximate a training-dependent effective memory length.d:T773,The headline result is on the public modded-nanogpt optimizer speedrun benchmark. Replacing Muon’s single-timescale momentum with the Bi-Maxwell memory reaches the target loss at step 2635, improving on the standing public record of 2690 steps at the time. The paper also reports a 40-step improvement over a bare tuned-Muon baseline without extra add-ons such as SOAP. The absolute gain is modest, but on a benchmark that has already been heavily tuned by the community, a further improvement from changing only the momentum structure is meaningful.

The second important result is a read-only measurement study over 8 independent training trajectories, aimed at estimating the optimal momentum memory length during training. The measured trend matches the theory: the preferred memory is shorter early in training and becomes longer later. The value of this experiment is less about the exact numbers and more about turning a usually opaque hyperparameter into something that can be observed and discussed over the course of optimization.

The paper also makes a point about evaluation discipline. The comparison is not against a weak in-house baseline, but against a public standing record on a benchmark that has been tuned adversarially over time. Since the rest of Muon is held fixed and only the memory mechanism is changed, the attribution is tighter than in many optimizer papers where gains can be confounded by recipe drift.

At least from the provided full text, the direct empirical validation is still centered on the public small-scale speedrun setup. The paper cites prior evidence that Muon itself has scaled to billion-parameter and trillion-token settings, but the new Bi-Maxwell variant is not yet shown in a full large-scale pretraining run here. So the empirical status is best read as theory-driven validation on a strong public benchmark, not yet a finalized frontier-scale recipe.e:T68d,Start with the Introduction to understand the framing: the paper is not merely rebranding momentum, but assigning distinct physical meanings to update direction and memory.
