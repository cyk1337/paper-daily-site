First, it builds a canary task for procedure execution. Each synthetic SOP contains 10 ordered steps plus a conditional 11th step. The prompt includes both a target SOP and a distractor SOP, and the model must enumerate the exact ordered steps under two paraphrased conditions. Evaluation includes step recall and branch correctness, but the key metric is invented_x: the number of step strings drawn from the global SOP vocabulary that do not belong to the target SOP or its own conditional branch. This isolates fabricated procedure content rather than generic recall errors.

Second, the paper compares compression operators at matched perplexity dose. It contrasts coherent low-rank truncation, implemented as per-tensor SVD truncation, with magnitude pruning, and uses quantization as an additional control. The design is important: by matching perplexity as closely as possible, the authors try to hold overall damage constant and test whether behavior still differs by operator. Their central finding is that coherent low-rank error induces procedure invention while pruning at similar perplexity often does not. This leads to the mechanism claim that the relevant axis is coherence times rate, not damage magnitude alone.

Third, the paper proposes a cheap data-free detector, a two-axis coherence screen, to predict which compressed builds will fail the canary. The exact implementation details are not fully visible from the provided text, but it is described as a fixed-threshold pre-flight check operating across architectures without labeled safety data. On Qwen3-8B, an SVD build at 1.17× perplexity yields +0.208 [0.042, 0.375], while the damage-matched prune arm is exactly 0.000 [0, 0]. On Llama-3.1-8B, at 1.29× perplexity, SVD gives +1.285 [0.743, 1.882] whereas pruning gives only +0.069 [0.021, 0.125], an 18× gap.
