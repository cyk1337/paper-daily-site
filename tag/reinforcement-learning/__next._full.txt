18:T5c2,Xiaomi-OCR-0 is a 0.8B VLM initialized from Qwen3.5-0.8B and trained under a unified image–instruction–response interface. The data contains both region-level samples and page-level samples. Region-level samples pair cropped text, table, or formula regions with recognition targets; page-level samples pair full document images with complete parsing targets, including content, structure, and reading order. The data engine has three components. First, an automated annotation pipeline uses PP-DocLayoutV3 to detect semantic regions, then sends crops to a heterogeneous expert pool. High-agreement labels are accepted; lower-agreement cases go through render-guided correction and re-evaluation; unresolved cases are selectively human-verified. The paper introduces Ensemble Triplet Consensus, which combines pool-wide agreement and agreement within a selected three-expert subset to rank candidate labels. Second, multi-factor sample mining uses expert agreement, model self-consistency, and semantic clustering to shape the training mixture. Third, synthetic generation targets coverage gaps and verified failure modes. The training recipe is progressive: Q-Mask-based text anchoring, multi-task continued pretraining, and Mix-RL on hard examples with verifiable rewards. The paper also compares Mix-RL with multi-teacher on-policy distillation, where distillation is more compute-efficient but longer Mix-RL reaches better final parsing and understanding performance.19:T546,The method has two stages. Stage one is REL-SFT. The authors build a 29.4K Reasoning-Evidence Localization chain-of-thought dataset, where each reasoning trace is aligned with page indices and normalized bounding boxes. Training is done across seven rendering resolutions, so the model learns to localize relevant evidence under compressed global views rather than simply reading everything at high fidelity. Stage two is GRPO. At inference, the model starts from low-DPI page images and iteratively generates a reasoning segment followed by an action: either answer directly or issue a region-enhancement call with a page index and bounding box. A tool then crops the aligned region from a higher-DPI rendering of the same page and returns it as an observation. The model incorporates that observation into subsequent reasoning until it answers or exhausts the call budget. A few design choices matter here: low- and high-resolution views share the same page coordinates, which avoids alignment drift; enhancement is local rather than full-page rerendering, which keeps added visual tokens under control; and policy learning must optimize not only correctness but also when to zoom, where to zoom, and when to stop. The paper also discusses font and point-size selection, highlighting that rendering choices materially affect downstream performance.1a:T446,The strongest results are on compressed long-document reasoning. On RULER v1 at 72 DPI, FocusVTC reaches 87.4 with about 2.9x input compression, compared with 57.5 for Glyph at roughly 3.0x compression. On LongBench, it slightly surpasses its text-input backbone, 56.40 versus 55.86, suggesting that visual compression does not necessarily hurt long-context understanding and may even help through page-level structure plus selective reading. On MRCR, the macro average over six length bins and 2/4/8 needles improves from 31.65 to 45.56, a gain of 13.91, indicating clear benefits in multi-round retrieval and evidence revisiting. The latency evaluation reports a 2.79x online end-to-end speedup on MRCR. On VTCBench, it obtains a 51.19 macro average across Retrieval, Reasoning, and Memory. General multimodal ability is preserved or slightly improved: MMMU rises from 65.12 to 66.73 and MME from 2424.02 to 2457.62. One notable ablation is font choice: at 72 DPI, DejaVu Sans improves LongBench from 54.93 to 56.40 over Verdana, showing that rendering is not a neutral implementation detail.1b:T50c,The paper introduces DSAR, the Deceptive Safety Alignment Rate, to measure safety inconsistency between the reasoning trace and the final answer within the same generation. The evaluation pipeline uses an LLM judge with detailed instructions. It performs sentence-level safety assessment over the reasoning trace, and also judges the safety of the full reasoning trace and the final answer. This makes it possible to identify cases where the model reasons toward harmful compliance but gives a safe refusal, as well as cases where apparently safe reasoning leads to an unsafe final response. The proposed mitigation is SARA, Safety-Aware Reasoning Alignment, an RL-based method with two objectives. The first objective strengthens reasoning-level safety awareness, meaning the model should explicitly detect harmful intent or unsafe candidate behavior during reasoning. The second objective enforces consistency between the reasoning trace and the final answer, so that safe reasoning is not overridden at the answer stage. The paper positions SARA against two common alternatives: off-policy SFT over fixed reasoning trajectories, which suffers from train-inference mismatch, and final-answer-only on-policy RL, which avoids imitation mismatch but leaves intermediate reasoning unsupervised.1c:T40a,The paper first compares two forward passes of the same policy over the same sampled response: one with the normal context and one augmented with a hindsight skill written after the trajectory. The skill makes the policy a better actor, but produces little change in next-token distributions. It instead changes hidden states across many response tokens. PR-OPD uses this observation directly. After a GRPO warm start, the policy continues on-policy RL. For each completed trajectory, the model writes a hindsight skill, then re-reads its own sampled response with that skill as a stop-gradient privileged teacher. The student processes the same response without the skill. Instead of matching teacher logits, the student hidden states are passed through lightweight per-layer MLP projectors and aligned to the teacher hidden states across layers. The policy, hindsight analyzer, and teacher share the same weights; the only added module is the projector, and there is no extra teacher model, skill library, or inference-time context.1d:T72d,The method has four stages. First, it defines circuit units at the output-dimension level of Transformer MLP projections, treating each dimension in the gate, up, and down projections as an editable unit. This is finer than layer-level or module-level masking and makes sparse selection feasible. Second, it performs supervised candidate-mask optimization. For prompts where the model fails, the authors construct teacher-corrected responses and freeze the model weights, optimizing only masks over internal units so that the masked computation better supports the corrected answer. The goal here is not to repair the model yet, but to identify which internal units matter for moving from failure toward success. The authors explicitly note that this stage suffers from a mismatch between offline references and free generation. Third, they introduce rollout-guided mask refinement, which is the central contribution. The masked model generates multiple rollouts, and the mask is updated with a group-relative policy-gradient objective using response-level task feedback. Intuitively, units that consistently matter for success or failure under actual generation are reinforced, while units that only look important under teacher forcing are filtered out. Unlike PPO- or GRPO-style updates that directly optimize model parameters, RL here is used to optimize circuit selection. Fourth, the refined mask is pruned into a final sparse circuit, and only the corresponding parameters are fine-tuned for targeted repair. Compared with prior work, the main differences are clear: the target is error localization rather than capability preservation, the localization stage is guided by generation-time RL rather than supervision alone, and the final update is circuit-restricted tuning rather than broad fine-tuning or LoRA over large modules.1e:T531,The evaluation uses two 8B instruction-tuned models: Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The main repair results are reported on math and medical tasks. In math, targeted repair raises GSM8K accuracy from 80% to 91% and improves MATH-500 from 75% to 78%.
