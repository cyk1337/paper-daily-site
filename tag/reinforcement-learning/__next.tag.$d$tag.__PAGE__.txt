9:T788,The core idea is Dense Reasoning Trace, or DRT. The goal is not just to make the model say less, but to make it reason in a different external format. DRT separates reasoning into two explicit channels: <visual> stores observed evidence from the image, while <think> stores compact logical derivations. Symbolic connectors and concise state transitions replace long natural-language sentences. This serves two purposes: reducing linguistic redundancy and disentangling observation from deduction so that assumptions are less likely to be written as if they were visual facts.

Training has two stages. The first is Dense Trace Initialization. High-quality multimodal instruction data are rewritten into the DRT format to create a DRT-SFT dataset, and supervised fine-tuning is used to internalize this reasoning mode. The key role of this stage is format adaptation rather than knowledge acquisition: the model learns to organize intermediate states in a compact structured space.

The second stage is Trace-Grounded Reinforcement Learning. The authors build a DRT-RL dataset using a tri-perspective verification pipeline, keeping only traces that satisfy three conditions simultaneously: correct final answer, logically consistent intermediate steps, and faithful visual grounding. They then optimize the model with a trace-grounded GRPO objective, augmented with process-based rewards and exploratory incentives. In effect, the policy is rewarded not only for being correct, but also for producing short, logically coherent, and visually grounded traces.

Compared with prior efficiency methods, this is not early stopping, token selection, or budget prediction within the same natural-language CoT space. It changes the reasoning expression space itself. Compared with standard CoT SFT/RL, it places much stronger emphasis on disentangling observation from deduction and on verifying grounding quality of intermediate traces.a:T6a4,The model is evaluated on five challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks, including text tasks and out-of-distribution video tasks. The headline result is a 5.5x improvement in token efficiency together with a 1.3-point accuracy gain over Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct under official prompting. That is the main empirical claim: shorter traces do not merely save tokens at the cost of quality; the structured format can improve the efficiency–accuracy tradeoff.

The paper also emphasizes component ablations rather than only final benchmark numbers. Dense Trace Initialization appears to be necessary for moving the model into the new reasoning format; without it, simply asking for concise reasoning would not reliably produce high-density traces. Trace-Grounded RL further improves faithfulness, especially under tight token budgets, where models otherwise tend to sacrifice logical completeness or grounding for brevity. The tri-perspective verification pipeline is important because it raises the quality of RL reference traces and filters out trajectories that are answer-correct but process-poor, or process-plausible but visually ungrounded.

The gains depend on a specific trace schema and a data rewriting pipeline, so transfer to other model families, modality mixes, or pure-text reasoning remains open. The 5.5x token-efficiency number should be read carefully: one needs to check whether it counts only output tokens or total tokens, and whether prompts are strictly matched. Stage 1 is Q-guided sample refinement. Given a state, the current flow policy maps Gaussian noise to an action. Instead of using that action directly, the method runs a finite number of MALA steps starting from the policy sample, targeting the Boltzmann distribution induced by the critic, p(a|s) proportional to exp(Q(s,a)/alpha). This is a practical choice: the method does not try to sample exactly from the target distribution, nor does it directly regress a critic-derived velocity field. It first generates a plausible action with the current policy, then uses critic gradients plus Metropolis correction to refine it toward higher-probability regions.

Stage 2 is self-target flow matching. The refined action is paired with the original base noise used to generate the pre-refinement action, and this pair becomes a source-target example for conditional flow matching. In effect, the model learns to map the same latent noise to a better action next time. The targets are therefore self-generated and improved by finite-step MCMC, not provided by an external oracle.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear. It does not rely on Q-weighted regression alone, does not require directly learning a score model for the target action distribution, and does not treat the critic merely as a weighting function. Instead, it uses MCMC as an intermediate refinement operator and then amortizes that refinement into the flow policy. The paper also includes an idealized distributional analysis showing monotonic improvement of a maximum-entropy objective and identifying the Q-induced energy distribution as a fixed point. That theory is not a full practical guarantee, but it explains why the refine-then-distill loop is sensible.14:T4f8,The experiments have two roles. The synthetic-distribution experiments test whether RFPO can preserve multimodality and fit complex target geometries, which is important because Gaussian actors often collapse such structure. The online continuous-control experiments test whether that expressive policy class translates into better RL performance. The headline claim is that RFPO matches or outperforms a standard Gaussian-policy baseline on almost all tasks.
