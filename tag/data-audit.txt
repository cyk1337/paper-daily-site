18:T4c8,The method starts from the geometry of the prediction-loss and predictive-entropy plane. A likelihood-only detector uses a horizontal threshold on loss. This fails when many non-members have low loss because they are predictable. The proposed detector instead uses an inclined boundary by correcting loss with predictive entropy. The resulting score has the algebraic form of Helmholtz free energy: token loss plays the role of an energy term, predictive entropy is the uncertainty term, and a coefficient similar to temperature trades them off, giving a score of the form loss minus an entropy correction before sequence-level aggregation. The theoretical analysis shows that when members and non-members have the same mean predictive entropy, the correction preserves the expected membership gap and its lower bound. When the loss gap and entropy gap are positively correlated, an appropriate correction reduces the variance of the gap while preserving its mean, improving standardized separation. The analysis is also extended to settings with a nonzero mean entropy gap. Compared with reference-model or perturbation-based calibration, ETD uses the target model’s own token-level predictive distribution more directly.19:T62e,The proposed audit primitive, Independence-Assumption Footprint (IAF), is anchored to what the dataset card explicitly says is modeled independently. Rather than making a vague claim that joint distributions may be wrong, the audit targets the exact combinations exposed by the resource’s own disclosure. For NPK-Korea, the card states that sex, income, education, and major field influence occupation independently and that interaction effects are not modeled. The audit therefore focuses on those axes and compares the synthetic joints against external official or institutional references.

The reference sources include KOSIS occupation-by-sex contingency tables, KEIS GOMS major-by-occupation data, Supreme Court of Korea name-by-birth-cohort statistics, and military-service-related cohort constraints. Where direct joint tables exist, the synthetic and official joints are compared directly. Where they do not, the authors use rule-implied checks, such as institutional constraints that should hold for certain sex/age combinations. Two negative controls are included to avoid over-interpreting every discrepancy as structural failure. The implementation also requires mapping free-text occupations into the KSCO taxonomy, and the authors release crosswalks, reference-table manifests, LLM-assisted label caches, and figure-generation scripts so the protocol can be retargeted to other locales and other persona datasets. They further replicate the same reference-ratio diagnostic across seven locales to show that audit outcomes are locale-dependent rather than universal.1a:T4fa,The main results are structural audit findings rather than model benchmark scores. For NPK-Korea, the paper finds that the demographic spine is broadly preserved: at the marginal level and in coarse demographic summaries, the synthetic population still looks Korean. But once occupation-linked joints are examined, the fidelity drops. The paper highlights two recurring failures: loss of fidelity in major × occupation structure, and attenuation or parity deviations in sex × KSCO structure. In practical terms, the synthetic data weakens the relationship between educational major and occupational destination, and it smooths out known sex-linked occupational segregation in the Korean labor market. So the dataset can look aligned on marginals while still misrepresenting socially important joint structure.

The transfer study across seven NPK locales and one non-NPK resource, PersonaHub, does not claim a universal failure pattern. Instead, it shows that auditability depends on locale-specific public references, taxonomy granularity, and the quality of dataset-card disclosure.
