18:T513,The paper is a controlled empirical study rather than a new architecture paper. It fixes total training tokens at each active-parameter scale and varies the amount of unique data, which directly changes the repetition rate. Dense Transformers are compared with multiple MoE configurations across 80M, 200M, and 1B active parameters, with the largest configuration reaching 8.5B total parameters. The MoE sweep varies expert count, expert granularity, and sparsity. Expert granularity refers to the width of each expert FFN relative to the dense FFN; compute-matched MoEs often use a granularity of 1/k when k experts are active per token. The data sweep covers single-domain corpora and mixed-domain settings, including web, code, scientific, and encyclopedic text, with both filtered and unfiltered variants. Beyond held-out loss, the paper analyzes domain-local degradation, regularization, router stability, expert activation patterns, and expert specialization. A key mechanistic finding is that router assignments stabilize early in training. After that point, each expert updates on a relatively small and nearly stationary subset of tokens. Repeating the corpus therefore repeats the same expert-specific token subsets, making sparse experts more prone to narrow memorization than dense FFNs.19:T4ee,The empirical pattern is consistent across scales and domains. On unique data, MoEs outperform dense models. As repetition increases, MoEs degrade earlier: at the 80M scale, dense models remain relatively robust until beyond roughly 8x repetition, while MoEs show clear degradation at 4x repetition. By 32x repetition, MoEs lose their advantage over dense models. The severity increases with sparsity and is better predicted by total parameter count than by active parameter count. Data quality filtering changes the magnitude but not the direction of the effect; some unfiltered settings overfit more, but filtering does not remove the MoE-specific sensitivity to repeated data. In mixed-domain training, degradation is mostly confined to the repeated domains, and non-repeated tokens from other domains provide some regularization. Among regularization methods, dropout and output masking are effective, especially for MoEs, while some other standard methods help little. With sufficiently high dropout, MoEs can still outperform dense models even at 64x repetition. MTP is added to improve efficiency and reasoning-related performance, and load balancing is handled without an auxiliary loss to avoid interference with the main objective. 2) Scaling laws: the Ling Scaling Laws are built from more than a thousand experiments and used as a low-cost “wind tunnel” for architecture and hyperparameter selection. This treats reasoning data as a shaping signal for the base model, not merely a late-stage booster. 4) Mid-training: the paper inserts a reasoning pre-activation stage between pretraining and alignment, extending effective context length and introducing CoT data. The implicit claim is that RL works better when the base model has already moved into a representation regime suitable for multi-step reasoning. 5) Post-training: DFT with differentiated system prompts is used to create a diverse reasoning-focused initialization, followed by Evo-CoT to progressively deepen reasoning trajectories. In RL, LPO defines sentences rather than tokens or full sequences as the action unit, aiming for better credit assignment and stability.
