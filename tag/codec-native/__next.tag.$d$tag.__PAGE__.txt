a:T547,Mage-VL uses a codec-native vision encoder called Mage-ViT. Instead of encoding uniformly sampled frames, the video is split into anchor frames, or I-frames, and predicted frames, or P-frames. I-frames are encoded densely. P-frames keep only 16×16 patches selected by motion vectors and residual energy, so static or low-information regions are not repeatedly passed to the language model. The model is trained with a five-stage curriculum. Stage 1 warms up image and short-video understanding with dense captions, using roughly four times the captioning data of LLaVA-OneVision2. Stage 2 adds image instructions, temporal grounding, spatial supervision, and more video captions. Stage 3 uses long-video captions to teach event ordering and longer-range dependencies. Stage 4 switches to codec video streams, where the predictive patch mechanism uses about one eighth or fewer visual tokens than dense frame sampling, allowing training on sequences about eight times longer than in Stage 3. Stage 5 converts previous video captions into a streaming format and trains a proactive gate on top of Mage-ViT. The gate decides when the model should respond; the full model is invoked only after a trigger. The key distinction from standard VLMs is that visual token sparsification is part of the pretraining design, not only an inference-time optimization.b:T491,The paper reports that Mage-ViT is trained from scratch on roughly 560M unlabeled images and 100M unlabeled video frames. It is claimed to be competitive with, and in several image and video benchmarks better than, encoders such as SigLIP2 and MoonViT that rely on much larger image-text pretraining. At the token level, the 16×16 patch selection mechanism reduces visual token consumption by more than 75%. During codec-stream training, video token usage is about one eighth or less of dense frame sampling. At inference time, the codec-native path gives up to about 3.5× wall-clock speedup over uniform frame sampling. Although the model is trained with H.265, it is reported to remain robust when paired with neural codecs such as DCVC, while further reducing token overhead. The final Mage-VL model is in the 4B parameter class and is reported to outperform larger Phi-family multimodal baselines, including Phi-4-multimodal-instruct at 5.6B and Phi-4-reasoning-vision at 15B, on many evaluated dimensions.
