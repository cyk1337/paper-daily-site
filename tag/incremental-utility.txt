17:T6ec,The method, One-Step-Train (OST), starts from the classical leave-one-out and influence-function view of data valuation. Exact LOO is too expensive because it requires retraining per sample. Influence functions avoid retraining but require inverse-Hessian computations, which are unstable and costly in large non-convex models. OST replaces both with a prospective one-step approximation: start from a fixed checkpoint, apply a single gradient step on one candidate sample, and estimate the resulting change on a validation anchor set. Under first-order approximation, the utility reduces to a gradient alignment score between the sample gradient and the validation gradient, avoiding Hessian inversion entirely.

Several design choices matter. First, scoring is done with a lightweight proxy model rather than the target model, which makes the method deployable. Second, the validation anchor set defines what counts as useful, so OST is target-conditioned rather than a universal quality score. Third, the paper studies which proxy checkpoint to use and finds a non-monotonic effect: around 5% warm-up works best, while fully converged proxies absorb synthetic noise and hallucinations, degrading ranking quality. Fourth, OST produces a ranking rather than a hard filter, so practitioners can choose top-k subsets under different compute budgets.

Compared with heuristic selectors such as DEITA, OST is closer to actual optimization objectives. Compared with LLM-as-a-Judge, it avoids external preference leakage and much of the inference cost. Its limitations are also clear: the approximation is local and first-order, it ignores higher-order sample interactions, and its quality depends on proxy-target alignment and on how representative the validation anchors are.18:T503,The experiments are built around multimodal mathematical reasoning with the Qwen family, especially Qwen3-VL-style SFT settings. The headline result is that OST reduces total GPU hours by about 17% when selection cost is included, and cuts the training-phase cost by about 43%. In model quality, it outperforms heuristic baselines such as DEITA and exceeds the LLM-as-a-Judge baseline by 1.8 points. The strongest empirical signal is the 'less is more' result: selecting only the top 20% high-utility samples beats the unfiltered full-SFT baseline by 8.8 points, indicating substantial negative-transfer samples in the raw synthetic pool.

The most informative ablation studies the proxy checkpoint. Selection quality is non-monotonic with proxy convergence, and the best ranking appears around 5% warm-up rather than at full convergence. This supports the paper's claim that a useful selector should be adapted enough to the target domain but not yet overfit to synthetic artifacts. The paper also includes qualitative evidence that OST removes toxic or logic-corrupting samples that damage reasoning trajectories.
