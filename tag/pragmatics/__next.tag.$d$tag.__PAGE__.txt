5:T42f,The authors treat sender intent as a first-class interpretable feature. Their main binary contrast is recognized versus evaluated: a user shares the same artifact, but in one case wants acknowledgment and in the other wants critique. The dataset is designed so that the final message suffix is identical across the pair; only a preceding clause changes the intent. Multiple lexical phrasings are used, and evaluation is done with leave-one-phrasing-out cross-validation so the probe cannot simply memorize wording. The core method is a linear probe on the hidden state at the generation-prompt position, where the model is about to answer. They then analyze depth: at which layers intent becomes decodable, and at which layers it actually starts to influence the output. Finally, they apply activation steering along a direction tied to the probe to test whether behavior can be shifted toward honoring the inferred intent without adding an explicit instruction. The central claim is not just that intent is decodable, but that representation and readout are dissociable.6:T4ff,The experiments span six models and four task families, with the paper emphasizing that the representation is already present in base checkpoints rather than being purely a product of instruction tuning. In the main setup on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, the authors use n=120 messages, extract the hidden state at the generation-prompt position, standardize it, reduce dimensionality with PCA to at most 40 components, and train an L2-regularized logistic probe. Generalization is tested with GroupKFold over phrasing templates, and chance is estimated with a shuffled-label permutation baseline rather than assumed from nominal class balance. The probe cleanly decodes intent and generalizes to cases where the intent is only pragmatically inferred rather than explicitly stated. A valence control suggests the probe is not merely reading warmth or emotional tone. The depth analysis is the most informative result: intent becomes decodable several layers before it reliably drives the output, and across models the difference appears as a model-specific readout stratification.
