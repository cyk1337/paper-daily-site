17:T6c0,The main proposal is column-tiled SVD. Instead of applying one global SVD to a linear map W, the input columns are partitioned into tiles and each tile is factorized separately. Each extracted mode is a mount (v, u, sigma), interpreted as trigger, write, and strength. The identity of a mechanism is the weight rule itself, not a post hoc semantic label.

The more interesting contribution is the evaluation stack. The paper argues that tile-local lift is a misleading metric because one-column tiles win almost by construction. So the authors evaluate mounts using full-write energy lift on real forwards: can the extracted directions explain the site’s actual write tensor, not just local tile output? They also measure weight coverage, sparse write coverage, and coverage lift over random dictionaries, plus a saturation criterion that checks whether a small number of modes already captures most of the useful signal. For residual-write sites only, they add a causal check: inject the write direction after the post-sublayer RMSNorm so it lands in the residual stream, then compare the resulting logit changes against an unembedding-based prediction using Spearman correlation and top-20 Jaccard.

A key practical point is the use of effective-path mounts. Raw mlp.up and attn.v weights fail the residual-shaped evaluation because the on-distribution computation is not the raw matrix alone. For mlp.up, the paper uses a gate-averaged effective map W* = diag(g_bar) W_up. For attn.v, it fits a ridge least-squares map to mixed-v, with a fallback involving W_o. This is an important methodological claim: if the interpreted object does not match the effective computation path, even a good decomposition will look unusable.18:T6d1,The experiments use Gemma-2-2B with 26 layers and seven linear maps per layer, for 182 site-layers in total. The corpus is built from WikiText-2 raw train: 86,109 tokens are collected and 16,384 are subsampled for analysis. Default tile widths are site-specific: 512 for residual and MLP maps, 256 for attn.k and attn.v, and 128 for attn.q, with a 64 fallback on hard layers. Experiments A and C use k=2 modes per tile, while experiment B sweeps k in {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}.

The headline result is that, with site-aware tiling and effective-path handling, all 182 site-layers pass the paper’s acceptance criteria: 52/52 for residual-write sites under A/B/C and 130/130 for the other sites under A/B. On the two residual-write sites, tiled SVD beats whole-matrix SVD, column sampling, and random baselines at nearly every depth, except mlp.down layer 25 where tiled and whole SVD are roughly tied but still pass under the ratio criterion. The paper also notes that column sampling can achieve tile-local lift around 0.999, which is precisely why local lift is excluded from the main judgment.

The coverage experiments show early saturation: on residual-write sites, coverage lift is already high at one or two modes per tile and then stays flat, suggesting that extra modes mostly add redundancy. For the steering check, the authors inject alpha*u with alpha=2 after the post-attention or post-FF RMSNorm, average last-token logit changes over up to eight texts, and compare them to the unembed-lens prediction. Passing requires either Spearman rho >= 0.05 or Jaccard@20 >= 0.05 for layers 6 and above.
