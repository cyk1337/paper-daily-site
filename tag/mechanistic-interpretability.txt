18:T78e,The proposed method, SCALPEL, is a contrastive sparse autoencoder designed to learn forget-selective features. The paper first formalizes an energy-bias view of reconstruction-based extraction. If activations decompose into multiple concept components, the marginal gain from improving reconstruction of component j scales with its energy. As a result, high-dispersion background concepts are favored over low-dispersion forget concepts. This is not presented as a full impossibility theorem, but as a useful incentive-level explanation for why standard extractors blend precise targets into broader features.

SCALPEL modifies the SAE objective with contrastive learning centered on forget anchors. Each forget example is augmented into paraphrases and rewritten variants, and different views of the same anchor are treated as positives. Negatives are sampled from retain data rather than from other forget examples. That design matters: using retain negatives helps separate target-specific structure from shared linguistic and task background, while avoiding the mistake of pushing related forget examples away from each other. After training, the SAE yields a fixed set of sparse forget-associated features. At inference time, the same feature set is available for every input, but each feature is subtracted only when activated by the encoder, producing an explicit and input-dependent internal filter.

Two details are especially important. First, the paper uses a norm-aware feature selection rule, because intervention strength depends both on encoder activation and on the magnitude of the decoder direction. Second, the theory section gives three supporting arguments: reconstruction induces energy bias, contrastive training favors target-selective sparse features, and high-scoring features admit a bound on expected background perturbation. These are best read as justification for the design, not as deletion guarantees.19:T648,Experiments are run in the controlled TOFU author-level unlearning setting across three model families. The paper compares SCALPEL against mechanistic baselines such as PCA, NMF, and standard SAEs, as well as optimization-based unlearning methods. The evaluation is multi-axis: forgetting effectiveness, retain preservation, feature selectivity, and robustness of the removed trace.

The main empirical picture is consistent. First, at the representation-extraction level, SCALPEL learns features that are more concentrated on the forget target and less entangled with retain-side background structure than reconstruction-only methods. The paper supports this with activation analyses and feature-level diagnostics, including residual-stream studies on Qwen. Second, as an unlearning intervention, SCALPEL achieves competitive forgetting relative to optimization-based baselines while causing less collateral damage on retained knowledge. Third, the norm-aware feature ranking improves over activation-only ranking, confirming that decoder magnitude matters for practical intervention quality. Fourth, ablations show that the contrastive setup is sensitive to positive/negative construction: treating other forget examples as negatives hurts selectivity because it breaks shared target structure.

The evidence is strongest in the controlled benchmark setting. The authors are explicit that this is not certified deletion and not a full solution to GDPR-style erasure. The method removes extractable internal traces, but that does not imply all target-related information has disappeared from the parameters.1a:T4de,Start with the introduction and Section 3. The energy-bias argument is the conceptual backbone of the paper; once that is clear, the SCALPEL design choices make sense. In the method section, focus on the augmentation scheme, the contrastive objective, the sparse coding setup, and the inference-time feature subtraction rule. The key design question is why negatives come from retain data rather than other forget examples. In the theory section, do not get stuck in every derivation; instead track the three main claims: why reconstruction favors background, how contrastive learning improves selectivity, and under what assumptions the background-perturbation bound holds. In experiments, read the main comparison tables first, then the ablations on norm-aware selection and contrastive sample construction.

A 30-minute skim is enough for the main method and findings. If reading more closely, the residual-stream analysis on Qwen around layer -4 is worth attention because it most directly illustrates the low-energy-target versus high-energy-background story. The main open question to keep in mind is whether this representation-level filtering remains stable on larger models, broader knowledge domains, and stronger adversarial prompting.1b:T734,The method is a six-stage circuit-discovery pipeline. They start from 21 hand-written neutral prompts in single languages and define the objective around the target language probability at the first generated token. They then introduce LIHA, a head-level attribution score computed via activation patching between a clean target-language prompt and a corrupted foil-language prompt. This identifies attention heads whose activations causally push the model toward the correct response language.

Next, they run Edge Attribution Patching over source-head to destination-layer edges. EAP uses a first-order Taylor approximation to estimate the effect of replacing an upstream clean activation with its corrupted counterpart, allowing many edges to be scored with one backward pass per sample. Because this is only an approximation, they cap the candidate set at 2,000 edges per model and then run exact activation patching on each candidate edge. Only edges with sign agreement between EAP and exact patching are retained. The surviving edges are assembled into a DAG, interpreted as the verified first-token broadcasting circuit. Finally, they validate the graph with boundary ablations: removing in-graph edges should hurt language identification, while removing out-of-graph edges should not.

The structural findings are more interesting than the raw scores. Four standalone models show a recurring deep-layer hub topology, where a small number of late heads appear to broadcast language identity forward. The Pythia models also show more explicit multi-hop routing chains. By contrast, Qwen2.5-1.5B Base and Instruct fail the same necessity/irrelevance separation and also have the weakest EAP-to-exact correlation. The authors interpret this conservatively as a pipeline-reliability issue rather than evidence that Qwen lacks such circuits.1c:T505,The study covers six models from four families, roughly 560M to 2.8B parameters: GPT-2, BLOOM-560M, Pythia-1B, Pythia-2.8B, and Qwen2.5-1.5B Base/Instruct. The edge search space grows with model size, from 792 scored edges in GPT-2 to 15,872 in Pythia-2.8B. For tractability, every model is truncated to a 2,000-edge candidate pool before exact verification. After sign-consistent exact patching, final graph sizes range from 816 edges for GPT-2 to 1,016 for Qwen2.5-1.5B.

The most important empirical result is not task performance but validation quality. The paper reports EAP-to-exact-patching correlations as a sanity check on the attribution stage. The four standalone models show stronger agreement and cleaner boundary-ablation behavior, which makes their extracted graphs more credible as functional circuits. The Qwen pair shows weaker correlation and weaker necessity/irrelevance separation, so those graphs should be treated with caution. Another useful aspect is that the analysis goes beyond listing important heads: it verifies head-to-layer edges, which is closer to a causal routing account.

That said, the dataset is tiny, only 21 prompts, so the claims should be read as a careful case study rather than a broad statistical statement about multilingual pretraining.1d:T40d,The method has three parts. First, the authors run activation patching across 25 models from 8 LLM families. They corrupt visual inputs with Gaussian noise or image swaps, cache clean and corrupted activations, then patch clean activations back into the corrupted run by layer and token category. The restoration score measures how much a patched site recovers the clean behavior. This identifies an early L0 attention-routing circuit in a subset of VQ-tokenized unified VLMs. Second, they formalize the mechanism with a three-gate diagnostic: residual-stream divergence, L0 attention-routing concentration, and codebook off-manifold rate.
