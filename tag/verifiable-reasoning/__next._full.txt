In the answer-conditioned condition, the generator sees both the problem and the gold answer, is asked to reach that answer, and the same correctness filter is applied. The setup fixes the generator, problem set, filter, training recipe, and data budget, so the intervention is isolated.

The mechanism analysis separates answer visibility from the instruction to rationalize. Merely showing the answer under a neutral instruction is much less harmful; the main damage comes from asking the model to derive toward the known answer. The observable symptom is early answer declaration. Answer-conditioned chains tend to state the answer near the beginning and then fill in the explanation. This is an off-policy shift: the training traces no longer match the model’s own search distribution when the answer is unknown. After fine-tuning, the student inherits this habit in its own generations. The method has three main components.

The first is a two-level group-relative update. On the Solver side, the update is close to GRPO, but replaces standard-deviation normalization with mean normalization so that the gradient better matches the MLRL weighted-pass@k objective. This gives rare successful trajectories larger weight. On the Proposer side, advantages are defined over candidate specifications based on the distribution of Solver outcomes, with the aim of generating tasks near the Solver’s competence frontier rather than trivially easy or impossible ones.

The second is iterative self-distilled SFT. The authors argue that sparse-reward exploration alone causes the Proposer to drift away from the valid-output manifold. They therefore repeatedly project the model back onto a region that reliably produces valid specifications and solutions before full RL. This is presented as a stabilizing requirement, not a minor warm-start trick.

The third is a UCB-guided Curriculum DAG. Newly proposed tasks are admitted only if they pass strict filtering, novelty checks, and Solver verification. UCB is used to select seeds from the existing DAG, balancing exploration and exploitation while preventing curriculum poisoning.
