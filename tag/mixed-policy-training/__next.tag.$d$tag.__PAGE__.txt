9:T8f7,方法叫 ActGuide-RL。核心思想不是把 action data 当监督标签直接 imitation，而是在 RL rollout 过程中把它作为“计划式参考引导”注入，帮助策略访问原本到不了的高价值状态。作者强调两件事：一是 guidance 只在必要时介入，二是 guided rollout 不能停留在外部辅助层面，而要被内部化回 unguided policy。

论文先给出 reachability dynamics 的形式化。策略在某个状态之后还能成功的上界记为 Ψ(s)，再定义 effective state-visiting mass 来衡量 rollout 过程中还剩多少“成功潜力”。当策略不断进入低 Ψ 的状态，质量会乘法式衰减；一旦 reward state 落在 out-region，普通 group RL 就拿不到正负对比，优势估计塌缩。这一套形式化虽然不是完整可计算理论，但足够解释为什么 zero RL 在难任务上会失效。

在此基础上，ActGuide-RL 引入 action guidance。给定任务和可用的动作轨迹数据，系统构造 plan-style reference guidance，在 rollout 时为模型提供一个可跟随但不强制照抄的行动参考。与直接 SFT 或 behavior cloning 的区别在于：这里 guidance 是在线探索辅助，而不是离线把策略拟合到示范分布。与给 CoT 的区别在于：作者刻意避免依赖后验合成 reasoning，因为那容易出现 rationalization，且成本高。

作者进一步分析了 guidance 的 benefit-risk trade-off。指导越强，越容易跨过 reachability barrier；但越强也越偏离当前策略分布，带来 off-policy shift，最终可能学不到真正可自主执行的策略。基于这个观察，方法上提出两条原则。C1 是 minimal intervention：把 guidance 作为 zero-reward fallback，仅在模型自己走不通时启用，并尽量自适应减少介入。C2 是 mixed-policy optimization：把 guided 和 unguided rollouts 一起用于训练，让模型把通过引导获得的探索收益内化回自身策略，而不是长期依赖外部提示。

从方法定位看，它介于三类方案之间：不是纯 RL，因为用了外部动作参考；不是纯 imitation，因为优化目标仍是环境 reward；也不是标准 SFT+RL 两阶段，因为没有先做冷启动监督拟合。这是它最有意思的地方。a:T661,The method, ActGuide-RL, uses action data as plan-style reference guidance during RL rollouts. The goal is not to imitate demonstrations offline, but to help the policy cross reachability barriers online so that reward-bearing states become visitable.

The paper first formalizes the problem through reachability dynamics. Let Ψ(s) denote the supremum success probability achievable from state s under any continuation policy. Based on this, the authors define an effective state-visiting mass that tracks how much remaining success potential is preserved along rollouts. When trajectories drift into low-Ψ regions, this mass decays multiplicatively. In hard tasks, the base policy may never reach states from which success is possible, which explains why zero-shot RL produces no learning signal.

ActGuide-RL then injects action guidance as a reference plan. The model can use this guidance to navigate toward promising states, but the guidance is not meant to become a permanent crutch. The paper emphasizes a benefit-risk trade-off: stronger guidance improves exploration, but it also increases off-policy distribution shift and may prevent the policy from internalizing the behavior.

This leads to two design principles. First, minimal intervention: guidance should act as a zero-reward fallback and be used adaptively only when necessary. Second, mixed-policy optimization: guided and unguided rollouts are jointly optimized so that the gains from guided exploration are absorbed into the unguided policy itself. This makes the method distinct from pure RL, pure behavior cloning, and the standard two-stage SFT+RL pipeline.b:T4f7,Experiments are conducted on search-agent benchmarks with different base models, difficulty levels, and both in-domain and out-of-domain settings. The main result is consistent improvement over zero RL, with larger gains on harder tasks where unguided RL fails to discover reward states.

For Qwen3-4B-Instruct, the reported gains over zero RL are +10.68 points on GAIA, +27.79 on WebWalkerQA, +19.00 on XBench, and +5.15 on BC-ZH. These are large enough to suggest that the method is solving a reachability bottleneck rather than merely polishing already-solvable tasks.

Another important result is that ActGuide-RL performs on par with the SFT+RL pipeline even without any cold-start initialization. That is the strongest practical claim in the paper, because reducing dependence on warm-start supervision is the whole point of the method.

The most important ablations concern guidance strength and policy mixing. The paper argues that more guidance is not always better; the best regime is to use guidance as a minimal fallback. A second key question is whether mixed-policy training truly improves the unguided policy, rather than only making the guided policy look better. Any separate reporting of guided versus unguided evaluation is especially informative here.c:T460,This is a good skim paper. Start with the introduction and the motivating figure to understand the in-region versus out-region distinction. Then read the reachability-dynamics section just deeply enough to grasp why zero-reward trajectories produce no useful gradient under group-based RL. After that, focus on the actual mechanics of action guidance, the minimal-intervention rule, and mixed-policy optimization. Finish with the main result tables and the ablations on guidance strength.

Three questions are worth keeping in mind. First, is this genuinely better than repackaged imitation learning, or does it preserve the exploration and credit-assignment advantages of RL? Second, how sensitive is it to the quality and coverage of the action data? Third, if the base model becomes much stronger, does the gain shrink quickly, implying that this is mainly a cold-start remedy rather than a general training paradigm?

A solid first pass should take about 25 to 35 minutes.
