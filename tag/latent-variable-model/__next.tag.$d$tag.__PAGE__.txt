9:T592,Cola2 uses a two-level hierarchy. The upper level dynamically segments text into semantic spans and compresses each span into continuous latents. The lower level uses a block-causal masked diffusion decoder to realize text. There are four key mechanisms. First, semantic segmentation is dynamic rather than fixed-size chunking, so the latent sequence can be shortened while better tracking semantic boundaries. Second, the compression rate is controlled by an auxiliary-loss-free negative-feedback mechanism, reducing dependence on manually tuned loss weights. Third, representation learning combines reconstruction and generation supervision: reconstruction encourages recoverable span-level information, while generation supervision makes the latents predictive beyond local copying. Fourth, representation learning is decoupled from prior fitting. The token-prediction loss is backpropagated through the latents into the encoder, allowing the encoder and decoder to co-adapt. The Diffusion Transformer prior learns the current latent distribution with Flow Matching, but its gradients do not update the encoder. At inference time, generated text is re-encoded block by block under a fixed span layout, keeping latent history aligned with the actual text context. Classifier-free guidance controls the strength of latent conditioning, giving a runtime knob between semantic adherence and autonomous context-based generation.a:T414,The paper reports two main experimental tracks.
