19:T7d9,The central object is a scenario-mediated skill graph. Skills are collected from ClawHub and public GitHub repositories, with the intent of grounding the graph in real command-line practice rather than synthetic taxonomies. Each skill is annotated with a precondition scenario, describing when the skill is applicable, and a postcondition scenario, describing the state after execution. Semantically compatible scenarios are then linked, producing a directed graph where scenarios are nodes and skills are transitions.

A sampled path in this graph defines a high-level workflow: a sequence of skills together with the intermediate states they should traverse. The key design choice is to control trajectory structure first and derive concrete tasks second. This is different from prior synthesis methods that start from a task seed and hope the resulting trajectories are diverse. Here, the workflow is the primary object, and the task instance is generated to realize that workflow.

The second major component is a multi-agent harness that turns an abstract workflow into an executable task instance. Different agents generate the environment, formulate the natural-language goal, produce an intended solution, and verify solvability and quality. Two validation layers matter: oracle-based verification ensures the task is actually solvable, and rubric-based evaluation checks task quality, consistency, and alignment with the sampled path. The paper also includes practical graph-construction steps such as scenario deduplication/merging and LLM-verified bridges, which are important because a graph that is too sparse will not support long workflows, while a graph that is too noisy will produce incoherent compositions.

A notable modeling choice is using scenarios as intermediate nodes instead of directly connecting skills to skills. This makes the graph closer to a state-transition abstraction, which is better suited for expressing applicability conditions, compositionality, and future expansion.1a:T678,The strongest evidence is in the scale and verification statistics of the synthesis pipeline. In one fully automated run, SkillSynth sampled 3,721 paths and produced 3,560 verified task instances, with a 95.7% oracle pass rate and an average cost of $27.3 per verified task. The resulting graph contains 57,214 filtered skills, 82,073 deduplicated/merged scenarios, and 185,529 LLM-verified bridges. This is large enough to function as a reusable synthesis substrate rather than a small proof of concept.

On task difficulty and diversity, the paper compares SkillSynth against single-skill seeds and randomly composed multi-skill seeds. SkillSynth tasks are harder and more varied: Claude Opus 4.6 needs 37 steps on average to solve them, and 121 tasks remain unsolved even after three independent rollouts. That result suggests the graph-guided workflows are inducing genuinely more complex multi-step behavior rather than merely longer task descriptions.

For downstream training value, the authors collect trajectories on the synthesized tasks, fine-tune Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B, and evaluate on Terminal-Bench 1.0 and 2.0. The reported conclusion is that the added trajectory diversity translates into better data efficiency and consistent performance gains across model scales. The provided text does not include the full benchmark table, so the exact deltas are not recoverable here, but the paper frames the gain as coming from distribution quality rather than raw sample count.
