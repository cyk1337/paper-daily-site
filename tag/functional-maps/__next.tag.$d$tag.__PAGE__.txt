a:T516,The approach imports the functional map framework from computational geometry. For each modality, embeddings over a shared dataset are used to build a weighted symmetric kNN graph (k=15) with Gaussian weights and an adaptive bandwidth σ set by the mean k-th neighbor distance. From this graph, the normalized Laplacian L=D^{-1/2}(D-W)D^{-1/2} is eigendecomposed; the leading (low-frequency) eigenvectors define a truncated spectral basis (an intrinsic coordinate system).
Cross-modal correspondence is represented as a compact linear operator C∈R^{k×k} mapping coefficients between the two Laplacian eigenbases. C is estimated via a regularized least-squares objective that fits paired anchors while penalizing Laplacian commutativity violation (encouraging C L_v ≈ L_t C), a standard structural regularizer in functional maps tied to near-isometry assumptions.
The paper highlights three theoretical attractions over ambient-space linear alignment (Procrustes/CCA): composability (maps multiply across domains), spectral regularization via low-frequency truncation, and analyzable approximation bounds under (approximate) isometry—while also noting that independent neural encoders may violate isometry, limiting functional maps as an alignment method and motivating their use as a diagnostic.b:T4d2,Setup: Flickr30k image-text pairs are encoded by frozen DINOv2 and MiniLM; kNN graphs and Laplacian spectra are computed per modality; alignment is learned with varying numbers of paired anchors.
Baselines: functional maps vs Procrustes, CCA, relative representations, plus CLIP as a reference.
Negative retrieval result: functional maps underperform all non-trivial baselines across supervision budgets. Example: with 100 anchors, i2t Recall@1 is 2.2% vs 12.1% (Procrustes) and 13.4% (relative representations), with the gap widening as anchors increase.
Spectral-geometry finding: the Laplacian eigenvalue spectra are very similar across modalities (normalized spectral distance 0.043), suggesting comparable intrinsic complexity. Yet the learned functional map C shows near-zero diagonal dominance (<0.05) and a large orthogonality error (70.15), alongside commutativity violations—indicating neither shared spectral orientation nor near-isometric correspondence.
Conclusion: a “spectral complexity–orientation gap”: independent encoders may converge in how much structure they capture, but not in how that structure is oriented, explaining why isometry-dependent functional maps fail to deliver strong retrieval alignment.c:T6ab,must_read (deep technical + guide)
