9:T70d,The method, TILR (Trajectory-Invariant Latent Refinement), follows a simple principle: identify a stable low-rank subspace first, then constrain refinement to that subspace. The authors collect hidden-state differences between stronger and weaker reasoning trajectories and study their structure across inputs, paraphrases, perturbations, and reference checkpoint choices. Empirically, these contrastive differences are highly concentrated spectrally: a small number of directions explain most of the variation between strong and weak trajectories. TILR uses this observation to estimate an invariant subspace that captures reasoning-associated directions that persist across semantically equivalent inputs and across reference settings. Once this subspace is learned, the usual contrastive latent update is projected into it, filtering out unstable components that are specific to a particular phrasing or trajectory realization. A second component, the adaptive alignment gate, modulates intervention strength based on how well the current trajectory aligns with the learned invariant structure. When alignment is high, the intervention is trusted more; when alignment is weak, the method suppresses the update to avoid injecting noisy or mismatched steering signals. This is the main conceptual difference from prior latent refinement methods: the paper rejects the assumption that the full strong-minus-weak hidden-state difference is uniformly meaningful. It treats only the low-rank, cross-condition stable component as reliable.
