处理变量 T 不是直接用 simplex 上的原始比例，而是先做 log-mixture 变换 z=log(t+ε)。这样做有两个原因：一是经验 scaling law 常表现为边际收益递减，log 空间更适合局部线性近似；二是 compositional data 在 simplex 上直接回归不稳定，Aitchison 风格的几何处理更自然。随后作者不去拟合完整响应面 μ(x,z)，而是在历史 mixture 支持域附近采用 partially linear approximation：Y≈g(X)+θ(X)^T z+noise。这里 g(X) 表示由数据状态决定的基线性能，θ(X) 是状态条件化的边际数据回报向量。θ_k(X)>0 表示在当前数据状态下提高第 k 个域占比通常有利，θ_k(X)<0 则意味着可能存在负迁移或技能冲突。

A key modeling choice is that X contains only pre-training statistics of the data pool, such as quality, difficulty, complexity, entropy, or style, and explicitly excludes any post-training model signal or evaluation outcome.

The treatment is not modeled directly on the simplex. Instead, the paper uses a log-mixture representation z = log(t + epsilon). This choice is motivated by two considerations: empirical diminishing returns are often closer to linear in log space, and compositional data are better handled with geometry-aware transforms than with raw simplex coordinates. Rather than fitting the full response surface mu(x, z), the paper adopts a partially linear local approximation, Y ≈ g(X) + theta(X)^T z + noise. Here g(X) captures the baseline performance implied by the data state, while theta(X) is the state-conditioned marginal return vector. Positive theta_k(X) means increasing domain k is locally beneficial under state X; negative values indicate local negative transfer or domain conflict.

To identify theta(X) from observational proxy runs, the paper uses Double Machine Learning and causal forests. The idea is to first model the predictable parts of both outcome and treatment from X, then residualize them and estimate the local linear effect in the orthogonalized space. This is the crucial step: it tries to disentangle “this pool performs better because the pool itself is better” from “this pool performs better because the mixture changed.” Once the conditional effect is estimated, the method performs a conservative trust-region update rather than jumping directly to an unconstrained optimum. That design is sensible because historical proxy runs only cover a limited region of the mixture space, and aggressive extrapolation would be unreliable.

Compared with methods such as RegMix, the main difference is not just a stronger regressor. The target itself changes. RegMix-style methods learn a static global surrogate from mixture to performance. CausalMix instead estimates a local, state-conditioned causal direction for how to move the mixture under the current pool. That makes it more suitable for distribution shift, transfer across model scales, and interpretation. Let A be the intervention, Y the primary outcome, X the full user persona, and L the observed subset of persona attributes explicitly specified in the prompt. The remaining attributes X\L are latent. In a valid randomized experiment, treatment assignment should not alter pre-treatment user attributes. But in LLM simulations, the model may infer different latent personas under different interventions, so the conditional distribution P(X\L | A, L) changes with A. That creates selection bias: even if the visible persona is the same, the effective simulated user is not.

The main diagnostic tool is the use of negative control outcomes. These are user attributes that should remain invariant under intervention and are causally upstream of it, such as race or other fixed background characteristics. If their distribution changes across treatment conditions in the synthetic experiment, that is evidence that the intervention has shifted the latent user population rather than merely changing the user’s response. This is a clean idea because it does not require access to ground-truth treatment effects; it only requires attributes that should not be affected by treatment.

For mitigation, the paper studies confounder adjustment at the persona-specification stage. The intuition is to reduce the model’s freedom to invent latent traits by explicitly including attributes that are relevant to both treatment and outcome. An important result is that more persona detail is not automatically better. Adjustment helps when the added attributes are causally relevant to the experimental mechanism. Adding weakly related attributes gives inconsistent benefits and can even worsen bias. That is a useful practical point: the issue is not prompt length, but targeted control of latent confounders.

At a deeper level, the paper is about a general property of generative models. When prompts are underspecified, the model fills in missing world state using correlations learned from observational data. That behavior is often desirable in generation, but it breaks identification assumptions in causal experiments.
