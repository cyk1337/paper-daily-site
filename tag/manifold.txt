17:T882,The method is MGAP, short for Manifold-Guided Adaptive Projection. It has three main components.

First, it estimates a language-prior subspace. The authors collect blind hidden states, meaning hidden states produced when visual information is removed or weakened, and treat them as samples dominated by language priors. They then run SVD on this bank and extract a low-rank prior subspace. This is a meaningful departure from prior work: instead of assuming priors are a single bias vector, the paper models them as a low-dimensional subspace in representation space.

Second, at each decoding step, the current hidden state is decomposed into two parts: the component inside the prior subspace and the orthogonal residual. Only the projected prior component is modified. The orthogonal component is preserved as much as possible. This is the key mechanism for avoiding the blunt global translation used by logit-level suppression methods.

Third, the attenuation is adaptive. MGAP does not always suppress the prior component with the same strength. It first detects vision-language conflict, then scales the suppression accordingly. Strong conflict leads to stronger attenuation; aligned vision-language evidence leads to weaker intervention. This directly matches the paper’s claim that priors have a dual role.

The paper also introduces a manifold-based diagnostic view. A bank of hidden states from normal decoding is used as a proxy for the semantic manifold, and kNN distance defines an off-manifoldness score. If an intervention increases this score substantially, the hidden state is considered to have departed from the manifold. This is not a formal theorem, but it is a useful empirical diagnostic for explaining why some hallucination-mitigation methods hurt fluency and fidelity.

Relative to prior training-free baselines, the differences are clear: intervention at hidden states instead of logits, subspace-selective modification instead of global subtraction, and conflict-aware adaptive scaling instead of a fixed suppression coefficient. The trade-off is extra machinery: a reference hidden-state bank, subspace estimation, and projection during decoding.18:T54a,The experiments are centered on POPE and CHAIR, with the main goal being the trade-off between hallucination reduction and descriptive fidelity. The paper’s main empirical claim is not just a better single operating point, but a better trade-off curve: at similar hallucination levels, MGAP preserves more faithful description; at similar fidelity levels, it reduces hallucinations more than prior training-free baselines.

The ablations support the design choices. Removing the prior subspace and applying global suppression makes manifold departure more likely. Removing adaptive conflict gating turns the method into a harder uniform suppression scheme and hurts visually aligned cases more. Moving back from hidden-state intervention to logit-level bias subtraction weakens the geometric advantage. The paper also shows that the proposed off-manifoldness score correlates with generation degradation, which supports the diagnostic framing.
