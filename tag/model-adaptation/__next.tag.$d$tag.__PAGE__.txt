a:T45c,AdaDSF has three components. First, it runs a calibration set through the dense model and computes cosine similarity between each layer’s input and output hidden states. Layers with lower similarity are treated as stronger transformation layers and receive higher token retention ratios; layers with higher similarity receive less compute. The raw statistics are converted into bounded retention ratios through temperature-style normalization, a deviation control term with β=10, sigmoid mapping, and a global correction to match a target average retention budget. Second, each sparse layer adds a small MLP router that scores tokens and selects the Top-K tokens for full Transformer computation. Unselected tokens bypass the block through the residual path. Third, training aligns sparse and dense trajectories with intermediate hidden-state matching and output-distribution distillation. Compared with MoD-like or fixed-schedule depth sparsity, the main difference is that the compute allocation is derived from the pretrained model’s own layer statistics rather than imposed as a uniform or heuristic policy.b:T75f,The method is a lifecycle-aware runtime harness called Life-Harness. It does not fine-tune the model, and it does not perform unconstrained online code search during evaluation. Instead, it analyzes training trajectories, localizes recurring failures, converts them into structured interventions, and then freezes the resulting harness for held-out evaluation.

The design has four layers.
1) Environment Contract Layer: calibrates tool descriptions, interface constraints, and environment-specific contracts before interaction. The goal is to reduce the mismatch between generic tool-use priors in the model and the actual rules of the environment.
2) Procedural Skill Layer: distills reusable procedures from training trajectories and retrieves them conditioned on the current task and state. The key choice is to keep these procedures external and auditable rather than absorb them into model parameters.
3) Action Realization Layer: validates, canonicalizes, and, when unambiguous, repairs model-generated actions before execution. It also blocks actions that would deterministically fail. This directly targets a common agent failure mode: the intent is roughly correct, but the action is malformed at the interface level.
4) Trajectory Regulation Layer: monitors post-execution dynamics and detects degeneracy patterns such as repetition, stagnation, invalid retries, or budget exhaustion, then triggers recovery behavior.

What distinguishes this from prior scaffold or harness optimization work is the framing. The harness is treated as a stable runtime interface, not as an arbitrary policy program to be searched. Failures are localized to stages of the interaction lifecycle. Evolution happens on training trajectories, while evaluation remains fixed. That makes the gains easier to interpret: they come from structured interface adaptation rather than hidden online learning.c:T6fd,The evaluation covers 7 deterministic agent environments from τ-bench, τ²-bench, and AgentBench, spanning household interaction, web shopping, operating-system control, database manipulation, and policy-constrained business workflows. The paper tests 18 model backbones, including instruction-tuned, reasoning-oriented, and agent-specialized models.

Three results matter most.
First, Life-Harness improves 116 out of 126 model-environment pairs, with an average relative improvement of 88.5%. This is a relative gain, not an absolute point increase, but the breadth of improvement is the main signal.
Second, the harness is evolved only from Qwen3-4B-Instruct trajectories and then reused across the other 17 backbones. This is the most convincing part of the paper because it supports the claim that the method captures reusable environment structure rather than model-specific patches.
Third, the method is complementary to model training. The paper reports that Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct with Life-Harness can outperform its tool-specialized derivative xLAM-2-32b-fc-r, while adding the harness to xLAM itself still yields further gains. That argues for interface adaptation as an additive layer, not merely a substitute for agent fine-tuning.

The introduction also gives a motivating gap: Qwen3.5-4B scores 74.0% on HMMT but only 43.1% on ALFWorld. The point is not the specific numbers; it is the mismatch between static capability and interactive performance. When reading the full paper, the most useful experimental detail is the layer-wise analysis of where gains come from: contract clarification, action canonicalization, or trajectory regulation. That determines whether the method is mostly fixing formatting errors or providing a more substantive runtime control abstraction.d:T434,This is a skim paper. Then read the four-layer design carefully and ask what failure mode each layer addresses, and why the authors chose a layered runtime abstraction instead of a single learned scaffold. In the experiments, prioritize three things: transfer across 18 backbones, coverage across environments, and complementarity with agent-specialized models.

A 30-minute read is enough to get the main idea. If you want to judge whether it has deeper value, focus on two questions.
