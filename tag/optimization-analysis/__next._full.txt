17:T5fb,The main analysis uses the one-step improvement bound of an L-smooth RL objective. Let g_RL denote the gradient of the reward objective and g_MTP the gradient induced by the MTP loss, scaled by a coefficient λ. After substituting the joint update into the smoothness bound, the MTP effect decomposes into a first-order correlation term and a second-order penalty. The correlation term is governed by the inner product between g_RL and g_MTP; it measures whether the MTP update moves in a reward-improving direction. The second-order term is always non-positive and captures the extra perturbation introduced by the auxiliary gradient. This decomposition explains three regimes. Detach sets the MTP gradient to zero with respect to the backbone, so it is stable but cannot exploit joint learning. Cross-entropy MTP treats all samples through token likelihood, while RL upweights high-reward trajectories and suppresses low-reward ones; the resulting gradient correlation is weak and usually cannot compensate for the perturbation. Policy-loss MTP initially aligns with the RL objective, but as optimization moves toward flatter regions, the alignment decays while the perturbation remains, producing a rise-and-fall training curve. Optimal Coefficient Calibration then chooses λ online from the closed-form optimum suggested by the quadratic bound. To avoid full-model gradient inner products in distributed RL, it uses a small-step log-probability-change proxy, making the calibration inexpensive enough for large-scale
