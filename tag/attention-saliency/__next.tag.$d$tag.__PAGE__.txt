9:T865,方法核心是把 AtMan 从一个事后解释工具，改造成训练时可优化的 saliency 估计器。标准 transformer 的注意力是对 pre-softmax score H 做 softmax，AtMan 在 H 上加一个可控掩码 H^AtMan。与直接删 token、改 embedding 或做输入扰动不同，这种做法不改变 token 序列本身，也尽量避免引入明显的分布偏移；它只是在注意力分配上抑制或增强某些 token 的影响。

作者把这个掩码限制在 CoT token 范围内，不动 prompt token，也不动 final answer token。原因很直接：prompt 不是模型可控输出，不适合作为 reward shaping 对象；而作者关心的是“答案是否依赖推理链”，所以要只操作 reasoning trace。初始化时，所有 CoT 相关 mask 设为负常数 c=-0.4，相当于先统一压低模型对推理 token 的注意力。然后固定模型参数，只优化 mask，使 teacher forcing 下正确答案 token 的交叉熵损失尽量恢复。直观上，如果某些 reasoning token 对答案很关键，那么要恢复答案概率，就必须把这些位置的 mask 往回调；最终学到的 mask 大小就可视为 token saliency 的近似。

在得到 mask 后，作者把其归一化，并对 CoT 区域做平均，构造一个 saliency reward。这个奖励与 outcome-based reward 一起放进 GRPO 训练中，目标是同时优化答题正确性和推理链的“有效性”。论文的关键设计不在 RL 框架本身，而在 reward construction：不是奖励更长的 CoT，也不是奖励某种模板化步骤，而是奖励那些在注意力层面被证明对答案恢复有帮助的 reasoning token。

这个方法与常见 process supervision 的区别在于，它不需要人工标注每一步是否正确；与纯 outcome RL 的区别在于，它不把所有正确答案都视为等价，而是偏好那些答案更依赖自身推理文本的轨迹。它的潜在问题也很明显：attention saliency 只是 faithfulness 的代理变量，而且只覆盖“通过注意力路径影响答案”的部分，不一定能完整反映残差流、MLP 变换或更深层的内部计算。a:T86b,The key idea is to turn AtMan, originally a post-hoc interpretability tool, into a differentiable saliency estimator used during training. In a transformer, attention is computed from pre-softmax scores H. AtMan adds a mask to H before the softmax. Compared with deleting tokens, perturbing embeddings, or masking inputs directly, this is a relatively controlled intervention: the token sequence stays unchanged, and the manipulation acts through attention allocation rather than through a large input-distribution shift.

The mask is applied only to CoT tokens, not to prompt tokens or final answer tokens. This design follows the training objective: the prompt is fixed and not under model control, while the final answer should depend on the generated reasoning trace. The mask is initialized with a negative constant c = -0.4 over CoT-related entries, which uniformly suppresses attention to reasoning tokens. Then, with model parameters frozen, the mask is optimized to restore the likelihood of the correct answer under teacher forcing. Intuitively, if some reasoning tokens are important for producing the answer, the optimization must relax suppression on those positions. The learned mask values are then used as a proxy for token saliency.

After normalization, the mask values over the CoT region are aggregated into a saliency reward. This reward is combined with standard outcome-based reward inside GRPO. The novelty is therefore not in the RL algorithm itself, but in reward construction: the model is encouraged not simply to be correct, and not simply to produce longer reasoning, but to produce reasoning whose tokens measurably support the answer under attention-based intervention.

Relative to process supervision, the method avoids manual step annotations. Relative to pure outcome RL, it distinguishes between correct trajectories that are more or less dependent on their own reasoning text. The main limitation is also clear: attention saliency is only a proxy. It does not fully capture all causal pathways in the residual stream, MLP computations, or other internal mechanisms, so it should not be equated with full faithfulness.b:T564,Experiments are run on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, with GSM8K and MMLU as the main evaluation tasks. The claimed result is not a large accuracy jump. Instead, the paper argues that combining the saliency reward with GRPO preserves answer quality while reducing extraneous reasoning. In other words, the generated CoT becomes shorter and more focused without a major drop in task performance.

The experiments are designed to test three things. First, whether the learnable attention mask can identify CoT tokens that matter for answer prediction. Second, whether using this signal as a reward changes the model’s reasoning behavior rather than merely shrinking output length. Third, whether the resulting training hurts final accuracy. The reported conclusion is that AtManRL reduces unnecessary reasoning while keeping performance broadly intact.

From a foundation-model perspective, the evidence is still early-stage. The model is only 3B, and the evaluation is limited to standard reasoning benchmarks. The available text does not indicate a deep scaling study or a broad stability analysis across mask initialization, optimization steps, layer choices, or reward coefficients. For larger-scale adoption, the missing questions are practical ones: training overhead, variance, reward hacking, and whether the saliency reward is genuinely different from a length-control heuristic.c:T5b4,For a skim, focus on definitions first, then on reward construction, and only then on the empirical claims. Start with the introduction and make sure the saliency-versus-faithfulness distinction is clear. The paper does not claim to solve faithful reasoning in full; it proposes saliency as an optimizable prerequisite.

In the method section, pay attention to three design choices: why the mask is restricted to CoT tokens, why it is initialized negatively, and why restoring answer likelihood under teacher forcing is used to infer token importance. The central question to keep in mind is whether the learned mask identifies genuinely necessary reasoning tokens or merely the tokens that the current model most conveniently relies on along a given trajectory.

In the experiments, look for two kinds of evidence: the trade-off between accuracy and reasoning length/redundancy, and ablations on the saliency reward itself. If the paper includes qualitative examples, they are worth reading because they reveal what kinds of reasoning text are removed after training. The right interpretation is modest: AtManRL seems to train the model to avoid low-utility reasoning and to keep the answer more dependent on the generated trace, not to prove that the trace is a complete explanation of the model’s internal reasoning.

If time is short, the GRPO details can be skimmed.
