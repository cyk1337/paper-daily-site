9:T462,The study reports more than ten million trials across 12 LLMs from four families and eight reasoning domains, including arithmetic, propositional constraints, quantum and physical systems, genetics, and molecular biology. Candidates that the receiver already supports are consistently more persuasive. On the arithmetic instrument, measured prior weights range from 0.20 to 0.65, below the rational value of 1. Source cues shift updates independently of candidate support. Identical evidence can help weaker models while harming stronger ones, and repeated evidence shows the predicted sub-additivity and order effects. The strongest results concern the separation between verification and use: models can correctly reject an external candidate in isolation yet still adopt it in 93% to 100% of propositional-constraint cases and up to 99.4% in held-out scientific reasoning. In the sharpest case, a model rejects every violating assignment when asked to check it, then adopts every rejected assignment when answering. The causal tracing analysis reports candidate promotion about nine times larger than truth suppression.a:T6ee,The central design choice is source-first construction. Starting from 214 public long-form sources, including technical incident reports and lesser-known literary narratives, the authors segment each source into overlapping windows and convert local context into evidence records: entities, events, attributes, temporal markers, quotations, local relations, and short summaries. These records are then linked into a typed source-internal graph, with edges added only when the surrounding text supports the relation.

Benchmark items are mined from this graph using seven evidence geometries: forward chains, intersections, comparisons, temporal reconstructions, causal fan-in, abductive explanations, and counterfactual branches. Only after a natural trail is grounded in the source do the authors write the public question, reference answer, key facts, and rubric. This is a meaningful departure from question-first datasets, where evidence is often assembled around a target answer.

The verification pipeline is also a major contribution. Promotion to the final benchmark is organized around four threats: source grounding, multi-hop necessity, shortcut resistance, and answerability. Grounding checks ensure that answers, evidence spans, key facts, and rubric criteria are all supported by the source. Leave-one-out and single-clue ablations remove items where a proper subset of evidence already determines the answer. No-document probes filter contamination and public-knowledge shortcuts. Evidence-conditioned checks verify that the hidden trail is sufficient when supplied. Human review and external audits further remove unsupported facts, redundant clues, ambiguous answers, and geometry mismatches. The paper is less about scale and more about benchmark fidelity.b:T4c7,On the construction side, the pipeline starts from 3,506 validation-tracked candidate trail-question artifacts mined from 214 long-form sources, and only 481 tasks survive into the public release, a 13.7% acceptance rate. That low survival rate is informative: naturally occurring multi-hop evidence trails are easy to overgenerate but hard to verify cleanly.

The experimental message is not just that current models struggle, but why they struggle. The benchmark is set up to distinguish evidence discovery from downstream reasoning. Models can improve substantially when the relevant trail is supplied, which suggests that a large part of the failure is not pure reasoning weakness but failure to identify the right source fragments or to preserve the relation among them. The ablation-based filtering is also important experimentally: by rejecting items solvable from a single clue or a reduced evidence set, the final benchmark enforces genuine multi-evidence necessity more strictly than many prior datasets.
