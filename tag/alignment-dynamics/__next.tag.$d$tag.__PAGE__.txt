Key methodological components:
1) Four preference settings capturing different relationships between objectives: distributional conflict, multi-attribute interaction, strong safety signal, and compatible response-quality objectives, each tested under both objective orderings.
2) Metrics beyond aggregates: in addition to objective-level relative preference accuracy and DPO-style relative reward margins, they track length-normalized policy margins for individual chosen–rejected pairs across checkpoints to localize which pairs degrade or improve.
3) Mechanistic probe: they measure gradient cosine similarity during sequential training (and analyze LoRA adapter movement) to test whether observed preference changes are explained by direct gradient conflict.
