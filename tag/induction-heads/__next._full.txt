Two main results are derived constructively. First, at finite attention scale, induction heads perform soft context matching: every candidate context in history is scored by its overlap with the current context (including non-contiguous matches), and next-token predictions are aggregated with weights exponential in that overlap. Attention scale controls the interpolation between soft matching across orders and hard exact k-gram counting, yielding a data-dependent Jelinek–Mercer-like smoother. Second, the BOS token acts as a sequence-independent attention target, adding constant pseudo-counts to context-specific transitions and implementing add-α smoothing. Under hierarchical priors, soft matching and BOS pseudo-counts combine into a hierarchical smoother.
