9:Ta81,The method has three main components. First, alignment is performed in shared text space rather than token-id space. The student generates a response under its own tokenizer and chat template, then decodes it into surface text. The teacher is not fed student tokens directly; instead, the teacher reconstructs its own context using its own chat template, appends the exact same student response text, and re-tokenizes the full sequence with its native tokenizer. Distillation is then applied only at positions where a student token and a teacher token correspond to exactly the same text span. This avoids inventing artificial token correspondences across incompatible vocabularies and also sidesteps differences in chat formatting. The trade-off is that not every token can be aligned, but the paper argues that sparse high-confidence alignment is preferable to dense but noisy alignment.

Second, the paper addresses the central failure mode in long-teacher to short-student OPD: uncontrolled response-length growth. In naive OPD, the teacher evaluates each student-generated step. If the teacher itself prefers much longer chains of thought, the student is repeatedly encouraged to continue generating, which causes the thinking segment and final answer to grow until truncation becomes common. To counter this, the authors add a student reference KL loss that keeps the current student policy close to its initial policy. The role of this term is not generic regularization; it specifically limits excessive policy drift toward a trajectory distribution the student cannot support within its context budget. The paper explicitly notes that larger teacher-student gaps typically require stronger KL regularization.

Third, the authors mask advantages on special termination tokens. Their observation is that direct teacher supervision often discourages the student from emitting tokens such as </think> or <|im_end|>, because the teacher would prefer to continue reasoning. If this signal is passed through unchanged, the student learns to delay termination and training quickly becomes pathological. By masking the advantages on these special tokens, the method prevents the teacher from directly penalizing termination behavior. It is a small intervention, but it targets a very specific instability in long-form reasoning distillation.

Overall, SimpleOPD is not a new OPD framework so much as a compact recipe that combines cross-tokenizer alignment, length control, and termination stabilization. The paper also emphasizes that the gains are obtained without supervised warm-starting on teacher trajectories; the transfer is achieved primarily through the on-policy process itself.a:T6f7,The experiments distill from the long-context reasoning model SU-01 into multiple shorter-context students from different families, including Qwen3, Qwen3.5, Intern-S2-Preview, GLM-4.7, and Gemma-4. The distilled students also improve on science-oriented benchmarks such as HLE and HiPhO, despite being trained only on mathematical reasoning data. In Stage 0, the model is trained as an explicit reasoning guardrail: given an instruction, a user prompt, and a model response, it generates a structured safety rationale and then predicts the final moderation labels. The rationale is used as training scaffolding rather than a deployment requirement.

In later stages, rationale tokens are progressively replaced by latent recurrent steps. Instead of decoding the reasoning trace, the model performs a fixed number of hidden-state updates and then directly predicts the safety labels. The central technical issue is that hidden-state recurrence creates an input distribution that differs from standard token embeddings seen during pretraining. Vanilla Coconut-style recurrence therefore becomes unstable or semantically weak in this setting.

To reduce this mismatch, the paper introduces Context-Prediction Fusion. The recurrent state combines contextual hidden-state information with predictive semantic guidance derived from the vocabulary embedding space. In effect, the model keeps the continuous contextual state but anchors it with token-space semantic predictions, which helps prevent drift during recurrence. The curriculum matters: the model first learns explicit safety reasoning, then gradually compresses that reasoning into latent steps. Stage 2: for each new training question, retrieve the most relevant path(s) from the bank and condition the teacher to follow the retrieved path when generating a detailed rationale. If the teacher produces a novel valid strategy not represented in the bank, it is buffered and periodically merged back, allowing the bank to grow.
