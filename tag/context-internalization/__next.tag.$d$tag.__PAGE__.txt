9:T5e0,The paper formalizes two student views: q_x = g_theta(y|x) and q_c = g_theta(y|x,c). Standard on-policy distillation with privileged context minimizes a KL from the teacher f(y|x,c) to the no-context student q_x. This enforces what the paper calls privileged fidelity, but it leaves q_c unconstrained.

To address this, the authors propose No-Context Anchoring (NCA). The no-context student output is treated as a stop-gradient anchor, and an additional consistency KL penalizes deviation of the context-conditioned student view q_c from q_x. The intuition is simple: if the privileged information has already been absorbed into the parameters, then reintroducing the context should not push the model into a different behavioral mode.

A few design choices matter. The anchor is the no-context student, not the teacher, because the deployment target is the no-context pathway. Stop-gradient is also important; otherwise the two views could co-adapt and weaken the primary objective. The method only requires one extra forward pass per step, so the overhead is modest.

Conceptually, the paper separates two goals that are often conflated. Privileged fidelity asks whether the no-context student can match the teacher with context. Context removability asks whether the context has become genuinely redundant. The paper argues, and empirically shows, that the first does not imply the second. Representation-level analysis further suggests that NCA changes internal states, not just final-token behavior.a:T498,The experiments span 12 configurations across different domains and model families. Three findings stand out. First, context-induced degradation is common. In most settings, adding the original context back hurts accuracy and inflates response length. The authors identify three regimes, with Regime A covering 7 of 12 settings: the no-context student already performs well, but the context acts as a perturbation and causes both accuracy loss and high harm.

Second, NCA reduces this harm in 11 of 12 settings and improves context-conditioned accuracy in the majority of cases. Third, it largely removes response-length inflation and, in many settings, even improves no-context performance, which is the main training objective.

The paper also introduces a useful metric, Harm = P(q_c wrong | q_x correct), which is more diagnostic than raw accuracy drop because it directly measures how often context flips a previously correct prediction into an incorrect one.
