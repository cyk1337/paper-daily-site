9:T633,The method, CLIK, is a context-conditioned parameter generator that emits LoRA weights for a frozen backbone. The design choice that stands out is joint generation. Instead of producing adapter fragments independently, CLIK first encodes the context with the frozen backbone itself and collects representations from all layers. A Transformer-based generator then uses cross-attention from adapter queries to these contextual representations and emits the full LoRA set jointly. The motivation is straightforward: LoRA updates live across multiple layers and modules, and long-context knowledge is unlikely to decompose cleanly into independent local pieces. Joint generation gives the model a way to coordinate updates across the network. At inference time, the generated LoRA is inserted into the frozen base model, and the model answers from a context-free query prompt. This makes the one-time cost of reading a document reusable across many later queries. The paper positions this especially for multi-turn settings, where repeated re-reading of long histories is costly. Another notable part is the analysis of generated adapters: the context-specific signal appears to concentrate in a compact substructure of the LoRA, suggesting that internalized knowledge may occupy a much smaller effective parameter subspace than the raw adapter size would imply. This enforces what the paper calls privileged fidelity, but it leaves q_c unconstrained.

To address this, the authors propose No-Context Anchoring (NCA). The no-context student output is treated as a stop-gradient anchor, and an additional consistency KL penalizes deviation of the context-conditioned student view q_c from q_x. The intuition is simple: if the privileged information has already been absorbed into the parameters, then reintroducing the context should not push the model into a different behavioral mode.

A few design choices matter. The anchor is the no-context student, not the teacher, because the deployment target is the no-context pathway. Stop-gradient is also important; otherwise the two views could co-adapt and weaken the primary objective. The method only requires one extra forward pass per step, so the overhead is modest.

Conceptually, the paper separates two goals that are often conflated. Privileged fidelity asks whether the no-context student can match the teacher with context. Context removability asks whether the context has become genuinely redundant. The paper argues, and empirically shows, that the first does not imply the second.
