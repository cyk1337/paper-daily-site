19:T691,SPARK has two parts: profiling and steering. It first estimates a behavioral boundary from accuracy as a function of task difficulty. It then measures hidden-state susceptibility by adding small noise to the input embeddings and tracking how strongly hidden states respond across layers. The key empirical finding is that raw susceptibility is not a clean reasoning signal because it is strongly coupled to prompt length. In many programmatic and algorithmic tasks, harder examples are simply longer, and the raw response attenuates with length.

To address this, the paper introduces length-controlled susceptibility. Instead of using the raw response, it compares each example to the expected response among inputs of similar length and uses the residual as a corrected activation signal. A positive residual indicates stronger-than-expected internal activation given the length; a negative residual suggests under-activation. SPARK then combines this corrected signal with cross-layer coordination to separate reasoning-active anchors from under-activated hard targets.

The steering stage extracts a reasoning direction from the active anchors, representing the latent shift from a baseline state to an active reasoning state. That direction is injected only into selected layers and only for the under-activated hard examples. This is the main methodological distinction from generic activation steering: intervention is diagnosis-guided, example-selective, and derived from active reasoning cases rather than from a global handcrafted direction. Layer selection is also informed by the susceptibility and coordination signals rather than applied uniformly across the network.1a:T5df,The main experimental asset is FRONTIER-4.5K, a 4,500-example programmatic reasoning benchmark with executable supervision and continuous difficulty annotations, covering symbolic composition, logical inference, and algorithmic reasoning. Algorithmic reasoning is the main diagnostic setting because it exhibits both a clear capability boundary and a strong length confound.

The results appear in three layers. First, the analysis result: raw hidden-state susceptibility decreases with input length and can therefore misrepresent reasoning quality. Length-controlled residual susceptibility is a better indicator of whether a hard example is genuinely under-activated. Second, the profiling result: corrected susceptibility plus cross-layer coordination can split hard failures into at least two groups, those that already show active internal reasoning signatures and those that do not. Third, the intervention result: SPARK-Steering improves performance on FRONTIER, GSM8K, and MATH-500, with larger gains on harder cases and less damage to easy cases than broad steering methods.

The provided text does not include trustworthy numerical tables, exact gains, model sizes, or full ablations, so those should be checked directly in the paper. That is the critical hinge of the paper. If the correction is weak, the rest of the profiling and steering story becomes much less convincing.
