a:Ta19,方法部分分四步，逻辑很完整。

第一步是定位跨尺寸关系。作者选 Pythia 1.4B→410M 作为主分析对，二者层数和 head 数相同，只缩宽度，因此可以把问题集中在 width transfer。用相同输入序列抽取各层激活后，作者用 ridge regression 拟合大模型表示到小模型表示的线性映射，发现 held-out R^2 达到 0.84，说明表示层面高度线性可对齐。相对地，参数层面对齐很弱，最干净的 embedding 情况也只有 R^2≈0.25–0.39。这一组结果奠定全文主线：函数相近，不代表参数坐标系相近。

第二步是解释为什么稠密权重投影会坏。作者先做了一个很关键的 bit-exact control：从 donor 模型提取权重，再按 head-aware 方式重建模型，logits 完全一致，排除了“权重导出/重组装配错误”的可能。随后分析 dense linear map 的结构性问题。Transformer 里很多子结构不是任意线性基都等价：RoPE 依赖成对维度，attention 依赖 per-head 分块，GELU 是逐元素非线性，LayerNorm 依赖逐特征均值方差。稠密投影把这些坐标混在一起，等于把原本有语义的轴打散。作者还做了分模块破坏分析，指出 LayerNorm 相关结构被破坏带来的影响最大。

第三步是检验“投影后剩下的误差里还有没有可学信号”。作者把最佳线性算子之后的权重残差拿出来，做了谱分析、一致性测试、patch-level learnability、cross-pair transfer，并和 shuffle control 比较。结论是这些残差在统计上和噪声不可区分，bootstrap 置信下 p≥0.99。也就是说，在这个粒度上不存在一个简单的零样本修补器，不能指望再加一个小校正网络就把投影救回来。

第四步是把可转移价值重新定义为初始化问题，并提出 Compensated Selection。这里有两个独立杠杆。其一是 least-squares compensation，对结构保持的子选取权重做闭式最小二乘补偿，目标是让被保留子网络在函数上尽量逼近原模型；它修的是“网络在算什么”。其二是 variance-preserving rescale，对选取后的权重做方差保持缩放，修复优化器看到的尺度，使继续预训练更稳定；它修的是“优化器如何看这些参数”。作者强调这两个杠杆作用在不同权重路径上，因此可以叠加。与 dense projection 不同，这个方法坚持结构保持 selection，而不是任意混基投影；与纯 subcloning 不同，它额外补了函数拟合和训练动态两件事。b:T783,实验设计比较扎实。主对是 Pythia 1.4B→410M：24 层、16 heads 都不变，residual width 2048→1024，head dim 128→64，MLP 8192→4096。这样层一一对应，便于分析。表示对齐用 10,000 条来自 pile-uncopyrighted 的 128-token 序列，前 500 条做 similarity subset，其余用于 mean-pooled 和 last-token 激活统计。评测以 WikiText-103 validation 的 sliding-window perplexity 为主，并补充 C4、2×上下文长度、以及 LAMBADA/ARC-Easy/HellaSwag/PIQA 零样本。

First, it localizes where cross-size similarity lives. The main pair is Pythia 1.4B to 410M, chosen so that depth and head count are unchanged and only width shrinks. Using shared inputs, the authors fit ridge regressions from large-model activations to small-model activations and obtain held-out R^2 of 0.84, indicating strong linear alignment in representation space. In contrast, parameter alignment is weak, with even the clean embedding case only around R^2 = 0.25 to 0.39. This gap drives the rest of the paper.

Second, it diagnoses why dense weight projection is destructive. A key control is bit-exact reconstruction: extracting donor weights and rebuilding the donor reproduces logits exactly, ruling out implementation or assembly artifacts. The failure is therefore in the projection mathematics. A dense linear map mixes bases that are not freely interchangeable in a Transformer. RoPE depends on paired dimensions, attention depends on headwise partitioning, GELU is elementwise, and LayerNorm depends on featurewise statistics. Once these axes are mixed, the projected model no longer respects the computation the architecture expects. The paper further decomposes the damage and finds LayerNorm-related breakage to be the single largest contributor.

Third, it tests whether the residual after the best-fit linear operator contains recoverable structure. The authors analyze residuals with spectral tests, consistency checks, patch-level learnability, and cross-pair transfer, all against shuffle controls. The result is negative: residuals are statistically indistinguishable from noise, with bootstrap comparisons giving p >= 0.99. That rules out a simple zero-shot correction layer at this granularity.

Fourth, it reframes conversion as an initialization problem and proposes Compensated Selection. The method has two independent levers. Least-squares compensation is the function lever: after structure-preserving weight selection, it applies closed-form least-squares corrections so the retained subnetwork better matches the donor’s computation. Variance-preserving rescale is the optimization lever: it restores parameter scale so the optimizer sees a healthy initialization during continued pretraining. These act on disjoint weight paths and therefore stack. This is the key distinction from dense projection, which mixes coordinates, and from plain subcloning, which preserves structure but does not explicitly repair function or optimizer scale.d:T7c2,The experimental setup is unusually controlled. The main analysis uses Pythia 1.4B to 410M, both with 24 layers and 16 heads, while residual width shrinks from 2048 to 1024, head dimension from 128 to 64, and MLP width from 8192 to 4096. This gives one-to-one layer correspondence and isolates width transfer. Representation analyses use 10,000 shared 128-token sequences from pile-uncopyrighted. Evaluation centers on strided WikiText-103 validation perplexity, with harder checks on C4, 2x context length, and zero-shot tasks including LAMBADA, ARC-Easy, HellaSwag, and PIQA.

The main quantitative findings are clear. Representation alignment is strong while parameter alignment is weak: activation ridge R^2 reaches 0.84, whereas parameter R^2 stays around 0.25 to 0.39. Dense projection collapses functionally, and the paper shows this is not due to extraction or reassembly errors. In matched-budget continued pretraining, Compensated Selection beats the strongest weight-subcloning baseline both in zero-shot quality and final endpoint. The headline numbers reported in the paper are final perplexity 84.0 ± 1.8 versus 89.7 ± 3.7, and a much faster recovery trajectory, with 18.5k versus 61.9k tokens to reach the same target quality. At 30M continued-pretraining tokens, the converted initialization is about 18x ahead of training the small model from scratch. The ordering also holds out of domain on C4, at 2x training context, and on a held-out depth-dominated pair, 410M to 160M, where the advantage over scratch remains around 13x. An additional 6.9B to 1.4B experiment suggests the pattern is not unique to one scale pair.

The ablations are especially useful. Least-squares compensation gives the best zero-shot functional recovery, while variance-preserving rescale gives the best final endpoint.
