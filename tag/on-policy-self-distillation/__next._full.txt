全文的主线是“一个症状，三个杠杆”。从给出的内容看，这个症状主要指 OPSD 中最突出的失败模式：dense self-distillation 信号会把模型过快拉向 teacher 条件分布，导致多样性下降、探索受限、甚至学到依赖特权信息的行为。围绕这个症状，文章把控制手段归纳为三类杠杆：
1）where to apply the signal：信号施加在什么 token、什么阶段、什么轨迹片段上；
2）what to show the teacher：teacher 拿到哪类 privileged information，参考解、hint、plan、环境反馈各自引入什么偏差；
3）when to let the guidance change：训练过程中 teacher guidance 是固定、退火、门控还是分阶段切换。

文章还专门强调 KL 方向的差异。它回顾了 forward KL 与 reverse KL 在 on-policy distillation 中的不同作用：forward KL 倾向覆盖 teacher 的多模态分布，reverse KL 更容易收缩到单一模式。MiniLLM 一类工作推动了 reverse KL 在 LLM 上的使用，但在 OPSD 场景里，这种 mode-seeking 倾向会进一步放大熵塌缩问题。这个讨论对理解为什么有些 OPSD 训练“看起来收敛很快，但泛化和探索变差”很关键。

从方法论上，这篇综述最有用的地方不是罗列论文，而是把很多后续变体放进统一因果框架：它们大多是在调节 privileged teacher 与 on-policy student 之间的信息落差，以及由此产生的偏置-方差-探索三角关系。19:T86b,The paper is organized as a diagnosis rather than a catalog. It first places OPSD in a lineage: SFT gives dense but off-policy supervision; RL and RLVR move to on-policy learning but with sparse end-of-trajectory rewards; on-policy distillation restores dense token-level supervision using an external teacher; OPSD and SDPO replace that teacher with the same model under privileged conditioning. This framing is one of the paper’s strengths because it makes clear which advantages and liabilities OPSD inherits from imitation learning, RL, and learning with privileged information.

The main analytical frame is the title’s “one symptom, three levers.” The dominant symptom is that dense self-distillation can over-concentrate the student toward the privileged teacher distribution, reducing diversity and encouraging reliance on information unavailable at inference time. The three levers are then organized as:
1) where to apply the signal — which tokens, trajectory segments, or training phases should receive distillation pressure;
2) what to show the teacher — what kind of privileged information is exposed, such as reference solutions, plans, hints, or environment feedback, and what bias each introduces;
3) when to let the guidance change — whether teacher guidance should be fixed, annealed, gated, or staged over training.

A particularly useful part of the review is its treatment of KL direction. It revisits the difference between forward KL and reverse KL in on-policy distillation: forward KL encourages mode covering, while reverse KL is more mode seeking. In LLM distillation, reverse KL became popular partly through MiniLLM-style formulations, but in OPSD that same tendency can amplify entropy collapse. This helps explain why some self-distillation runs look sample-efficient and stable early on, yet end up with poorer exploration and weaker generalization.

The paper’s real contribution is conceptual unification. Many later variants are shown to be different ways of managing the information gap between privileged teacher and on-policy student, and the resulting trade-off among bias, variance, and exploration.1a:T64f,As a critical review, it does not present a new unified benchmark suite. Its empirical value lies in synthesizing what the literature has already established and where disagreement remains. The paper notes that early OPSD results in mathematical reasoning were promising: accuracy could approach RL-style training while using far fewer generated tokens. That promise explains the rapid growth of the area.

At the same time, the review argues that several failure modes are now well documented:
1) entropy and diversity collapse, where dense teacher supervision narrows the student’s output distribution;
2) capability regression, where previously acquired abilities degrade under repeated self-distillation, especially when the target style becomes too tied to reference solutions;
3) dependence on privileged information, where the student is implicitly trained against token-level distributions shaped by information unavailable at test time;
4) limited strategy discovery, where OPSD is better at sharpening reasoning patterns already present in the base model than at discovering genuinely new ones, in contrast to some RLVR settings.

The paper is careful about what is settled and what is still implementation-dependent. Outcomes vary with KL choice, the type of privileged information, whether guidance is annealed, and whether distillation is applied selectively to uncertain or erroneous tokens. If the practical question is whether OPSD can replace RL, the review’s answer is cautious: it offers dense supervision and token efficiency, but its bias is not something that disappears with minor tuning.1b:T78b,ViGOS explicitly decomposes the student output into two stages: a visual description first, then reasoning plus final answer. The supervision is decomposed accordingly. The visual-description segment is supervised by an image-only perception teacher, which has access to the image but not the reference answer. This forces token-level targets in the early segment to be grounded in visual evidence. The later reasoning and answer segments are supervised by a privileged reasoning teacher that can see the reference answer, but only after the student has already produced the visual description. This preserves answer-guided dense supervision where it is useful, while preventing privileged information from directly shaping the early perceptual claims.

The method also uses a reference teacher for invalid rollouts. The rationale is practical: once the student trajectory has drifted into an invalid format or broken task constraints, continuing segmented on-policy supervision may amplify noise. The reference teacher is then used to restore output structure and reduce format drift. This is a stability-oriented compromise between strict on-policy learning and controllable supervision.

A notable aspect is that the paper does more than prompt the model to “describe before reasoning.” It changes the supervision graph of OPSD so that different token spans are scored by teachers with different information access. The paper also introduces PALR, the Privileged Answer Leakage Rate, to quantify how much of the teacher correction on a fixed student rollout is attributable to the privileged answer rather than the image. The point of PALR is not a single headline number, but a way to make shortcut risk analyzable. Relative to vanilla OPSD, the main innovation is teacher-context assignment rather than a new loss. Relative to sparse-reward RL, ViGOS keeps the sample-efficiency advantage of dense token-level supervision.1c:T5a3,The evaluation is broad and aligned with the paper’s claim: preserve the gains of OPSD on standard benchmarks while improving robustness to language-prior shortcuts. The benchmarks cover general vision-language ability, expert-level multimodal reasoning, visual mathematics, spatial grounding, and stress tests designed to expose reliance on textual priors. The central empirical result is that ViGOS largely retains the benchmark improvements of vanilla OPSD on standard tasks, while performing better on prior-sensitive evaluations where genuine image dependence matters.

The most informative ablations are the obvious ones. First, a direct comparison against vanilla OPSD tests whether the shortcut problem is real in practice. Second, removing the image-only perception teacher checks whether privileged supervision alone causes the model to regress toward weaker grounding.
