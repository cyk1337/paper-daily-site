18:T445,The proposed method is single-rollout PPO with a Monte Carlo Pass@k critic. For each partial solution, a credit model predicts the probability that at least one of k continuations will end with a correct answer. This predicted Pass@k value is used as the critic for policy optimization. Instead of using GAE with temporal-difference bootstrapping, the critic is trained against Monte Carlo outcome labels tied to eventual success. Token-level advantages are obtained from value changes along the trajectory: a continuation receives positive credit if it increases the estimated chance of future success, and negative credit if it lowers that estimate. A terminal correction term keeps the dense credit signal aligned with the observed final reward. The paper emphasizes Pass@4 as the main operating point. Compared with Pass@1, it gives a more selective signal: easy prefixes are downweighted, while prefixes that remain solvable but uncertain receive more training pressure. As k grows, the value approaches a reachability question: whether any successful continuation exists from the prefix.19:T499,Uni-OPD uses a dual-perspective recipe. From the student side, it improves exploration with two balancing strategies. Offline difficulty-aware balancing shapes the training distribution by sample difficulty, so training is not dominated by trivial examples or by examples that are too hard to learn from. Online correctness-aware balancing then uses the student’s current rollout correctness to keep training focused on informative states, especially near the boundary between correct and incorrect behavior. From the teacher side, the paper introduces outcome-guided margin calibration. Vanilla OPD trusts the teacher’s token probabilities on student rollouts, but those rollouts can be out of distribution for the teacher. The proposed calibration uses outcome reward as a global anchor: after aggregating token-level teacher scores over a trajectory, correct trajectories should rank above incorrect ones by a margin. If this order consistency is violated, the teacher signal is adjusted. This is meant to handle teacher degradation on unusual student prefixes, fluent but wrong reasoning chains, conflicts between multiple teachers, and unstable multimodal supervision.1a:T445,FunFuzz combines autoprompt-style initialization, multi-island evolutionary search, and coverage-guided fitness. It first distills a base prompt from documentation and examples, similar in spirit to prior LLM-assisted fuzzers. The key change is that each island receives its own topic-specific seed instruction, so different subpopulations start from different semantic regions. Each island maintains an independent population and performs local search using feedback from an instrumented compiler. Fitness is not raw coverage alone; it is built from incremental coverage under multiple compilation configurations, so candidate ranking reflects more robust behavioral novelty. Candidates are prioritized by this fitness signal, while compiler-internal failures serve as bug oracles for crashes and internal compiler failures. To avoid stagnation, the framework periodically migrates a small number of high-value candidates from stronger islands to weaker ones. The design choice is deliberate: full isolation wastes useful discoveries, but full sharing would homogenize the search too quickly.1b:T6b3,PAINT has two coupled components.

The first is partial-solution adaptive masking. Instead of always exposing the full verified solution or using a fixed masking ratio, the method adapts the amount of visible solution context based on rollout–reference overlap. If the student rollout is far from the reference, the privileged branch sees more of the solution, producing stronger corrective guidance. If the rollout is already close, the method hides more of the reference to preserve diversity and avoid forcing one canonical trace. This directly controls the sharpness-versus-diversity trade-off in the privileged target distribution.

The second is sparse teacher energy interpolation. The method compares the base view and the privileged view on the same student-generated prefixes, then ranks token positions using an entropy-ratio-style statistic that captures where the privileged context changes local uncertainty the most. Only those selected mismatch positions receive interpolated teacher targets before applying the distillation loss. The key idea is not to replace the student target everywhere, but to inject teacher guidance sparsely and locally at decision-relevant positions.

Conceptually, the paper moves privileged self-distillation away from “give the teacher more information and distill the whole sequence” toward “use extra context to locally re-score the student’s own states, then distill only where that re-scoring is informative.” Compared with off-policy distillation, the prefixes are on-policy. Compared with GRPO/RLVR, the supervision is dense and token-level. Compared with external-teacher distillation, the teacher is simply another conditional view of the same model.1c:T612,The experiments cover Qwen3-8B, 4B, and 1.7B on three competition-level math benchmarks. The headline result reported in the text is consistent gains over the prior on-policy self-distillation baseline, with macro Avg@12 improvements of 0.8 to 2.1 points. The method also matches or exceeds GRPO across scales while using short single-rollout training instead of grouped long rollouts, leading to better rollout-token efficiency.

The most informative findings are in the ablations. Full-solution exposure is not always optimal; partial exposure is often better, supporting the claim that too much privileged information can collapse the target toward a single trace. Adaptive masking outperforms fixed masking, suggesting that rollout–reference overlap is a useful alignment proxy. Sparse interpolation beats uniform distillation across all positions, which supports the paper’s claim that supervision should concentrate on mathematically consequential tokens rather than stylistic continuation. Relative to SFT, the gains appear to come from on-policy prefix alignment rather than merely having reference traces. Relative to GRPO, the method offers a better efficiency trade-off by replacing some high-variance sequence-level credit assignment with dense local supervision.
