The first is tree-aware credit construction. Multi-turn interaction naturally exposes branching prefixes: from the same history prefix, one can sample multiple alternative continuations, forming a rollout tree. This isolates the effect of decisions made after a given turn while holding the preceding context fixed. Terminal verifier rewards at the leaves are then recursively backed up through the tree to produce turn-level credit. The point is not to spread a final reward uniformly across all tokens, but to exploit outcome differences among sibling branches under a shared prefix to obtain more informative relative credit for each turn.

The second is posterior self-distillation. Instead of plugging the backed-up credit directly into REINFORCE or PPO weights, the method maps turn-level credit through an affine transformation relative to the frozen behavior policy π_old, producing a bounded teacher distribution for each turn. This teacher can be interpreted as a posterior target: actions with higher backed-up credit get their probability increased, lower-credit actions get suppressed, but the target remains anchored to the behavior policy. The current policy π_θ is then trained to minimize a Forward-KL divergence to this teacher.

Two properties are central. First, at the tangent point where π_θ equals π_old, the gradient of the distillation objective recovers the exact tree policy-gradient direction. Second, unlike a fixed collection-time advantage weight, the student-teacher residual shrinks as the student approaches the target, becomes zero at the target, and reverses sign after overshoot. That gives the optimization a finite destination and is the paper’s answer to sequential mini-batch staleness.

The paper also claims a structural result: because the affine map from credit to target distribution commutes with tree backup, matching a backed-up parent target is gradient-equivalent to aggregating descendant distillation losses. This is important because it ties the target construction to the recursive tree credit mechanism rather than treating it as an ad hoc heuristic.

Relative to PPO, the difference is conceptual and practical: PPO remains a ratio-weighted policy-gradient method with clipping, whereas TreeOPSD turns the update into distribution matching against a frozen target. 更具体地说，对同一道题 x，当前策略会采样 K 条解题轨迹。训练某一条 student trajectory 的某个前缀时，作者从同题样本里随机选 M 条 peer solution，再额外取一条来自其他题目的 cross-problem solution 作为 control。冻结的初始模型分别在“student prefix + 某条同题 peer”和“student prefix + cross-problem control”条件下给出 next-token 分布。两者之差可以看成该 peer 对教师分布施加的 residual shift。SRD 不直接平均这些 shift，而是做一个很保守的聚合：只保留那些在多个 peer 上方向一致的坐标，并把保留坐标的幅度截到所有支持该方向的 peer 中最小的那个。然后把这个 shared residual 加回冻结模型在 student 原始输入下的分布，形成 teacher target，学生再用 forward KL 去拟合。

The key idea is not to imitate any single peer solution. Instead, SRD measures how different same-problem peers consistently shift a frozen model’s token distribution relative to a shared control.

For a problem x, the current policy samples K trajectories. When training on a student-generated prefix, the method selects M same-problem peers and one cross-problem solution as a control. A frozen copy of the initial model predicts next-token distributions under two conditions: the student prefix plus a same-problem peer, and the student prefix plus the cross-problem control. Their difference is a peer-induced residual shift. SRD then aggregates these residuals conservatively: it keeps only coordinates whose direction agrees across peers, and sets the retained magnitude to the smallest supporting magnitude among those peers. This shared residual is added back to the frozen model’s distribution under the original student input, producing a teacher target. The student is trained with forward KL on that target.

Three design choices matter. First, the cross-problem control removes generic distribution shifts caused by simply appending a solution-like text, so the retained residual is more problem-specific. Second, the minimum-supported magnitude acts as a conservative intersection rule, reducing the influence of noisy or wrong peers. Third, peers are used only to construct the teacher during training; the student itself conditions only on the problem and its own prefix, so inference cost does not increase.

Compared with RLVR or GRPO, SRD needs no reward and no answer-level ranking. Compared with standard on-policy distillation, it does not rely on an external stronger teacher. Compared with prior on-policy self-distillation, it does not require reference answers.
