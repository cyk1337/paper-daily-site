The contribution is diagnostic rather than algorithmic, but it is substantial. First, it reframes OPSD as behavior distillation under shifted conditioning contexts, not reliable capability distillation. Second, it shows that gains live in a narrow regime: model size, rollout length, training duration, teacher context, think/no-think mode pairing, and KL choice all interact, and small changes can turn mild gains into steady degradation or collapse. Third, it identifies which signals are actually useful for monitoring. Initial forward KL is not predictive of the signed downstream effect, while training traces such as length growth, entropy changes, and formatting failures are more informative. For researchers working on RLVR, self-training, or R1-Zero-style bootstrapping, the paper is a useful warning: dense token-level supervision does not automatically mean good credit assignment, especially when teacher and student have almost no capability gap.1a:T739,The paper keeps the standard OPSD setup and performs a careful controlled dissection. The student samples on-policy reasoning trajectories. The teacher is a frozen copy of the student at initialization, but it receives privileged context such as a full reference solution or a verified answer. At every student-visited prefix, the teacher produces a next-token distribution, and the student is trained to match it. The default objective is a clipped forward KL, where each vocabulary contribution is capped to avoid extreme token-level gradients. The key technical contribution is the experimental decomposition. First, the paper explicitly treats student and teacher reasoning modes m_S and m_T in {Think, No-think} as first-class variables. This is crucial: the teacher signal changes strongly with reasoning-mode mismatch, often more than with the semantic content of the privileged context itself. Second, the authors ablate teacher context construction in detail, comparing full-solution context, answer-only context, and variants that remove semantic content while preserving prefix structure. The striking result is that removing semantics alone changes the signal much less than changing the prefix distribution and mode alignment. Third, the paper compares KL variants and finds that the effect of forward versus other divergences is model-dependent. This suggests the issue is not simply that the teacher is better calibrated, but that teacher sharpness, rollout mismatch, and optimization stability interact in nontrivial ways. Fourth, the paper tests token-selection redesigns based on loss or entropy, aiming to focus updates on more informative positions, but these do not provide a consistent fix. In many cases, one OPSD variant beats another OPSD variant but still fails to beat the base model. 2) Divergence-from-negative with token gating: the student is trained to reduce alignment with π_neg on its sampled trajectory, i.e., push probability mass away from tokens favored by the negatively conditioned teacher.
