To match the actual training procedure where the dataloader chunks text into fixed context windows and only computes loss on batch-aligned prefixes, the authors further define ENTD-T, a training-aligned variant that serves as a more realistic optimization target. To efficiently count arbitrary-length k-gram occurrences in massive corpora without full corpus scans, the authors adapt the ∞-gram method built on suffix arrays. Controlled pretraining runs across model sizes from 0.1B to 1.5B first confirm that small initialization reduces validation loss, but gains decay sharply with model size. Two architectural factors suppressing these gains are isolated. First, the RMSNorm stability constant epsilon enters a saturation regime when hidden state variance drops below epsilon, making normalization invariant to weight scale and erasing initialization effects. Second, small initialization exacerbates the attention sink effect — over-concentration of attention weights on the first sequence token — which is mitigated by gated attention. Combining a reduced epsilon value with gated attention restores small initialization benefits across model scales, producing improved scaling behavior. Weight dynamics tracking and token-level loss decomposition identify γ=1 as a critical optimum, where residual and identity pathway contributions are balanced.
