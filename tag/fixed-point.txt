19:T868,方法由两个模块组成。第一阶段是非循环 backbone，文中实现为 Transformer，它根据上下文先产生每个位置的初始输出 embedding，相当于对下一个 token 的隐表示做一次快速猜测。第二阶段是 attractor module，一个通常更小的循环网络，它不直接输出 token，而是接收 backbone 的初始 embedding，并通过迭代映射把该 embedding 推向一个固定点。最终在这个平衡态 embedding 上做解码，得到词表分布。

关键改动在于训练方式。传统 looped LM 会把 recurrent block 展开 T 步，然后做 BPTT，训练内存和计算都随 T 增长。Attractor Model 则把 refinement 写成 z*=f(z*; x, h0) 这样的 fixed-point equation，其中 h0 是 backbone 的初始提议。前向时用求解器迭代到收敛，反向时不对所有中间步做显式回传，而是用 implicit differentiation 计算梯度，因此训练显存不再线性依赖迭代步数。这一点直接借鉴 DEQ，但论文的设计不是把整层网络都做成 equilibrium，而是只在输出 embedding 空间上求平衡，降低了优化难度，也保留了标准 Transformer backbone 的表达和并行优势。

作者强调两阶段结构的重要性：backbone 负责给出一个足够接近解的初值，attractor 只做局部修正。这样做比“从零开始循环推理”更稳定，也更便宜。另一个重要点是 adaptive refinement。因为前向是按收敛停止，训练和推理时每个 token 所需的迭代次数可以不同，不再受固定 loop depth 限制。这比显式 looped Transformer 更自然地支持 test-time compute 调节。

论文还提出 equilibrium internalization：随着训练进行，backbone 输出会越来越接近 attractor 的固定点，导致求解器需要的步数逐渐减少。这个现象不是额外蒸馏损失带来的，而是在纯 next-token 训练下自发出现。可以把它理解为：固定点求解器在训练早期像一个老师，提供更好的隐表示目标；训练后期 backbone 学会直接落在这个 attractor basin 附近，于是慢路径被部分吸收到快路径里。1a:T483,This is a meaningful contribution for pretraining and model architecture research. First, it moves recurrent latent computation from an interesting but fragile idea toward a more usable architectural primitive: effective refinement depth no longer implies linearly growing training memory, and the number of refinement steps is chosen adaptively by convergence rather than fixed in advance. Second, it brings DEQ-style implicit layers back into language modeling in a more practical form. Instead of making the whole Transformer an equilibrium system, it uses a standard backbone plus a smaller attractor module that solves for equilibrium only in output-embedding space. That is a much more realistic design for large-scale LMs. Third, the observed phenomenon of equilibrium internalization is important. Even though the model is trained only with next-token loss, the backbone gradually learns to produce embeddings closer to the fixed point, reducing the need for iterative solving. That has direct implications for latent reasoning, train-time versus test-time compute, and how slow internal computation may be absorbed into a fast path over training.1b:T841,The architecture has two stages. A non-recurrent backbone, implemented as a Transformer, first proposes an initial output embedding for each token position. This is a fast latent guess for the next-token representation. A separate attractor module, typically smaller and recurrent, then refines that embedding by iterating toward a fixed point. The final token distribution is decoded from the equilibrium embedding rather than from the backbone proposal alone.

The key change is in how the recurrent part is trained. Standard looped language models explicitly unroll the recurrent block for T steps and backpropagate through all steps, which makes memory and optimization cost scale with T. Attractor Models instead formulate refinement as a fixed-point equation, roughly of the form z* = f(z*; x, h0), where h0 is the backbone proposal. The forward pass runs a solver until convergence, and the backward pass uses implicit differentiation rather than storing and differentiating through every intermediate iterate. This is the main source of constant-memory behavior with respect to refinement depth.

A second important design choice is the two-stage decomposition. The backbone is responsible for producing a good initialization, while the attractor performs local correction. This is more stable and cheaper than asking a recurrent module to do all the work from scratch. The model also supports adaptive refinement naturally: different tokens can require different numbers of solver iterations at train and test time, depending on convergence. That is a cleaner route to variable test-time compute than fixed-depth looped Transformers.

The paper also identifies equilibrium internalization. During training, the backbone’s initial prediction moves closer to the attractor’s fixed point, so the solver needs fewer iterations to reach approximate equilibrium. This happens without any explicit distillation loss. One useful interpretation is that the equilibrium solver acts like a latent teacher early in training, and the backbone gradually absorbs part of that computation into its direct prediction.1c:T759,The experiments cover two regimes: large-scale language-model pretraining and hard reasoning with tiny models. In language modeling, Attractor Models are compared against standard feed-forward Transformers and explicitly unrolled looped baselines. The reported result is that Attractor Models achieve better language-modeling performance under comparable training budgets while remaining more stable and avoiding the linear memory growth associated with increasing recurrent depth in looped baselines. The constant-memory claim with respect to effective refinement depth is one of the central empirical results.

In the reasoning regime, the model outperforms prior tiny recursive reasoners on difficult tasks and appears to mitigate the pathological scaling behavior sometimes seen in small recurrent models, where increasing model size can hurt performance. The paper attributes this to the stability and inductive bias of fixed-point refinement compared with explicit recurrent unrolling.

The most interesting evidence is in the training dynamics rather than a single benchmark score. The paper shows convergence behavior and accuracy-versus-iteration curves indicating that, as training progresses, the backbone proposal gets closer to the final fixed point and the number of solver iterations needed for approximate convergence decreases. Performance does not collapse as this happens. That is the clearest empirical signature of equilibrium internalization.

When reading the full paper, the most important numbers to extract are: the relative size of the attractor versus the backbone, average solver iterations and how they change over training, memory and compute differences versus looped baselines, and the exact gains on language modeling and reasoning benchmarks. Those details determine whether the method is only elegant conceptually or also compelling in practice.1d:T537,This one deserves a careful read. On the second pass, read the fixed-point formulation, solver details, and implicit differentiation carefully. That is where the practical value of the method lives.
