5:T472,The authors modify GPT-2 with four attention variants inspired by human working memory, all trained from scratch rather than added after pretraining. The first is a fixed-width attention window, a hard locality constraint that limits each token to a recent context span and models capacity limits. The second and third are exponential and logistic decay mechanisms, which softly downweight distant tokens to model recency effects. The fourth is a primacy-recency mechanism intended to capture both early-sequence and recent-token advantages. A key methodological point is that these constraints are present from the start of training, so they shape representation learning rather than merely altering inference behavior. Models are trained on developmentally plausible corpora of 10M and 100M words and evaluated on BLiMP grammatical judgments, alignment with human reading-time data via surprisal, attention visualizations, and structural probing. Compared with ALiBi-style soft biases, the fixed-width variant is a stronger intervention because the model cannot bypass the locality constraint when long-range access would be convenient.6:T556,The main result is that fixed-width attention helps the most in the low-data regime. On 10M and 100M word training sets, constrained models—especially the strict local-window variant—substantially outperform standard GPT-2 on BLiMP grammatical accuracy. The excerpt provided does not include the full result tables, so exact numbers cannot be reproduced reliably here, but the qualitative pattern is clear: the less data available, the larger the benefit; as data increases, the gap narrows. A second result is improved psychometric alignment: constrained models produce surprisal estimates that better predict human reading times, suggesting more human-like incremental processing. A third, more interesting observation is that this alignment degrades for all models at the larger scale, which the authors connect to the broader idea that next-token language modeling and human comprehension do not automatically converge with scale. Finally, attention visualizations and structural probes indicate that locality constraints encourage more specialized and linguistically interpretable heads, along with more explicit syntactic encoding, whereas the unconstrained baseline remains more diffuse.
