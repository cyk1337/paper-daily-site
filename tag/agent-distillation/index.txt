18:T7f5,方法上，作者提出 hierarchical agent distillation，把蒸馏上下文的来源拆成两个二元变量：历史来源 H∈{T,S}，动作前缀来源 A∈{T,S}。于是得到四种配置：TT 表示 teacher history + teacher action prefix；ST 表示 student history + teacher action prefix；TS 表示 teacher history + student action prefix；SS 表示 student history + student action prefix。学生始终学习在给定 (h,u) 下匹配 teacher 的 next-token 分布，但 (h,u) 的构造方式不同。

这个框架的关键不只是列出四种组合，而是把三个原来缠在一起的设计问题拆开：第一，在哪一层跟随 student；第二，各种配置在训练中占多少比例；第三，这些比例是否应随训练阶段变化。作者采用 self-distillation 设定来控制变量：teacher 和 student 从同一 checkpoint 初始化，teacher 冻结，但 teacher 额外拿到 student 在部署时不可见的 task guidance g，相当于 privileged-information teacher。这样做的好处是排除了架构差异、模型容量差异和初始化差异，把比较集中在上下文分布本身。

从机制上看，ST 是最有意思的配置。它让历史由 student rollout 产生，因此能覆盖 student 真正会到达的状态；但当前动作前缀由 teacher 生成，因此 teacher 在 token 级监督时仍处在自己熟悉、连贯的局部轨迹上，不会被 student 的错误 prefix 绑死。相反，SS 虽然最“on-policy”，但 teacher 需要在 student 已经写偏的动作前缀后继续给监督，这时监督信号可能变得不自然、低熵或不稳定。TS 通常介于两者之间：历史仍是 teacher-clean 的，但动作前缀来自 student，已经引入局部偏移。作者还进一步研究 mixture 和 schedule：不是固定选一种配置，而是在固定预算下混合多种配置，并比较相同 on-policy 比例但不同配对方式的效果；再进一步比较前期偏 TT、后期再引入 SS 的动态课程是否优于静态混合。19:T852,The proposed framework, hierarchical agent distillation, factorizes context generation into two binary choices: history source H∈{T,S} and action-prefix source A∈{T,S}. This yields four configurations: TT (teacher history, teacher prefix), ST (student history, teacher prefix), TS (teacher history, student prefix), and SS (student history, student prefix). The student always learns to match the teacher’s next-token distribution on the resulting context (h, u), but the way (h, u) is constructed differs across configurations.

The key contribution is not just enumerating four cases. It disentangles three design decisions that are usually conflated: where to follow the student, how much each source configuration should contribute, and whether that allocation should change over training. To control confounders, the authors use self-distillation: teacher and student start from the same checkpoint, the teacher is frozen, and only the teacher receives privileged task guidance unavailable to the student at deployment. This removes differences in architecture, scale, and initialization, so the comparison isolates the effect of context distribution.

Mechanistically, ST is the most interesting case. Student-generated histories expose the teacher to the states the student actually reaches, which improves corrective supervision. But teacher-generated action prefixes keep token-level supervision on coherent local continuations, avoiding the situation where the teacher must continue from a malformed student prefix. SS is fully on-policy, but that is exactly why it can be weak: the teacher is queried on contexts that may already be locally broken. TS sits in between: the global state is clean, but the local token prefix is student-induced and can still degrade supervision quality.

The paper then studies mixtures and schedules. Instead of committing to one configuration, it mixes TT/ST/TS/SS under a fixed budget and compares mixtures with the same aggregate amount of student-generated data but different pairings. It also studies dynamic allocation over training, such as front-loading TT and delaying SS.1a:T6e8,The experiments are organized into three stages. Stage 1 evaluates the four configurations in isolation to separate the effects of history source and action-prefix source. The main result is consistent: ST is generally the strongest standalone configuration, while SS is the weakest across the tested settings. This supports the paper’s central claim that student histories and student prefixes have very different roles.

Stage 2 studies mixed training. A useful result here is that one cannot summarize a mixture by a single “on-policy fraction.” Two mixtures can have the same proportion of student-generated histories and student-generated prefixes, yet perform differently depending on how those sources are paired across TT/ST/TS/SS. The best tested mixtures emphasize hybrid configurations, especially ST, while retaining some TT and a small amount of SS. The role of SS is subtle: it is weak on its own, but a modest allocation can still help in a mixture by exposing the student to fully deployment-like rollouts.

Stage 3 studies timing. Front-loading TT improves early learning because teacher-clean contexts are easier and more stable.
