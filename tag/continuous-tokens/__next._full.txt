19:T863,EVA builds an autoregressive Thought-Visual sequence. Given an image and a query, the model generates alternating textual thoughts T_i and latent visual states A_i: (T1, A1), (T2, A2), and so on, until it decides enough evidence has been gathered and outputs the final answer. Several design choices matter here. First, Latent_slot is continuous, not a symbolic tool command. It is trained to approximate task-relevant visual features, effectively standing in for the visual result that an external tool would have returned. Second, the model does not regress to all image features uniformly. It uses saliency-weighted supervision to focus the latent state on semantically relevant regions for the current question and reasoning step, reducing interference from irrelevant background. Third, the number of slots is adaptive rather than fixed, so the model can allocate more internal visual exploration to harder examples. To support this, the authors build EVA-SFT-230K, where each sample contains a random and context-dependent number of latent slots.

Training is staged. Stage 1 teaches the model to generate Latent_slots. Stage 2 teaches the model to actually use them in downstream reasoning, rather than treating them as inert placeholders. Stage 3 applies GSPO-style RL to improve exploratory visual reasoning. The most informative part of the paper is the RL analysis. The authors identify a “transition window” immediately after each latent slot, where policy deviation becomes much larger than at other token positions. These tokens are where the model has to translate the latent state into subsequent textual reasoning, so their distribution shifts sharply under joint optimization. To stabilize training, they propose D-GSPO: the policy objective is computed only on discrete text probabilities, while gradients are still allowed to flow back through the latent slot; in parallel, a stronger localized KL penalty is applied specifically to the transition-window tokens. The trade-off is clear: preserve exploration through latent states, but avoid letting RL aggressively rewrite both the continuous and discrete channels at once.1a:T53f,The experimental evidence falls into three buckets. First, EVA consistently improves over pure textual CoT and explicit tool-invocation baselines on multiple visual reasoning benchmarks, suggesting that latent visual states provide useful intermediate evidence rather than just extra model flexibility. Second, EVA is more inference-efficient than conventional tool-use pipelines across resolutions, because it avoids repeated external cropping/detection and visual re-encoding; the advantage grows with higher-resolution inputs. Third, the stability analysis is central: naive joint GSPO over latent and text leads to much larger KL divergence, gradient norms, and training volatility in the transition window, while D-GSPO suppresses these instabilities and preserves the performance gains.

The most useful ablation-level takeaways are: adaptive slot counts outperform fixed slot counts; saliency-weighted alignment is better than uniform regression to full-image features; and the three-stage curriculum is better than one-shot end-to-end training.
