9:T718,STAGE is not a new reward model and not a new PPO or DPO loss. It is an active-set controller wrapped around PPO. The method has four main components. First, cumulative retention: at stage k, only the active set P_k contributes to the scalar reward, but once an objective is admitted it remains active in all later stages. This differs from strict sequential curricula that drop old objectives when moving to a new one. Second, probing-based ordering: before the main run, the method performs a short probing phase with all preference dimensions active. It measures early reward gain and volatility for each dimension, then defines a difficulty score that favors low gain and high variance, producing a hard-to-easy admission order. In the paper’s example, creativity, reasoning quality, and numerical sensitivity fall into the harder cluster, while helpfulness, clarification, QA behavior, and ethical compliance are easier. Third, stability-guided admission: within each stage, the controller tracks the batch-mean normalized reward for every active objective and its running maximum within that stage. The gap between current reward and stage-best reward defines a deviation signal, which is then aggregated over a window. A new objective is admitted only when recent deviations are small enough, or when a patience budget is exhausted. The goal is to avoid introducing a new conflicting objective before the current active set has stabilized. Fourth, adaptive weighting: within the active set, weaker objectives receive more weight so that easy or already-strong dimensions do not dominate the PPO reward. The key distinction from reward soups, Pareto-style methods, or gradient surgery is that STAGE mainly controls temporal objective admission rather than only changing scalarization or gradient combination.a:T567,The experiments use two backbones, Qwen3-0.6B and Llama3-8B-Instruct, with 15 preference dimensions during training and 16 held-out automatic benchmarks for evaluation. The provided text does not include the full numeric tables, but the paper reports that STAGE improves the automatic-evaluation average over simultaneous training and shared-budget adapted baselines. The ablations are more informative than the headline: removing cumulative retention, removing the reward-deviation gate, or replacing the probing-derived order all hurts performance, suggesting that the method’s gains do not come from a single cosmetic component. The paper also analyzes active-set growth dynamics and the efficiency-stability trade-off controlled by patience. Short patience admits new objectives faster but increases interference before earlier objectives settle; long patience is more stable but less efficient. That result is expected, but it supports the controller design. First, it constructs a steering vector associated with the target behavior. The target model produces base responses for a small set of prompts, and a stronger teacher rewrites those responses to express the desired behavior. These contrastive rewrite pairs are then used to extract a latent direction in activation space, which serves as the steering vector. The teacher is used only in this offline setup stage, not repeatedly inside the RL loop.

Second, during policy optimization, the current policy is rolled out under multiple steering intensities along that vector. This creates a set of student-generated trajectories that vary in behavior strength while remaining anchored to the current policy. The optimization then proceeds in a GRPO-like multi-objective setup, where the primary reward captures task correctness and the behavioral reward is tied to the steering intensity. The paper interprets this as a form of latent self-distillation: instead of training on teacher-written traces, the student generates its own traces under controlled movement in latent behavior space.

The distinction from baselines is clear. Compared with reward shaping, VSPO changes the rollout distribution before scoring it, which helps expose sparse behaviors more often. Compared with teacher-trace SFT, it avoids direct imitation of off-policy teacher outputs. Compared with RLAIF or online rewriting, it removes repeated teacher calls from the training loop and reuses a fixed latent control signal.c:T555,The experiments are run on MATH and MMLU-Pro with four target behavior types.
