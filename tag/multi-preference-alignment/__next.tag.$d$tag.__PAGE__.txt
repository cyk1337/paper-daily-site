5:T5f1,VSPO works in two stages. First, it constructs a steering vector associated with the target behavior. The target model produces base responses for a small set of prompts, and a stronger teacher rewrites those responses to express the desired behavior. These contrastive rewrite pairs are then used to extract a latent direction in activation space, which serves as the steering vector. The teacher is used only in this offline setup stage, not repeatedly inside the RL loop.

Second, during policy optimization, the current policy is rolled out under multiple steering intensities along that vector. This creates a set of student-generated trajectories that vary in behavior strength while remaining anchored to the current policy. The optimization then proceeds in a GRPO-like multi-objective setup, where the primary reward captures task correctness and the behavioral reward is tied to the steering intensity. The paper interprets this as a form of latent self-distillation: instead of training on teacher-written traces, the student generates its own traces under controlled movement in latent behavior space.

The distinction from baselines is clear. Compared with reward shaping, VSPO changes the rollout distribution before scoring it, which helps expose sparse behaviors more often. Compared with teacher-trace SFT, it avoids direct imitation of off-policy teacher outputs. Compared with RLAIF or online rewriting, it removes repeated teacher calls from the training loop and reuses a fixed latent control signal.6:T555,The experiments are run on MATH and MMLU-Pro with four target behavior types. The main empirical claim is that VSPO consistently outperforms reward shaping, teacher-trace SFT, and RLAIF-style baselines while preserving correctness on the primary task. The paper emphasizes several qualitative properties rather than only endpoint scores.

First, behavior control is more reliable because rollouts are explicitly sampled at different steering intensities, rather than waiting for the desired behavior to appear naturally. Second, the method induces milder distributional shift from the base model; the paper points to Figure 6 for this comparison. Third, it creates a natural curriculum along the behavior axis during training, illustrated in Figure 7, instead of forcing the model immediately into a high-intensity behavior regime.

There is also a theoretical component under a bandit abstraction. The authors argue that when the target behavior is sparse, VSPO improves iteration complexity over reward-shaped GRPO by effectively upsampling rare but useful behavioral patterns.
