18:T4e4,The paper formulates OPD as minimizing a token-level f-divergence between student p_θ and teacher q along prefixes sampled from the student.
- Define the density ratio u_{θ,n} = q(y_n|prefix)/p_θ(y_n|prefix). The objective is an expectation over student rollouts of f(u_{θ,n}) (with a negative sign interpreted as reward).
- The common baseline stops gradients through r_θ^f = -f(u_{θ,n}), yielding a REINFORCE/PPO-like estimator that only keeps the score-function term.
- The authors show this is generally biased because r_θ^f depends explicitly on p_θ (hence θ). Differentiating the true objective produces two components: (i) the score-function term and (ii) a direct reward-gradient term from ∂f(u)/∂θ.
- Combining them yields a corrected token-level weight/advantage of the form w_f(u) = -f(u) + u f'(u). This is the essence of OPD+.
- Implementation is positioned as minimal (claimed “one-line change”), effectively replacing the stop-grad reward weight with the corrected w_f(u) (or equivalently allowing proper gradient flow).
The paper further discusses how reverse-KL and other divergences behave differently under stop-grad vs correct gradients, explaining why prior empirical conclusions can be confounded by this choice.19:T7c6,The method has two parts. First, the paper formalizes behavioral indistinguishability as an empirical, bounded claim rather than an absolute property. A student S is (epsilon, q, t, A)-behaviorally indistinguishable from teacher T if no adversary in class A, with query budget q and compute budget t, can distinguish their outputs with advantage greater than epsilon over a specified prompt distribution. This framing matters because it forces the evaluation to state what prompts are considered, what attacker is allowed, and what resources are available.

Second, the authors instantiate this definition with a multi-view evaluation pipeline instead of a single metric. They use: (1) response-only discriminators to detect stylistic or surface artifacts; (2) prompt-response discriminators to test whether the answer is teacher-like in context; (3) semantic similarity metrics; (4) policy-level agreement measures for safety and refusal behavior; and (5) a pairwise teacher-identification judge that sees two answers to the same prompt and decides which one is more likely from the teacher. They also apply A/B-swap consistency filtering to reduce judge position bias.

The empirical setup uses two model families, Qwen and Llama. For each family, they compare the teacher, the original base student, and a LoRA-distilled student trained on black-box teacher responses. The choice of LoRA is important: it reflects a realistic low-cost distillation regime and also means the paper is measuring how much behavior can be transferred through limited-capacity adaptation, not through full-parameter imitation.

A further practical component is data acquisition under query budget. The paper compares disagreement-guided sampling against stratified random sampling. The result is notable: disagreement-guided acquisition does not consistently outperform the simpler coverage-oriented baseline, suggesting that diversity and category coverage remain strong defaults for behavioral distillation.1a:T71c,The experiments are built around a controlled 5,000-prompt behavioral probe suite covering general QA, reasoning, coding, summarization, style/format control, ambiguous prompts, safety-boundary prompts, instruction conflict, domain-technical questions, and robustness perturbations. The authors explicitly position this as a probe distribution rather than a natural deployment distribution.

Three findings stand out. First, LoRA distillation improves semantic similarity and makes learned discriminators less effective, so black-box response distillation does transfer a substantial portion of observable behavior. Second, this does not imply behavioral indistinguishability. The clearest number comes from the pairwise teacher-identification setup: for the Qwen family, distinguishing advantage drops from 0.158 for the base student to 0.081 after LoRA distillation. That is a meaningful reduction, but still clearly above zero. Third, residual distinguishability is concentrated in specific categories rather than spread uniformly. Style/format prompts, robustness perturbations, and domain-technical prompts remain the easiest places to tell teacher and student apart. This suggests that distillation learns answer content more readily than stable response policy and boundary behavior.

The query-budget experiments also matter. Under limited API calls, disagreement-guided acquisition does not consistently beat stratified random sampling. That is a useful practical result: if the goal is behavioral transfer, broad prompt coverage may be more reliable than more elaborate active-selection heuristics.

Overall, the evidence is cumulative rather than tied to one metric. Semantic similarity improves, discriminators weaken, and pairwise judging becomes harder, but none of these indicate full behavioral matching.1b:T48e,Start with the introduction, especially the distinction between output matching and behavioral indistinguishability. That is the central framing move. Then read the formalization section carefully to understand what epsilon, q, t, A, and the prompt distribution are doing in the definition. After that, focus on the evaluation design and the main pipeline figure, because the novelty is in how the paper operationalizes indistinguishability rather than in a new training method.

If reading selectively, prioritize three parts: (1) the pairwise teacher-identification protocol and A/B-swap consistency filtering; (2) the construction of the 5,000-prompt probe suite; and (3) the category-wise results plus the query-budget comparison. These sections tell you whether the framework is credible and whether it changes practical distillation choices.
