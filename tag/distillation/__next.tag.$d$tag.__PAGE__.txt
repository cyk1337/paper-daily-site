第一条是 Simplex-DMD。student 在每个位置直接输出词表上的概率向量，把它视作 simplex 上的连续 token relaxation，再映射到 embedding 空间并加噪。由于整个路径是连续可导的，可以使用 pathwise gradients。为了估计 noised student distribution 的 score，方法引入一个 auxiliary denoiser，思路延续了 Diff-Instruct / DMD 一类 continuous-data distribution matching 方法，但这里适配的是离散文本经 simplex relaxation 后的连续表示。它的优点是低步数下训练和采样都更稳定，缺点是 relaxation 可能带来偏差，尤其在高质量、低熵文本区域会受限。

第二条是 Reinforce-DMD。这里 student 仍输出 categorical distribution，但训练时从中采样离散 token，再 embedding、加噪，并通过 REINFORCE 估计梯度。为了构造 reverse-KL 所需的密度比，作者训练一个 discriminator 来估计 noised student 与 noised data 之间的 density ratio。这样做避免了 simplex relaxation 的连续近似偏差，更贴近真正的离散生成分布，但代价是方差更高、训练更难，需要更仔细的稳定化设计。论文还把两种方法都扩展到 multi-step generation，而不是只做 one-step distillation，这一点很关键，因为语言生成质量通常对少量中间 refinement step 很敏感。

Student samples are embedded and noised, producing a noised student distribution, which is then matched to the corresponding noised data distribution through a generic discrepancy. This formulation subsumes prior distribution-matching approaches and extends naturally to multi-step generation. The authors then specialize the discrepancy to reverse KL and derive two methods under the same student architecture.

Simplex-DMD keeps the student outputs as probability vectors over the vocabulary. These are treated as continuous simplex-valued token relaxations, embedded, and corrupted with Gaussian noise. Because the whole path is differentiable, training can use pathwise gradients. To estimate the score of the noised student distribution, the method introduces an auxiliary denoiser, in the spirit of DMD and Diff-Instruct, but adapted to discrete text represented through continuous simplex relaxations. This route is attractive at very low sampling budgets because it is relatively stable and efficient, though it may suffer from approximation bias due to the relaxation.

Reinforce-DMD instead samples categorical tokens from the student distributions, then embeds and noises those discrete samples. Gradients are estimated with REINFORCE, and a discriminator is trained to estimate the density ratio between the noised student and noised data distributions, which is needed for the reverse-KL objective. This avoids the bias introduced by continuous relaxations and stays closer to the true discrete generation process, but it pays with higher variance and more delicate optimization. Importantly, both methods are extended to multi-step generation rather than only one-step distillation, which matters because language quality is often sensitive to a small number of refinement steps.

A notable design choice is the use of distribution matching rather than trajectory matching. The student is not forced to imitate the teacher’s denoising path. It only needs to match the target distribution. That gives the student more freedom and makes the comparison between parameterizations cleaner. The first layer is SFT. Pistis-27B and Pistis-9B are built on Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.5 respectively. The Thinking variants are trained on structured multimodal reasoning data, while the Agentic variants additionally include agentic trajectories covering long-horizon planning, iterative reasoning, tool use, and mixed text-vision interaction. This stage is important because the paper explicitly notes that on-policy distillation is sensitive to student-teacher overlap and can fail without a strong cold start.

The second layer is IDRL. Instead of using a static weighted sum of distillation and RL losses, or running distillation and RL as separate stages, IDRL alternates between on-policy distillation updates and RL updates within a single training loop. In the distillation step, the student is trained against the teacher distribution on student-generated sequences, giving dense token-level supervision. In the RL step, the policy is optimized against task reward. The motivation is that a static joint loss can create objective interference within the same update, while a purely staged pipeline prevents the two signals from interacting during training. Interleaving is meant to decouple the objectives in time while still allowing them to shape the same evolving policy. The claimed benefits are better exploration, higher retained policy entropy, and more stable optimization.

For long-horizon agentic training, the paper adds PAS, or step-level positive-advantage suppression. This targets a common failure mode in trajectory-level RL: if a trajectory succeeds overall, some ineffective or even wrong intermediate actions may still receive positive credit because they share the trajectory’s positive advantage. PAS suppresses positive advantage on such steps, reducing this hitchhiking effect. The exact formula is not visible from the provided text, but the intent is clear: not every action on a successful trajectory deserves reinforcement.

The third layer is Pistis-Auto-Harnessing. PAH does not change the policy weights or the tool interface. Instead, an Optimization Agent proposes bounded revisions to the inference harness using development trajectories, validates them against a development metric, and keeps only those revisions that improve the metric. The selected harness is then frozen for evaluation.
