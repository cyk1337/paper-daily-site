17:T816,方法上，FSN 保留了 Kuramoto attention 的 score map，只改 value/update law。每个 token 的隐状态不是实向量，而是 k 维相位向量，位于环面上；对应的复数表示是单位模 phasor。注意力分数仍由 query/key 相位相干性、门控和 rotary drift 组成，softmax 保持因果约束。核心变化在更新方向。基础 Kuramoto 层的更新是对 attended token 相位做吸引式耦合，形式上是对 sin(theta_u - theta_t) 的加权和，目标是相位一致。FSN 则引入带谐波的复值耦合核，对每个被关注位置 u，不再主要耦合到 z_u，而是耦合到 z_{u+1}，也就是该位置的后继 token 状态。作者证明，这等价于 Kuramoto–Sakaguchi 耦合里引入一个 frustration angle，而这个角度不是手工参数，而是由数据自身的局部转移 delta_u = theta_{u+1} - theta_u 决定。这样一来，模型不是把当前位置拉向“和检索片段一致”，而是拉向“相对检索片段应当偏移多少”。

更细一点看，FSN 的更新核包含两部分：w0 对当前 attended token 的静态 frustrated coupling，w1 对 successor 的一阶延迟耦合；两者都支持多个谐波 n=1..N，默认 N=3，系数是逐坐标的复数。极坐标下，模长对应 harmonic gain，相位对应 frustration angle，因此每个系数都能直接映射到同步文献中的耦合对象。这个设计的一个优点是可解释性比普通 MLP value mixing 更强。另一个优点是它严格包含若干特例：N=1, w0=1, w1=0 退化回 Kuramoto attention；更一般的实系数对应 Daido harmonic coupling；w0=0 则是纯 successor coupling。作者还讨论了初始化问题：当 frustration angle 全在零附近时，角度方向存在局部对称，早期优化容易不稳定，因此初始化需要打破这种对称性。文中还有一个去掉 FFN 的变体，用对 learned collective modes 的 gated coupling 替代前馈层，想测试性能到底来自 frustrated coupling 还是来自常规 Transformer block 的其余部分。18:T809,FSN keeps the score map from the base Kuramoto attention and replaces the update law. Token states live on a k-dimensional torus as phase vectors, with unit-modulus complex phasors as the working representation. Attention scores are still computed from phase coherence, learned gates, and rotary phase drift, under a causal softmax. The main change is in how attended information acts on the current token.

In the base Kuramoto layer, the update is an attractive coupling term, a weighted sum of sin(theta_u - theta_t), which pushes phases toward agreement with attended tokens. FSN replaces this with a harmonic complex-valued coupling kernel and a one-step delay. For an attended position u, the model can couple not only to z_u but also to z_{u+1}, the successor state. The key theoretical point is that coupling to the successor is equivalent to Kuramoto-Sakaguchi coupling with a frustration angle given by the data transition delta_u = theta_{u+1} - theta_u. So the offset is not a free learned bias; it is induced by the sequence itself. This turns the layer from consensus-seeking to transition-seeking.

The kernel has two parts: a static frustrated coupling term over the attended token itself, and a delayed coupling term over successors. Both are expanded over harmonics n=1..N, with per-coordinate complex coefficients; the default uses N=3. In polar form, magnitudes are harmonic gains and phases are frustration angles, which makes the layer unusually interpretable relative to standard value mixing. The construction strictly contains several special cases: standard Kuramoto attention when N=1, w0=1, w1=0; Daido-style harmonic coupling for real coefficients; and pure successor coupling when w0=0. The paper also discusses an initialization issue: zero frustration angles create local symmetries in the early loss surface, so initialization must break that symmetry. A variant without the FFN replaces the feed-forward block with gated coupling to learned collective modes, to test how much of the gain comes from the coupling law itself.19:T6ca,The experiments are character-level language modeling on text and code, with matched-parameter and matched-epoch comparisons. The headline result is on enwik8 at roughly 1M parameters: FSN beats a tuned Transformer at matched epochs, 1.6050 vs 1.6258 bits per character, and also after convergence, 1.5953 vs 1.611. The most informative analysis is the copy-depth decomposition, where positions are bucketed by the longest suffix that appeared earlier in context. This is exactly the regime where attention is expected to help. Kuramoto attention loses badly to the Transformer in high copy-depth bins, by as much as +0.4231 bpc. FSN reverses that pattern and outperforms the Transformer in every bin with depth four or greater. That strongly supports the paper’s central claim: successor-based frustrated coupling fixes a predictive deficiency rather than merely improving average optimization.

The authors also run a parameter-doubling ladder up to 8M parameters, where the matched-epoch advantage persists. Training cost is the main downside. FSN is several times slower per epoch. However, when measured by wall-clock time to reach the Transformer’s mean loss, FSN needs only 0.70x the time at 4M and 8M, while requiring 1.23x and 1.56x at 1M and 2M. So the layer is more expensive per step, but some of that is recovered through faster convergence at larger scales. The no-FFN variant reaches 1.6452 bpc on enwik8 versus 1.6258 for the Transformer baseline, suggesting the frustrated coupling itself carries substantial modeling power, though the full FSN remains clearly better.
