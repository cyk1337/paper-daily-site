9:T548,Engineering-heavy but carefully scoped:
- Task: from 76 real policy documents, recover the requirements that the author visually singled out. Labels come from the document’s own OOXML run properties (not human subjective judgment), scored by a deterministic matcher.
- Formatting-aware parser: parse .docx via python-docx into a flat sequence of blocks; split into FormattingSpan units where formatting is constant; each span carries 12 formatting attributes.
- SemanticRole hints: apply only high-confidence formatting→semantics rules (e.g., red text/red highlight→critical_warning; other visible highlight→warning; strikethrough→deprecated). They explicitly treat these as hints and avoid medium-confidence mappings to prevent misleading the model.
- Serialization: hybrid Markdown for familiar cues plus custom tags for non-Markdown cues ([COLOR:#FF0000], [HIGHLIGHT:YELLOW]) and structural tags for document-level metadata.
- Attribution: span-level leave-one-out over cue classes to identify which cues drive the effect.
- Cue recovery vs cue meaning: a preregistered test checks whether merely preserving cues is enough or whether the agent must be told what the cues mean.
The key design choice is prioritization: preserve the cues that are both common and systematically lost (color/highlight), and validate via deterministic scoring.a:T40f,Main empirical claims (as described in the full text; some details are qualitative in the provided excerpt):
- Prevalence: about one-third of real documents contain such visually marked requirements/exceptions.
- Downstream impact: formatting-blind ingestion increases the false-positive rate on unflagged requirements by ~4×, producing confident wrong answers rather than abstentions.
- Robustness: the effect holds across six answering models from two vendors.
- Cue attribution: nearly all measurable benefit comes from color and highlighting; bold/italic/monospace/strikethrough contribute little in their setting.
- Preregistered comparison: providing cue semantics (not just preserving cues) yields a modest gain.
- Negative results: progressive disclosure doesn’t reduce token cost; a typed layer shows no benefit for two models that only sparsely attempt the task.
For replication, the most important artifacts are the OOXML-to-label mapping, the deterministic matcher, and the leave-one-out protocol with uncertainty estimates.b:T74c,EGI-Bench is a benchmark suite plus a scoring framework rather than a new standalone benchmark.

The first layer is execution unification. The authors consolidate eight enterprise-facing agent benchmarks into a single repository with one CLI entry point. Each benchmark keeps its native scaffold, such as ReAct loops, MCP clients, or multi-turn dialogue harnesses, while the suite uses per-benchmark virtual environments and a shared LiteLLM routing proxy. The intended unit of evaluation is the underlying LLM rather than a particular agent framework implementation.

The second layer is fine-grained sub-metric routing. Instead of averaging benchmark-level overall scores, the suite maps individual sub-metrics into six EGI dimensions, M1 through M6: task outcome accuracy, tool actioning, planning, retrieval grounding, safety/abstention, and engineering quality. This is meant to fix two common issues: benchmark overalls are internally aggregated in inconsistent ways, and a naive average across benchmarks hides the structure of capability trade-offs. The paper emphasizes that the routing table is declarative and auditable, which is important if the aggregate score is meant to support release decisions.

The third layer is runtime-budgeted evaluation modes. The suite offers quick and deep modes, determined by per-benchmark runtime budgets rather than manually selected tasks. Quick mode targets at most 180 minutes of wall-clock time, while deep mode targets roughly 12 hours. This is a practical design choice for real evaluation pipelines: one mode for frequent regression checks, another for more serious release gating.

Methodologically, this is evaluation systems engineering rather than a new measurement theory. Its usefulness depends on whether the metric routing is sensible and whether the resulting aggregate signal is stable and interpretable.c:T5ae,The main empirical result is a 14-model quick-mode benchmark covering GPT, Claude, and Gemini families, used to show that the unified EGI score exposes capability specialization that is often invisible in any single benchmark's overall score. In other words, a model may be strong at tool use and planning but weaker at abstention or engineering quality, and a flat average can hide that structure.

The provided excerpt does not include the full numeric tables, but several experimental choices are worth noting. First, the suite is not trying to outperform SWE-bench, GAIA, or BFCL on their home axes; it is trying to compose eight benchmarks into six deployment-relevant dimensions. Second, it introduces release-gate semantics, including configurable floors on dimensions such as safety or tool reliability, which is closer to how real deployment decisions are made than a single scalar average. Third, the quick-versus-deep split creates an opportunity to study the trade-off between evaluation cost and ranking stability.

The persona controls the simulated customer behavior. Training examples can start from the initial user request or from an intermediate dialogue prefix, so the policy learns decisions at different depths of the interaction rather than only the first move.

The policy model only acts in the environment. That makes the signal much closer to the real agent objective than token-level imitation.
