a:T48e,The work has three components. First, PROBE-Sim is a tabletop simulator built with PyBullet and Blender-EEVEE, using a UR5 robot with a Robotiq gripper. Scenes contain 15 tightly packed objects sampled from 1,796 scanned assets across 481 categories, with object metadata such as names, colors, categories, and physical descriptions. The design emphasizes physical realism, scalable procedural generation, and verifiable ground truth. Second, PROBE-Bench contains 150 tasks spanning six question types: Count, Find, Compare, Size, Reduce, and Beneath. Agents can call perception tools such as scene parsing and object finding, and manipulation tools such as grasp-and-remove and pushing. The important design choice is that perception and manipulation are embedded in a single agent loop, so the model must decide when to inspect, when to manipulate, and when to answer. Third, Probe-Agent distills successful tool-use trajectories from a strong teacher, Gemini 3.1 Pro, into smaller open-weight students such as Qwen3-VL and NVILA via supervised fine-tuning. Distilling only successful trajectories is meant to teach stable strategies rather than noisy exploration.b:T499,A two-stage recipe.
Stage 1 (action prior): train an encoder–decoder on unconditioned action trajectories only. The encoder compresses an action chunk into a latent embedding intended to capture macro motion semantics over time. The decoder reconstructs the chunk conditioned on the latent, trained with a flow-matching objective to model continuous, potentially multimodal action distributions.
Stage 2 (VLA training): transfer the prior into a VLA policy with three coupled mechanisms: (1) reuse the pretrained decoder as the action output head; (2) early-stage latent distillation—use the encoder’s latent from ground-truth action chunks as a structured target to quickly anchor the VLM-predicted action features, then gradually relax this constraint to allow end-to-end adaptation; (3) history compression—use the encoder to summarize past state-action trajectories into a single latent token injected into the VLM as temporal context.
