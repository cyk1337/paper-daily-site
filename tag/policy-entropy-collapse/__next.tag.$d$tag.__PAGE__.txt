9:T58d,The paper derives a first-order analysis of token-level entropy dynamics under GRPO. The entropy change of a token decomposes into the product of the trajectory-level advantage and a local entropy sensitivity function determined by the next-token distribution. This yields an advantage-surprisal quadrant view: positive-advantage low-surprisal tokens tend to decrease entropy, positive-advantage high-surprisal tokens tend to increase it, and the signs are reversed under negative advantage. Since low-surprisal tokens occur much more often, the realized update is biased toward entropy reduction. The authors also identify a near-criticality property: a mild perturbation to the weights of specific tokens can flip the direction of entropy evolution; beyond the critical threshold, the weight mostly controls magnitude rather than sign. STARE uses this observation in three steps. It selects entropy-critical tokens using batch-internal surprisal quantiles, amplifies the effective advantage of positive-advantage high-surprisal tokens, attenuates that of negative-advantage high-surprisal tokens, and activates the intervention only when batch-averaged entropy falls below a target. Compared with DAPO-style clip-higher, it remains active even when importance ratios stay close to one in near on-policy training.
