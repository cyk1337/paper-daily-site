18:T622,The method has three parts. First, trajectory reconstruction. The model is treated as a flow-matching or ODE-based generator with a learned velocity field. Given a real video encoded into the pretrained VAE latent space, the authors integrate the velocity field backward from the clean latent toward noise, producing an approximate reverse trajectory. This allows them to rerun the backbone at each time step and collect block-level hidden states and attention patterns. The appendix discusses approximation error and the conditions under which the recovered trajectory is a faithful proxy. Second, linear probing. They train lightweight probes on intermediate transformer states to predict physical plausibility and some quantitative physical variables. The point is not to maximize probe capacity, but to localize where and when physical information becomes linearly accessible. Third, causal intervention. They perform block-level noise interventions and use a probe-surprise style analysis to identify which components carry or amplify the physical signal. This goes beyond standard probing: it asks not only whether the information is readable, but also whether perturbing specific computations changes the signal and downstream generation. Compared with predictive representation learners such as V-JEPA, the model here is not trained with an explicit future-prediction objective. Compared with standard diffusion interpretability work, the analysis is anchored on real videos embedded back into the model’s trajectory rather than on arbitrary generated samples.19:T56d,The experiments are organized around a few core phenomena rather than a broad benchmark sweep. First, on real videos with known physical plausibility labels, the recovered trajectories show that physical plausibility is linearly decodable from intermediate diffusion-transformer states. The signal is not uniform across the network: it tends to peak at certain depths and noise levels, often in intermediate blocks rather than at the input latent or final layer. Second, the paper reports that some quantitative physical variables are also decodable, which suggests the model is not merely storing a coarse plausible-versus-implausible cue. Third, the strength of the signal depends on trajectory fidelity: when the reconstructed path better follows the model’s learned continuous dynamics, probe performance improves, supporting the claim that physical structure emerges from denoising dynamics. Fourth, intervention experiments show that perturbing specific blocks weakens decodability or changes generation behavior, indicating that these layers are involved in forming and transmitting the signal rather than passively correlating with it.
