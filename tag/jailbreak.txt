The authors select 100 harmful request cores from published benchmarks and wrap each with 9 published attack families, yielding 900 attacked prompts. Two target models answer the same prompts under identical settings, producing corpus A and corpus B from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and gemma-3-12b-it. Eight graders then label every response with the same four-way rubric: refuse, evasive, comply, or gibberish. Gemini-2.5-flash is used as a reference grader, but explicitly not as ground truth.

The key analytical move is to decompose the usual jailbreak success rate into two quantities. One is the refusal boundary: whether the model declined the request at all. The other is the compliance rate: among responses that are not refusals, how often the model actually delivers the harmful request. Across graders, the refusal boundary mostly shifts by a moderate number of points. The compliance rate, however, can change by multiplicative factors because some graders systematically relabel compliant outputs as evasive. The paper then validates this behavior on held-out graders, new harmful requests, and a third target-family model.

A second important contribution is diagnostic rather than algorithmic. Aggregate agreement can hide the problem because easy refusals dominate the corpus. A collapsing grader can still show high overall agreement with the reference. Per-class label counts expose the failure immediately, without human labels and without extra calls. 这个设计有几个关键点。第一，它不是把 harm 判定放在 token 级，而是放在语义级终端续写上，这样能避免局部 token 模式和真实有害行为之间的错位。第二，它显式利用了自回归模型的条件概率，因此得到的是一个 reachability probability，而不是 judge 打分的启发式分数。第三，它把问题表述成 probabilistic model checking 里的 reachability 问题，用离散时间马尔可夫链和 PCTL 风格的“eventually reach harmful state”来组织计算，这让指标定义比较干净。

Starting from the prompt, BCA expands a bounded continuation tree up to a finite horizon, keeping only continuations that are sufficiently probable. Nodes are token prefixes, edges are next-token conditional probabilities, and terminal continuations are labeled as harmful or safe by a semantic harm evaluator. BCA then computes the probability of eventually reaching a harmful terminal within this bounded tree, which the paper calls bounded harm reachability.

Several design choices matter. First, harm is evaluated semantically at terminal continuations rather than by token-level heuristics, which better matches the actual safety question. Second, the score is probability-aware: it uses the model’s conditional distribution instead of only sampled outputs.
