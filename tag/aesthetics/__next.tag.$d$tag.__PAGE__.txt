9:T792,The method has four layers. First, the evaluation framework. Video quality is decomposed into three top-level dimensions: Visual Aesthetics, Visual Fidelity, and Visual Plausibility, further broken into 15 fine-grained criteria. The point is not taxonomy for its own sake, but to create an interpretable coordinate system for both annotation and reward outputs. Second, data construction. The authors synthesize around 20K high-quality prompts, generate candidate videos from multiple mainstream text-to-video models, and ask aesthetics experts to provide pairwise preferences under the 15-criterion rubric. This yields about 19K training preference pairs and a 2.5K-pair benchmark, AesVideo-Bench. The key detail is that preferences are structured along VA/VF/VP rather than being generic “better/worse” labels. Third, reward model training. AesRM is built on a VLM, but instead of using the standard encode-and-score setup with an auxiliary regression head, it casts reward modeling as a generative next-token prediction task. The motivation is to stay aligned with the VLM’s original objective and better exploit its pretrained reasoning ability. Training proceeds in three stages: atomic aesthetic capability learning on atomic aesthetics datasets, cold-start instruction tuning on the preference data, and GRPO-based optimization with verified rewards. Fourth, the interpretable CoT variant. AesRM-Base outputs pairwise judgments directly, while AesRM-CoT also produces criterion-level reasoning. The authors observe that low-quality CoT can amplify hallucinations, so they synthesize CoT with a self-consistency procedure: sample multiple teacher reasoning trajectories, rank or aggregate them with a consistency metric, and distill the higher-quality traces. During GRPO, they further add a process reward based on similarity between teacher and student CoTs, so optimization is not driven only by the final preference label.a:T4a0,The main confirmed scales are roughly 20K prompts, 19K training preference pairs, and 2.5K benchmark pairs. The model family includes AesRM-Base and AesRM-CoT, evaluated against existing aesthetics models on multiple video-aesthetics benchmarks. The most important findings are qualitative and structural rather than a single headline metric. First, the hierarchical rubric plus expert preference data improves consistency and interpretability in aesthetic judgment. Second, CoT is not automatically helpful: low-quality CoT increases false judgments, while ensemble/self-consistency CoT improves both performance and stability. Third, process reward provides additional gains for CoT-style reward models, suggesting that optimizing only the final preference outcome is insufficient when reasoning quality matters. On the application side, AesRM is plugged into two post-training strategies, Flow-RWR and Pref-GRPO, and the resulting videos show better lighting, composition, and detail in qualitative comparisons.
