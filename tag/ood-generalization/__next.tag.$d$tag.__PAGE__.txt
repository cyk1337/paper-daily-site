b:T450,GeoSD combines two terms defined in a shared geometry over next-token distributions:
1) Local control via Hellinger distillation: gradients are implicitly weighted by teacher–student probability overlap. Tokens that the teacher assigns high probability to but the student assigns near-zero probability no longer dominate the update; shared mass transfers strongly and grows as agreement increases.
2) Global control via a proximal penalty: a Fisher–Rao distance to a recent checkpoint constrains accumulated movement (drift) in predictive distributions over training.
3) Optimization alignment: since both terms live in the same geometry, the paper motivates natural-gradient-style updates to make each step the smallest necessary change in prediction space while improving the objective.
Mechanistically, the paper argues KL matching often achieves agreement by collapsing alternatives (draining probability mass), producing brittle, overconfident policies; GeoSD strengthens the teacher’s top choice while keeping plausible alternatives “within reach,” which matters under distribution shift.c:T405,Using the accuracy-saturated computational reproducibility benchmark CORE-Bench Hard as a case study, the work extracts remaining value from saturated benchmarks across three layers.
