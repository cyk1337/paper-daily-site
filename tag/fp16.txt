19:Tb3a,论文的方法设计很工整，基本按“现象确认—根因排查—行为解释—因果定位”四步展开。

第一步是行为确认。作者在 LLaMA-2-7B、Mistral-7B-v0.3、Gemma-2-2B 三个开源模型上，用 GSM8K 测试 greedy、top-k、top-p 三种解码策略，比较 cache-ON 与 cache-OFF 的输出。结果显示三类模型、三类采样条件下 token divergence rate 都是 100%。这一步的关键不是“差异存在”，而是排除了采样噪声：连 greedy 都分叉，说明问题来自执行路径本身。

第二步是根因识别。作者指出 cache-ON 与 cache-OFF 在 kernel 结构、内存布局和累加顺序上不同，而 FP16 加法非结合，因此同样的数学表达式会得到不同舍入结果。为了把“相关性”推进到“因果性”，他们做了受控 FP32 falsification：把相关计算切到 FP32 后，KV divergence 降到 2.5×10^-8 的噪声地板，相比 FP16 的 full-model accumulated KL divergence 下降超过 8 个数量级。这个实验很有说服力，因为它不是简单比较不同精度精度高低，而是利用更高精度消除“累加顺序差异被舍入放大”的机制。

第三步是解释一个表面矛盾。论文观察到：越早发生 token flip 的样本，KL divergence 往往越高，看起来像是在说“早翻转不是因为误差积累，而是因为误差本来就大”。作者进一步分析 decision boundary，指出早翻转和高 KL 的共同原因是模型本身更不确定，而不是 divergence 累积机制失效。也就是说，误差是逐步积累的，但只有当 logits 靠近决策边界时，较小的数值偏移才会更早转化为离散 token 差异。这个解释把“数值误差大小”和“行为可见性”区分开了。

第四步是因果定位。作者使用 activation patching 尝试通过替换 residual stream hidden states 来恢复 cache-ON/cache-OFF 的一致性，结果中位恢复率不到 1%，而且同时 patch 所有层时平均恢复甚至为负。这是一个很强的负结果：说明 divergence 主要不在 residual stream，而在 KV cache 状态本身。换句话说，后续层即便拿到“更正确”的 residual，也无法抹掉已经写入缓存并在后续 attention 中反复使用的误差。论文还讨论了 GQA 的放大效应：一个共享 key/value 头上的 FP16 舍入误差会被广播到多个 query heads，因此误差是相关放大，而不是独立平均掉。这解释了为什么某些现代架构可能比标准 MHA 更敏感。

整体上，这篇论文的技术贡献不在提出新算法，而在把一个工程默认假设拆开验证，并给出足够细的机制证据。相比只报告“cache 会导致输出不同”，它更进一步回答了为什么不同、差异如何传播、什么时候会影响最终 token、以及应该去哪里修。1a:T427,For inference and systems research, this matters more than it first appears. KV caching is the default in nearly all production LLM serving stacks, and many optimization papers implicitly treat cache-ON and cache-OFF as interchangeable baselines. This paper shows that such equivalence cannot be assumed in FP16. The implications are at least threefold. First, reproducibility: the same model, prompt, and hardware can produce different outputs solely because cache usage differs, even under greedy decoding. Second, systems methodology: if serving, benchmarking, debugging, interpretability, or safety analysis are performed under mismatched cache settings, conclusions may be confounded by execution-path bias. Third, mechanistic analysis: the paper localizes the divergence to the stateful KV cache rather than the residual stream, which is where much interpretability work tends to focus. For foundation-model researchers, the broader lesson is that the inference path is part of model behavior; implementation details are not always behaviorally transparent.1b:Tae9,The paper is structured as a clean causal investigation.

First, it establishes the phenomenon behaviorally. The authors compare cache-ON and cache-OFF decoding on three open-weight models—LLaMA-2-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Gemma-2-2B—using GSM8K under greedy, top-k, and top-p decoding. Across all model and decoding conditions, token divergence occurs in 100% of cases. The key point is that greedy decoding also diverges, which rules out sampling randomness and ties the effect directly to the execution path.

Second, it identifies the numerical root cause. Cache-ON and cache-OFF differ in kernel structure, memory layout, and accumulation ordering. Because FP16 arithmetic is non-associative, mathematically equivalent computations can round differently. To move beyond correlation, the paper performs a controlled FP32 falsification. When the relevant computation is moved to FP32, KV divergence drops to a noise floor of 2.5×10^-8, more than eight orders of magnitude below the FP16 full-model accumulated KL divergence. This is strong evidence that the mechanism is accumulation-order sensitivity amplified by FP16 rounding.

Third, the paper resolves an apparent contradiction in the behavioral data. Early token flips are associated with higher KL divergence, which might seem to contradict an accumulation story. The authors analyze decision-boundary proximity and show that both early flips and higher KL are jointly explained by model uncertainty. The divergence still accumulates over time, but it becomes behaviorally visible earlier when logits are already close to a decision boundary. This separates numerical accumulation from behavioral threshold crossing.

Fourth, the paper localizes the effect causally. Using activation patching on residual-stream hidden states, the authors attempt to recover equivalence between cache-ON and cache-OFF. Recovery is negligible: median recovery is below 1%, and patching all layers simultaneously yields negative mean recovery. This is a strong null result. It implies that the divergence is not primarily a residual-stream phenomenon; it lives in the KV cache state itself. Once rounding error is written into the cache and repeatedly reused by later attention operations, downstream residual interventions cannot undo it. The paper also argues that grouped-query attention amplifies the effect because one shared key/value rounding error is broadcast to multiple query heads, producing correlated rather than independent errors.

The technical contribution is therefore diagnostic rather than algorithmic. The paper takes an unexamined systems assumption, tests it rigorously, and provides mechanistic evidence for where the discrepancy comes from, how it propagates, and why standard residual-stream interventions fail to fix it.1c:T92e,The experiments cover three models—LLaMA-2-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Gemma-2-2B—on GSM8K with greedy, top-k, and top-p decoding. The headline result is that token divergence between cache-ON and cache-OFF reaches 100% in all nine model-by-decoding conditions. This establishes that the effect is systematic rather than occasional.

On accuracy, cache-ON outperforms cache-OFF in 8 of 9 conditions, with significance under McNemar tests after Bonferroni correction at p<0.05. The paper is careful not to interpret this as a claim that cache-ON is intrinsically better. Instead, the asymmetry is treated as evidence of systematic execution-path bias: if two paths are supposed to be equivalent but one consistently shifts outcomes in one direction, the divergence is structured rather than random.

The root-cause experiment is especially important. Switching to FP32 reduces KV divergence to 2.5×10^-8, more than eight orders of magnitude below the FP16 full-model accumulated KL divergence. The reported per-step accumulation ratio is roughly 150–270× across models, while the mean relative accumulation error stays approximately flat at about 0.036% regardless of sequence length. This suggests that the per-step error scale is largely architecture- and implementation-determined, while sequence length mainly controls how often the error is written and reused.

For the early-flip versus high-KL question, the uncertainty-based explanation is supported in 8 of 9 conditions using t-tests with p<0.05. In the localization experiment, residual-stream activation patching yields less than 1% median recovery, and patching all layers together produces negative mean recovery. That is one of the paper’s strongest mechanistic results: residual correction does not repair the divergence and can even worsen it.

Methodologically, the paper is strong in three ways: it shows consistency across models and decoding strategies, it includes a falsification experiment rather than relying on correlation, and it reports an informative negative result. The limitations are also clear: the models are still moderate in size, the task suite is narrow, and the study does not yet cover larger models, long-context settings, or a broader range of serving kernels.
