18:T481,MoEvolve is a bidirectional distillation framework with a recovery-then-improvement schedule. The original low-budget model M_k and the expanded model M_k′ start from the same parameters and differ only in the number of experts activated per token. First, M_k is used as the teacher to recover M_k′ through on-policy distillation. The student samples trajectories from its current policy, and the teacher provides dense token-level next-token distributions along those trajectories; the KL objective uses stop-gradient teacher logits. This stage repairs the behavioral disruption caused by increasing k. Second, the recovered M_k′ is improved with RLVR, using SAPO to reduce instability from the mismatch between rollout and training policies. Third, the direction is reversed: the strengthened M_k′ becomes the teacher for the original M_k, transferring the gains back to the low-budget inference setting. Compared with external-teacher OPD, the teacher is fully compatible by construction. Compared with self-distillation using privileged hints, the method does not require hand-designed intermediate signals or ground-truth partial solutions.19:T885,方法名是 TreeOPSD，核心由两步组成。

第一步是 tree-aware credit construction。作者利用多轮交互天然存在的前缀分叉结构：对同一个历史前缀，采样多个后续 continuation，形成 rollout tree。这样可以把“某个 turn 之后的选择是否有帮助”与此前历史解耦。然后在树上做 recursive backup，把叶子节点的终局 verifier reward 回传成各个 turn 的 backed-up credit。这里的关键不是简单把终局 reward 平均分摊到所有 token，而是利用共享前缀下不同分支的结果差异，得到更细粒度的 turn-level 相对信用。

第二步是 posterior self-distillation。作者不是直接拿 backed-up credit 做 REINFORCE/PPO 权重，而是相对于冻结的行为策略 π_old，通过一个仿射映射把每个 turn 的 credit 变成一个有界的 teacher distribution。这个 teacher 是“后验”意义上的目标分布：高 credit 的动作概率被抬高，低 credit 的动作概率被压低，但整体仍 anchored 在行为策略上，因此不会失控。随后训练当前策略 π_θ 去最小化与 teacher 的 Forward-KL。作者强调两个性质：1）在 π_θ = π_old 的切点处，这个 distillation objective 的梯度与 tree policy gradient 一致；2）随着当前策略接近 teacher，梯度会自然衰减，到达目标后为零，若超过目标则反向，因而比固定 advantage 权重的多轮 mini-batch 更新更稳定。

文中还给出一个结构性质：由于 credit 到 target distribution 的仿射映射与 tree backup 可交换，父节点目标的匹配在梯度上等价于对子孙节点蒸馏损失的聚合。这一点很重要，因为它说明 turn-level target 不是拍脑袋定义，而是和树上的递归 credit 传播保持一致。

与 PPO 的区别很清楚：PPO 仍是 ratio-weighted policy gradient，加 clipping 抑制过大偏移；TreeOPSD 则把优化对象从“沿某个梯度方向上升”改成“逼近一个冻结 teacher 分布”。与 OPD/OPSD 的区别在于 teacher 不来自外部强模型，也不依赖 privileged context，而是直接由 rollout tree + terminal reward 构造。1a:T417,The paper tackles two coupled problems in long-horizon agentic RL with verifiable rewards. First, when supervision comes only from a terminal verifier reward, intermediate turns receive extremely sparse and noisy credit. Broadcasting one trajectory-level advantage across the whole rollout over-credits irrelevant actions in successful runs and under-credits useful actions in failed ones. Second, when a fixed rollout batch is reused across sequential mini-batch updates, the current policy drifts away from the behavior policy that generated the data, creating policy staleness. Existing methods usually address one side at a time: critics or process reward models provide denser supervision but add training complexity and their own failure modes, while PPO-style importance weighting and clipping control drift without defining a finite target that the optimization can converge to. The paper aims to derive turn-level supervision directly from rollout trees and terminal rewards, then turn the RL update into a posterior distillation problem.1b:T9fa,The method, TreeOPSD, has two main components.

The first is tree-aware credit construction. Multi-turn interaction naturally exposes branching prefixes: from the same history prefix, one can sample multiple alternative continuations, forming a rollout tree. This isolates the effect of decisions made after a given turn while holding the preceding context fixed. Terminal verifier rewards at the leaves are then recursively backed up through the tree to produce turn-level credit. The point is not to spread a final reward uniformly across all tokens, but to exploit outcome differences among sibling branches under a shared prefix to obtain more informative relative credit for each turn.

The second is posterior self-distillation. Instead of plugging the backed-up credit directly into REINFORCE or PPO weights, the method maps turn-level credit through an affine transformation relative to the frozen behavior policy π_old, producing a bounded teacher distribution for each turn. This teacher can be interpreted as a posterior target: actions with higher backed-up credit get their probability increased, lower-credit actions get suppressed, but the target remains anchored to the behavior policy. The current policy π_θ is then trained to minimize a Forward-KL divergence to this teacher.

Two properties are central. First, at the tangent point where π_θ equals π_old, the gradient of the distillation objective recovers the exact tree policy-gradient direction. Second, unlike a fixed collection-time advantage weight, the student-teacher residual shrinks as the student approaches the target, becomes zero at the target, and reverses sign after overshoot. Given a reference policy and temperature, there is a Gibbs target under which the KL-adjusted reward is constant on the support of the target distribution. GGPO does not estimate this target policy explicitly, and it does not apply learner-to-behavior importance weights. Instead, it uses groups of responses sampled from the behavior policy for the same prompt, and measures how far the within-group KL-adjusted rewards are from satisfying that constancy condition.
The resulting objective minimizes a log-mean-exp contrast of KL-adjusted rewards inside each response group. This is different from GRPO-style relative advantage updates, and also different from AGRO, which minimizes the variance of the same adjusted reward. The distinction matters: GGPO and AGRO share the same regularized optimum under common support, but they induce different optimization geometry and different finite-group guarantees.
The paper makes two theoretical moves. First, in the on-policy population setting, the GGPO objective is exactly the KL-regularized optimality gap. That gives the loss a clean interpretation: it is not merely a heuristic group contrast, but a direct measure of suboptimality under KL regularization. Second, in the off-policy setting, the analysis does not try to recover an unbiased estimate of that population quantity. Instead, it proves a direct reward comparison: under bounded coverage, the raw finite-group loss controls the gap between the learner’s expected reward from a single sample and the behavior policy’s best-of-K reward, up to explicit terms for regularization, reference-behavior mismatch, group size, and finite-sample error.
