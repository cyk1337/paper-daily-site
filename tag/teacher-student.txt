19:T6a5,The proposed Replayed-Prefix On-Policy Distillation (ReOPD) framework maintains an offline pool of teacher trajectories collected as a byproduct of teacher RL training, requiring no extra environment interaction. During training, prefixes are sampled from this pool; the student generates its own action at the target step, environment observations are read directly from stored trajectories instead of executing live environment calls, and the teacher only provides per-token distribution supervision at the current step rather than generating full trajectories. Theoretically, the gap between ReOPD and the ideal interactive OPD objective is decomposed into two terms: a student occupancy mismatch term from using non-student-generated prefixes, and a teacher reliability term from error when the teacher provides supervision on OOD student-induced histories, proving fully on-policy training is not universally optimal. Guided by this decomposition, multi-turn OPD is framed as reliability-aware prefix distribution design, using a geometric bridge between student and teacher prefix occupancy. A simple step-decay sampling schedule is used for implementation: early in training and for early interaction steps, more student-on-policy prefixes are used when the teacher-student gap is small and the teacher remains reliable; later in training and for late interaction steps, more replayed teacher prefixes are used to reduce noise from unreliable teacher supervision. The framework naturally supports multi-environment training by merging teacher trajectories from separate environments into a single offline pool, eliminating the need to deploy all environments simultaneously during distillation.1a:T4d6,This is a systematic study rather than a novel KD algorithm. After the student generates a trajectory, the sequence is detached during loss computation. Under this stop-gradient view, the per-token KL gradient can be written in a REINFORCE-like form, with a local reward close to log π_T(x_t | prefix) - log π_θ(x_t | prefix). When student-generated prefixes become out of distribution, the teacher’s maximum next-token probability drops. The proposed remedy, Lookahead Group Reward, evaluates the student’s top-K candidate tokens by looking one step ahead: a candidate is preferred if it leads to higher teacher confidence at the next position, since that implies more discriminative future supervision. Key reasoning:
- Off-policy Teacher Decay: teacher supervision at position t is conditioned on the student prefix y^S_{<t}, which is off-policy for the teacher and drifts away from the teacher’s aligned distribution.
- The paper proposes a measurable diagnostic via a recoverability gap: compare teacher accuracy from the original prompt A_T(x) versus accuracy when forced to continue from a student-generated prefix A_T(x | y^S_{<t}); the gap grows with t.
- ESR avoids training on positions where the teacher is no longer in its “natural reasoning state.”
Explanatory findings:

2) Sub-mode Commitment: ESR students may commit to a supported sub-mode of the teacher distribution instead of chasing the dominant mode; this can sometimes outperform the teacher.

The intuition is simple: do not imitate every teacher continuation uniformly; emphasize teacher behavior that the student is likely to encounter under its own decoding dynamics and that is associated with better outcomes.
