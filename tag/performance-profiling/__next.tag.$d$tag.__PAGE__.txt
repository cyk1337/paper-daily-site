9:T655,FleetSieve treats profiling as downstream decision identification. For each workload-class/configuration cell, it maintains intervals for sustainable capacity and tail latency rather than a single estimate. It then solves two fleet allocations under the same resource constraints and lexicographic objective: a conservative allocation using lower capacity and upper tail bounds, and an optimistic allocation using upper capacity and lower tail bounds. The objective first enforces minimum fulfillment floors for critical classes when feasible, then maximizes the minimum satisfaction ratio, then total served goodput, and finally reduces fragmentation.

The key idea is the acquisition rule. FleetSieve does not measure the cell with the largest uncertainty, nor does it try to cover the grid uniformly. It measures the configuration expected to reduce disagreement between the conservative and optimistic downstream allocations. In other words, a cell with high uncertainty is not important if no plausible outcome would change the fleet decision. Conversely, a cell becomes important if resolving it could flip SLO feasibility or alter resource allocation for a critical workload.

The paper also introduces a conditional decision certificate with three states: Certified-Feasible, Undecided, and Certified-Infeasible. The wording is careful. The certificate is not distribution-free or unconditional; it is valid only under the empirical uncertainty model used to bound the unknown performance table. That restraint is a good sign: the method is presented as an engineering decision tool, not as a universal guarantee.a:T491,The evaluation is intentionally narrow and concrete: one 31B open-weight decoder model, one H100 node, FP8, a vLLM-based serving stack, speculative decoding disabled, and candidate TP degrees {2, 4, 8}. Each profiling cell is measured for 300 seconds. The main metric is how many profiling GPU-seconds are needed to recover the oracle aggregate fleet decision.

The headline result is modest but real: on the fixed H100 measurement grid, FleetSieve reaches the oracle aggregate decision with 22,200 GPU-seconds, a 6.9% reduction relative to uniform profiling. The paper is refreshingly explicit that gains are selective rather than universal: the reduction is larger for Chat and absent for Code. That matters because it clarifies when the method helps. If unresolved cells can still change the global allocation, decision-aware profiling is useful. If the allocation is effectively determined early, the extra machinery may not buy much.
