18:T62d,The method consists of three translation stages and a diagnosis-repair loop. T1 maps the natural-language input x to a formal expression y_orig. T2 back-translates y_orig into natural language x'. T3 re-formalizes x' into y_rt. The system then checks whether y_orig and y_rt are equivalent under the target formalism Σ. In the instantiated setup, Σ is many-sorted first-order logic and equivalence is checked with an SMT solver.

The key idea is to treat roundtrip consistency as a semantic preservation test. If all three stages preserve meaning, the original and roundtrip formalizations should be equivalent. The authors are careful about the limitation: equivalence is necessary but not sufficient. The pipeline may converge to a wrong but self-consistent fixed point where both formalizations agree while still misrepresenting the original text. To address that, they add an independent NLI-based analysis to test whether equivalence-passing cases indeed show lower semantic drift.

When equivalence fails, an LLM judge inspects the full tuple (x, y_orig, x', y_rt) and predicts which stage is responsible. Repair is then scoped to that stage only. This is the practical core of the paper: verification is not just a score, but a mechanism for localized correction. The paper also separates syntax repair from semantic verification. Since LLM-generated formulas may be unparsable or ill-typed, each formula is given up to five LLM-based correction attempts before semantic equivalence is checked. That layered design avoids conflating parser failures with semantic failures.19:T4c0,The evaluation uses 150 traffic rules from the Texas Transportation Code, formalized in many-sorted first-order logic. The models are Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.2. The headline result is that diagnosis-guided repair substantially improves formal equivalence rates: from roughly 45%–61% without repair to 83%–85% after repair, for both models. The paper also compares against a random-stage repair baseline, showing that the gain is not just from repeated retries. Localizing the faulty stage matters.

For Claude, the full pipeline nearly doubles the number of successful repairs relative to random repair while reducing cost per successful repair by 41%. That is an important systems result: the method improves both effectiveness and efficiency.

The second important result is the independent NLI analysis. Samples that pass the formal equivalence check exhibit lower semantic drift than those that fail, which supports the claim that roundtrip equivalence is a meaningful proxy for faithfulness, even if it is not a complete guarantee.
