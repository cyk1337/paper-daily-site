19:T94c,技术路线分三层。

第一层是连续可微 rollout 下的理论化。作者采用 differentiable rollout / additive-noise parameterization，把策略采样写成由噪声驱动的可微变换，然后分析 PPO/GRPO 的 actor 更新。结论是，在这种参数化下，actor 梯度的期望里会出现沿轨迹反传的 costate，而这个 costate 的条件期望等于 value function 对状态的梯度，也就是 value gradient。这里的关键不是把 PPO 变成传统 actor-critic，而是说明即便没有显式训练一个 V 网络，actor 反向传播本身也携带了与 value gradient 同型的信号。

第二层是把这个结论搬到离散 transformer。真实 LLM 不是连续动作控制，而是离散 token 采样。作者讨论了离散采样造成的 sampling gap，并证明 autodiff 计算得到的经验 costate 可以近似连续 BPTT costate，误差受两个因素控制：一是采样离散化带来的 gap，二是策略熵。直观上，分布越尖、采样越接近 argmax，离散动作对隐藏状态的扰动越像一条局部可微路径；同时 transformer 的 attention 让后续 token 的损失能通过上下文表示回传到前面 token 的隐藏状态，于是形成一种“绕过采样瓶颈”的 credit transport。论文强调 attention 不是 value function，但它提供了 value-gradient 信号传播的结构通道。

第三层是 RL-impact decomposition。作者把 RL 带来的潜在收益拆成 usable value-gradient signal 与 reachable reward headroom 的乘性或组合关系。前者衡量当前 checkpoint 的内部表示和策略形状是否允许 reward 信号有效回传；后者衡量当前策略离 reward 饱和还有多远。如果模型太弱，虽然 headroom 大，但 value-gradient 信号可能传不动；如果模型太强，虽然 credit transport 好，但 headroom 已经很小，RL 收益也有限。于是最适合做 RL 的 checkpoint 应该处在一个中间区间：既足够成熟，能传递信用；又没有完全饱和，还留有可优化空间。

与现有解释的区别在于，作者不是从 advantage normalization、group sampling 或 KL regularization 这些训练技巧出发解释 GRPO，而是从反向传播结构本身解释“critic-free 为何不完全 value-free”。这使得论文更像在建立一个机制模型，而不是给出局部经验法则。1a:T48e,The contribution is more than a post hoc story for GRPO. It gives a concrete mechanism-level bridge between pretraining and post-training. A common narrative says RL mainly works by exploration plus scalar reward shaping, but that does not explain why long-sequence, terminal-reward, critic-free optimization can still improve reasoning so reliably in transformers. The value-gradient hypothesis offers a more architecture-aware explanation: backward passes through hidden states and attention already carry an approximate credit-transport signal. For pretraining researchers, this matters in two ways. First, RL effectiveness may depend not only on reward quality, but also on whether a checkpoint has learned representations that support this kind of differentiable credit transport. Second, the question of when to start RL becomes measurable rather than purely heuristic. In terms of contribution type, this is primarily an explanatory framework with predictive implications, not a new RL algorithm. But if the hypothesis holds up, it can influence checkpoint selection, mid-training strategy, cooldown design, and the choice of base models for RL post-training.1b:T9c1,The technical argument has three layers.

First, the paper analyzes actor updates under a differentiable rollout / additive-noise parameterization. Policy sampling is written as a differentiable transformation of exogenous noise, and the PPO/GRPO actor gradient is studied in that setting. The result is that the expected actor update contains a trajectory-wise backward-propagated costate whose conditional expectation matches the gradient of the value function with respect to state, i.e. the value gradient. The point is not to reintroduce a learned critic under another name, but to show that even without an explicit value network, the actor backward pass itself can carry a value-gradient-like signal.

Second, the paper transfers this picture to discrete transformer policies. Real LLMs generate discrete tokens, so exact pathwise differentiation is unavailable. The authors analyze the sampling gap induced by discretization and show that the empirical costate obtained by autodiff can approximate the continuous BPTT costate, with error controlled by the sampling gap and policy entropy. Intuitively, when the policy is sharper and sampling stays closer to a locally stable mode, the discrete trajectory behaves more like a differentiable path. Meanwhile, transformer attention provides a structural route for later losses to propagate back through contextual representations, partially bypassing the token-sampling bottleneck. Attention is not itself a value function, but it acts as a transport mechanism for value-gradient-like credit.

Third, the paper derives an RL-impact decomposition. The expected benefit of RL is separated into usable value-gradient signal and reachable reward headroom. The first term captures whether the current checkpoint’s representations and policy geometry allow reward information to propagate effectively. The second captures how much reward improvement is still available before saturation. A weak checkpoint may have large headroom but poor credit transport; a very strong checkpoint may have good transport but little remaining headroom. The best RL starting point should therefore lie in an intermediate regime: mature enough to transmit credit, but not yet saturated.

Relative to prior explanations of GRPO, the novelty is that the paper does not focus on advantage normalization, group sampling, or KL regularization as the main reason for success. Instead, it explains critic-free RL through the backward structure of transformers themselves.1c:T7f2,The experiments are organized around three questions: whether the proposed signal can be observed in real transformers, whether it predicts RL gains, and whether it helps choose checkpoints.

The first set of experiments validates the correspondence between empirical costates and value-gradient-like signals. The authors extract a costate or credit signal from autodiff in discrete transformers and compare it with the target quantity suggested by the continuous-relaxation analysis. The reported alignment improves as the sampling gap shrinks and policy entropy decreases, which matches the theory. In other words, the actor backward pass looks more value-gradient-like when the model operates in a moderately confident but not fully collapsed regime.

The second set tests the predictive power of the RL-impact decomposition. Multiple checkpoints along pretraining are evaluated, with usable value-gradient signal and reward headroom measured before RL, then compared against actual PPO/GRPO gains. The main result is that RL gains are typically not monotonic in checkpoint maturity. Early checkpoints have more headroom but weaker credit transport; late checkpoints are more stable but closer to reward saturation. Intermediate checkpoints often benefit the most.

The third set likely includes ablations over sampling temperature/entropy, reward structure (terminal-only versus process reward), PPO versus GRPO, and analyses of where attention-based credit transport appears across layers or token positions. The emphasis is not benchmark chasing, but mechanism validation and predictive correlation.

The provided excerpt does not include the full tables, so model sizes, number of checkpoints, task suites, and exact gain values cannot be quoted reliably here. When reading the paper, the most important things to verify are: (1) the concrete computable proxy used for usable value-gradient signal, (2) its correlation or ranking consistency with downstream RL gains, and (3) robustness across entropy regimes and task lengths.1d:T7a1,This one is worth a careful read. A good order is: theory first, mechanism validation second, recipe implications third.

Start with the introduction and setup. The key is not the GRPO formula itself, but the motivating puzzle: how can long-range credit assignment work without a critic? That question anchors the whole paper.

Then read the continuous-relaxation theory carefully. Focus on three points: what assumptions are required by the additive-noise / differentiable-rollout setup, how the costate appears through BPTT, and why its conditional expectation matches the value gradient. This determines whether the paper is making a real derivation or only an analogy.
