Stage 1 is architecture conversion. From Qwen3-0.6B-Base, every fourth layer remains native GQA, leaving 7 full-attention layers and replacing the other 21 with KDA. The KDA stack uses 16 heads, head dimension 128, causal short convolution with k=4, L2-normalized q/k, low-rank forget gates, beta write gates, gated RMSNorm, and NoPE. This yields a hybrid model rather than a pure linear-attention model: 75% of layers use O(1) recurrent state, while the retained GQA layers still have linearly growing KV cache. A notable engineering point is gate initialization. The paper shows that near-total retention destabilizes the recurrent state, while excessive forgetting performs worse than random; their adopted g6 initialization gives a median forget half-life of about 6.3 tokens.

Stage 2 is two-step distillation. The first step is layer-wise hidden-state alignment: each KDA layer is trained against the corresponding teacher hidden state, with the teacher frozen and errors detached across layers. Only KDA parameters are updated in this phase, 193.6M trainable parameters total, at 8,192 tokens per step. This reduces CE from 9.48 to about 4.13, but C-Eval stays at chance. The second step is end-to-end forward-KL distillation on pretraining text. The objective is pure forward KL between teacher and student next-token distributions, without mixing in CE. Trainable parameters now include KDA, all MLPs, and all LayerNorms, 457.9M in total, while embeddings, tied lm_head, and the 7 retained attention layers remain frozen. To fit a single 32GB GPU budget, they implement chunked fp32 KL instead of materializing full logits. After 7,000 steps over 57.3M tokens, validation CE reaches 2.9385 versus the teacher’s 2.8108, a gap of only +0.128 nats, with validation KL 0.160, yet C-Eval is still only 28.8%.

Stage 3 is diagnosis and repair. The diagnostic is a four-permutation multiple-choice test: 161 clean four-option questions are constructed from the training corpus with zero benchmark overlap, and each question is evaluated under four cyclic rotations of option labels. A content-following model should track the correct option as labels rotate; a model that simply forgot should fail roughly uniformly. Instead, the converted model predicts “A” 81.06% of the time, and 106 of 161 questions keep the same predicted label under all four rotations. The average margin on the correct option is negative, -0.117 nats. This points to label-interface collapse rather than uniform forgetting. The repair stage then runs 1,000 steps of completion-only KL on teacher-verified MCQ permutations, poetry, and translation QA, explicitly targeting the question-option-answer interface. The authors apply Logit Lens to track the layer-by-layer evolution of answer-option logits in MCQA prompts. For each sample, they cache hidden states from every transformer block in a single forward pass. At each layer, they take the hidden state at the answer position, apply the model’s final layer norm, project it through the unembedding matrix, and extract only the logits for the option-label tokens such as A/B/C/D. This yields a ranking trajectory across layers. HDL is then defined as the point beyond which the relative ranking of options remains stable.

That definition is stronger than simply checking whether top-1 stops changing. It captures whether the model’s internal preference structure has settled. The paper then tests whether this phenomenon is architecture-level rather than an artifact. It does so through cross-model and cross-dataset evaluation, LoRA before/after comparisons, varying the number of answer options, and changing label formats. The label-format result is important: alphabetic labels and Arabic numerals produce sharper HDLs than Roman numerals, which hints that tokenizer statistics and token geometry affect how clearly commitment can be read out.

Relative to prior work, the contribution is diagnostic rather than algorithmic. No learned exit policy is trained, and no layers are removed. The paper tries to measure committed inference as a naturally emerging property of standard transformers. The main caveat is also clear: the analysis depends on Logit Lens, whose faithfulness on intermediate layers is still debated, and the setup is restricted to single-token answer labels rather than open-ended generation.e:T5a4,The study covers four model families—Qwen, Llama, Granite, and Mistral—on four MCQA benchmarks.
