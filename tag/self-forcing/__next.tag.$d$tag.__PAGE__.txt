9:T756,The method unifies two complementary views. At the objective level, consistency models are treated as forward-divergence-like, trajectory-preserving, and mode-covering, whereas DMD is treated as reverse-divergence-like and directly aligned with the student-generated distribution. At the causal training level, teacher-forcing plays the role of stable offline supervision on teacher trajectories, while self-forcing plays the role of on-policy refinement under autoregressive rollouts.

Causal-rCM combines them as teacher-forcing CM plus self-forcing DMD. In the first part, the student learns few-step causal distillation on offline causal contexts and teacher trajectories, which gives a stable initialization under correct histories. In the second part, the student is optimized on its own rollouts so that the distilled few-step sampler better matches inference-time behavior. The paper argues that this complementarity can be realized either jointly or in a staged forward-to-reverse schedule.

A useful part of the paper is its systematic treatment of initialization for self-forcing DMD. It compares several existing choices, including ODE-pair regression, diffusion-forcing-style causal adaptation, and hybrid TF/DF initialization, against TF-CM initialization. The claimed takeaway is that reverse/on-policy optimization alone is too brittle when coverage is poor, while a forward/offline consistency stage provides a much better basin for subsequent DMD refinement.

The engineering layer is also nontrivial. The authors implement teacher-forcing continuous-time consistency models, including sCM and MeanFlow variants, in an autoregressive causal-mask setting. The custom-mask FlashAttention-2 JVP kernel is the key systems piece that makes this practical. Third, the overall method is presented as a leading open recipe for AR diffusion distillation in streaming video generation and interactive world models.
