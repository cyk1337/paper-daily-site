9:T6ef,TIDE casts fine-grained evaluation as decision-tree induction. The input is a benchmark and a behavior matrix Y over models and samples, where entries can be accuracy, rubric scores, or pass rates. The key design choice is to sharply separate the role of the LLM judge from the role of the supervision signal. The judge only proposes candidate split rules from sample content and assigns samples to candidate groups. It does not decide which capability structure is correct.

Selection is driven by a behavior-based information gain computed from the behavior matrix itself. The gain combines two impurities: one encourages intra-model consistency within child nodes, meaning a given model behaves more coherently inside a slice; the other encourages inter-model separability, meaning different models become easier to distinguish on that slice. This makes the induced tree behavior-aligned rather than semantically clustered.

That distinction matters. Existing fine-grained methods often rely on benchmark metadata, hand-written weakness lists, or external taxonomies with fixed granularity. Hierarchical methods may also build trees from semantic similarity or external ontologies. TIDE instead uses behavior as supervision and semantics only as a source of candidate hypotheses. The ablations are designed to test whether the method works because the LLM proposes plausible rules, or because the behavior-based objective truly selects informative splits; the paper argues that the latter is the main signal.

The method still has clear limits. Interpretability depends on the quality of judge-generated rules, and the tree can only reorganize the benchmark it sees; it cannot repair missing benchmark coverage. But within those limits, it is a clean evaluation framework.a:T60c,The experiments cover five frontier models on four benchmarks, with emphasis on stability and diagnostic resolution rather than leaderboard performance.

1. Stability: the paper reports dev-to-test consistency in weakness rankings, suggesting that the induced trees are not merely overfitting idiosyncratic development-set partitions.

2. Fine-grained amplification: worst-leaf accuracy drops reach as high as 37.91 points. This is the central empirical claim: models that look close under aggregate scores can be far apart on specific capability slices.

3. Concrete findings: on MMLU-Pro, TIDE identifies a 16.7-point leaf-level gap between Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.4 on microeconomic market reasoning. On DROP, it finds a 41.2-point regression from GPT-5.2 to GPT-5.4 on large-number computation. These are useful examples because the slices are induced from behavior rather than pre-annotated categories.

4. Transferability: the paper claims that diagnostic trees transfer across benchmarks. This should be read as partial structural reuse of capability slices, not as evidence of a universal ontology.

5. Ablations: the most important ablation result is that behavior-based split selection, not just the LLM-generated rule pool, drives the diagnostic value. Without that, the method would risk collapsing into taxonomy generation plus ordinary grouping.
