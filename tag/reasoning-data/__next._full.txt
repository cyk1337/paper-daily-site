18:T440,LeAct treats CoT z as a latent variable connecting state x to expert action y*, with an explicit reason-then-act factorization pθ(z,y|x)=pθ(z|x)pθ(y|x,z). Training data provides (x, π*(·|x)) but no z.

Algorithmically, it uses a backward proposal and an importance-weighted objective:
- Backward proposal q_bwd(z|x,y): prompt the student to generate multiple candidate CoTs conditioned on (x,y).
- Use an N-sample IWAE bound to derive gradients that become a weighted SFT update over candidates: each candidate gets weight w_i = pθ(z_i,y|x) / q_bwd(z_i|x,y), normalized to ~w_i. Intuitively, this is an E-step-like explanation selection: keep explanations that increase the model’s own probability of reproducing the expert action pθ(y|x,z).
- The M-step target is the expert policy π*: closed-form when a full distribution is available; a point mass when only a single action is observed.

Compared to naive CoT generation (which suffers from rationalization), the load-bearing component is the closed-loop ranking by action-likelihood improvement, not linguistic plausibility.19:T4b0,The method has two stages. First, the authors build OpenCoF-17K, a dataset of 17,312 videos across 11 task families. Each example contains an initial conditioning image, a textual prompt, and a target reasoning video. Videos are normalized to 480p, 15 fps, and 81 frames to reduce optimization mismatch across sources. The curation pipeline combines instance-based rendering, expert-guided rendering, procedural scene synthesis, and repurposed existing videos. Wan2.2-I2V-A14B is then fine-tuned on this dataset to produce Wan-CoF, testing whether diverse temporal supervision alone can elicit Chain-of-Frame behavior. Second, the paper studies two reasoning-token designs for DiT-based video generation. Visual Reasoning Tokens are inserted into the visual latent sequence and are meant to capture lower-level visual cues. Textual Reasoning Tokens are appended to the text-conditioning sequence and provide higher-level semantic priors. The two designs intervene at different places: one directly reshapes latent visual generation, while the other modulates denoising through the conditioning path. Attention analysis is used to inspect their effects across layers, denoising steps, space, and time.1a:T452,The method is a controlled experimental framework rather than a new optimizer.
- World generator: each instance is grounded in a synthetic knowledge graph combining (i) a dynamic event/state component (object-centric facts updated over time) and (ii) a static kinship graph.
- Two orthogonal difficulty knobs: D (reasoning depth) controls the length of the target inference chain; T (environment complexity) controls the amount/structure of distractors and interactions around that chain.
- Four task families from the same world: deductive state tracking (forward), abductive recovery (missing event/initial fact), inductive rule induction, and analogy-based relational transfer.
- Training protocol: pretrain on a restricted easy region (shallow D, low T) to make the base distribution controlled; then apply GRPO-based RLVR with different data allocation recipes over the D×T grid (depth-focused, complexity-focused, joint coverage, staged curricula).
- Evaluation emphasizes spatial generalization over the D×T grid and cross-family transfer via correlation/cluster analyses, not just average scores.1b:T411,CoRD, or Collaborative Reasoning Decoding, treats each reasoning step as a high-level decoding unit. At each step, multiple teacher LRMs propose candidate next steps conditioned on the same current prefix. The system scores the resulting partial trajectories and extends promising ones. The method has three central components. First, prompt-guided step segmentation makes reasoning steps more consistent across teachers. Second, predictive perplexity scores a partial reasoning prefix by measuring how well the ground-truth answer is predicted given that prefix; this provides an early signal without waiting for a complete trace. Third, beam search maintains multiple candidate trajectories, which helps preserve paths that initially look weaker but later lead to useful self-correction or strategic shifts. Compared with S1- or LIMO-style curation, CoRD collaborates during generation rather than selecting after generation.
