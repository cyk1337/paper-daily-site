a:T8b9,方法叫 PTD-PO，Privileged Tutoring Distillation Policy Optimization。整体框架是在标准 RLVR，文中以 GRPO 为代表的 group-based policy optimization 之上，加一个针对失败轨迹的 tutoring distillation 分支。

核心思想不是让 teacher 看到标准输入加答案，而是给 frozen reference model 额外提供 answer-free hints。提示信息包括视觉 grounding cues 和高层 reasoning guidance，用来帮助 teacher 在不泄露最终答案的情况下，给出更合理的 token 分布。student 仍然只在原始、无答案上下文下生成。这样 teacher 和 student 处在信息不对称的条件下，属于 learning with privileged information 的变体。

为什么要这样设计，而不是直接 answer-conditioned self-distillation？作者的论点是，答案条件会让 teacher 分布过于尖锐，student 学到的是“朝答案收缩”的捷径，而不是如何恢复正确推理路径。PTD-PO 则试图只提供方向性纠偏，不提供终点本身，因此能在 failed rollout 上给出 dense signal，同时保留探索空间。

技术上还有一个关键点：teacher 和 student 的上下文不同，直接做全词表 KL/JSD 对齐既不稳定也费显存。为此作者提出 Top-K Jensen-Shannon Divergence with tail compensation。直观理解是，只对 teacher/student 分布中最重要的 top-k token 做精细对齐，其余长尾概率质量用补偿项近似，从而在保持分布对齐稳定性的同时降低内存开销。这个设计比单纯 top-k KL 更稳，因为 JSD 本身对分布错配更温和，而 tail compensation 避免把长尾完全截断。

训练流程上，PTD 模块主要作用于 RLVR 中的失败样本，把 outcome-level 的 0/1 或稀疏 reward 转成 token-level 学习信号。论文还强调它与不同 RLVR optimizer 兼容，说明蒸馏项是附加在 policy optimization 外层，而不是绑定某个特定算法。

方法的潜在限制也比较清楚：hint 的构造质量决定 teacher 是否真能提供“教方法不教答案”的信号；如果 hint 过强，仍可能形成隐式泄露；如果 hint 太弱，teacher 分布和 student 差别不大，dense supervision 的收益会下降。b:T979,The method is PTD-PO: Privileged Tutoring Distillation Policy Optimization. It starts from a standard RLVR setup, instantiated with group-based policy optimization such as GRPO, and adds a tutoring distillation branch targeted at failed rollouts.

The key design is to avoid answer-conditioned teachers. Instead, a frozen reference model receives answer-free hints, including visual grounding cues and high-level reasoning guidance. These hints are meant to help the teacher infer a better corrective direction without revealing the final answer. The student policy, however, still operates under the original answer-free context. This creates an asymmetric teacher-student setup in the spirit of learning with privileged information.

Why not simply use answer-conditioned self-distillation? The paper argues that answer-revealing contexts make the teacher distribution too sharp and too deterministic. The student then learns to collapse toward the known answer rather than learning how to recover a valid reasoning path. PTD-PO tries to provide directional guidance instead of endpoint information, so failed rollouts can receive dense token-level supervision while preserving exploration.

A second technical contribution is the distillation objective. Because teacher and student are conditioned on different contexts, full-vocabulary KL or JSD alignment is both memory-heavy and potentially unstable. The paper proposes a Top-K Jensen-Shannon Divergence objective with tail compensation. Intuitively, it aligns the most important top-k probability mass explicitly while approximating the remaining tail mass with a compensation term. This reduces memory cost and should be more stable than naive top-k truncation. Using JSD instead of a one-sided divergence also makes sense under context mismatch, since it is more symmetric and less brittle.

Operationally, the PTD module is applied mainly to failed trajectories, converting sparse outcome rewards into token-level learning signals. The paper also claims compatibility with different RLVR optimizers, suggesting that the distillation term is modular rather than tied to one specific algorithm.

The main limitation is obvious: the quality of the hints matters a lot. If the hints are too strong, they may still leak the answer indirectly. If they are too weak, the teacher offers little additional guidance over the student and the dense supervision benefit shrinks.c:T57b,The experiments cover LVLMs from 2B to 8B parameters on multimodal reasoning benchmarks. The headline claim is that PTD-PO consistently outperforms plain RLVR and distillation-based baselines, while maintaining higher policy entropy, improving recovery from failed rollouts, and reducing the memory overhead of online distillation.

The most important evidence to inspect is not just average benchmark accuracy, but the trade-off the method is supposed to improve:
1. whether gains are consistent across model scales;
2. whether PTD-PO beats external-teacher OPD and answer-conditioned self-distillation in the joint space of performance, training cost, and entropy preservation;
3. whether Top-K JSD with tail compensation actually saves memory and remains stable relative to full-vocabulary or naive top-k distillation;
4. whether failed-rollout recovery analyses show real credit-assignment benefits rather than generic regularization.

The abstract does not provide concrete benchmark numbers, so the main tables matter. When reading, pay special attention to which open LVLM backbones are used, how large the gains are on each benchmark, whether entropy-collapse curves are visibly delayed, and how the hint ablations behave. The hint ablations are especially important: visual cues only, textual reasoning hints only, and both together. Those details determine how convincing the method really is.d:T433,This is a good skim paper. Start with the introduction, because the framing is the main value: pure RLVR is too sparse, external teachers are too expensive, and answer-conditioned distillation encourages shortcuts. That setup explains why the proposed method exists.

In the method section, focus on two parts. First, inspect the privileged tutoring inputs: what exactly is included in the hints, and how the paper argues they do not reveal the answer. Second, read the Top-K JSD with tail compensation objective carefully. That part determines whether the contribution is merely an engineering memory trick or a real solution to asymmetric distillation instability.

In the experiments, prioritize the main result tables, entropy curves, failed-rollout recovery analyses, and comparisons among different distillation objectives. A 25-35 minute read should be enough for the main idea.
