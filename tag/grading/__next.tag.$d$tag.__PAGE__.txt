5:T720,The framework has two phases. Phase 1 is Edit-SFT. From existing rollouts, the model extracts two step-level internal signals offline. The first is posterior belief over the final mark. At each reasoning-step boundary, the partial chain is appended with a final scoring scaffold, and the model’s next-token logits over valid mark tokens are read out. This gives a posterior distribution over the final integer score, from which the expected mark and its change relative to the gold mark can be computed. A step is therefore labeled as moving the model’s belief closer to or farther from the correct score.

The second signal is grounding. Using mask-based scoring, the method estimates whether a reasoning step depends on the student answer, the rubric, and prior reasoning context. This is meant to detect steps that are weakly anchored in external evidence or rules. Using these two signals together, Edit-SFT does not rewrite the whole chain of thought. Instead, it identifies the first atomic step where the reasoning either drifts away from the correct final mark or becomes weakly grounded, and revises only that local segment. A rubric checklist is supplied as privileged context to guide the edit, but the output is not forced into a rigid checklist format.

Phase 2 is Edit-RL. Rather than using plain outcome RL, the method adds belief-guided reward shaping on top of a group-relative outcome reward. In addition to rewarding closeness of the final mark to the gold score, it penalizes excessive mid-trajectory belief drift. Small fluctuations are treated as benign exploration, but large deviations away from the gold mark incur a penalty proportional to the excess drift. This encodes the task structure that grading should progressively converge rather than wander arbitrarily and only be judged at the
