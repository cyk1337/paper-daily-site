a:T4cf,There is no new model architecture; the main contribution is experimental design. The authors evaluate on the full BIRD dev split (1,534 examples) with matched prompts, matched decoding, and execution-based accuracy. They compare three open model families: Qwen2.5-Coder, CodeLlama, and Llama-3.x. Two controls matter. First, they explicitly separate a 2023 family (CodeLlama) from 2024 families (Qwen2.5-Coder and Llama-3.x), so that family differences are not naively conflated with size effects. Second, all comparisons are paired on the same examples and tested with exact McNemar significance tests, rather than relying on unpaired confidence intervals. The ablated recipe has three components: schema linking, self-correction, and self-consistency. Schema linking is tested in two forms: a lexical pruner and a stronger embedding retriever using all-MiniLM-L6-v2, top-k=6 tables, tuned for at least 95% gold-table recall. Self-correction lets the model revise its own SQL draft. Self-consistency samples five candidates and selects by execution-guided majority vote. Cost is reported from wall-clock runtime and GPU-hour pricing, not from token proxies, which makes the frontier more relevant for actual deployment decisions.b:T5dc,The clearest result is the dominance of model generation over raw size. CodeLlama is much weaker and nearly flat with size: 20.93, 21.64, and 24.51 for 7B, 13B, and 34B. To avoid over-attributing this to family identity, the paper adds a 2024 Llama family under matched FP8 serving. That supports the main claim that generation matters more than raw size. Among inference recipes, self-correction is the most robust gain: +3.06 on CodeLlama-7B, +3.65 on Llama-3.1-8B, +2.61 on CodeLlama-34B, and roughly +1.24 to +3.45 on Qwen variants. Gains shrink for stronger models, as expected; for Llama-3.3-70B the gain is only +1.04 with p=0.08. Schema linking yields a notable negative result. Even when the lexical linker is replaced by an embedding retriever with 96.5% gold-table recall, the paper finds no significant improvement over lexical linking or no linking in the tested settings. Self-consistency is judged poor value because it requires sampling and multiple executions while delivering limited accuracy gains.
