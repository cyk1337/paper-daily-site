18:T5b3,The approach is intentionally end-to-end and depends on three coupled steps:
1) Axis decomposition and per-axis scaling fits (Sec. 4): formalize the training corpus as a tensor over tasks T, environments E, and trajectories per (t,e) cell. Define cardinalities DT=|T|, DE=|E|, and a trajectory axis Dτ capturing sampling density/volume, plus a reward-density control knob. Fit independent power-law exponents αT, αE, ατ by varying one axis while controlling others.
2) Pricing synthesis with a common ruler (Sec. 5): use the real-data scaling laws to convert synthetic data into “real-equivalent” units. Introduce κ∈[0,1] as an axis-specific discount (effectiveness of synthetic vs real) and identify a critical synthetic-to-real ratio r* where performance degradation/collapse begins. Reported thresholds are strongly ordered: rE*≈9.4, rT*≈3.4, rτ*≈1.4, with a mechanistic interpretation tied to how strongly each axis is grounded by external truth.
3) Compute-optimal allocation (Sec. 6): solve a constrained optimization over model size N and axis budgets (DE,DT,Dτ) under total compute C, incorporating synthesis costs/discounts. Derive closed-form scaling prescriptions: DE*∝C^0.34, DT*∝C^0.27, Dτ*∝C^0.13, N*∝C^0.26. Further refine the environment axis into world diversity vs environment-structure diversity, yielding αW>αV≈αT>ατ, which maps cleanly to engineering choices (build new worlds vs expand API/structure).19:T4a0,Coverage is designed to test robustness across models, algorithms, and domains rather than a single benchmark:
- Models/algorithms: Qwen3-4B/8B/14B; both GRPO and PPO.
- Domains: five domains including math+code, SWE, web, and UI/tool-use.
- Total: 21 experimental settings (as stated).
Key quantitative takeaways:
1) A direct failure case for scalar “token” scaling: two Qwen3-8B runs with identical total tokens and FLOPs but different (task, environment, trajectory) allocations differ by 14 percentage points in success rate.
2) Stable exponent ordering across settings: αE>αT>ατ; with environment split, αW>αV≈αT>ατ.
3) Synthetic mixture limits: collapse thresholds rE*≈9.4, rT*≈3.4, rτ*≈1.4, implying trajectory-heavy synthetic rollouts are the most fragile.
4) The compute-optimal allocation derived from fitted laws is validated at a held-out compute scale, supporting the claim that the fitted exponents are predictive for budget planning.
A practical interpretation consistent with the reported numbers: once coverage over (t,e) cells is decent, additional trajectory resampling yields the lowest marginal gains and hits synthetic bias issues earliest.1a:T44a,Read it in the order that matches the dependency chain:

Deep-dive prompts: whether reward density behaves consistently across domains; and whether common RL practices that emphasize more rollouts are implicitly spending budget on the least efficient axis.1b:T4dd,ProbePO adds a measurement layer on top of GRPO. First, it selects a small number of reasoning-boundary pivots using a reward-free uncertainty score, so the continuation budget is spent on states where value information is likely to matter. Second, for each selected pivot, it fixes the generated prefix, samples a capped number of continuations from the current rollout policy, scores the completed answers with the verifier, and averages those rewards to estimate the expected terminal reward of that partial solution. These probe continuations are used only for measurement, not as additional policy-update trajectories. Third, it shrinks the sparse value estimates toward the GRPO group mean μ_g to reduce variance. Differences between successive prefix-value estimates define segment-level adjustments. These adjustments are centered into a zero-mean residual and added to the original GRPO advantage. As a result, the mean token-level advantage of each trajectory remains exactly the GRPO value, while different reasoning segments receive different credit. If no pivot is selected, the method recovers GRPO. Compared with dense probing methods such as VinePPO, ProbePO trades exhaustive measurement for sparse, budgeted credit refinement.1c:T675,PGB Curriculum has two main components. The first is gradient-based learning-unit discovery. Motivated by an NTK-style view, the paper treats sample-level gradients as approximate descriptors of local optimization effects, projects them into a lower-dimensional representation, and clusters samples based on those representations. The resulting curriculum units are not grouped by task type or difficulty, but by whether they induce similar parameter-update directions under the current policy. The rationale is simple: group-level scheduling is meaningful only if samples inside a group have reasonably consistent optimization effects.

The second component is a predictive multi-armed bandit. Instead of using recent average advantage or success rate as a direct estimate of utility, the method trains a lightweight temporal predictor that jointly models external learning signals and internal policy states to forecast each group’s future utility. Those predicted utilities are then converted into a Boltzmann sampling distribution, increasing the probability of high-utility groups. The key distinction is that the scheduler predicts future usefulness rather than smoothing past usefulness, and it conditions on policy state rather than only on visible reward-like signals.

The method is designed to avoid two common failure modes. Some samples are already mastered and provide little marginal gain if oversampled. Others are currently too hard, producing nearly indistinguishable rewards and weak gradients. PGB tries to keep sampling mass on the region that is most useful for the current policy, and to move that region as the policy improves.1d:T48d,The empirical results are straightforward. Relative to uniform-sampling GRPO, PGB improves in-domain performance by 8.8% to 14.3% and out-of-domain performance by 14.7% to 16.3%. - Two design questions: which channel should be prioritized, and how to enforce that priority.
- Anchor selection via drift: briefly optimize each channel in isolation and pick the channel that induces the least policy drift as the anchor (intuition: exploitable channels cause large shortcut-driven shifts).
- Gated aggregation: break symmetry by construction—auxiliary-only success is scored no better than failing both channels, while joint success remains better than primary-only success.
