9:T49f,The work has two main components. First, it builds a controlled evaluation protocol on the Speech Accessibility Project dataset. The same audio samples are evaluated under matched prompt conditions with progressively richer clinical context: zero-context control, diagnosis guidance, condensed speech characteristics, speech production, voice quality, condensed full profile, speech plus voice, full clinical profile, and follow-up correction. This design isolates the effect of context from sample difficulty. Second, the authors benchmark nine promptable audio-language models and identify three failure modes: models that are robust but do not benefit, models that degrade as more context is added, and models that are highly format-sensitive. They also examine the apparent gains of Audio Flamingo 3 and argue that the effect is mostly reduced hallucination rather than genuine use of clinical information. To test learnability, they then perform context-dependent LoRA adaptation using a mixture of prompt formats.
