Source-choice labels are obtained through answer-alias matching, while disclosure labels are produced by an LLM judge with categories such as one-sided answers, conflict-aware leaning answers, strict unresolved disclosure, weak disclosure, and truncation. For source choice, the paper fits a mean-difference direction in the residual stream: the normalized difference between the residual centroids for Document 1 and Document 2 answers. This direction is used both as a readout and as a steering vector. In Qwen, the selected source-choice direction is at layer 13 and the mean_decision position; steering is applied at the output of layer 12 so that layer 13 receives the edited residual. The paper then uses CETT to identify sparse MLP neurons whose activation and down-projection norm imply a large contribution to the MLP residual write. The actual neuron writes are projected onto the source-choice direction to test whether those sparse writers feed the same coordinate. Conflict disclosure is modeled separately and appears later, at layer 17 and pre_response_last in Qwen.19:T7f9,The work has two components: the benchmark and the harness. On the data side, LongVideo / 4DMemoryBench includes both multi-evidence questions within a single long video and cross-episode, cross-season, and full-series questions. The task mix covers visual, textual, temporal, and spatiotemporal reasoning. The important design choice is not just longer videos, but evidence dispersed across multiple temporal scales and scene transitions, forcing episodic recall and multi-hop temporal binding.

On the system side, the paper introduces the 4DMemory Harness / 4DMemory Agent. The design principle is task-independent storage plus goal-directed recall. The system first builds reusable, question-independent memories from long video streams, and only after a question arrives does it perform query-conditioned retrieval and specialized evidence acquisition. This is meant to decouple task duration from the native context window of the underlying model. Unlike methods that depend on internal vision-token pipelines or model-specific post-training, the harness is presented as a model-flexible inference framework with a plug-in style interface.

From the full text, the harness integrates four complementary skills, 22+ specialized tool models, and model-specific retrieval/routing components. The core idea is not to train one monolithic model, but to decompose long-horizon reasoning into stages: memory construction, coarse-to-fine retrieval, specialist evidence acquisition when needed, and final reasoning by the foundation model. The paper repeatedly emphasizes that long-horizon performance should be studied as a composition problem across memory, retrieval, tool use, and reasoning, not just as a context-length problem.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear. It evaluates systems rather than only models. It avoids tight coupling to a single model’s internal token pipeline. And it explicitly treats memory construction, retrieval, skill selection, and reasoning as separate bottlenecks that can fail independently.1a:T695,The key confirmed scale numbers are: about 900 hours of video, 1,000+ videos plus two multi-season TV series, and 5,000+ manually reviewed QA pairs. A notable property is that a single question may depend on more than 100 hours of ordered source video. The experiments are organized around comparing base models, context settings, harness usage, and different memory/tool configurations rather than optimizing one leaderboard score.

The main findings are straightforward. First, current foundation models remain far below human-level performance on long-horizon 4D tasks, especially when evidence is distributed across scenes and time scales. Second, simply increasing the context window gives limited returns; even when relevant evidence is retrieved, models still struggle with long-chain temporal and causal integration. Third, the 4DMemory Agent consistently improves over direct end-to-end prompting of the base model, suggesting that memory-centric RAG and specialized evidence acquisition are necessary for this regime.

Retrieved mechanisms seed candidate implementations; compilation, execution, and profiling provide empirical verdicts on the evidence used; these verdicts influence later retrieval and search. The evolutionary search maintains multiple mechanism-distinct implementation lines, so the agent can explore different implementation families rather than prematurely committing to one route.1d:T508,SteerSearch modifies fixed-goal search-reasoning RL into training on interactive trajectories with injected user interventions. The policy is exposed to goal changes after some reasoning or retrieval has already happened, so it must reuse prior evidence while pivoting to the new target. The method has three main ingredients. First, randomized intervention timing: the new user goal is injected at varying points in the trajectory, preventing the model from overfitting to a single interruption pattern. Second, intermediate-answer masking: this discourages the model from prematurely collapsing onto the old answer, making the trajectory more reusable after a goal shift. Third, Contrastive Context Reward (CCR): an auxiliary reward designed to encourage post-intervention reasoning that is aligned with the updated user goal, not merely with the original trajectory.
