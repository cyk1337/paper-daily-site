18:T8fd,方法上最关键的设计有三层。第一层是任务构造。作者选了 graph coloring 和 Sudoku 两个程序化生成、可渲染成图像的任务。它们既需要从图像中读取结构化信息，也需要在此基础上做离散推理，适合把感知和推理拆开。每个样本都配有一个 canonical textual perception 表示 p*，完整保留完成任务所需的视觉信息。模型输出被强制写成“先 perception，后 reasoning”的格式，因此可以直接比较模型生成的 perception 与 oracle perception 是否完全一致，并进一步做 counterfactual reasoning evaluation：把真实 perception 喂给模型，看它在“看对了”的前提下能否推理对。

第二层是机制分析。对 SFT，作者不是只看 token 数，而是同时统计 perception token 占比、loss 占比和梯度范数占比。以 Qwen3-VL 在 graph coloring 上为例，perception 只占 2.5% 的 CoT token，却只贡献 1.3% 的 loss、8.5% 的 gradient norm，说明训练信号确实被 reasoning 主导。随后他们做 loss reweighting，把 perception 部分乘以不同权重，验证感知准确率会随权重上升而改善，但 reasoning 会下降，形成平滑 trade-off。为了避免手工调参，他们进一步引入动态多任务平衡方法，自动调 perception/reasoning 的权重。

对 RL，作者分析的是 reward coupling。标准 outcome reward 只看最终答对与否，因此它与 reasoning correctness 的相关性高，与 perception correctness 的相关性低。文中报告多数设置下，reward 与 reasoning correctness 的相关系数在 0.65 到 1.00 之间，而与 perception correctness 只有 0.34 到 0.43。于是他们加入显式 perception reward，或者用已有工作中的 surrogate reward 近似感知奖励，再观察端到端、感知、推理三者如何变化。结果同样出现 trade-off：感知奖励加大后，perception 提升，但 reasoning 可能受损。

第三层是干预验证。SFT 侧，作者比较固定重加权和动态平衡；RL 侧，比较真实 perception reward 与 surrogate reward。这里的重点不是某个具体权重最好，而是证明“训练信号分配”就是造成不对称优化的主要原因，而不是模型架构本身天然更擅长推理。19:T94c,The method has three important layers. First is task construction. The authors use graph coloring and Sudoku rendered as images. Both require reading structured visual information and then performing discrete reasoning on top of it. Each sample comes with a canonical textual perception representation p*, which fully captures the visual information needed for the task. Model outputs are constrained into a perception-first, reasoning-second format, so perception can be directly scored by exact match against the oracle representation. This also enables counterfactual reasoning evaluation: feed the model the ground-truth perception and measure whether it can reason correctly once perception is no longer the bottleneck.

Second is mechanism analysis. For SFT, the paper goes beyond token counts and measures token share, loss share, and gradient-norm share of the perception segment. On Qwen3-VL for graph coloring, perception occupies only 2.5% of CoT tokens, contributes 1.3% of the loss, and 8.5% of the gradient norm. This shows that optimization is heavily dominated by reasoning tokens. The authors then apply loss reweighting to the perception segment. Perception improves as its weight increases, but reasoning degrades, yielding a smooth trade-off. To avoid manual tuning, they also test dynamic multi-task balancing methods that adaptively adjust perception vs reasoning weights.

For RL, the key concept is reward coupling. Standard outcome rewards only depend on final correctness, so they correlate strongly with reasoning correctness but much less with perception correctness. Reported correlations are r=0.65-1.00 for reasoning and only r=0.34-0.43 for perception in most settings. The authors then add explicit perception rewards, and also test surrogate rewards from prior work when ground-truth perception rewards are unavailable. Again, the intervention exposes a trade-off: stronger perception reward improves perception but can weaken reasoning.

Third is intervention-based validation. On the SFT side, they compare fixed reweighting and dynamic balancing. On the RL side, they compare oracle perception rewards and surrogate rewards. The point is not that one specific weighting scheme wins everywhere, but that training-signal allocation itself explains the asymmetry much better than a vague claim that the architecture is simply better at reasoning.1a:T788,Experiments cover two open VLM families, Qwen3-VL and InternVL3.5, on graph coloring and Sudoku. The most informative results come in several groups.

First, the baseline diagnosis. Under both SFT and RL, reasoning accuracy improves much more than perception accuracy. Moreover, when conditioned on oracle perception, reasoning is often already fairly strong, which means end-to-end failures are still dominated by perception. This pattern is stable across both model families and both tasks.

Second, evidence for token imbalance in SFT. On Qwen3-VL for graph coloring, perception tokens account for only 2.5% of the sequence, but only 1.3% of the loss and 8.5% of the gradient norm. Increasing the perception loss weight raises perception accuracy and lowers reasoning accuracy, but end-to-end accuracy peaks at a modest nonzero upweighting. Relative to standard SFT, this gives up to +13.8 points. Dynamic multi-task balancing improves further, reaching up to +18.2 points over standard SFT.

Third, evidence for reward coupling in RL. Standard outcome reward correlates with reasoning correctness at r=0.65-1.00, but with perception correctness at only r=0.34-0.43. Adding perception reward shifts optimization toward perception; in most settings, end-to-end performance peaks at a nonzero perception-reward weight, with gains up to +6.0 points over standard RL. When oracle perception reward is unavailable, surrogate rewards can still help. The paper evaluates several existing surrogate designs and finds that reward-perception correlation is a useful predictor of whether a surrogate will improve perception. The strongest surrogate gives +3.2 points.

The experimental style is controlled rather than broad. The paper is not trying to win on many benchmarks. It is trying to establish a causal chain: diagnose asymmetry, propose a mechanism, intervene on that mechanism, and observe the resulting trade-off and optimum.1b:T465,For a quick read, start with the introduction and the task formulation. The whole paper depends on understanding how perception and reasoning are disentangled in both outputs and evaluation. Then focus on three sets of figures and tables:
1. The comparison among end-to-end accuracy, perception accuracy, and reasoning accuracy under oracle perception. This tells you where the actual bottleneck sits.
2. The SFT token/loss/gradient allocation analysis and the perception-weight sweep. This is where the optimization argument becomes concrete rather than intuitive.
3. The RL reward-correlation analysis and the perception-reward sweep. The most reusable idea here is the correlation-based diagnostic, not a particular reward formula.

Expected reading time is about 30-45 minutes. If the goal is only to extract the main lesson, read the introduction, analysis framework, SFT mechanism section, RL mechanism section, and conclusion. If the goal is to adapt the idea to your own training setup, pay closer attention to the output-format design, the counterfactual evaluation protocol, and how dynamic balancing is integrated.1c:T64c,The method is simple: multiply each question’s SDPO loss by a dynamic weight derived from the current pass rate. SDPO itself uses the same model as both student and teacher. The teacher is prompted with privileged feedback, such as a correct solution or execution error, and the student is trained to match the teacher through a KL- or JS-based token-level distillation loss. This gives much denser supervision than GRPO, where every token in a rollout shares the same scalar advantage.

The paper’s main technical contribution is the analysis. Prior work linked learnability under binary rewards to the variance term p(1-p), where p is the success probability for a question. Here the authors extend that framework to normalized rewards, as used in GRPO. Under normalization, the leading p(1-p) factor is absorbed, so the remaining per-question update magnitude scales with \sqrt{p(1-p)} rather than p(1-p). This still peaks at p=0.5, which explains why GRPO naturally focuses on intermediate-difficulty questions.

That analysis motivates SC-SDPO. For each question, the current batch of G rollouts gives an empirical pass rate \hat p.
