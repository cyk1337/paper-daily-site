c:T808,The method, PartRep, performs selective repetition instead of duplicating the entire prompt. Given an input sequence t, it chooses a subsequence t_part and appends only that subsequence near the end of the prompt. The goal is to create late-position echoes for the most informative tokens while staying within a repetition budget τ.

The formulation is deliberately constrained: the selector must output a subsequence of the original prompt, not a summary or a rewritten version. This keeps the selector lightweight, since it only needs to make binary keep/drop decisions per token. A more general compressor would require generation in vocabulary space and would likely erase the efficiency advantage.

For token importance, the paper uses token-wise negative log-likelihood as the main signal. The intuition is simple: high-NLL tokens are hard to predict from the preceding context, so their information is not redundantly encoded elsewhere. Repeating them should therefore provide the largest marginal gain when placed later in the sequence. The paper argues, both conceptually and empirically, that repeating high-surprisal tokens is more useful than repeating low-surprisal or random tokens.

Exact NLL, however, would require a full forward pass, which defeats the purpose. To avoid that, the authors train a lightweight gate that predicts the top-τ high-NLL tokens from early-layer hidden states of the target model. The gate is implemented as a small two-layer MLP with attention on top of intermediate representations, enabling token selection during mid-prefill. The selected tokens are then appended through a short natural-language bridge, and the model performs a single final generation pass.

Mechanistically, PartRep does not change model parameters and is not a retrieval method. It exploits a known property of autoregressive transformers: tokens placed later in the sequence are more accessible under causal attention. The key claim is that prompt information is highly non-uniform, so only a small subset of tokens needs to be echoed.d:T595,The paper evaluates on 8 benchmarks, including GSM8K and RULER, and across several decoder-only model families such as Qwen2.5, Llama3.2, and Gemma4. The main result is that PartRep largely preserves the accuracy gains of full repetition, and in some settings slightly exceeds it, while using only 59.4% of the KV cache budget and 79.0% of the prefill FLOPs. This is the central empirical point: the benefit of repetition does not require duplicating the whole prompt.

The gains appear on tasks that require stronger context integration or long-context access, which is consistent with the method’s motivation. The paper also studies token-selection strategies to validate NLL as an importance signal. Even though the provided excerpt does not include all tables, the method description makes clear that the authors compare oracle-like NLL selection, learned gate selection, and weaker baselines such as random or heuristic choices. The important takeaway is not the exact score gap, but the pattern: true NLL ranking is a strong selector, and the lightweight gate is accurate enough to recover most of that benefit without paying the full cost.

Another strength is that the paper treats efficiency as a first-class metric. It reports not only task accuracy but also prefill FLOPs and KV cache footprint.
