18:T4e7,The authors model long-context performance using three predictors: non-embedding parameter count N, short-context pretraining tokens Ds, and long-context continual-pretraining tokens Dl. LongPPL is used as the proxy metric. The proposed empirical law is an additive power law with an offset, where model size, short-context data, and long-context data each contribute separate power-law terms. This separation is important because LCCP starts from a pretrained model; its behavior is not equivalent to training from scratch with a single combined token budget. For monitoring, the paper compares linear-decay and constant-learning-rate trajectories. Linear decay gives stable endpoint performance but requires the total budget to be fixed in advance. A constant learning rate makes cumulative Dl a natural progress coordinate, but raw checkpoints are noisy and underperform budget-matched linear-decay endpoints. The Constant-Last4 protocol evaluates the average of the four most recent consecutive checkpoints. This reduces checkpoint noise and makes constant-LR observations closer to linear-decay performance. With N and Ds fixed, the law reduces to a one-dimensional power law in Dl, allowing later LongPPL to be predicted from early LCCP checkpoints.19:T472,The scaling-law fit uses 109 configurations across five model scales, varying short-context and long-context token budgets under a linear-decay schedule. The fitted law reproduces observed trends and extrapolates to held-out model scales within the tested budget regime. The monitoring experiments apply Constant-Last4 on constant-LR LCCP trajectories and show that the same law remains usable; the paper reports extrapolation from smaller settings to an approximately 100B-token LCCP run for a 1.22B model. Capability validation is done along a 32K LCCP trajectory with RULER, where aggregate performance keeps improving with more long-context tokens and shows no clear saturation in the evaluated range. The authors also test whether the gains survive later stages: 128K context extension and supervised fine-tuning are applied with identical downstream procedures, and the benefits from larger LCCP budgets remain visible on long-context and agentic benchmarks. The provided text does not expose the exact benchmark scores or percentage gains, so the safest reading is about trend predictability rather than a universal optimal budget.1a:T5da,The method has three components. First, dynamic leader selection. For each token, among the Top-K selected experts, one expert is sampled as the leader according to normalized routing scores rather than deterministically taking the top-scoring expert. This prevents one expert from always dominating the unmodified update path. Second, gradient accumulation. Each expert stores a recent history of output gradients as an orthonormal basis using Gram-Schmidt, forming a compact subspace memory of past update directions. Third, gradient projection. The leader keeps its original task gradient. Each follower removes the component of its output gradient that lies in the leader’s accumulated gradient subspace, keeping only the orthogonal component. As a result, co-routed experts processing the same token are discouraged from moving in highly redundant directions.

This differs cleanly from prior work. It does not redesign the router, does not require extra forward or backward passes, and does not enforce diversity through an auxiliary loss in parameter space or representation space. It acts directly on expert output gradients that already exist in the standard training pipeline. In that sense, it is a backward-time intervention rather than a new objective. The extra cost comes from maintaining per-expert gradient bases and performing projections.
