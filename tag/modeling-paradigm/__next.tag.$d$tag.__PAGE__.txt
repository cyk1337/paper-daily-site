a:T457,Discrete Latent Reasoning, or DLR, has two main components. The first is stochastic latent codebook construction. Textual chain-of-thought traces are rendered into images, encoded with a DeepSeek-OCR2 encoder, and then discretized through stochastic clustering or vector quantization to form a latent vocabulary. The goal is to make each latent token a semantically recoverable unit, with reported compression up to about 20x. The second component is augmented latent LM training. The model vocabulary and output head are expanded so that natural-language tokens and latent tokens can be generated in a single sequence. Training has three stages: latent-text alignment to place latent embeddings into the LLM space, latent SFT to teach valid latent trajectories, and latent RL using outcome and process rewards. Compared with continuous latent methods, the key design choice is that intermediate computation is converted into explicit token prediction, which makes supervision and reward assignment much cleaner.
