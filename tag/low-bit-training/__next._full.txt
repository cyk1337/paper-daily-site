18:T9ae,MuonQ has three main components, all built around the idea of preventing quantization noise from inducing systematic directional drift.

The first is pre-quantization normalization. The authors argue that if gradients or momentum are quantized directly at each step, the absolute scale of the quantization error changes with the state magnitude. Over many steps, this can accumulate into a preferred direction. Since Muon preserves direction and discards singular-value magnitudes, such bias is especially damaging. MuonQ normalizes the state before quantization so that each step contributes errors of comparable magnitude. The goal is not simply to reduce one-step MSE, but to make accumulated error more isotropic over time.

The second is structural decomposition. Direct low-bit quantization of the full momentum matrix can perturb singular vectors, and those perturbations survive the orthogonalization step. MuonQ instead decomposes the momentum structurally and quantizes the singular factors separately, so that quantization error is pushed more into singular-value magnitudes than into singular-vector directions. This matches Muon’s update rule: singular-value errors are largely washed out by the polar operation, while directional errors are preserved. The paper also proposes a truncated top-k variant to trade off memory and fidelity. The important point is not decomposition for its own sake, but deliberate error reallocation into the part of the representation that Muon is less sensitive to.

The third is μ-law companding quantization. The authors argue that Muon needs higher resolution near zero because, after polar decomposition, different singular directions are treated more evenly than in Adam-like optimizers. Uniform quantization wastes bins on large magnitudes and under-resolves the dense region near zero, where subtle directional information can still matter. Companding compresses the dynamic range before quantization and allocates more effective resolution to that dense region.

Together, these components form a coherent design: normalization controls the geometry of accumulated error, structural decomposition controls where the error lands, and companding improves resolution where Muon is most sensitive. Compared with prior Muon quantization work, the novelty is that the method is derived from the optimizer’s polar-update mechanism rather than from generic low-bit heuristics or mixed-precision retention of dominant components.19:T796,The main empirical claim is focused and important: on GPT-style and LLaMA-style pretraining setups, pure 4-bit MuonQ tracks full-precision Muon closely in both training loss and downstream accuracy, while reducing optimizer-state memory by up to 7.3x. This matters because the paper positions prior work as follows: 8-bit Muon is generally workable, but naive pure 4-bit quantization degrades badly, and existing methods often rely on mixed precision for dominant singular components. MuonQ is meant to close that gap.

The most informative experiments are likely four types. First, training-curve comparisons among full-precision Muon, naive 4-bit Muon, prior 8-bit or mixed-precision baselines, and MuonQ. The shape of the loss curve matters as much as the endpoint. Second, downstream evaluations after pretraining, to verify that low-bit optimizer states do not silently damage generalization. Third, ablations over the three components: normalization only, decomposition only, companding only, and different top-k truncation settings. If the paper is convincing, these should show complementary gains rather than a single dominant trick. Fourth, mechanism-oriented analyses such as singular-vector perturbation, directional similarity after quantization, or deviation in the polar update. Those are the experiments that directly support the directional-fidelity thesis.

The hard facts visible from the provided text are: experiments cover GPT-style and LLaMA-style models; the target is pure 4-bit Muon state quantization; MuonQ remains close to full-precision Muon in training loss and downstream accuracy; and optimizer-state memory is reduced by up to 7.3x.
