First, it formalizes a clique of Bernoulli questions linked by logical relations, and defines the coherent polytope as the set of marginal probability vectors realizable by some joint distribution consistent with those relations. Each component produces local empirical marginals from K samples, and local Joint-Coherent Decoding projects those marginals onto the local coherent polytope to remove within-component incoherence. Second, the paper proves a product-structure dichotomy. Under owner-selected aggregation, local coherence guarantees global coherence for all inputs if and only if the joint coherent polytope factorizes into a Cartesian product of local polytopes. Once tighter cross-component coupling exists, there are necessarily cases where every component is locally coherent but the composition is globally incoherent. The paper then derives a closed-form magnitude prediction for the residual in Rayleigh-quotient form, linked to specialist-panel covariance. Third, it provides runtime repair and testing. Given a composed quote and cross-component constraints, the system computes ε* by projecting onto the joint coherent polytope. A hierarchical Boyle-Dykstra projection deterministically repairs the quote by moving it back into the feasible set. An anytime-valid e-process gives a sequential test for coherence violations with false-positive control at arbitrary stopping times.
