a:T7f5,The setup is deliberately narrow and controlled. The authors use the camera-relative direction subset of ViewSpatial-Bench and decompose items into pure horizontal, vertical, and depth axes, which avoids mixing different spatial factors. They evaluate both the native 18-way task accuracy and a within-axis binary accuracy. On top of this task decomposition, they run a standard probe-and-steer pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. A five-fold cross-validated logistic probe is trained on PCA-50 residual-stream features at the decision token, layer by layer, to decode each axis. Steering directions are then extracted either from class mean differences or from back-projected probe weights, and injected as a training-free activation edit in the L20-L24 band, scaled by residual-stream standard deviations and concentrated on top-k neurons with a single global coefficient. Up to this point, the setup mirrors the usual latent-knowledge recovery recipe. The key addition is the vision-ablation arbiter. The image is replaced by a gray blank while keeping the prompt, answer options, and decoding unchanged. The signed gap between real-image and blank-image performance is then used to classify each axis into one of three regimes: grounded-correct if real-image performance is higher and blank performance collapses toward chance; grounded-inverted if real-image performance is below chance but blank performance returns toward chance; prior-driven if real and blank conditions are statistically indistinguishable, including under steering. The paper also includes robustness checks against the objection that a gray blank is an unnatural input, using multiple ablations and mismatched but in-distribution real images. In later sections, the authors analyze the geometry of depth inversion and test minimal corrective edits. Some models can be fixed by a training-free rotation in representation space, while others require a trained low-rank edit, suggesting a spectrum from localized geometric misalignment to more distributed errors.b:T80a,The headline result comes from the three-axis analysis on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Behaviorally, horizontal is strong, vertical is near chance, and depth is below chance. Probing tells a very different story: all three axes are decodable from the residual stream, with vertical being the most decodable. The reported within-axis probe accuracies span roughly 73% to 97%, with vertical around 94% and horizontal around 85%. The training-free projection result looks even more convincing under the standard interpretation: vertical task accuracy rises from about 59% to 79%, while horizontal remains largely unchanged, exactly the pattern one would describe as unlocking latent knowledge. The gray-image arbiter overturns that interpretation. Horizontal drops from about 82% with the real image to 49% with the blank, so it is genuinely vision-grounded. Depth rises from 31% with the real image to 45% with the blank, showing that the error requires looking at the image and is therefore grounded but inverted. Vertical stays essentially unchanged, about 59% versus 60%, and the projection gain is almost identical with and without vision, roughly +21 versus +19, indicating that the decoded and amplified signal is a prior rather than visual grounding. The broader sweep covers 14 VLMs from 6 LM families, ranging from 2B to 27B parameters. The recurring pattern is that horizontal tends to be grounded-correct in capable models, vertical is often prior-driven, and depth inversion emerges with scale within families. Another important result is the task boundary: the same models that invert camera-egocentric depth can still be grounded-correct on near-field and metric depth, which argues against a generic lack of depth understanding and points instead to a specific coordinate-mapping failure. In the correction study, depth inversion replicates in 7 of 8 tested models across 5 families.
