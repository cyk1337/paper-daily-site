9:T5f3,The method is D^3: Dynamic Directional Graph-Constrained Data Scheduling. The paper formulates one epoch of training as a discrete dynamical system over parameters. The dataset is partitioned into train-units, either at the sample level or batch level. Because gradient updates do not commute in a non-convex landscape, the final parameter state depends on the order of train-units, so scheduling becomes a combinatorial optimization problem over permutations.

The key idea is a dynamic influence graph. A directed edge from i to j represents whether training on i now is expected to reduce the future loss on j. This is defined through look-ahead loss reduction, with a parameter gamma controlling the temporal scale of the interaction: smaller gamma captures more local effects, while larger gamma approximates longer-range influence. This is the main conceptual move of the paper. Instead of asking whether a sample is intrinsically good or hard, the method asks whether it prepares the model for later data.

The schedule is then derived as the solution to an optimization problem constrained by this graph structure, so the training sequence respects the learned directional dependencies. The paper also mentions scalable approximations for graph construction and solving, aimed at making the method practical in large-scale settings.
