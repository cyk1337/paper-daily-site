5:T4c8,The main contribution is a benchmark construction framework plus validation protocol. The paper first dissects failure modes in prior MIA evaluations. Temporal-cutoff benchmarks such as WikiMIA confound membership with time-dependent changes in writing style and content. Open-model train/validation splits are also problematic when only the training set is deduplicated, because duplicate structure itself becomes a membership cue. Even deduplicating validation against train is not fully sound, since it alters the non-member distribution. Based on this analysis, the authors build cleaner splits for two open LLM families, aiming to keep member and non-member examples matched in source distribution while differing only in whether they were seen before a fixed training point. They then run blind baselines—classifiers with no access to the target model, using only the raw text—to verify that the split is not trivially separable. Only after this sanity check do they benchmark standard MIA methods and report metrics such as AUC and TPR at low FPR. The strongest part of the paper is its treatment of benchmark validity: it makes the i.i.d. assumption explicit and turns it into an operational evaluation
