The proposed world plus event stream decomposition unifies ordinary video, interactive video, speech, behavior, and language into one causal sequence modeling framework. Three implications matter for foundation model research. The main change in v0.3 is not architecture but a reframing of the training objective and sequence representation. Any video is decomposed into a persistent world and a time-varying event stream. The world captures stable context over the timescale of a clip or interaction: environment, visual style, subject identity and appearance, ambient sound, voice characteristics, and other durable conditions. Events are localized changes: subject behavior, camera motion, environmental change, speech, and other sounds. The world is represented as a flexible structured record whose schema can vary by clip or task rather than being globally fixed. Each event has a time interval, a free-form description of the change, and an association to a character or to the environment. The pretraining task becomes: given the world and incoming inputs, predict how the world streams forward unit by unit in real time. For downstream interaction, the world configures scene, character, ambient sound, and voice; streaming user text/audio/video drive the model's event stream. The event stream interleaves spoken words with parenthesized natural-language behavior directives such as “(frowning slightly) What did you say?” This replaces a fixed action vocabulary with open-vocabulary actions grounded in the world, while conditioning audio-video generation through the same path as the language stream, so no new latency-critical component is added. This lets the model condition future behavior on both the user and its own previous actions. The full stack is causal: audio and video VAEs, audio-visual encoders, audio and video decoders, and the temporal Transformer are all designed for streaming. The generation path avoids a hidden text bottleneck; speech and visual latents are generated from the same persistent state. At inference time, the model is scheduled as a thinker-performer pipeline. The thinker encodes current user observations, runs the short token-causal Transformer pass, predicts language, and updates KV-cache. The performer receives the new KV context and runs the flow-matching solver for the next audio-visual latent unit. The previous latent unit is returned to the thinker, decoded, and emitted.
