19:T6cb,The method is deliberately split into two layers. First is the belief model. Let Y∈{0,1} denote whether the junior agent would ultimately be correct if it completed the response on its own. At each generation step, the model emits a competence-evidence signal, such as normalized token entropy or next-token margin. These signals are not treated as calibrated probabilities. Instead, they are mapped, from labeled trajectories, into a competence posterior B_t=P(Y=1|e_1,...,e_t). Under an idealized conditional i.i.d. signal model, the paper derives a simple log-odds recursion and proves that the posterior process is a martingale. Second is the stopping decision. Continuing incurs junior-model token cost but may reveal more evidence; escalating incurs senior-model cost plus handoff overhead. This yields a finite-horizon optimal-stopping problem. The paper derives a closed-form myopic threshold and then characterizes the optimal policy via dynamic programming, proving that the optimal rule is a time-varying threshold in posterior space. A notable technical point is that the threshold result is obtained through monotonicity arguments, without relying on concavity or monotone-likelihood assumptions on the raw signal. The regret analysis is the other key contribution. Excess cost is tied explicitly to posterior miscalibration, and the plug-in policy gets a finite-sample guarantee decaying on the order of 1/sqrt(n) with the number of labeled calibration trajectories. On the implementation side, inference is streaming, thresholds are computed offline by backward induction, and handoff is done at the text level rather than through hidden-state transfer, which keeps the method compatible with practical API-style cascades.1a:T4ff,The empirical section is structured to validate theory rather than maximize benchmark coverage. First, controlled simulations with a known data-generating process are used to test the paper’s predictions: posterior separation behavior, threshold structure, and the dependence of regret on calibration quality. This is where the Chernoff-rate separation result is made visible empirically. Second, the authors run a preregistered real-model protocol on a Qwen2.5-Coder cascade. The goal is not a broad benchmark sweep, but checking whether the theoretical predictions survive contact with an actual LLM system. The paper states that two predictions are confirmed in this real setting, with the most important practical message being that a learned competence posterior is better than thresholding raw uncertainty directly, and that calibration quality dominates the final cost. Third, the paper studies how the amount of labeled calibration data affects policy quality, matching the finite-sample plug-in regret result.
