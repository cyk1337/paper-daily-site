9:T57b,A BDLM splits a length-L sequence into B contiguous blocks of size M=L/B. Blocks are generated left to right, while tokens inside a block are denoised in parallel. During training, each target block b is corrupted at a sampled diffusion time and denoised conditioned on the clean prefix x(<b). The total objective is a sum over per-block losses. The paper exploits this structure. In conventional implementations, training builds a length-2L representation containing a clean copy and a corrupted copy, then applies a block-causal attention mask. Under context parallelism, both clean and corrupted K/V are sharded and communicated. The mismatch is that clean prefixes are shared by many later blocks, while corrupted K/V are local to one block. Block parallelism assigns each complete corrupted-block computation to a rank, keeping that block’s corrupted Q/K/V, activations, and gradients local. This removes block-specific cross-rank communication. The drawback is replication of long overlapping clean prefixes. Context-sharded block parallelism fixes this by sharding the clean sequence across the same ranks: each rank owns clean K/V shards that can serve all local block computations whose prefixes include those shards, and backward contributions are accumulated at the shard owner.
