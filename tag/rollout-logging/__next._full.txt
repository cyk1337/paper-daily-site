1b:T5ec,The paper has three main components. First, an ecosystem audit: the authors inspect 50 popular training and evaluation repositories and check whether they preserve rollouts, expose failed/errored/skipped runs, and specify scoring rules. The central finding is simple and important: none of the audited repositories systematically report failed, errored, or skipped rollouts alongside headline scores. Second, a taxonomy of reporting-rule discrepancies: the paper catalogs 37 cases where differences in task-success definitions, cost/token accounting, or timing measurement can change the reported result even when the underlying evidence is fixed. The key point is not merely that conventions differ, but that these differences can materially alter conclusions. Third, the paper proposes rollout cards, a minimum-sufficient publication format in the lineage of datasheets and model cards. A rollout card includes task specification, agent actions, environment dynamics, terminal status, and the reporting rule behind each score, including which fields the rule reads and which it ignores. The authors also release a reference implementation and 21 card exports. The design choice is deliberate: instead of forcing a single evaluation stack, they require publication of re-analyzable evidence plus the rule used to score it.
