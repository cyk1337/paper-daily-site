1b:T4d4,The authors first introduce Layer-wise Compositional Divergence (LCD), a metric that measures per-layer representation separation between negated and affirmative captions. Combining LCD measurements with per-layer visual alignment scores, they identify a "compositional peak layer" where syntactic information is maximally preserved, and a stable "anchor layer" located before the sharp rise in visual alignment, confirming the representational collapse in final layers. Based on this observation, they propose PeakPatch, a lightweight correction system that keeps CLIP fully frozen and adds two small modules: an Embedding Correction Network (ECN) that uses cross-attention to extract negation signals from the compositional peak layer, anchors these signals to stable representations from the anchor layer, and predicts a deviation vector to inject lost syntax into final-layer embeddings; a Score Correction Network (SCN) that compares peak-layer representations, corrected embeddings, and image embeddings to output bounded scalar corrections for cosine similarity scores.
