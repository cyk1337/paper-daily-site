19:T5ea,Two complementary solutions are proposed, each sidestepping positional uncertainty through a distinct mechanism and producing a different form of any-order inference:
1. Insertion-based masked diffusion (FlexMDM): Adds token insertion capability to standard MDMs, breaking the fixed-canvas constraint. Tokens are no longer committed to a fixed final position during unmasking, as later insertions can shift existing tokens, reducing the positional commitment burden. This approach yields structural any-order inference: generation alternates across different nodes of the parsed program tree, rather than completing one contiguous region at a time. It is implemented by fine-tuning Dream-Coder 7B, a general-purpose code generation MDM, to support insertion operations.
2. Latent-space masked diffusion (LatentMDM): The core modeling contribution. It moves masked diffusion from discrete token space to a continuous latent space, shifting decision granularity from individual token positions to masked latent segments, elevating the prediction interface to a coarser semantic granularity and eliminating token-level positional uncertainty at the root. This approach yields semantic any-order inference: the model searches over semantic generation orders in latent space, completely unconstrained by left-to-right token order. Unlike prior embedding-space diffusion methods (e.g., DUO, 𝕊-FLM), LatentMDM does not predict around individual token positions, but directly operates on semantic-level latent segments.1a:T520,Experiments are conducted in two scales and domains:
1. 7B parameter code generation: FlexMDM is fine-tuned from Dream-Coder 7B, evaluated on four benchmarks: HumanEval, HumanEval+, MBPP, and MBPP+. Results show FlexMDM outperforms the base Dream-Coder 7B on Pass@16 for HumanEval and HumanEval+, and leads on Pass@1 for MBPP and MBPP+, with overall on-par performance. Qualitative analysis confirms FlexMDM exhibits genuine structural any-order generation (jumping across AST nodes), while standard MDM with confidence decoding produces outputs nearly identical to strict left-to-right decoding.
2. 125M parameter math reasoning: LatentMDM is pretrained from scratch on TinyGSM, compared against baselines including standard MDMs, embedding-space methods (DUO, 𝕊-FLM), and an autoregressive model with KV caching. Under matched inference budgets, LatentMDM outperforms all baselines.
Key analysis experiments: First, verification of causal collapse — outputs from confidence-based decoding in standard MDMs have extremely high algorithmic similarity to outputs from strict left-to-right decoding.
