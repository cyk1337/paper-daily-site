9:T531,WSR-Tune performs a weight-space reparameterization. Instead of representing each weight matrix in the canonical basis (one basis element per coordinate), it introduces a new basis {\u03d5_ij} with coefficients \u007e{w}_ij. The basis is constructed to be *safety-conditioned*: directions that strongly control safety behavior are placed into a small set of basis directions (a safety subspace), while the remaining directions span a complementary subspace intended for downstream adaptation.

Fine-tuning then becomes: freeze the coefficients associated with the safety subspace; update only the complementary coefficients. Conceptually, this is a coordinate change designed to make “freeze a small part” actually meaningful for safety, because the frozen part is aligned with safety-relevant directions rather than arbitrary native coordinates.

How it differs from prior work:
- Prior safety-preserving FT typically operates in the original parameterization (freeze layers/neurons, constrain update directions, low-rank adapters, post-hoc weight repair). Those methods implicitly assume safety is localized or identifiable in native coordinates.
- WSR-Tune argues safety is distributed in native coordinates and proposes to *change the coordinates* so that safety becomes compact and thus protectable with simple freezing.a:T40e,Skim plan (30–45 minutes):
