1a:T69d,The setup follows standard OPD. For a prompt x, the student samples an on-policy trajectory y. At each context h_t=(x,y_<t), the teacher provides token-level supervision. The ideal objective is a reverse KL between student and teacher policies. In practice, the paper uses the sampled-token k1 approximation, leading to a policy-gradient style OPD objective: a sampled token is reinforced when the teacher assigns it higher probability than the student, and suppressed otherwise.

The contribution is an experimental decomposition rather than a new distillation algorithm. RQ1 keeps the domain fixed to math and varies training difficulty, evaluation language, and reasoning horizon. RQ2 examines cross-domain transfer between math, code, and science, while distinguishing same-origin teacher-student pairs from cross-origin pairs. Same-origin means the teacher and student come from the same base-model lineage, for example an SFT checkpoint and an RL-trained teacher derived from it. Cross-origin means they come from different bases. RQ3 studies multi-teacher OPD, where prompts are routed to domain-specific teachers, and tests whether routing isolates teacher effects.

The key mechanism-level result is that OPD does not require the teacher to solve the training problem. Even prompts on which the teacher never succeeds can still be useful, because the supervision is applied to the student’s visited states and encodes local teacher preferences over continuations. Same-origin teachers transfer more globally across languages, horizons, and domains. Cross-origin teachers tend to improve the trained distribution more narrowly and may transfer less than a weaker same-origin teacher.1b:T460,The provided content does not include the full numeric tables, but it lists a diverse set of teacher models, including Qwen3-32B, Light-R1-14B, Polaris-7B/4B, OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B/7B, and JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B.

The reported findings are consistent across the controlled settings. First, in-domain math transfer is largely insensitive to training-problem difficulty. Bucketing problems by teacher or student pass rate does not materially change the usefulness of OPD, and unsolved problems remain useful. Third, cross-domain transfer exists in both directions, but the origin relationship dominates: same-origin teachers move the student closer to the teacher across domains, while cross-origin teachers mostly help the trained distribution. Setup: a steering vector v is injected into the residual stream at a chosen layer during inference (activation addition), typically built as a mean activation difference from contrastive prompt pairs.
CAST parameterizes a sanitized vector by projecting v away from a learned safety-degrading subspace S: v* = v - P_S(v). Since the true dimensionality is unknown, the main experiments use a rank-1 approximation with a learned unit direction r̂: v* = v - r̂ r̂^T v.
Learning r̂ is posed as a Lagrangian constrained optimization with primal-dual updates, balancing three requirements:
- Safety restoration: on a harmful prompt distribution D_harm (multiple jailbreak styles), the steered model with v* should refuse at least as often as the unsteered baseline.
- Effect preservation: on D_effect, outputs under v* should remain close to outputs under the original v.
- False-refusal control: on D_benign, v* should not significantly increase over-refusals relative to baseline.
