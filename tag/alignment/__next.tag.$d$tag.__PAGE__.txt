9:T5eb,HRS shifts distribution matching from output calibration to hidden-state editing.
1) Formulation: for each item x, predict a categorical rating distribution p(r|x) that matches the empirical human judgment distribution, covering both ordinal and nominal label spaces.
2) Oracle residual study: assuming access to human ratings, solve for an additive hidden-state correction Δh at a chosen transformer layer such that the downstream frozen layers produce a distribution closer to humans. Empirically, (i) intervening at a single layer captures most of the attainable gain, and (ii) the set of oracle corrections is largely low-rank—most improvement is preserved in a small subspace.
3) Human-Residual Steering: learn an input-conditioned intervention without human labels at test time. A small predictor g takes the layer hidden state and the original rating logits, outputs low-dimensional coefficients a, and a learned basis B maps them to Δh = B a. Apply h' = h + Δh at that layer; keep the backbone frozen while backpropagating through the frozen downstream stack into g and B.
4) Training objective: optimize proper scoring rules on individual annotations (multiple ratings per item), with objectives tailored to ordinal vs nominal judgments to encourage calibrated distribution prediction.
5) Positioning: compared to output-level calibration, HRS can express richer, input-dependent distribution reshaping; compared to distributional fine-tuning, it is far cheaper to deploy but may have a lower ceiling.a:T4e9,On-Policy Self-Training has four main stages. First, from a topic descriptor, the model synthesizes a question and privileged information, typically including an answer or reasoning context. No human-written problem or reference solution is used. Second, the generated samples are filtered. Pseudo-verification checks whether the problem is well formed by asking the model to reproduce the answer in a fresh context and under paraphrasing. A consensus gate checks whether the problem is still useful for the current student: if the student already reproduces the answer consistently across on-policy rollouts, the sample is discarded. This makes the curriculum move as the student improves. Third, training uses on-policy self-distillation. The student sees only the question, while the teacher sees the question plus the synthetic privileged information and provides dense next-token targets at states visited by the student. This asymmetry matters: SFT or RL would optimize directly against the synthetic answer, while OPST uses that answer as context that lets the teacher generate a richer reasoning trajectory from its parametric knowledge. Fourth, the loop is repeated across rounds, with an EMA teacher used to reduce self-induced distribution drift.b:T70f,The method has three main components. First, the authors build a controlled set of moral dilemmas from four classic works in PG19 instead of relying on synthetic dilemmas. Gemma-4 31B extracts a situational premise P and two competing duty assertions D1 and D2. A Z3 SMT solver verifies that P∧D1 is satisfiable, P∧D2 is satisfiable, and P∧D1∧D2 is unsatisfiable. This filters out cases where a trivial compromise would satisfy both duties. Out of 185 raw candidates, 156 pass all three checks. Second, each dilemma is paired with five ethical lenses: Kantian deontology, utilitarianism, virtue ethics, care ethics, and social contract theory, yielding 780 training prompts. The policy is initialized from instruction-tuned Gemma-4 E4B, described as 4.5B effective parameters and 8B total parameters. For each prompt, it generates 8 completions with a separated thinking block and visible answer. Third, rewards come from a self-judged tournament. The same policy judges its own completions pairwise using a prompt based on Wachsmuth’s 15-dimensional argument-quality taxonomy. It reasons over the dimensions but emits four preferences: logic, rhetoric, dialectic, and overall quality. The overall preference is used as the reward. All 28 pairs among 8 completions are judged in both presentation orders, producing 56 judgments per prompt. Presentation flips are treated as ties. Because ties are explicit, the authors use a Rao-Kupper preference model rather than a plain Bradley-Terry model, then convert latent strengths into RLOO advantages. Masked thinking restricts the policy gradient to visible answer tokens. Compared with scalar outcome rewards or label-only moral training, this setup directly optimizes relative argument quality while controlling for trivial compromise and order bias.c:T47b,The experiments address judge reliability, training dynamics, and transfer. First, the self-judge’s argument-quality preferences broadly align with human annotators. Increasing judge scale mainly reduces presentation flips rather than changing the task into a different preference problem. Second, RL induces a prescriptive register. The policy becomes more decisive, uses more forward-looking obligations, and relies less on caveats and retrospective justification. The change is not simply dogmatism; it is a shift toward a confident normative style. Third, transfer to external moral judgment benchmarks is negative or mixed. On MoralChoice, ELEPHANT, and MORABLES, presentation flips are roughly halved, showing that consistency improves. However, sycophancy stays flat, framing acceptance rises, and accuracy falls. Ablations over dilemma source and masked thinking reproduce the same pattern, which suggests that the consistency-validity split is not an artifact of a single data source or masking choice. The authors build an open-ended advice setup with four domains—Study, Career, Startup, and Scientific Research—covering 20 conceptual questions, each with 10 fixed paraphrases. Most comparisons use prompts that include both a superlative and a temporal cue, such as “best” and “right now,” and later isolate wording effects explicitly.

Their metric is intentionally narrow: the share of responses whose recommendation contains explicit AI/ML terms. They first detect candidate mentions with a fixed lexical list, then use two independent LLM filters—Qwen3.6-27B and DeepSeek-V4-Flash—to remove cases where the term appears outside the recommendation itself. The final score averages valid judgments from both filters. This is a sensible choice because it measures how often a user actually receives an AI recommendation, rather than relying on a vaguer semantic notion of “AI-leaning.”

The main design has two layers of controls. First, they compare released Base–Instruct pairs and also run controlled post-training on 11 pretrained checkpoints with 6 different recipes. This separates “instruction tuning happened” from “what data and objectives were used.” The key result is that generic SFT leaves pro-AI prominence roughly unchanged, while topic-specific AI examples and helpfulness-oriented preference data can raise it, and removing AI-related instruction examples can lower it below the starting pretrained model. Second, they test inference-time factors by varying system messages and prompt wording. Prompt wording—especially temporal and superlative framing—has a much larger effect than AI identity cues in the system prompt.

The statistics are also stronger than average for this kind of behavioral study. Effects are computed on matched question-level comparisons, with the 20 questions treated as the statistical units rather than the 10 paraphrases. Confidence intervals come from bootstrap resampling within domains, and significance is tested with an exact sign-flip test over question-level effects, followed by Holm correction within prespecified hypothesis families.e:T71d,Several numbers matter. First, the base models are not neutral. Across ten matched open-weight Base–Instruct pairs, base checkpoints already include explicit AI/ML terms in recommendations at a substantial rate: 26.80% on average. The released instruction-tuned counterparts are much higher, with an average increase of 32.48 percentage points. The abstract-level summary reports roughly 27% for pretrained models and 59% for released instruct models, which is consistent with the body text.

Second, the controlled post-training results are the most informative part. Generic SFT does not materially increase pro-AI prominence. But removing the 7% of instruction examples that concern AI reduces the rate by 14 percentage points, pushing it below the pretrained starting point. In the other direction, data that reward helpfulness increase the rate by 9 points. This is the strongest evidence in the paper because it shows that the shift is not an inevitable consequence of instruction tuning; it depends on what is demonstrated and what is rewarded.

Third, the measurement pipeline appears reasonably reliable. The two LLM filters produce nearly identical effect estimates, with r = 0.990. On 200 detector-positive responses labeled by a human expert, the automatic measure reaches 85.2% agreement, with 96.9% precision and 86.6% recall. So the metric is not perfect, but it is mostly conservative rather than noisy.

Temporal and superlative phrasing significantly increase AI recommendations, while explicitly identifying the assistant as AI adds little or no extra increase beyond the broader context manipulation. If you want to go deeper, check evaluator agreement, domain-wise heterogeneity, and whether different model families show systematically different base priors.10:T418,The paper studies a narrow but important failure mode of reasoning models: a visible chain of thought is only useful for oversight if it remains coupled to the computation that actually drives the model’s output. That coupling can degrade during training. Compression may remove distinctions that matter to a monitor. Optimization against a monitor may teach the model to produce monitor-friendly text without preserving the underlying decision signal. Synthetic-document training may induce out-of-context generalization where the CoT looks plausible but no longer tracks the action. The paper reframes this as a representation transfer problem. CoT is an intermediate representation learned for the source task of producing high-reward outputs; monitoring is a downstream transfer task that tries to infer behavioral properties from that representation. The central question is therefore not how to make CoTs longer or more human-like, but how to train them so they retain output-relevant information that unknown future monitors can still use.11:T60c,The method optimizes conditional mutual information between the chain of thought and the model output, given the prompt: I(CoT; Output | Prompt). The intuition is straightforward. If observing the CoT sharply reduces uncertainty about the output beyond what the prompt already tells us, then the CoT is carrying output-relevant distinctions that a downstream monitor may exploit. The paper does not stop at maximizing average mutual information. It emphasizes a lower-tail objective that focuses optimization pressure on the least informative trajectories. That design choice is sensible because monitorability failures are often tail failures: a small fraction of opaque trajectories can dominate safety risk.

A second ingredient is a conditional chi-squared regularizer. Its role is to limit changes in the output distribution conditioned on a given CoT, making it costly for the model to arbitrarily remap the semantics of CoT strings. Without such a constraint, a model could in principle improve the objective by creating a more tightly coupled but privately coded relationship between CoT and output. The regularizer is therefore a safeguard against semantic drift and selective legibility. Another notable design choice is that the procedure uses neither behavioral labels nor monitor feedback. This distinguishes it from direct monitor-reward approaches, which can overfit to a specific monitor and provide sparse supervision. Here the training signal is about preserving a general statistical coupling, not about satisfying one evaluator.12:T554,The empirical setup uses three controlled degradation mechanisms, described as model organisms: CoT compression, optimization pressure against a monitor, and out-of-context generalization from synthetic documents. In all three settings, the method reportedly recovers most or all of the lost monitorability. That is the strongest part of the evidence, because it shows the objective can repair monitorability after known damaging training pressures rather than merely polishing already decent traces.

There it improves judged monitorability, improves prediction of unsafe behavior by both CoT-based monitors and activation probes across three benchmarks, and increases the prevalence of consequential reasoning. Even without the full result tables in the provided text, the structure of the evaluation is informative. To reduce token-level noise, responses are divided into contiguous blocks. Each block is summarized by the mean positive log-ratio D_i. AP-OPD responds by scaling down all signed supervision coefficients in the block after a detected jump. The preceding block serves as a local reference, and the scale is chosen so that the weighted average penalty of the post-jump block matches the previous block’s original mean.
