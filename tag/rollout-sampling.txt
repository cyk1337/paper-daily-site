1a:T7db,The method has two layers.

First, the paper analyzes why 50% is the right target. For binary rewards, reward entropy is maximized at p=0.5. Under GRPO/RLOO, the magnitude of relative advantage signal is also strongest when successes and failures are balanced. The number of success-failure contrastive pairs peaks near k=N/2 in a group of size N, and group filtering is most likely to retain groups in that middle regime. This gives a principled explanation for why groups like 1/8 or 7/8, while not fully discarded, are still low-value training samples.

Second, the paper introduces Prefix Sampling (PS) to steer skewed groups toward that regime. With N=8 rollouts per group, groups with k in {0,8} are discarded, groups with k in {3,4,5} are used as-is, groups with k in {1,2} are treated as too hard, and groups with k in {6,7} as too easy. For too-hard groups, PS replays a successful prefix as a head start for new continuations. For too-easy groups, it replays a failing prefix as a handicap. The goal is not simply to make trajectories easier or harder, but to move the continuation pass rate toward the high-information middle.

A key implementation detail is that the method works in stateful multi-turn environments by reconstructing the environment state through the replayed prefix, while masking replayed prefix tokens from the loss. Only the fresh continuation generated by the current policy is optimized. This matters because otherwise the method would partly collapse into behavior cloning on old trajectories. The paper also uses an adaptive controller that chooses a prefix ratio r_b in [0.05, 0.95] for each pass-rate bucket, determining how much of a trajectory to replay.

Relative to prior hinting or filtering methods, the differences are clear: no external teacher or oracle solution is required, the control target is group pass-rate distribution rather than single-trajectory success, and the method is designed for long, stateful agent rollouts rather than static problem settings.1b:T50d,The main empirical claim is improved training efficiency at matched final performance.

1. SWE-bench-style agentic RL: on Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B, Prefix Sampling reaches the baseline’s best verified score in up to 1.76x fewer benchmark-evaluation steps. The reported end-to-end speedups are 2.01x for Qwen3-14B and 1.55x for Qwen3-32B, with no loss in final performance.

2. Transfer beyond software engineering: the paper also mentions gains on AIME 2025 reasoning, suggesting the method is not tied to one environment family. Still, the core setting remains binary-reward, group-based RLVR.

3. Mechanistic evidence: the authors claim that the gains come from steering rerollout pass rates toward 50%, not from generic replay or data augmentation. This is an important point. If the plots indeed show pass-rate redistribution as the mediator, the paper’s argument is much stronger.

4. Ablations to check closely: whether both directions matter (head starts for hard groups and handicaps for easy groups), whether adaptive prefix length beats fixed replay length, whether masking replayed tokens is necessary, and how sensitive the method is to bucket boundaries.
