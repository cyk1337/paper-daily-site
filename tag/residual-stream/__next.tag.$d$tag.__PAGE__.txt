These two components are structurally decoupled but synergistic. As N increases, temporal augmentation makes the extra streams more useful, while sparse updates keep them affordable. A practical issue is comparability: if the SAE lives in residual space, how can it be evaluated with the same intervention protocol as a standard SAE? The first major finding is spectral. Trained Jacobians exhibit a monotonic depthwise transition: early layers are strongly non-normal and dominated by complex eigen-structure, consistent with rotational or shear-like dynamics, while later layers are closer to symmetric operators that behave more like selective amplification or contraction. The paper reports that roughly 98% of eigenvalues occur in complex-conjugate pairs, which means rotational structure is not incidental.

The second finding appears when Jacobians are composed across depth. Even if individual layers are not extremely low-rank, the product of Jacobians funnels perturbations into a tiny set of effective directions. Across all three architectures, the number of effective channels is only about 4 to 40. This gives a concrete form to the idea of progressive dimensional bottlenecking in the residual stream.

The third finding links dynamics to topology. The authors build activation-correlation graphs over residual-stream units and run community detection. Units that sit at community boundaries are preferentially amplified or suppressed by the Jacobian, and the sign depends on operator type: near-symmetric layers tend to amplify boundary units, while strongly non-normal layers tend to suppress them.
