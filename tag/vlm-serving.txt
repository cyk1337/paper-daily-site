17:T6e6,The core method is pipelined sharding. Instead of coarse layer-level offloading, the model is partitioned at sub-layer granularity. These shards are then scheduled across GPU and CPU depending on runtime conditions: execution phase, context length of in-flight requests, CPU thread count, PCIe bandwidth, and available VRAM.

The scheduler is benchmark/profile-guided rather than fully online-optimized. It precomputes timing estimates for a set of predefined token tiers, and for each tier selects the best among three candidate execution plans. The plans differ mainly in how aggressively they use PCIe transfers and whether they overlap large tensor movement with compute. Two plans allow movement of large tensors such as weights or KV cache and try to hide transfer cost behind concurrent CPU/GPU work. The third plan minimizes large PCIe traffic and restricts transfers mostly to small activations, which is preferable when bandwidth is tight or transfer overlap is not worthwhile.

A few design choices matter. It is not limited to single-request decode; it explicitly supports mixed batches where requests may be in different phases. VRAM budget is treated as a first-class runtime constraint. The abstraction also extends to MoE models, which suggests the scheduler is not overfit to dense transformers.

For VLMs, the paper combines pipelined sharding with three engineering optimizations, grouped as VLMOpt: offloading vision tensors to CPU, enabling flash attention in the vision encoder, and avoiding overlapping VRAM allocation peaks between the vision and language parts.
