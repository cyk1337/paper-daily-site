19:T5fb,The authors construct a closed-form fact-storing MLP and show that it is equivalent to a Hebbian memory with a sketched quadratic kernel. The central technical move is to analyze decoding margin under softmax decoding. Earlier work typically assumes that the query is the clean key itself, so it only studies noiseless separability. Here, the query is treated as the imperfect output of attention, which means the MLP must create enough logit margin on the correct value to survive query noise and still support factual recall inside a Transformer block.

The theory proceeds in three layers. First, under favorable embedding geometry, the construction achieves fact capacity F=Θ(W/log W), closing a large optimality gap relative to prior constructions; the paper states a log^11 F improvement over earlier bounds in the isotropic setting. Second, the margin and capacity analysis is generalized to arbitrary embedding geometries through four multiplicative geometric statistics that capture clustering and anisotropy in key and value embeddings. Third, the construction is inserted into Transformer blocks, where the authors prove and demonstrate factual recall under noisy queries.

The paper also includes a practical extension, MLP Swapping. If an MLP can be treated as a modular fact memory, then one can edit knowledge by replacing the MLP rather than by distributed fine-tuning. That is not the main theoretical contribution, but it supports the broader claim that MLPs can function as explicit, manipulable memory modules.1a:T5ff,There are four main empirical results.

First, on capacity and parameter efficiency, the proposed construction is far more efficient than the NTK MLP baseline at matched fact count. The reported gap ranges from 10x to 10^4x in parameter count, with the strongest gains coming from the best data-dependent kernel construction. This suggests that the asymptotic improvement is not merely formal.

Second, for arbitrary embedding geometries, the generalized theory predicts empirical decoding margin scaling very well, with R^2≥0.95. This is important because real LLM embeddings are strongly anisotropic; a theory restricted to isotropic embeddings would have limited explanatory power.

Third, inside Transformer blocks, the construction supports factual recall under noisy queries while retaining optimal-capacity scaling. Compared with the NTK baseline, Transformer blocks using the proposed construction require about 15x to 63x fewer parameters at matched fact count. The key point is not just memory in isolation, but memory that remains usable inside the Transformer computation.

Fourth, the MLP Swapping experiment shows near-perfect fact editing while avoiding off-target changes. Prior methods reportedly degrade to around 30% score when editing 10% of the fact set. This gives a concrete downstream consequence of modular fact storage.

The headline numbers to remember are F=Θ(W/log W), R^2≥0.95 for geometry-aware margin prediction, and the 10–10^4x plus 15–63x parameter-efficiency gains over NTK-based baselines.1b:T4f5,For a skim, start with Section 2.1 on decoding margin, then move to Sections 4 and 5. Section 2.1 contains the real conceptual difference from earlier fact-storage theories: the paper is not only about whether an MLP can memorize facts, but whether those facts can still be decoded under the noisy queries produced inside a Transformer. In Section 4, focus on the capacity theorem and the four geometric statistics introduced for arbitrary embedding geometries. The useful question is what each statistic penalizes and how that maps to anisotropic LLM representations. In Section 5, look at the Transformer factual-recall experiments and the MLP Swapping demonstration to judge whether the construction is more than a clean theoretical object.

If time is short, prioritize Figures 2, 3, and 5. Figure 2 shows parameter efficiency, Figure 3 shows factual recall inside Transformers, and Figure 5 shows how well the geometry-aware theory explains margin scaling. A 25 to 35 minute read is enough to capture the main line.
