训练上只更新一个从零开始的 LoRA adapter，底座模型冻结。初始时所有边开放。每一轮先用因果重要性估计给当前存活边排序，然后剪掉一批最弱边；接着在新边掩码下训练 LoRA，使适配后的模型尽量复现原模型输出。这里的目标不是重新学任务标签，而是通过剩余通路逼近原模型 next-token distribution，因此它更像 constrained distillation。随后用 held-out controller 决定是否接受本轮剪枝：只有当目标行为准确率仍达标，且 off-target 语言建模能力没有明显恶化时，才保留这次删除，否则回退到上一状态。这个 accept/reject 机制很重要，因为它把“更小电路”约束在“行为保真 + 通用能力保真”之内，而不是单纯追求 sparsity。

A transformer is represented as a directed graph over residual-stream writers and readers. Writers include token embeddings, each attention head’s residual contribution, and each MLP output; readers include downstream attention/MLP inputs and final logits. Edges represent information flow from an upstream writer to a downstream reader.

A key design choice is how edges are ablated. Instead of zeroing an edge, the method uses paired clean/corrupted prompts and replaces the clean activation flowing through a closed edge with the corresponding activation from the corrupted prompt. This counterfactual replacement avoids pushing activations off-manifold, making edge necessity tests more causally meaningful than naive zero ablation.

Training updates only a freshly initialized LoRA adapter while keeping the base model frozen. Starting from all edges open, each round ranks surviving edges by causal importance, removes the weakest ones, and then trains the LoRA adapter to reproduce the original model’s outputs through the remaining graph. This is closer to constrained distillation than task finetuning: the goal is to match the original next-token distribution under a restricted internal pathway. A held-out controller then accepts the deletion only if both target-task accuracy and off-target language-modeling capability remain within tolerance; otherwise the method rolls back. The smallest accepted graph is denoted C1.

This differs sharply from frozen circuit search methods such as EAP-IG: those estimate importance under fixed weights, whereas Circuit Condensation allows low-rank adaptation to reshape where the behavior lives. The paper explicitly tests the same search loop without weight updates and finds that it loses badly, which isolates the gain to behavioral reshaping rather than better search alone.

Another strong point is the fidelity analysis. The authors do not stop at task accuracy on the adapted model. They also measure KL to the original model’s next-token distribution and test whether the condensed circuit predicts failure modes of the unmodified model better than size-matched frozen circuits. This is important because otherwise one could end up explaining only the modified network.b:T6f6,The evaluation spans 4 behaviors, 8 models from 4 families, and 3 random seeds. The paper then shows why smaller circuits matter operationally. The condensed circuits make exhaustive subset tests, all-pairs ablations, and edge-level necessity checks feasible. The provided material states broad preservation across reasoning, coding, multilingual, long-context, and agentic benchmarks, but does not include all per-benchmark numbers.14:T72b,The core proposal is the Spense format. Instead of forcing the entire weight matrix into standard 2:4 sparsity, the matrix is split into two contiguous regions: one remains dense, the other is pruned with the standard 2:4 pattern. The practical advantage is immediate: the dense region can be executed with existing dense GEMM libraries such as cuBLAS, and the sparse region with existing 2:4 sparse GEMM libraries such as cuSPARSELt. This avoids custom compiler work and avoids activation expansion or expensive runtime transformations used by prior relaxed-sparsity methods.

The reason this contiguous split is possible is the permutation invariance of the intermediate dimension in GLU-style MLPs. If the same permutation is applied consistently across the gate, up, and down projections, the MLP function is unchanged. That lets the method choose a subset of intermediate channels to keep dense, reorder them into a contiguous block, and prune the rest into the 2:4 region.

On top of this format, the paper proposes two one-shot pruning methods. SpenseGPT is the simple baseline: keep the first p% of intermediate indices dense and apply SparseGPT to prune the remaining region into 2:4 sparsity. SpenseGPT+ improves this by selecting dense indices using an activation-aware importance score inspired by Wanda, combining each intermediate index’s contribution across the gate, up, and down projections. SparseGPT is then used on the remaining weights to minimize layer output reconstruction error.

A central empirical point is that dense-index selection matters enormously. The paper reports that with the same dense budget, AIME can vary from 76.67 down to 8.89 depending on which indices are kept dense. So the method is not just about relaxing sparsity; it is about identifying the right subspace to preserve in dense form.15:T4a6,The evaluation uses Qwen3-32B and Seed-OSS-36B, with benchmarks including AIME, GPQA, LiveCodeBench, and IFEval. The main claim is that SpenseGPT+ preserves quality across these tasks while achieving up to about 1.2× end-to-end decoding speedup over the dense baseline on B200 GPUs with FP8 precision in vLLM.

One of the most useful analyses is the sparse-GEMM speed study. The paper shows that sparse kernels only approach their theoretical benefit when the GEMM M dimension is large enough to amortize overhead. When M is small, sparse GEMM can even be slower than dense GEMM. This is an important reality check for autoregressive decoding, where many operations run in regimes that are not ideal for sparse kernels.
