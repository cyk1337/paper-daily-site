18:T854,方法上，LM2LM 把自动化 LM 开发形式化为一个 sequential decision problem。每个任务对应一个目标模型家族、参数规模、训练预算和 reference config；proposer 负责生成自然语言 proposal，builder 把 proposal 转成可执行修改，执行后得到相对 reference 的验证损失改变量。训练目标不是直接预测实验结果，而是用 RL 提升 proposer 生成高收益 proposal 的能力。

为了解决真实奖励昂贵的问题，作者引入 Research World Model, RWM。它不是单独训练的回归器，而是一个冻结的大语言模型，输入包括任务描述、reference 配置、历史实验记录以及候选 proposal，输出该 proposal 相对 baseline 的预期 gain。也就是说，RWM 通过 in-context experimental records 来做 outcome prediction，再把预测 gain 变成 RL reward。这样避免了为每个 proposal 都跑一次完整训练。

但作者的重点不只是“用 LLM 当 reward model”，而是处理分布漂移。随着 proposer 经 RL 更新，它会越来越倾向于生成 RWM 评分高、但历史记录覆盖不足的 proposal。固定记录下的 RWM 会在这些区域系统性高估，RL 随后放大这种偏差。为此，LM2LM 引入 on-policy calibration：周期性地从当前策略生成的 proposal 中，挑选 RWM 排名靠前的一部分做真实执行，把测得的真实 gain 追加进 RWM 的上下文知识，再继续训练。这个设计有两个关键点。其一，不是随机补数据，而是专门校准当前策略最可能 exploit 的高分区域；其二，不重新训练 reward model，而是通过更新 in-context records 让冻结 LLM 的判断重新锚定到真实结果。

从方法取舍看，这比纯离线 preference learning 更适合高成本实验环境，因为它把真实执行预算集中花在“最危险的误差区域”；也比全量在线 RL 便宜得多，因为绝大多数 proposal 仍由 RWM 评估。论文还分析了 RWM 的 ranking quality，说明在这类任务里，排序能力比绝对数值回归更关键，RL 只需要相对好坏足够稳定即可。19:T84a,LM2LM formulates automated LM development as a sequential decision problem. Each environment specifies a target model family, size, training budget, and a reference configuration. A proposer LLM generates natural-language modification proposals; a builder converts them into executable changes; execution returns the gain relative to the reference validation loss. The proposer is trained with RL to generate proposals with higher expected gains.

The key scalability device is a Research World Model (RWM). Instead of executing every proposal, LM2LM uses a frozen LLM to predict the gain of a candidate proposal conditioned on the task description, the reference setup, and a set of prior experimental records provided in context. These predicted gains become RL rewards. This is not just a generic reward model. The design relies on the frozen LLM’s ability to reason over structured experimental history and produce useful relative rankings among proposals.

The more important contribution is how the paper handles reward drift. As the proposer improves, it shifts toward proposals that score highly under the RWM but may lie outside the support of the initial experimental records. A fixed surrogate then becomes vulnerable to systematic overestimation, and RL amplifies that error. LM2LM addresses this with on-policy calibration: at intervals during training, it takes a subset of high-scoring proposals from the current policy, executes them for real, and appends the measured outcomes to the RWM context. This keeps the surrogate grounded on exactly the region of proposal space that matters for the current policy.

That choice is deliberate. Randomly collecting more experiments would be wasteful. Re-training a separate reward model after each batch would also be heavier. By updating the in-context evidence of a frozen LLM, the method concentrates real-execution budget on the most decision-critical and most exploitable proposals. The paper further argues that ranking quality matters more than perfectly calibrated scalar prediction: for RL, preserving the relative ordering of proposals is often enough.1a:T56e,The experiments evaluate proposal quality in held-out LM-development environments using real execution. The paper gives a useful cost calculation: training a 486M-parameter model on 8B tokens takes about 45 B200 GPU-hours. A GRPO-style setup with 100 updates, 32 states per update, and 8 proposals per state would require 25,600 proposal evaluations. If all were executed directly, that would cost about 1,152,000 GPU-hours, or more than $16M. This establishes why surrogate-based training is necessary.

Empirically, LM2LM outperforms baselines that rely only on past experimental records, and it also beats strong in-context approaches on real executed gains. The paper additionally claims that a Qwen3.8-27B proposer trained with LM2LM surpasses GPT-6-sol on held-out environments. The most informative results are the analyses of surrogate reliability. Early in training, the RWM provides useful proposal rankings. Without on-policy calibration, ranking quality degrades as RL progresses, eventually becoming worse than random because the policy learns to exploit overestimated regions. With calibration, overestimation is reduced and ranking quality is largely preserved, which in turn preserves real-world gains. The critical ablations are the no-RL baseline, RWM reward without calibration, calibration frequency and budget, and the strategy for selecting which proposals to execute.1b:T457,The direct value for pretraining research is small. The more relevant signal is at the pretrain-posttrain interface, especially for coding agents and execution-grounded RL. Three points matter. A step samples B prompts and n responses per prompt. Rewards are binary and converted to within-prompt advantages. The gold reward checks the final answer against a reference answer. Near the sampling policy, the GRPO update is a sum of prompt gradients. If the majority answer for a prompt is correct, the MV and gold prompt gradients are identical. The entire difference between the two batch updates comes from prompts where the majority answer is wrong. The paper decomposes the MV update relative to the gold update into shrinkage and misdirection. Shrinkage is the component along the gold direction and mainly changes the effective step size. Misdirection is the orthogonal component and is the part that changes the optimization direction.

The key claim is that misdirection is controlled by cross-prompt coherence of label errors. Independent wrong labels can be frequent and still average out at realistic batch sizes. Coherent wrong labels, such as a reward that systematically prefers confident outputs, frequent outputs, or outputs favored by a learned reward model, leave a directional bias that batch averaging cannot remove. The first key mechanism is paired current-policy counterfactual rollouts. For selected prompts, the current policy is forced to answer once in TH and once in NT. Compared with sequence-level policy gradients over the whole output, this better matches the hierarchical structure of the decision process.

先看 IterSynth。它把一次 deep search 拆成 Planner 和 Synthesizer 两个交替角色，但这两个角色不是两套模型，而是同一个 LLM 在不同 role prompt/格式下的两种行为模式。Planner 只看压缩后的 summary state，负责判断还缺什么信息、下一步该搜什么、何时继续探索。Synthesizer 读取新检索到的证据，对 summary 做增量更新：吸收有用事实、处理冲突、丢弃噪声、维护当前结论和未解决问题。这样，summary 不再是外部 memory module 的被动压缩结果，而是 agent policy 的显式输出和持久状态。

这个设计同时解决两个问题。对 role coupling，它通过角色分工降低单步决策的能力混杂度。Planner 不需要在长证据块里写最终答案，Synthesizer 也不需要决定全局搜索策略。对 context accumulation，它不再把完整历史一直塞回上下文，而是让 summary 成为受控、紧凑、可更新的状态表示。相比多 agent 系统，它不需要多模型协作和额外部署复杂度；相比已有 summary-based agent，它把摘要更新纳入训练目标，而不是把 summarization 当外接工具。

RDPO 的关键不是简单加一个中间奖励，而是按角色做 credit assignment。它把终局 outcome reward 和 turn-level rubric evaluation 结合起来，再分别为 Planner 和 Synthesizer 计算 group-relative advantages。这样做的动机很直接：Planner 的好坏更多体现在 query 是否有信息增益、是否识别到知识缺口、是否避免冗余搜索；Synthesizer 的好坏则体现在证据整合、冲突处理和 summary 更新质量。如果沿用普通 GRPO 或 outcome-only RL，两个角色共享一条回报，梯度会把不同错误来源混在一起，尤其在长轨迹里更严重。RDPO 通过 role-specific advantage 让更新方向更贴近各自职责。

The Planner reads only the compact summary state and decides what information is still missing, what to search next, and whether exploration should continue. The Synthesizer reads newly retrieved evidence and updates the persistent summary by integrating useful facts, resolving conflicts, filtering noise, and maintaining the current state of knowledge. In this design, the summary is not an external memory artifact. It is the explicit, learned state of the search process.

This addresses both target problems. Role coupling is reduced because planning and evidence integration are no longer forced into the same local decision. Context accumulation is reduced because the agent no longer carries the full search history forward; it carries a controlled, compact, updateable state instead. Compared with multi-agent systems, this avoids extra models and coordination overhead. Compared with prior summary-based agents, it makes summary updating part of the policy itself rather than an auxiliary external module.

Training follows an SFT-to-RL recipe. SFT teaches the Planner–Synthesizer protocol, role formatting, and basic iterative search behavior. But the authors explicitly note that imitation mainly teaches the interaction pattern, not the deeper decisions of when to search, what evidence to preserve, or how the two roles should coordinate. That is why they introduce RDPO.

RDPO combines terminal outcome rewards with turn-level rubric evaluations and computes group-relative advantages separately for Planner and Synthesizer actions. The motivation is straightforward: the Planner should be rewarded for identifying information gaps, issuing informative queries, and avoiding redundant exploration, while the Synthesizer should be rewarded for evidence integration, conflict handling, and summary quality. Standard outcome-only GRPO mixes these error sources together, which is especially harmful in long trajectories. Role-specific advantages make the gradient signal better aligned with each role’s responsibility.

Conceptually, the difference from ReAct is not just the addition of summaries. IterSynth changes the search loop from a linear action history into an iterative process centered on an explicit summary state. That is the main modeling contribution.2c:T60c,The headline result is that IterSynth-8B reaches 50.7% average performance across five long-horizon deep-search benchmarks: GAIA-text-only, xBench-2505, xBench-2510, BrowseComp, and BrowseComp-ZH. This is 4.2 points above the strongest prior agent at 8B or below, while remaining competitive with several 30B-scale agents at less than one third of the parameter budget.

The training ablations are particularly informative. The SFT baseline scores 44.1%. Outcome-only GRPO raises this to 48.9%. RDPO further improves it to 50.7%. So RL itself contributes a large gain, and role-decoupled credit assignment adds another 1.8 points on top. The paper notes that this extra gain is strongest on exploration-heavy benchmarks, which suggests that RDPO improves search behavior rather than only answer polishing.

A key diagnostic appears in Appendix H: even with a 64K context window, more than 59% of ReAct trajectories on BrowseComp fail to terminate before context exhaustion. This directly supports the paper’s claim that the bottleneck is not simply context length, but the way search state is represented and updated.

IterSynth is not only a trainable 8B-agent recipe; it also works as a prompting paradigm for frontier models such as Claude-4.5-Opus and DeepSeek-V3.1, consistently outperforming ReAct and IterResearch, with gains of up to 10 points over ReAct on BrowseComp-ZH. The main challenge is not the existence of labels, but how to make those labels valid. The paper identifies three constraints. First, supervision must be applied under rollout-consistent draft-conditioned states. The k-th drafted token is predicted conditioned on previous drafted tokens, not on the final executed prefix after verification. To avoid train-test mismatch, GrowMTP records the draft trajectory and reconstructs the hidden states corresponding to the actual draft-conditioned context that produced each proposal. Second, learning across drafting depths is coupled. A deeper token only matters if earlier drafted tokens are accepted, so depth-wise training should reflect that dependency rather than treating each position as an independent classification target. The paper introduces a depth-coupled acceptance (DCA) loss based on an acceptance-chain surrogate, with a logarithmic transform to increase relative gradient weight on low-chain-value cases. Third, supervision beyond the first rejection is invalid for execution, because deeper drafted tokens are conditioned on a rejected token and therefore lie off the executed prefix. GrowMTP gates the loss after the first rejection and excludes those suffix positions. A further design choice is to detach draft-head updates from the policy backbone, so the online drafting module does not directly perturb policy learning. This sacrifices possible gains from joint optimization, but keeps the method modular and stable.
