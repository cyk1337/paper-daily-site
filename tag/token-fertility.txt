17:T639,The main method is a parallel-corpus audit of tokenizers. The authors argue that non-parallel corpora confound language effects with topic, register, and sentence-length differences. By holding meaning constant across translations, differences in token counts can be attributed more directly to the interaction between language, script, morphology, and tokenizer vocabulary coverage. They evaluate 20 African languages against 11 current frontier and open tokenizers.

The metric design is careful. After Unicode NFC normalization, they compute word count, token count, character count, and UTF-8 byte count under a fixed convention shared across all languages. Token counts exclude BOS/EOS and other special tokens to avoid sequence-overhead artifacts. Word segmentation follows a uniform Unicode UAX-29 rule rather than language-specific heuristics, prioritizing comparability over linguistic tailoring. The paper then derives five metrics: fertility (tokens per word), premium relative to English, characters per token, bytes per token, and context efficiency under a fixed window size. Corpus-level aggregation uses sum-then-divide rather than mean-of-ratios, which is the right choice for stability.

A practical contribution is the translation of fertility into deployment-facing quantities: USD and local-currency costs, latency multipliers, and effective context-window erosion. This makes the tokenizer issue legible to system builders, not just NLP researchers. The inclusion of local-currency volatility in the cost model is also a realistic touch for the target deployment setting.18:T4d5,From the provided full-text content, the study evaluates 20 African languages and 11 tokenizers, with corpus-level aggregation and bootstrap confidence intervals. The paper also instantiates the cost model in three deployment scenarios: bank customer service, clinical triage, and agricultural SMS assistance.

What is clear is the intended empirical claim: African languages incur a measurable and often substantial tokenization penalty relative to English, and that penalty compounds into higher cost, slower generation, and reduced effective context capacity.
