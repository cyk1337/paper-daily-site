18:T5dc,ViTok-v2 is a continuous latent ViT autoencoder. An encoder maps an image x into a latent z with spatial downsampling factor f and channel dimension c. The compression ratio is defined as r=3f^2/c. Lower r preserves more information and improves reconstruction, but it produces a larger latent space that is harder for a downstream flow or diffusion model to learn. The paper makes several design changes around this axis. It introduces native-resolution training with a NaFlex-style setup and 2D RoPE, replacing fixed absolute positional embeddings so that a model trained at 256p can operate at 512p, 1024p, and varying aspect ratios. It scales the decoder aggressively: the largest model has 5B parameters, with a 4.5B decoder and a 463M encoder, more than 14 times larger than the 350M ViTok scale. The emphasis on decoder scale is important because, at a fixed bottleneck, reconstruction depends heavily on how well the decoder can invert the compressed representation. The paper also introduces a DINOv3 perceptual loss. A frozen DINOv3 model provides feature-level reconstruction supervision, replacing both LPIPS and GAN losses. This removes discriminator instability while keeping a perceptual signal beyond pixel L1. Finally, the paper compares KL, tanh, and LayerNorm latent regularization and finds little effect on generation quality at scale, making simpler deterministic encoders viable. The second is native-resolution evaluation: models trained at 256p generalize to 512p, 1024p, and different aspect ratios, whereas earlier ViT tokenizers with fixed positional embeddings degrade outside the training setup. The third is the loss study: DINOv3 perceptual supervision replaces LPIPS and adversarial training, enabling stable large-scale training while preserving high-quality reconstructions. The fourth is joint AE-generator scaling: 350M and 5B autoencoders are paired with 450M and 1.2B flow models.
