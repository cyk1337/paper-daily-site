9:T5b8,The method has three layers. First, formalization: the benchmark score is written as a composition of stage-specific functions rather than a static dataset-plus-metric object. This makes it possible to localize failure to a specific stage. Second, auditing: under a common framework, the authors inspect eight benchmark implementations and identify 15 recurring failure modes, including prompt formatting, stop sequences, token budgets, handling of reasoning spans, output parsing, label aliases, binary versus partial-credit scoring, invalid-response treatment, and aggregation. Importantly, they do not stop at anecdotal examples. They run controlled perturbations, changing one pipeline field at a time while holding the rest fixed, and measure the effect on scores and rankings. Third, standardization: they build a harness that exposes and standardizes nine configuration fields wherever task semantics allow, so that differences caused by benchmark-specific evaluator choices can be separated from differences caused by the task itself. Two analyses are especially useful. PCA on the 23-task by 10-model score matrix shows that the first principal component explains 95.25% of the variance, suggesting that many tasks mostly measure the same broad capability axis.
