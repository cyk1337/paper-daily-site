18:Ta2b,技术路线可以概括成一句话：用 streaming k-PCA 持续追踪梯度主子空间，并把上一时刻的子空间和优化器状态平滑迁移到下一时刻，而不是周期性从零开始重估。

先看问题设定。对某个权重矩阵层，梯度 g_t 是高维的。GaLore 类方法会维护一个低秩基 U_t，把梯度投影到这个 rank-k 子空间里，在低维空间中做 Adam 类更新，再映射回原空间。难点在于 U_t 需要跟着训练变化。GaLore 的做法是隔若干步做一次 SVD 重算，这样每次更新 projector 都像一次 cold restart：旧子空间信息被丢掉，Adam 一阶二阶矩也很难自然继承。OSD 则通过额外优化过程去适配移动子空间，但实现更重，也更难稳定。

Ori 的核心判断是：如果 U_t 与 U_{t-Δ} 在小 Δ 下通常很接近，那么最合理的做法是 warm-start tracking。具体实现上，作者采用受 Oja++ 启发的 streaming k-PCA 更新。每一步都用当前梯度对已有基做轻量旋转，使基逐步贴近新的主方向。其单步代价是 O(dk^2)，相比频繁做全量 SVD 要轻得多，因而可以支持高频甚至 gap-free 的 projector 更新。

但仅仅更新基还不够。低秩优化器真正容易出问题的地方，是基变了以后 optimizer state 怎么办。Ori 在这里加入 drift-aware bootstrapping：把上一时刻子空间基作为当前初始化，并对 Adam 风格的一阶矩、二阶矩做跨基转移。这样做的直觉是，如果子空间只是小角度旋转，那么旧统计量并没有失效，直接丢弃会造成不必要的优化震荡。文中的 drift coefficient ρ 用来控制 carry-over 强度，实验显示 ρ≈0.99 较好，说明“保留连续性但不过度僵化”是有效区间。

从算法结构看，Ori 不是把低秩空间当成静态压缩容器，而是把它当成一个随训练动态演化的状态变量。这个视角与 GaLore 的周期重算有本质区别：GaLore 更像 snapshot estimation，Ori 更像 online tracking。与 OSD 相比，Ori 不依赖额外的非凸追赶过程，而是直接利用 streaming PCA 的结构更新，因此计算和实现都更干净。

理论部分给出了带 drift 的 dynamic regret 分析。作者证明在 noiseless setting 下可得到 sublinear dynamic regret，且对相邻时刻子空间漂移量的依赖较温和。这个理论不是全文最重要的部分，但它至少说明 Ori 的设计不是纯经验拼装：当子空间连续漂移时，在线跟踪而非反复冷启动，确实能得到更合理的误差累积性质。19:T4d6,This is directly relevant to pretraining. First, it reframes low-rank optimization as a training-dynamics problem rather than a pure compression trick: the bottleneck is not only low rank, but stale projectors. Second, it gives a practical answer for memory-constrained pretraining: if the gradient subspace drifts slowly, then frequent, lightweight, continuous updates are better matched to the dynamics than infrequent, heavy recomputation. Third, the gains appear in the very low-rank regime, which is where these methods are actually hard. At generous ranks many approaches work reasonably well; the real test is when each projector dimension is expensive. On 1B LLaMA pretraining, Ori reduces the gap to full-rank Adam by up to 69.3% under a rank-128 optimizer state versus embedding dimension 2048, while remaining compute-efficient. That makes the contribution operational, not merely conceptual.

The contribution spans three layers: an empirical characterization of gradient-subspace drift, a new optimizer design, and drift-aware theoretical guarantees. It does not replace mainstream full-rank training, but it is highly relevant for memory-limited pretraining, continual training, and the broader study of low-rank optimizers.1a:Tb1b,The method can be summarized as follows: continuously track the principal gradient subspace with streaming k-PCA, and smoothly transfer both the basis and optimizer statistics across time instead of repeatedly re-estimating the subspace from scratch.

Consider a weight matrix whose gradient at step t is high-dimensional. Gradient-subspace methods maintain a rank-k basis U_t, project the gradient into that subspace, perform an Adam-like update in low dimension, and map the update back to the full space. The hard part is keeping U_t aligned with the current gradient geometry. GaLore handles this by periodic SVD refreshes, which behave like cold restarts: previous subspace information is discarded, and optimizer moments do not naturally carry over. OSD moves toward continuous adaptation but does so through an additional non-convex optimization loop.

Ori starts from a different empirical premise: if U_t is close to U_{t-Δ} for small Δ, then warm-started tracking should be more efficient than repeated re-estimation. The paper instantiates this with a streaming k-PCA update inspired by Oja++. At each step, the current gradient induces a lightweight rotation of the existing basis, nudging it toward the new principal directions. The per-step cost is O(dk^2), much cheaper than frequent full SVD, which makes high-frequency or effectively gap-free projector updates feasible.

A second key piece is optimizer-state continuity. Updating the basis alone is not enough, because low-rank optimizers also maintain first and second moments in the projected space. Ori introduces drift-aware bootstrapping: the previous basis initializes the current one, and Adam-style moments are transferred across successive bases. The intuition is simple: if the subspace only rotates slightly, then old moments are still informative, and resetting them would inject unnecessary optimization noise. The drift coefficient ρ controls how much carry-over is preserved; empirically, a small but strong carry-over around ρ≈0.99 works well.

Conceptually, Ori treats the low-rank subspace as a dynamic state variable that evolves with training, not as a static compression container. That is the main difference from GaLore’s snapshot-style recomputation. Relative to OSD, Ori avoids an extra non-convex catch-up loop and instead uses a structured streaming PCA update, which is cleaner computationally and algorithmically.

The theory section provides dynamic-regret guarantees under drift. In the noiseless setting, the paper derives sublinear dynamic regret with mild dependence on the consecutive drift parameter. This is not the main reason to read the paper, but it does show that the design is not purely heuristic: when the target subspace moves continuously, online tracking has a better cumulative-error story than repeated cold restarts.1b:T795,The experiments are organized around two settings: LLaMA-1B pretraining and RoBERTa-Base finetuning.

The pretraining result is the main one. On a 1B LLaMA pretraining task, Ori is tested in a very low-rank regime where the optimizer-state rank is 128 while the model embedding dimension is 2048. This is a stringent setting and a good stress test for projector staleness. The headline result is that Ori reduces the performance gap to full-rank Adam by up to 69.3% relative to prior low-rank baselines. The paper also presents a performance-memory frontier where Ori sits on the favorable lower-left boundary, indicating better accuracy under low memory and low compute.

For finetuning, the paper evaluates RoBERTa-Base across 16 scenarios with even smaller ranks, 8 or 4. Ori outperforms competing methods in 10 of the 16 cases. The gains are less uniformly dominant than in pretraining, which is unsurprising given higher task variability and noisier optimization in finetuning.

The ablations are especially informative. Increasing the projector update period consistently hurts performance, directly quantifying the cost of staleness. This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence in the paper because it isolates the mechanism behind the gains. A second ablation shows that adding a small carry-over, with drift coefficient around ρ≈0.99, improves accuracy, confirming that moment transfer is not a cosmetic detail but a core part of the optimizer. The paper also measures gradient-subspace drift empirically and shows that after warm-up, neighboring subspaces remain close over short windows, which supports the streaming-tracking premise.

From a pretraining-engineering perspective, the most useful takeaways are not just the benchmark wins but three empirical rules: gradient subspaces drift slowly, stale projectors are especially costly at low rank, and preserving optimizer-state continuity is better than repeated resets.1c:T698,This one is worth a careful read.
