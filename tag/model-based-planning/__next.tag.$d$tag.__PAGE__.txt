9:T42c,The method reframes WM learning from “reconstruct the next observation text” to “preserve the action distribution induced by the state.” Since the full action distribution is inaccessible, it builds a step-level proxy:
1) Freeze a Reference Agent.
2) Define Behavior Consistency Reward (BehR): for the logged next action, compare its likelihood under the reference agent when conditioned on the real state vs the WM-predicted state; reward states that keep the logged action equally (or more) likely, focusing learning on decision-critical content.
3) Optimize the WM with an RL algorithm (GRPO is used) using BehR as dense step reward, producing BehR-WMs.
4) Evaluate with Pairwise Consistency Ratio (CR_pw): among tasks that succeed when executed directly in the real environment, measure the fraction that remain successful when the action sequence generated in the WM is replayed in the real environment—capturing long-horizon functional fidelity.
The paper also discusses reduced offline-eval calibration gap and preliminary lookahead-planning benefits.a:T497,The provided full-text excerpt includes the key experimental constructs but not the full results tables/scale details. What is clearly described:
- Decision-critical perturbation test (DI vs DT): standard text metrics and content-only LLM judges rate the decision-breaking “Drop Target” perturbation as less severe (metric inversion), while BehR correctly ranks DI above DT and remains usable as a dense training signal. A broader 7-perturbation study and a 32B reference-agent replication are said to be in Appendix F/F.2.
- Objective comparison: initial evidence that replacing BehR with surface/structured rewards does not recover the same task-level gains.
- Long-horizon fidelity: in the primary setting, BehR training substantially improves CR_pw while keeping single-step prediction quality stable or better; transfer is more heterogeneous in near-ceiling regimes.
- Downstream: BehR-WMs reduce offline-evaluation calibration gap and show preliminary promise for lookahead planning.
