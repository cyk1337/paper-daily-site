b:T828,The method has three main components. First, the taxonomy is extended beyond the 12 dimensions of ESSENTIAL-WEB with two new ordinal dimensions: timeliness and cultural specificity. Timeliness measures how temporally durable a document’s value is, from highly time-sensitive content to fully evergreen material. Cultural specificity measures how tightly content is tied to a particular cultural or geographic context. The paper validates that these are genuinely new signals: pairwise NMI with existing dimensions is low, with timeliness reaching at most 0.21 and cultural specificity at most 0.11. Second, the authors build a scalable annotation stack. They use Qwen2.5-32B to annotate 14 million web documents, distill those annotations into a 0.5B generative annotator, and then train a 73M multi-task MLP on top of E5 embeddings for high-throughput corpus-wide prediction. The MLP runs at roughly 50 times the throughput of the generative annotator while maintaining strong exact-match agreement. This is an important systems choice: the goal is not only label quality, but making semantic annotation cheap enough to run over production-scale corpora. Third, they introduce a two-pass search framework for compound filters. Because each taxonomy dimension is ordinal and can be thresholded in multiple ways, exhaustive search over conjunctions and disjunctions is combinatorially expensive. Pass 1 evaluates dimensions independently at small scale to identify the strongest single signals. Pass 2 constructs compound filters from the top-performing dimensions and evaluates them at the same scale. Only the most promising candidates are then taken to full scaling-law style experiments. A central empirical finding is that timeliness appears in nearly all strong compound filters, suggesting that temporal durability is more predictive of transferable pretraining value than many standard quality heuristics. The paper also studies the trade-off between strict and broad filters: strict filters increase reasoning and coding density, while broader filters preserve factual coverage.c:T5d6,The headline result is that taxonomy-guided filtering can make mid- and even low-tier web data outperform unfiltered top-tier data. It also surpasses unfiltered top-tier data by 6.7% on reasoning and 13.7% on coding. The effect persists further down the quality ladder: for data two tiers below the usual production threshold—data that many pipelines would likely discard entirely—filtered subsets improve over their unfiltered baseline by 22.3% on reasoning and 19.5% on coding, and they exceed unfiltered top-tier data on coding benchmarks. Another important result is methodological: the ranking induced by the low-cost two-pass screening is consistent with the ranking from full scaling-law experiments, which validates the screening procedure as a reliable proxy rather than a toy heuristic. In ablations, timeliness is the strongest single dimension and the most stable ingredient in high-performing compound filters. Cultural specificity contributes complementary signal rather than dominating performance. The paper also shows a clear capability trade-off: stricter filters help numerical reasoning and coding more, while broader filters retain more factual knowledge breadth. The paper formulates this as a sequential decision problem and derives a stage-wise token-savings decomposition, emphasizing that earlier rejection yields larger savings. This is importantly different from prior early-exit methods, which usually try to shorten correct reasoning traces at inference time.
