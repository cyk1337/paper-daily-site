17:T49a,The core idea is a two-level rubric system. The upper level is a structured meta-rubric that captures how a complete answer should be organized. It includes typed components such as simple knowledge, strict lists, flexible lists, and processes, along with importance tiers like answer-critical, valuable, and context, all grounded in cited evidence. The lower level is a mechanically compiled flat checklist of binary items used for grading.

This split is the main contribution. Rich structure is needed to express completeness faithfully, but judges are more reliable on flat pass/fail checks. GAMUT keeps the expressive representation for rubric authoring and verification, while using the compiled binary form for low-variance automatic scoring.

The benchmark targets what the paper calls everyday deep research: realistic, image-grounded, knowledge-seeking questions that require multi-step web research and synthesis. Construction uses frontier search-capable LLMs together with expert human annotators over multiple rounds, with human-verified web evidence. A text-only variant is also released to separate visual identification from completeness of knowledge synthesis.18:T47f,The method, DR-Rubric, has two stages. Stage I is Information Elicitation. Given a query, a tool set, and an interaction budget, an agentic research model performs iterative search, retrieval, verification, and synthesis to build an evidence trace. The goal is not to answer the query directly, but to discover what a good answer should contain: domain facts, structural constraints, likely omissions, and typical failure patterns. Stage II is Rubric Synthesis. The collected evidence is converted into a formal rubric made of atomic constraints that can be checked independently and used for partial credit.

These rubrics are then used as RL rewards. Compared with a single holistic judge score, rubric rewards are denser and more interpretable because they decompose quality into multiple local criteria. The paper also studies bootstrap rubric generation, where the model eventually generates its own rubrics instead of relying on a stronger external model. The interesting training dynamic is that the first bootstrap step tends to specialize the model toward reasoning, while later bootstrap rounds partially restore broader agentic performance.19:T4de,C2 (“Cooperative yet Critical”) jointly trains:
- A rubric generator that is optimized to produce rubrics that *help* a downstream verifier, rather than merely plausible rubrics.
- A rubric-augmented verifier that learns both to make preference judgments and to decide whether a rubric should be trusted.

The key mechanism is to synthesize contrastive rubric pairs based on the *directional shift* a rubric induces in the verifier’s decision: some rubrics move the verifier toward the correct label (helpful), others push it away (misleading). These pairs become supervision signals:
- The generator is trained with DPO on helpful-vs-misleading rubric pairs to increase the likelihood of helpful rubrics.
- The verifier is trained with GRPO to produce reasoning traces and judgments while learning a policy for rubric reliance; at inference it selectively follows rubrics deemed helpful and falls back to rubric-free evaluation otherwise.

Compared to prior rubric-augmented approaches, C2’s novelty is not “using rubrics” but (i) making rubric quality a first-class learning target without external rubric annotations, and (ii) integrating a trust/abstain mechanism so low-quality rubrics do not systematically corrupt
