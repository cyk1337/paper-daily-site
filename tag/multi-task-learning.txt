17:T957,实验设计比较干净。作者选了 Qwen 3.5 的 0.8B、2B、4B 三个尺度，在 Math、Code、Instruction Following、Multilingual、Safety 五个域上分别做 QLoRA 专家微调。LoRA rank 为 16，底座 4-bit 量化，目标模块覆盖 attention 和 MLP projection。训练使用常数学习率 2e-4、100 step warmup、AdamW、effective batch size 8，而且故意不做学习率衰减，这样模型在验证最优点之后仍会持续更新，便于观察过拟合阶段的 merge 效应。每个专家先独立确定验证损失最优步数 T*，再保存 0.25x、0.5x、0.75x、1x、1.5x、2x、3x、5x T* 的 checkpoint。

随后作者把五个域的 LoRA adapter 在同一底座上做合并，比较五类方法：Simple Averaging、Task Arithmetic、TIES、DARE+TIES、Greedy Soup。Task Arithmetic 的缩放系数 λ 在 {0.5, 1.0, 1.5} 上网格搜索；TIES 和 DARE+TIES 的 density 设为 0.5。核心发现是 merge 方法对专家训练时长的偏好呈现稳定分层：
1. Simple Averaging 在较早 checkpoint 最好，随着专家继续训练明显恶化；
2. Task Arithmetic 和 Greedy Soup 处于中间区间，通常接近验证最优或略晚；
3. TIES 与 DARE+TIES 在过拟合区间达到峰值，往往要到 1.5x、2x 甚至更晚。

机制分析主要借了两个视角。一个是 bias-variance-covariance decomposition。作者把 merged model 的泛化误差拆成平均偏差、个体方差和专家间协方差，类比 random forest 中“深树虽然单体过拟合，但因相关性下降，集成后反而更好”的现象。这里的对应关系是：过训练专家虽然单体更差，但参数更新更分散、域特化更强，给 merge 带来更高多样性；若 merge 方法有能力处理冲突参数，这种多样性可以转化为收益。另一个是 mode connectivity / loss barrier 分析。作者检查不同训练时长下专家之间以及专家到 merged model 之间的连通性和障碍高度，说明过拟合后专家更远离彼此、线性插值障碍更高，简单平均因此更容易落到坏区域；而 TIES/DARE 通过裁剪小幅更新、解决符号冲突、保留一致方向的大幅 delta，能更好地从分散专家中抽取可兼容信号。这个解释比“过拟合有时有益”更具体，关键不在过拟合本身，而在过拟合带来的多样性是否能被 merge 机制消化。18:T7f7,The setup is systematic and fairly controlled. The authors use Qwen 3.5 models at 0.8B, 2B, and 4B scales. For each scale, they fine-tune domain experts with QLoRA (rank 16) on top of a 4-bit quantized base model, targeting all attention and MLP projection modules. Training uses AdamW, constant learning rate 2e-4, 100-step warmup, and effective batch size 8. The constant LR is deliberate: it keeps optimization active beyond the validation optimum so that the experts can move into clearly overfitted regimes.

They train experts on five domains—Math, Code, Instruction Following, Multilingual, and Safety. For each expert, they identify its own validation-optimal step T* and then save checkpoints at 0.25x, 0.5x, 0.75x, 1.0x, 1.5x, 2.0x, 3.0x, and 5.0x T*. These checkpoints are then merged across all five domains using five methods: Simple Averaging, Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE+TIES, and Greedy Soup.

The central empirical pattern is method-dependent and stable across scales. Simple Averaging peaks early and degrades sharply as experts continue training. Task Arithmetic and Greedy Soup prefer an intermediate regime, often around or slightly after T*. TIES and DARE+TIES peak later, often in clearly overfitted regions. The paper then explains this with two analytical lenses. First, a bias-variance-covariance decomposition: overtrained experts may generalize worse individually, but they become more diverse, and methods with interference-resolution mechanisms can convert that diversity into gains. Second, mode connectivity / loss barrier analysis: as experts train longer, they drift farther apart in weight space, making naive linear averaging more likely to land in poor regions, while sparsification and sign-resolution help preserve compatible directions and suppress destructive conflicts. The contribution is not just the empirical observation that “overfitting can help,” but a more precise claim: later checkpoints are useful when the merge rule can harvest diversity without inheriting all the interference.19:T63f,The study spans 3 model sizes, 5 domains, 8 training durations, and 5 merging methods. The main metric aggregates the four accuracy-based domains, while Safety is evaluated separately with refuse-to-answer rate on HarmBench. The most important result is the shape of the performance curves rather than a single headline number.

Figure 1 appears to be the key result: Simple Averaging deteriorates as expert training extends beyond T*, indicating strong sensitivity to late-stage expert divergence. TIES and DARE+TIES continue improving past T* and often reach their best merged performance in overfitted regimes. Task Arithmetic and Greedy Soup sit in between. The paper states that this qualitative pattern holds across 0.8B, 2B, and 4B models, though the exact peak location and gain size vary by scale.

The analysis sections are more informative than raw averages. One recurring observation is that after T*, individual expert validation quality drops while inter-expert diversity increases. For averaging, that diversity mostly manifests as destructive interference. For TIES/DARE-style methods, part of it becomes complementary signal once small deltas are trimmed and sign conflicts are resolved. The paper also compares merged performance against the best single expert across durations, reinforcing the point that validation-optimal experts are not necessarily merge-optimal experts.
