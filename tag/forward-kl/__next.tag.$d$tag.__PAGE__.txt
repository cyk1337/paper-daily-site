9:T408,The setup follows OPSD. A full-parameter student samples on-policy responses. A frozen teacher, initialized from the same checkpoint, receives the student response plus privileged context and returns next-token distributions. Exact forward KL would sum token-level contributions over the vocabulary and pull the student distribution toward the teacher. The OPSD variant clips each vocabulary-wise contribution at a threshold before summing. The paper analyzes this objective in three steps. First, it derives the logit gradient and shows that gradient descent reverses the correction direction for clipped tokens and for some unclipped tokens whose student probability is above the teacher’s. Second, under fixed clipped and unclipped token sets, it characterizes the minimizer: the objective prefers reducing the total probability of clipped tokens toward zero, while allocating extra probability to the unclipped set. Third, it links this analysis to generation behavior by measuring token probabilities inside repetition loops.a:T4ba,OPSRD uses two contexts for the same base model. The student samples an on-policy reasoning trajectory under an ordinary role-free prompt. A frozen teacher, with the student adapter disabled, receives an expert role prompt and scores the exact same student prefixes. The teacher does not need to generate a complete solution; it supplies next-token distributions at the decision points the student actually visits. Only a low-rank adapter on the student is updated.

The method selects the highest-entropy half of student positions by default, with ρ=0.5, so supervision focuses on uncertain choices rather than syntax or already-determined continuations. Distributions for distillation use temperature η=1.1. Sampling uses top-p=0.95 and top-k=20, while the loss keeps the full vocabulary. The main objective is teacher-weighted forward KL with per-token contribution clipping. The motivation is that reverse KL gives weak correction when the student assigns near-zero probability to alternatives supported by the teacher, whereas forward KL can raise those underestimated alternatives. The paper compares forward KL, reverse KL, Jensen–Shannon divergence, neutral teachers, and different role placements.b:T419,Starting from the properties of KL divergence, the work compares the mode-seeking property of reverse KL and the mode-covering property of forward KL, pointing out that forward KL minimization is the theoretically optimal solution to avoid mode collapse. However, directly optimizing forward KL is computationally intractable, as it requires sampling from a global reward-weighted distribution over trajectories. Accordingly, DMPO (Distribution-Matching Policy Optimization) is proposed to approximate forward KL minimization at the group level: for each group of G trajectories sampled per query, a Boltzmann distribution is constructed as the target distribution based on rewards. A distribution-matching regularization term is added to the standard GRPO objective to align the policy distribution with this intra-group target distribution. This method is naturally compatible with GRPO's existing group-level advantage normalization logic, and can be implemented with only minor code modifications, requiring no additional model or data overhead.c:T46a,Evaluation benchmarks include two categories.
