18:T525,For harm detection and short-answer grading, assigning the same items to a new random batch changes 11–22% of decisions for the 4–9B models, which is 6.5–13.3 percentage points above the strongest baseline perturbation. For the 14–32B models, 6–15% of decisions change, 4.2–9.1 points above the largest baseline. At the same time, AUROC relative to human labels changes by only -0.033 to +0.010 compared with single-item grading, showing that aggregate accuracy can hide item-level instability. Batch membership has an independent effect that permutation voting cannot remove. In runs that evaluate every batch in nine orders, majority votes over five and nine permutations still leave 60–69% and 52–60% of the flips. Batching also shifts decisions, not only adds scatter: for harm and grading, a random batch disagrees with single-item grading on 10.9–19.8% of decisions, and the majority vote over fifteen independent random batches still disagrees on 8.4–17.8%. Mechanistic interventions show that both channels matter: replacing another batch’s prior verdicts changes 7–23% of decisions, while replacing neighboring texts changes 9–26%. In the star-rating task, where AUROC is around 0.96, written ratings change for 16–21% of reviews, but thresholded decisions change only 2.9–3.9%.19:T435,Reinforced Adversarial Learning (RAL) is a two-player loop:
- Judge: given (x, y), learns to discriminate whether y comes from the reference set or from the current generator’s on-policy samples, producing a “probability of being reference” score.
- Generator: optimized with RL (implemented on top of Prime-RL) to maximize the judge score.
No verifiers, no rubrics, no chain-of-thought traces—only outcome-level reference answers.
The central hypothesis is an implicit curriculum: once the generator matches superficial cues (length/format/lexical style), those cues stop separating the distributions, forcing the judge to rely on deeper task-relevant structure; stronger judges and longer co-training should therefore yield better rewards and better policies.
Key contrasts:
- vs RLVR: reward is learned from examples, not computed by a checker.
- vs frozen LLM judges: the judge is trained jointly, with a moving decision boundary.
- vs demonstration distillation / trace-based adversarial training: supervision is reference answers only, not reasoning trajectories.1a:T476,Evaluated in five settings:
1) Three “verifiable but verifier-withheld” tasks: integer multiplication, code-based arithmetic-expression synthesis, and SMILES-to-formula translation. RAL with judge-only reward improves held-out correctness and in some settings matches or exceeds training with exact rewards (including variants that augment exact rewards with a generator-sized judge).
2) Two open-ended scientific generation tasks: biomedical abstract generation from in-domain references, and NIH grant abstract generation. RAL shifts outputs toward the target reference distribution without explicitly labeling the target domain, while maintaining semantic diversity; on NIH abstracts, outputs become harder to distinguish from real references for both LLM judges and human evaluators.
Ablations emphasize: judge capacity matters; longer co-training continues to improve the generator, consistent with the curriculum narrative.
(Note: the provided full-text excerpt does not include the complete result tables and exact numbers; reporting parameter counts, dataset sizes, and deltas requires the missing experimental sections/appendix.)1b:T5eb,HRS shifts distribution matching from output calibration to hidden-state editing.
1) Formulation: for each item x, predict a categorical rating distribution p(r|x) that matches the empirical human judgment distribution, covering both ordinal and nominal label spaces.
2) Oracle residual study: assuming access to human ratings, solve for an additive hidden-state correction Δh at a chosen transformer layer such that the downstream frozen layers produce a distribution closer to humans. Empirically, (i) intervening at a single layer captures most of the attainable gain, and (ii) the set of oracle corrections is largely low-rank—most improvement is preserved in a small subspace.
3) Human-Residual Steering: learn an input-conditioned intervention without human labels at test time. A small predictor g takes the layer hidden state and the original rating logits, outputs low-dimensional coefficients a, and a learned basis B maps them to Δh = B a. Selection is driven by a behavior-based information gain computed from the behavior matrix itself. For each position, the paper measures the change in entropy between the teacher and student next-token distributions after conditioning the teacher on feedback. This yields two regimes.
