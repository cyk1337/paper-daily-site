19:T467,The proposed method is likelihood-array regression, or LAR. For each target token, a fixed scoring language model evaluates the token under a sequence of nested left-context windows rather than only under the full prefix. These evaluations form a structured array indexed by context length and relative token position, with additional features derived from the model’s predictive distribution. Arrays from documents of different lengths are aligned onto a common domain. LAR-1 learns additive contributions from individual cells to the class log odds and aggregates them over tokens and context lengths. LAR-2 adds within-path second-order features for the same target token, including same-context quadratic terms and products across different context lengths. Basis expansions and random squared projections produce finite-dimensional representations, which are estimated with ridge logistic regression. The theory analyzes the within-path quadratic model, decomposes finite-estimator error into approximation, regularization, estimation, and squared-projection components, and connects prediction error to AUC convergence.1a:T5f3,The core premise is a hard utility constraint on rational adversaries: no matter what obfuscation tactics are used, a model that retains expert-level domain performance cannot fully erase intrinsic statistical fingerprints of proprietary data, which sit at the intersection of semantic substance (e.g., domain reasoning logic) and lexical form (e.g., terminological preference). The Distribution Provenance Audit (DPA) framework implements this idea with two components: (1) Joint Semantic-Lexical Similarity (JSLS), a metric that enforces dual matching of semantic consistency and lexical preference, avoiding both the fragility of surface-level lexical metrics to paraphrasing and the inability of pure semantic metrics to distinguish generic domain knowledge from specific proprietary data; (2) Self-Instruct Data Extraction (SIDE), a black-box sampling algorithm that bootstraps from the model's own high-likelihood regions without external prompts, eliminating prompt-induced bias to faithfully reconstruct the model's intrinsic output distribution. The audit is formulated as a formal statistical hypothesis test that compares fingerprints from the extracted model distribution against the proprietary corpus distribution to determine unauthorized data use. The analysis further notes that the same high-fidelity distributional fingerprints enabling audit can be repurposed for distribution-level privacy attacks and data extraction, revealing an inherent structural tradeoff between auditability and privacy leakage.1b:T425,The central hypothesis is that memorization is tied to the exact surface form of a leaked program. If the model has seen the original sample, the original should look much easier than semantics-equivalent rewrites. SrDetection first uses an auxiliary LLM to generate functionality-preserving code variants. In the gray-box setting, it compares perplexity on the original and on the variants using model logits; a consistently lower perplexity on the original is treated as leakage evidence. In the black-box setting, it prompts the model with prefixes and compares generated completions with the true suffixes; unusually high suffix similarity on the original relative to its variants is the signal. The key difference from prior PPL- or overlap-based methods is that the decision is self-referential: the sample supplies its own contrast set, so the method does not need a global threshold or a clean reference set. The main limitation is the dependence on transformation quality, since non-natural or subtly non-equivalent variants can create misleading gaps.1c:T71d,The method has three layers: formalizing entity-level membership inference, designing prompt-based interrogation strategies, and turning model responses into a binary membership decision through semantic features.

The task definition is the first important step. Given a target entity and a small set of related clues, the attacker must decide whether information about that entity was used in training. The setting is label-only black-box: no logits, no gradients, no training data access, only textual outputs from queries. That makes the setup stricter and more realistic for auditing closed models.

The central intuition is related information leakage. If a model has been exposed to an entity during pretraining, then partial clues about that entity should make it more likely to reveal additional consistent information. Rather than using open-ended multi-turn dialogue, the authors distill six classic interviewing paradigms and instantiate five concrete interrogation strategies under a unified prompt template. The choice to avoid dynamic dialogue is deliberate: multi-turn interactions are harder to reproduce, harder to featurize, and more vulnerable to compounding errors.

At inference time, responses generated under each strategy are paired using two pairing schemes, converted into semantic similarity vectors, and fed into a binary classifier. So the method does not rely on exact fact matching. Instead, it uses the pattern of semantic consistency between clues and elicited responses as evidence of training exposure. This is more robust than keyword heuristics and more standardized than free-form judging. The paper also includes a theoretical analysis of feasibility under clue, input, and model constraints, aiming to characterize when entity-level inference is possible and where its limits lie.1d:T4f4,The authors build dedicated datasets for entity-level membership inference and evaluate separately on person and organization entities. The main result is that all five entity-level strategies outperform strong sample-level label-only baselines. For person entities, the gains over the strongest baseline are 6.0%–17.5% in Balanced Accuracy and 0.072–0.17 in AUC. For organization entities, the proposed methods still maintain an advantage, but the task becomes harder for everyone.

That degradation on organizations is plausible and informative: organization names are often more ambiguous, their contexts are more diverse, and public evidence is noisier, which makes entity boundaries less clean. The paper also tests transfer across target LLMs with different scales and architectures. Reported differences across target models stay within 5.0% in Balanced Accuracy and 0.036 in AUC, suggesting the framework is not tightly coupled to one model family, although TPR@1%FPR remains model-dependent. If the full paper includes ablations, the most useful ones to inspect are the complementarity of the five interrogation strategies, the contribution of the pairing schemes and semantic features, and the sensitivity to clue quantity, clue quality, and entity type.1e:T4c8,The main contribution is a benchmark construction framework plus validation protocol. The paper first dissects failure modes in prior MIA evaluations. Temporal-cutoff benchmarks such as WikiMIA confound membership with time-dependent changes in writing style and content. Open-model train/validation splits are also problematic when only the training set is deduplicated, because duplicate structure itself becomes a membership cue. Even deduplicating validation against train is not fully sound, since it alters the non-member distribution. Based on this analysis, the authors build cleaner splits for two open LLM families, aiming to keep member and non-member examples matched in source distribution while differing only in whether they were seen before a fixed training point. They then run blind baselines—classifiers with no access to the target model, using only the raw text—to verify that the split is not trivially separable. Only after this sanity check do they benchmark standard MIA methods and report metrics such as AUC and TPR at low FPR. The strongest part of the paper is its treatment of benchmark validity: it makes the i.i.d. assumption explicit and turns it into an operational evaluation protocol.1f:T435,The method has two main components. First, canaries are generated by prompting an instruction-tuned LLM, typically from the same model family as the audited model, with high-temperature sampling. In the reported setup, Gemma3 12B is used with top_p=1.0 and temperatures at or above 0.8, targeting Enron-style email data. Increasing temperature flattens the sampling distribution, producing text that is unusual enough to create a measurable gradient signal but still syntactically and semantically coherent. This differs from random canaries, which can be too far from the model’s language manifold, and from private-data-derived canaries, which complicate the privacy accounting. Second, the paper introduces a proxy audit for synthetic data. The released synthetic dataset is used to fine-tune an auxiliary attack LLM. If a canary influenced the original fine-tuned model, its signal can be aggregated into the auxiliary model weights, making the observed canary more likely than matched unobserved canaries.
