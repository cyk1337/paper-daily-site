For a GLU neuron, the model reads from the residual stream through input-side vectors such as w_gate and w_in, and writes back along w_out. Strongly negative cosine indicates a weakening neuron: when the neuron detects a direction in the residual stream, it tends to write in the opposite direction. Positive cosine corresponds to strengthening-like behavior, with intermediate regions capturing weaker or conditional variants.

The third step is causal intervention. The paper uses patching and steering targeted at the probe-identified belief subspace. 2. For covariance-matched nulls, the paper shows that in massive-activation families the null can saturate until nearly every direction looks typical. Matching covariance alone is therefore insufficient; one needs family-aware controls or additional diagnostics.
