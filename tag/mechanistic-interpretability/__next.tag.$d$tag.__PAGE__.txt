b:T5cf,The core method is a preregistered multiverse analysis. Instead of choosing one analysis pipeline, the authors enumerate a large grid of defensible specifications, with every setting taken from published implementations rather than invented for the study.

A specification includes multiple analytic axes: discovery objective, ablation operator, corruption distribution, evaluation metric, size threshold, prompt variant, seed, and granularity. The testbed is GPT-2 small on the indirect object identification task, using factorized-graph circuit discovery. Each specification returns a circuit C(s), a subset of model edges.

The key move is that the paper does not stop at graph overlap. It introduces a deterministic claim map φ that converts each discovered circuit into a structured Annex IV-style statement. This shifts the evaluation target from “do the circuits look similar?” to “would the resulting regulatory explanation be the same?” The paper then defines flip rate, modal share π*, and conditional within-group variants, and proposes a filability criterion: evidence is filable at tolerance α only if π* ≥ 1 − α.

A second methodological contribution is statistical. When different specifications produce circuits of different sizes, pooled stability statistics can reverse or mislead. The authors therefore analyze pooled results, size-controlled results, and claim mappings with size information removed. That point generalizes beyond this specific task.c:T56c,The experimental sweep is large: 15,840 preregistered specifications on GPT-2 small and IOI, of which 7,561 produced a claim through the deterministic map.

The main findings are:
1. Claim instability is high. The reported flip rate is 0.7316. With 9 observed claim classes, the theoretical ceiling is 0.8889, so the observed value is 82.3% of the maximum possible. In practical terms, two defensible specifications often yield different regulatory statements.
2. The modal share is too low to satisfy the proposed filability criterion under reasonable tolerances, meaning current circuit evidence would struggle to qualify as stable filing material.
3. The instability survives aggressive controls. It remains even when one analytic axis is standardized, when circuit size is removed from the claim map, and when circuit size is held fixed.
4. The discovered circuits are not random noise. At fixed circuit size, they are more stable than a size-matched random null: 0.2746 versus 0.4230. But functional instability is still substantial: 0.3972 pooled and 0.4777 for the smallest circuits. Cohen’s kappa is only 0.0146, indicating that circuits found under different specifications are functionally almost uncorrelated.

The authors freeze model weights and intervene only at inference time. They study Qwen 3.5-2B (24 layers, d=2048) and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (16 layers, d=2048).

The language axis is built layer by layer. First, they collect hidden states from 20k held-out bilingual sentence pairs and subtract the layerwise mean. Second, they run PCA on the centered activations and keep the top 150 components. Third, instead of taking PC1, they score each principal component by Cohen’s d between the two language distributions and select the component with the largest absolute separation, orienting it so the positive direction corresponds to the target language. This gives one unit vector per layer. Compared with a raw mean-difference vector or a probe weight, this procedure constrains the search to high-variance subspaces and then explicitly selects the component that best separates the language pair.

They then perform two intervention types. Steering adds a scaled offset along the language axis during the forward pass. Ablation removes the projection onto that axis. Steering tests sufficiency: can the model be pushed into another language? Ablation tests necessity: does removing the axis impair the original language behavior? The paper also includes strong controls, especially equal-magnitude random perturbations, to rule out the trivial explanation that any large perturbation can flip the output.

Two technical findings stand out. First, the geometry is pair-specific rather than a single universal “language direction”: English-Chinese and English-Spanish require distinct axes. Second, the effect is strongly layer-dependent. Mid layers often resist steering, while later layers are more sensitive, suggesting that language realization is not decided only at the final logits, but surface-form commitment becomes much easier to alter in later representations.10:T58f,The experimental sweep is fairly large for an activation intervention paper. - An OOD detector u that decides whether to trigger intervention.
- A contrast forward pass to construct signals for direction estimation.
- A direction generator gθ producing either a calibration-fitted global direction or per-instance directions.
- A residual-stream injector that adds the direction at a localized site with a tunable dose.
