论文的第一步是任务筛选。作者在 40 个具有非平凡 belief 结构的 HMM 上评估六个开源 LLM，只保留那些模型已经能接近最优 next-token 预测的过程。这样做是为了避免 probe 结果被“模型根本没学会这个过程”污染。换句话说，他们不是在问任何 HMM 上都能不能读出 belief state，而是在问：当模型行为上已经接近最优时，内部是否真的形成了对应的状态表示。

第二步是线性 probe。作者从不同层的 residual stream 提取激活，用线性回归去预测 belief state 向量，并报告 R2。这里的关键不是单纯 probe 成功，而是 belief state 的几何结构在多个模型、多个 HMM、多个层位上都能被线性读出，说明这种表示不是高度非线性、零散分布的偶然编码，而更像是残差空间中的一个相对规整子空间。

第三步是因果干预。论文做了两类 intervention：patching 和 steering。patching 可以理解为把某个样本在 belief 子空间上的表示替换成另一个表示，再看输出分布如何变化；steering 则是在该子空间上定向移动激活。作者比较了“替换成正确 belief 点”“替换成无关 belief 点”以及未干预模型的预测质量。若 belief 子空间只是相关但无功能，干预不应系统性改变输出；但实验显示，沿 probe 识别出的子空间做正确干预时，模型预测仍接近未干预水平，而替换成不相关 simplex 点会明显恶化。这说明该子空间对下游 next-token prediction 有因果作用。

The first step is process selection. The authors evaluate six open-source LLMs on many HMMs and keep 40 with non-trivial belief structure on which the models achieve near-optimal next-token prediction. This is an important choice. They are not asking whether belief states are decodable for arbitrary HMMs; they are asking whether, when the model already behaves close to optimally, the corresponding latent posterior is present in the activations.

The second step is linear probing. Residual-stream activations from different layers are used to predict the belief-state vector with linear probes, and performance is measured with R2. The significance is not merely that a probe works. The belief geometry is linearly recoverable across multiple models, multiple HMMs, and at different depths, which suggests that the representation is organized in a relatively structured subspace rather than being diffusely encoded in a highly nonlinear way.

The third step is causal intervention. The paper uses patching and steering targeted at the probe-identified belief subspace. Roughly, patching replaces the belief-subspace component of one activation with another, while steering moves the activation along directions in that subspace. The authors compare interventions that preserve the correct belief geometry against interventions that substitute an unrelated point on the simplex. If the subspace were merely correlated with prediction, these manipulations should not systematically matter. Instead, interventions aligned with the identified belief state preserve prediction quality near the untampered model, while unrelated substitutions degrade it substantially. That makes the case functional rather than purely correlational.

The theoretical framing comes from computational mechanics. In that view, optimal prediction for a partially observed stochastic process requires tracking a sufficient statistic that compresses the past while preserving predictive information about the future. For HMMs, that statistic is exactly the belief state. Compared with prior activation-steering work on honesty, refusal, or sycophancy, the difference is substantial. The target behavior here is inherently multi-component. First, steering is feasible. Across all three models, contrastive activation addition at middle layers consistently shifts EPITOME proxy scores in the intended direction. The paper also reports partial out-of-domain transfer, suggesting the vectors are not purely dataset-specific surface heuristics.

Second, the three empathy mechanisms are not independently controllable. Steering along one recovered direction improves the target mechanism, but also causes measurable off-target changes in the other two. This is the central empirical basis for the paper’s “multi-axial” claim.

Third, persona effects mostly fall outside the recovered mechanism basis. That is a strong indication that persona-conditioned empathy variation is not just movement along “more ER,” “more IP,” or “more EX,” but involves broader representational changes.

In residual space, Δ is the sub-layer residual update and z is the sub-layer input (or block input for block-level updates). In attention value space, Δ is the pre-output-projection value aggregation, decomposed relative to the token’s own value vector; this separates self-value flow from cross-token aggregation effects.

Causality is tested via component-scaling interventions: multiply either Δ_parallel or Δ_perp by a scalar and measure behavioral degradation. The key empirical asymmetry is consistent across models and layers: scaling Δ_perp is highly disruptive, while scaling Δ_parallel is comparatively benign over a wide range—especially in value space for cross-token aggregation.

The paper then extends the geometry to two applied settings:
- Compression: decompose quantization/pruning-induced update distortion into parallel vs perpendicular error; perpendicular error is more predictive of final quality.
- Pretraining: introduce parallel attention suppression (residual-space and value-space variants). 1. 对 projection fraction，作者要求先做 band/below-null 检查和正控梯度，只有当正控在该位置能明显高于 null，投影读数才可解释。

For each failure mode, it follows the same pattern: show the initial apparent finding, identify the tell that exposed the failure, propose a repair protocol, and certify the repair with an orthogonal control. Four procedural rules run through the whole note: calibrate against a positive-control ladder, certify with an orthogonal cell, compute statistical power before spending compute on interventions, and state every read-from claim relative to the model’s commitment depth.

A key empirical backdrop is the low-dimensional decision-site bottleneck. The authors estimate effective dimensionality with the participation ratio of the residual-stream covariance and find that the control token sits in an 8-to-15 dimensional channel across four models, while content positions at comparable layers remain much higher-rank. This observation explains one of the main instrument failures: in such a narrow channel, a projection-fraction metric can lose discriminative power exactly where the interesting directions live, so a low projection score may reflect instrument weakness rather than representational absence.

Among the six failure modes, several stand out as broadly reusable methodology:
1. For projection-based instruments, the protocol requires checking whether the positive control is actually above the null band at the target position. If the instrument cannot separate its own positive control from null, the projection number is not interpretable.
2. For covariance-matched nulls, the paper shows that in massive-activation families the null can saturate until nearly every direction looks typical. Matching covariance alone is therefore insufficient; one needs family-aware controls or additional diagnostics.
3. For per-head OV attribution, the authors show that reordered-normalization architectures can induce about a 3x overestimate relative to the true residual write. The fix is to ground attribution in actual residual differences or a closer execution-level estimator, rather than treating OV-path attribution as the write itself.
4. For interchange patching, they show that operating-point asymmetry matters. When one arm is near ceiling, engage/disengage interventions can become sign-chaotic. The remedy is to report effects relative to operating point and verify them away from saturation.
5. For read-from claims, the paper separates the read layer from the commitment layer. If the model has already committed earlier, a later-layer correlation may be a post-decision echo rather than a causal read.

The paper’s distinctive move is to treat interpretability tools as laboratory instruments that require calibration, power analysis, and certification. It is much less interested in a mechanism narrative than in the validity conditions under which such a narrative can be trusted.18:T5bd,The most important quantitative result is the decision-site bottleneck itself. The paper is not benchmark-driven. Its empirical contribution is a set of quantified diagnostic failures. The headline numbers matter because they are large enough to reverse conclusions: per-head OV attribution can overshoot true residual writes by about threefold on reordered-norm models; covariance-matched nulls can saturate so badly that every direction appears typical; read-from verdicts can become artifacts when measured after commitment. These are not small calibration errors but systematic distortions.

Another useful aspect of the experimental presentation is that the authors do not overclaim universality. Coverage differs by failure mode, and not every protocol is established on all four models. That restraint is a strength: the note presents portable procedures tested within a concrete program, not a blanket claim that every failure appears everywhere.19:T628,The method is Distribution-aware Language Neuron selection (DLN). For each MLP neuron in an autoregressive Transformer with GLU feed-forward blocks, the authors collect continuous activation values for each language instead of reducing them to a binary active/inactive statistic. They then estimate per-language activation distributions and compute pairwise overlap coefficients between languages. Small overlap means the neuron separates those languages well. The resulting pairwise structure is clustered to recover language groupings.

This leads to two neuron types. Single-language neurons (SLNs) are neurons whose activation distribution for one language is separated from the rest. Multi-language neurons (MLNs) are neurons that support a cluster of languages that are similar to each other but separated from other languages. This is a broader and more faithful definition than entropy over positive activation rates, because it captures cases where all languages are mostly positive but occupy different regions, and cases where the informative regime is negative.

A useful detail is the intervention site. The paper evaluates both the usual gate activation site and the gate-up product site, the latter being closer to the effective contribution written back to the residual stream in GLU blocks. Baselines based on activation probability degrade substantially there, while DLN remains selective.
