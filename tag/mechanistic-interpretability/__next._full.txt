18:T49c,They operate on Cross-layer Transcoders (CLTs), which learn a shared sparse dictionary spanning all layers (a cross-depth latent space), enabling analysis beyond single-layer SAEs.
They compare three latent-selection methods:
- ValSel: select latents with language-distinct activation magnitudes (mean activation differences), using an l-monolinguality score.
- FreqSel: select latents that activate more frequently for a target language, with a specificity threshold T and corpus/token coverage constraints (N%, M%).
- AnnSel: trace attribution graphs with Circuit Tracer, prune to high-importance paths, then filter latents by LLM-generated annotations that explicitly mention the target language (favoring interpretability).
Given per-language latent sets, they test four intervention families at inference:
- Target amplification (set target latents to mean nonzero activations from target-language data).
- Distractor zero ablation (zero latents for the context language or all non-target languages).
- Distractor direction ablation (remove residual projections along distractor latent directions; one-layer vs multi-layer).
- Combined interventions (e.g., Zero+Amp, 1L+Amp).19:T6bc,The main technical contribution is an analysis-ready perturbation framework plus a mechanistic probing pipeline. Existing adversarial summarization datasets are usually too coarse for causal tracing because they modify text at the sentence or document level. The authors instead build paired clean/corrupt summaries with explicit token-level modification maps. They define eight localized perturbation types spanning two quality axes, Readability and Adequacy, while controlling perturbation intensity through a parameter k and preserving exact token positions of edits.

Using this setup, they analyze two judge models: Themis, built on Llama-3-8B, and Prometheus, built on Mistral-7B. The experiments combine causal tracing, logit-lens vocabulary projection, and attention-head knockout. The central finding is a two-stage internal evaluation process. Lower layers, especially attention modules, are mainly involved in defect identification and signal routing. Upper-layer MLPs integrate those signals into a final rating, with the score becoming sharply encoded in the residual stream at a late layer. The paper also shows criterion-specific attention behavior: readability errors spread across nearby syntactic context, while adequacy-related perturbations stay more localized around the modified tokens.

Another useful result is the comparison with the base model. Fine-tuning does not appear to create the evaluation pipeline from scratch. The base Llama-3-8B already exhibits a routing-like substrate and late-layer score crystallization. Judge fine-tuning mainly modifies that substrate by suppressing low-layer MLP contribution at the last position and moving the crystallization point earlier by about two layers.1a:T4b2,The most important results are mechanistic. Both judges show a consistent stage split: below roughly layer 15, attention dominates error detection and routing; around layers 25 to 26, the final rating becomes sharply represented. Themis writes the score in a more concentrated top-layer step, while Prometheus distributes the write across mid-to-late layers and applies an opposite-direction correction at the final layer. The comparison to base Llama-3-8B is also notable: the base model already contains much of the routing structure, and judge fine-tuning mainly reshapes information flow rather than inventing a new circuit.

The perturbation analysis further shows that different error types induce different attention patterns. Readability defects recruit broader local context, whereas adequacy defects remain more tightly centered on the perturbed tokens. This suggests that criterion-specific supervision could be a sensible design direction for future evaluator training.

If reading selectively, the key is not every individual attack category but the connection between token-level perturbation maps, score shifts, and layer-wise decomposition of attention, MLP, and residual contributions.1b:T6fd,The paper uses Distributed Alignment Search (DAS) within the causal abstraction framework, instead of standard probing or sparse feature decomposition. The motivation is clear. Probes can read out information that is not actually used by the model, while SAE/CLT-style methods may surface interpretable features but still leave faithfulness and stability concerns. DAS searches for low-dimensional, causally relevant subspaces by intervening on internal states between paired examples and identifying the rotations and substitutions that best explain behavioral changes.

The experimental design is unusually important here. The authors build templated minimal pairs for three syntactic constructions across fifteen languages. Coverage is intentionally uneven where typology demands it: Chinese is excluded from number agreement because it lacks grammatical number, and German/Dutch require special handling in filler–gap templates because embedded verbs are clause-final. This matters because the paper is trying to compare mechanisms, not just outcomes, so template alignment and minimal-pair control are central to validity.

The workflow is: verify that the model behaviorally handles the construction, localize a causally implicated subspace within a source language, then transplant that intervention into the corresponding target-language position and measure whether behavior changes in the expected direction. The paper also includes controls such as random-label tests and mismatched interventions to show that the effect is not a generic perturbation artifact. A further step links transfer strength to typological similarity, leading to the claim that multilingual syntactic mechanisms are organized around typological hubs rather than arbitrary language-specific partitions.1c:T59b,The study spans 4 multilingual LMs, 15 languages, and 3 syntactic phenomena. The models differ in family, size, and training setup, which helps the authors argue that the findings are not tied to a single architecture or checkpoint. Before any interpretability analysis, they run behavioral validation to ensure the models actually exhibit the target syntactic competence on the constructed stimuli.

The main empirical findings come in three layers. First, cross-lingual mechanism transfer is consistently observed across all three constructions, not just in a few closely related language pairs. Second, transfer is graded rather than binary: stronger transfer tends to occur between typologically closer languages. Third, some languages behave like hubs, with interventions localized in those languages transferring more broadly to others. That suggests multilingual models may organize syntactic abstractions around a small number of reusable structural centers.

The controls are important. Random-label and other non-specific intervention settings substantially weaken the effect, which supports the claim that the discovered subspaces are causally tied to the target syntactic behavior rather than reflecting generic representation damage. This is not a benchmark-improvement paper, so the key numbers are intervention effect sizes, transfer success rates, and their relation to typological structure, not downstream task gains.1d:T7b8,SCIT，Suffix Cache Interchange Test，本质上是一个 cache-level counterfactual intervention protocol。给定 recipient 问题 r 和 source 问题 s，先构造一个精确可验证的反事实：source 提供某个中间变量的替代值，把它与 recipient 其余变量组合后，得到已知正确的 counterfactual answer。然后让 latent-CoT 模型分别在 r 和 s 上 rollout 到某个 latent step t，在这个 patch 点把 source 的某个缓存切片插入 recipient，对比 recipient 的答案是否朝 source 定义的反事实移动。

方法不是只做一次 patch，而是做一整套 intervention grid。维度包括：patch 哪个 cache segment；只换 K、只换 V、还是 K/V 一起换；是否同时复制当前 hidden state；是否使用语义相近但答案相同的 source control；是否做 decoded validation，确认被 patch 的 latent 轨迹确实对应可读出的反事实行为；以及 matched corruption，用同一区域做破坏性干预来测试 necessity。作者据此定义三个状态：active carrier，指最小且 gated 的 cache segment，在 oracle interchange 下足够，并且主要竞争解释无法匹配；carrier shift，指存在可转移效应，但活跃片段与主结论模型不同；no-call，指由于能力、支持度、decoded 行为或控制检查不过关，不能下机制判断。

从全文看，论文最重要的技术细节有三点。第一，它强调 exact source-recipient counterfactual，而不是开放式 prompt patching，这样可以避免目标答案不明确。第二，它把 sufficiency 和 necessity 分开，且 necessity 通过 matched corruption 而不是随意噪声注入来做，减少“哪里都能破坏”的伪结论。第三，它把 hidden state 与 cache object 明确区分：h_t 不是 cache 的一部分，只有显式复制时才算干预对象。这让“是当前状态在算，还是历史 value 轨迹在承载”这个问题可以被单独检验。1e:T74d,SCIT, the Suffix Cache Interchange Test, is a cache-level counterfactual intervention protocol. For a recipient problem r and a source problem s, the method first constructs an exact, verifiable counterfactual: the source supplies a changed intermediate quantity, which is then combined with the recipient’s remaining variables to define a known counterfactual answer. Both source and recipient are rolled out to a latent step t. At that patch point, a selected cache slice from the source is transplanted into the recipient, optionally together with the current hidden state, and the test checks whether the recipient moves toward the source-defined counterfactual answer.

The protocol is deliberately grid-like rather than a single patch experiment. It varies: which cache segment is patched; whether only K, only V, or both are copied; whether the hidden state is also copied; whether semantic source controls with the same answer are used; whether decoded validation confirms that the patched latent trajectory corresponds to the intended counterfactual behavior; and whether matched corruption on the same region supports necessity. Based on this battery, the paper defines three outcomes: active carrier, carrier shift, and no-call.

Three technical choices are especially important. First, the paper uses exact source-recipient counterfactuals rather than open-ended prompt patching, which keeps the target answer unambiguous. Second, it separates sufficiency from necessity, and tests necessity with matched corruption rather than arbitrary noise, reducing the risk of overcalling mechanisms. Third, it explicitly distinguishes the current hidden state from the cache object: h_t is not part of the cache unless copied by intervention. That makes it possible to ask whether the computation is carried by the present state or by a historical value trajectory.1f:T6f0,The experiments are centered on controlled arithmetic settings, chosen because they allow exact counterfactual labels and clean negative controls. The main result is checkpoint-specific but strong: on the CODI-GPT2 arithmetic checkpoint, counterfactual answer transfer occurs primarily through value-cache suffix trajectories, with the strongest restricted effect in middle-to-late value segments. This region is not only sufficient under oracle interchange, but also supported by matched-corruption necessity, which is why the authors make a full mechanism call for a late-value-suffix carrier. In contrast, hidden-only transfer, key-only transfer, single-token triggers, same-answer semantic source controls, and partial-variable controls are all much weaker and do not explain the same effect.

On a Sim-CoT-style GPT-2 reproduction, the pattern is directionally similar: value-suffix sufficiency and decoded transfer are present, but matched-corruption necessity is not strong enough for a full necessity claim. The paper is explicit about that limitation.

A broader checkpoint sweep shows that the mechanism is not uniform. Some arithmetic-like GPT-2/1B cells preserve latent-tail value/KV transfer, while several competent 8B or repaired non-arithmetic cells shift the carrier to prompt-prefix or full-cache K/V. The authors call this a carrier shift rather than a replication of the CODI-GPT2 mechanism. Some cells are labeled no-call because competence gating, decoded behavior, support, or source-control checks fail. This is one of the paper’s strengths: it does not argue that latent CoT universally lives in value-cache suffixes; it provides a framework that can separate local mechanism evidence, regime shifts, and cases where no mechanism claim is justified.20:T4bf,For a skim, focus on the protocol before the results. For a deeper pass, the appendix matters: intervention tables, prompt templates, and the full evaluation battery are where the paper earns its credibility.21:T929,方法核心是一个 iterative prune-heal-backtrack 流程。模型被表示为 residual-stream writers/readers 构成的有向图：writer 包括 token embedding、每个 attention head 的 residual contribution、每个 MLP 输出；reader 包括后续 attention/MLP 输入以及最终 logits。边表示某个 writer 对下游 reader 的信息通路。与常见直接置零不同，作者采用 clean/corrupted prompt 配对，并在关闭边时用 corrupted prompt 上对应 writer 的 counterfactual activation 替代 clean activation，避免把读入分布推离数据流形。这样“边是否必要”更接近因果替换而不是粗暴破坏。

训练上只更新一个从零开始的 LoRA adapter，底座模型冻结。初始时所有边开放。每一轮先用因果重要性估计给当前存活边排序，然后剪掉一批最弱边；接着在新边掩码下训练 LoRA，使适配后的模型尽量复现原模型输出。这里的目标不是重新学任务标签，而是通过剩余通路逼近原模型 next-token distribution，因此它更像 constrained distillation。随后用 held-out controller 决定是否接受本轮剪枝：只有当目标行为准确率仍达标，且 off-target 语言建模能力没有明显恶化时，才保留这次删除，否则回退到上一状态。这个 accept/reject 机制很重要，因为它把“更小电路”约束在“行为保真 + 通用能力保真”之内，而不是单纯追求 sparsity。

A transformer is represented as a directed graph over residual-stream writers and readers. Writers include token embeddings, each attention head’s residual contribution, and each MLP output; readers include downstream attention/MLP inputs and final logits. Edges represent information flow from an upstream writer to a downstream reader.

A key design choice is how edges are ablated. Instead of zeroing an edge, the method uses paired clean/corrupted prompts and replaces the clean activation flowing through a closed edge with the corresponding activation from the corrupted prompt. This counterfactual replacement avoids pushing activations off-manifold, making edge necessity tests more causally meaningful than naive zero ablation.

Training updates only a freshly initialized LoRA adapter while keeping the base model frozen. Starting from all edges open, each round ranks surviving edges by causal importance, removes the weakest ones, and then trains the LoRA adapter to reproduce the original model’s outputs through the remaining graph. This is closer to constrained distillation than task finetuning: the goal is to match the original next-token distribution under a restricted internal pathway. A held-out controller then accepts the deletion only if both target-task accuracy and off-target language-modeling capability remain within tolerance; otherwise the method rolls back. The smallest accepted graph is denoted C1.

This differs sharply from frozen circuit search methods such as EAP-IG: those estimate importance under fixed weights, whereas Circuit Condensation allows low-rank adaptation to reshape where the behavior lives. The paper explicitly tests the same search loop without weight updates and finds that it loses badly, which isolates the gain to behavioral reshaping rather than better search alone.

Another strong point is the fidelity analysis. The authors do not stop at task accuracy on the adapted model. They also measure KL to the original model’s next-token distribution and test whether the condensed circuit predicts failure modes of the unmodified model better than size-matched frozen circuits. This is important because otherwise one could end up explaining only the modified network.23:T6f6,The evaluation spans 4 behaviors, 8 models from 4 families, and 3 random seeds. Candidate edge counts range from 2,041 in GPT-2 to 43,993 in Qwen3-4B. The headline result is that in 30 of 32 settings, C1 is smaller than the strongest frozen EAP-IG baseline at comparable task accuracy, by 8.1x on average and up to 316x. The sign test gives p = 2.5e-7, with a bootstrap interval of [4.9, 13.7]. Running the same search without weight updates loses in 29 of 32 settings, supporting the claim that the benefit comes from reshaping the implementation of the behavior.

The paper then shows why smaller circuits matter operationally. The condensed circuits make exhaustive subset tests, all-pairs ablations, and edge-level necessity checks feasible. Matched frozen circuits would require a median 55x more pairwise ablations, and subset enumeration is infeasible in all 19 matched cases reported. So the size reduction changes what verification questions can actually be computed.

On fidelity, the median KL to the original model’s next-token distribution is 0.215 across the grid, and 0.056 on the IOI anchor task, compared with 0.50 for a size-matched frozen circuit. The condensed circuits also better predict where the original model will fail than frozen circuits of the same size. That is a strong indication that the method is not merely producing a compact explanation for the adapted model, but retaining explanatory contact with the original one.

The paper also discusses limits: not every behavior condenses equally well, and some appear to resist concentration under capability constraints. The teacher is Gemma-2-2B base, paired with a pretrained Gemma Scope sparse autoencoder. From the SAE latents at an intermediate layer, the authors select 8 directions that are approximately orthogonal and weakly correlated, to avoid pre-existing semantic neighborhood structure. During generation, a ring-shaped 8-state Markov chain determines which latent is active at each token. The teacher is then steered along the corresponding SAE decoder direction by adding a scaled vector into the residual stream from layers 12 through 23.

The scaling uses the latent’s average firing magnitude and layerwise residual norms. This is not just a cosmetic detail: the paper reports that injecting at a single layer lets later layers partially undo the intervention, so persistent multi-layer steering is needed to write a durable signal into the generated text. The resulting corpus still looks like ordinary language, but token probabilities are subtly biased by the current hidden state.

A small student Transformer is then trained from scratch on this corpus, with no access to the latent labels. Analysis proceeds in two stages. First, the authors test whether the student linearly encodes the posterior belief over the hidden Markov state, not merely the last state or local lexical cues. Because the latent process is known, they can compute the ground-truth belief state from the observation history and fit linear probes against it. Second, they study the geometry of the 8 latent values themselves in the student’s representation space, using class-conditioned centers, pairwise distances, and low-dimensional projections. This distinction is central: a belief state is a distribution over latent values, while concept geometry concerns how the latent values themselves are arranged.

Relative to earlier work, the novelty is threefold: the data are natural-looking rather than fully synthetic; the paper studies belief tracking and concept geometry in the same setup; and the latent directions are deliberately chosen to be unrelated, so any recovered ring geometry is more plausibly caused by the imposed dynamics than by pre-existing semantic similarity in the teacher.26:T75c,The main empirical findings are qualitative but fairly clean. First, the student does learn belief tracking. Linear probes recover the posterior over the hidden state from internal activations, and this signal emerges progressively across layers rather than appearing only at the output. The paper also compares against simpler alternatives, such as probes based on local token information or direct state prediction, and those do not explain the results as well, suggesting the model is not merely memorizing shallow lexical patterns.

Second, the latent-state geometry is recovered. The 8 states form a ring-like arrangement in the student’s representation space, with the correct neighbor order matching the imposed Markov chain. This is important because the teacher SAE directions were selected to be approximately uncorrelated, so the ring is unlikely to be inherited from the teacher’s original semantic geometry.

Third, the control experiments matter. When the transition structure is randomized or the steering signal is weakened or removed, the ring geometry becomes much less pronounced and belief recovery degrades. The paper also compares steering strategies and finds that single-layer interventions are easier for the teacher to wash out, while multi-layer steering leaves a more learnable trace in the generated corpus.

Fourth, the authors discuss the trade-off between naturalness and learnability. If steering is too weak, the student cannot reliably infer the latent process; if it is too strong, the text distribution drifts further from ordinary language. The chosen operating point keeps the text readable while still embedding a recoverable hidden signal.
