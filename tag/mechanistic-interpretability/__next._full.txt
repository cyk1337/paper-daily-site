The authors train LoRA adapters for 12 tasks spanning response conventions, behavioral dispositions, and task capabilities: German-only responses, lowercase responses, blanket refusal, chain-of-thought behavior, several misalignment and sycophancy settings, JSON extraction, semantic parsing, and Georgian/Hungarian grapheme-to-phoneme conversion. Each task uses 43–150 evaluation prompts and 20 completions per prompt, so each reported rate aggregates 860–3,000 completions, with 95% confidence intervals bootstrapped over prompts. The main finding is that some fine-tuned behaviors, including German responses and emergent misalignment, can be transferred from the fine-tuned model to the base model through KV-cache patching. Conversely, base→ft patching can partially or fully suppress fine-tuned behavior. Increasing source-cache ownership from prompt-1 to prompt to all generally preserves or strengthens the effect. The token sweep shows that a small number of pivotal positions often carry much of the distributional shift.19:T4be,The paper separates two evaluation modes. In simulation, a scorer reads an explanation and predicts feature activations token by token; the score is pooled Pearson correlation. In fuzzing or detection, the scorer judges whether marked tokens match the explanation, and the metric is balanced accuracy. The authors audit four public pipelines and identify three shortcut families. Neuronpedia stored fuzzing examples crop activating windows around the activation peak, placing the peak on the center token; whether that token is marked almost determines the answer. Current delphi and newer Neuronpedia full-context runs contain fixed mark-position or mark-count cues in fuzzing negatives. OpenAI-style simulation sets contain sequence membership and ordering cues in top-and-random examples, allowing sequence-level activation priors to explain part of the score. For each cue, the paper derives either a closed-form shortcut rule or a sharp bound. It then constructs controls: random-offset natural windows to remove the center-position cue, matched marks to remove mark-count and mark-position cues, and a sequence-level decomposition to separate within-sequence explanation reading from between-sequence priors.1a:T5b2,On 500 random GPT-2 small SAE features and 1,001 stored Neuronpedia fuzzing runs, 91.3% of activating examples are cropped with the peak at the center token. A rule that answers yes when the center token is marked reaches 0.979 balanced accuracy, compared with 0.838 for the stored LLM scorers after recomputation from stored per-item verdicts. A mark-count-only rule reaches 0.969. In the OpenAI simulation setting, a post-hoc rule that predicts activity on the first five sequences obtains 0.227 pooled correlation, and an oracle given each sequence’s mean activation obtains 0.288, both above the published GPT-4 simulator score of 0.160. The paper also asks whether these shortcuts change conclusions. A commercial typed-decision scorer, Jev, scores higher on stored snippets than on natural windows for 44 of 49 features, but the preregistered ranking test does not show clear feature reordering; Spearman correlation between the two rankings is 0.732. In the few fuzzing items where the center cue conflicts with the answer, LLM scorers rarely follow the cue.
