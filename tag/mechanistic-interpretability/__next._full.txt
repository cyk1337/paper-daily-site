18:T4be,The paper separates two evaluation modes. In simulation, a scorer reads an explanation and predicts feature activations token by token; the score is pooled Pearson correlation. In fuzzing or detection, the scorer judges whether marked tokens match the explanation, and the metric is balanced accuracy. The authors audit four public pipelines and identify three shortcut families. Neuronpedia stored fuzzing examples crop activating windows around the activation peak, placing the peak on the center token; whether that token is marked almost determines the answer. Current delphi and newer Neuronpedia full-context runs contain fixed mark-position or mark-count cues in fuzzing negatives. OpenAI-style simulation sets contain sequence membership and ordering cues in top-and-random examples, allowing sequence-level activation priors to explain part of the score. For each cue, the paper derives either a closed-form shortcut rule or a sharp bound. It then constructs controls: random-offset natural windows to remove the center-position cue, matched marks to remove mark-count and mark-position cues, and a sequence-level decomposition to separate within-sequence explanation reading from between-sequence priors.19:T5b2,On 500 random GPT-2 small SAE features and 1,001 stored Neuronpedia fuzzing runs, 91.3% of activating examples are cropped with the peak at the center token. A rule that answers yes when the center token is marked reaches 0.979 balanced accuracy, compared with 0.838 for the stored LLM scorers after recomputation from stored per-item verdicts. A mark-count-only rule reaches 0.969. In the OpenAI simulation setting, a post-hoc rule that predicts activity on the first five sequences obtains 0.227 pooled correlation, and an oracle given each sequence’s mean activation obtains 0.288, both above the published GPT-4 simulator score of 0.160. The paper also asks whether these shortcuts change conclusions. A commercial typed-decision scorer, Jev, scores higher on stored snippets than on natural windows for 44 of 49 features, but the preregistered ranking test does not show clear feature reordering; Spearman correlation between the two rankings is 0.732. In the few fuzzing items where the center cue conflicts with the answer, LLM scorers rarely follow the cue. A small scorer trained on stored snippets loses 63% of its score on natural windows, while the same architecture trained without explanations reaches 83% of its held-out score. On natural windows, Jev scores 0.331 against 0.013 for shuffled explanations, clearing explanation-free position and sequence priors; an open 8B simulator also beats its shuffled control there, 0.194 versus 0.019.1a:T8e7,方法上，作者延续 representation engineering 的思路，但把对象从“拒答”这类近似二元门控行为，换成了连续的生成式修辞策略。具体做法是：先构造一个 1000 条的配对数据集，每个概念都要求模型分别给出 literal explanation 和 analogical explanation。数据来自 ELI5 相关社区问答，经过两轮 LLM 过滤与标注：先筛掉不适合做结构类比的意见类、政策类问题，再按 biology、chemistry、CS/technology、earth/environment、economics、engineering、history/society、physics 八个领域做近均匀采样。训练/验证/测试切分为 600/100/300。

在每一层，作者取最终 prompt token 的 residual activation，分别对 analogy 与 literal 两组求均值，做 difference-in-means，得到该层的 Analogy Vector。推理时在指定层把该向量按系数 α 加到残差流上，观察输出风格是否朝类比解释偏移。这里有两个技术点比较关键。第一，作者系统比较了七组 matched base/instruct 模型，覆盖 Gemma-2、Llama-3.1、Qwen-2.5，参数规模 2B 到 32B，用同一套提取与干预流程来比较“方向是否存在”和“方向是否可用”。第二，他们发现高系数干预会出现 mode collapse，原因不是语义方向本身失效，而是 lexical entanglement：向量里混入了明显的词汇触发成分，尤其是 “Imagine” token 的 unembedding 方向约占总范数的 10%，会把模型硬推向模板化开头。为此作者把向量投影到与该 token 方向正交的子空间，范数只下降 1%–5%，说明主体信号仍是语义性的，而不是单纯的词触发。

更有意思的是作者提出的“behavioral accessibility”框架。不是只看能否提取出方向，而是看该方向注入后，模型分布是否真的发生可传播、可利用的变化。这里他们用两类信号来刻画：一类是输出质量和 analogy score 的提升；另一类是干预前后 token 分布的 KL divergence。后者被作者解释为 wiring diagnostic：即使模型最终没生成高质量类比，只要 KL 明显上升，说明这条方向至少接入了生成路径；如果 KL 很低，则更像是几何上有痕迹，但没有被行为电路有效读取。1b:T926,The method follows the representation-engineering line of work, but moves from near-binary behaviors such as refusal to a continuous generative style. The authors build a paired dataset of 1,000 concepts, each eliciting both a literal explanation and an analogical explanation. The source is ELI5-style community QA data, filtered in two stages with an external LLM: first to keep only mechanism-like concepts suitable for structural analogy, then to assign each item to one of eight domains for near-balanced sampling. The final split is 600/100/300 for train/validation/test.

For each layer, they collect the residual activation at the final prompt token and compute a difference-in-means vector between analogy and literal examples. This yields a layer-wise Analogy Vector. At inference time, they add the vector with coefficient alpha to the residual stream before the attention and MLP blocks, then measure whether the output shifts toward analogical explanation style.

Two technical details matter. First, they run the same extraction-and-steering pipeline on seven matched base/instruct pairs from Gemma-2, Llama-3.1, and Qwen-2.5, spanning 2B to 32B parameters. This lets them compare not just whether the direction exists, but whether it is behaviorally usable. Second, they identify a concrete failure mode at high steering strength: lexical entanglement. The vector partially aligns with the unembedding direction of the token “Imagine,” which accounts for about 10% of the vector norm and can force templated generations. They remove this by orthogonalizing the steering vector against that token direction. The norm drops by only 1%–5%, suggesting the main signal is semantic rather than lexical.

The broader contribution is the behavioral accessibility framework. The paper argues that extracting a direction is not enough; one must test whether the intervention actually enters the model’s generation circuitry. To measure this, they use both output-side quality metrics and KL divergence between steered and unsteered token distributions. They interpret KL as a wiring diagnostic: even if the final generations are not yet good analogies, a substantial KL shift indicates that the direction is connected to the decoding pathway. Low KL suggests a representation that is geometrically present but behaviorally inert.1c:T5d8,The central empirical result is that the Analogy Vector can be extracted from every base model tested, but accessibility varies continuously with instruction exposure rather than following a clean base-versus-instruct split. Gemma-2 base models, where pretraining and instruction tuning are more cleanly separated, show flat steering landscapes and much lower KL divergence than their instruct counterparts. The direction is there, but it barely moves behavior. Qwen-2.5 base models, where instruction-like data is more entangled with pretraining, already show partial accessibility. Llama-3.1 sits between these two regimes.

In the lesion path, the authors first train an aphasia subtype classifier on clinical data from AphasiaBank. The classifier labels generated text as resembling different aphasia profiles, such as Broca-like or Wernicke-like behavior. They then systematically ablate attention heads in frozen LLMs and use the classifier to identify which ablations induce which subtype-like deficits. The key point is that the intervention is not evaluated only by generic quality drop, but by the type of linguistic impairment it produces. This lets the authors associate some language functions, such as grammatical fluency or semantic coherence, with relatively discrete structural circuits.

In the steering path, they compute subtype-specific intervention vectors from residual-stream activation differences between processing human aphasic transcripts and healthy-control transcripts. Adding these vectors at selected layers during generation pushes the intact model toward the corresponding aphasia profile. They also test whether subtracting such directions can partially reverse lesion-induced deficits. Conceptually, lesioning targets structural hardware, while steering targets representational state.

The most important result is not a simplistic localization story. The paper finds a dual picture: some language functions can indeed be induced or disrupted through relatively specific circuits and directions, but those functions are also strongly entangled. Different aphasia subtypes share substantial overlap in their lesion-sensitive components, and their steering vectors have high cosine similarity. So the internal organization looks neither cleanly modular nor uniformly diffuse. It looks like a densely distributed system with vulnerable critical substructures.

A notable design choice is the use of a clinically informed classifier as the main diagnostic interface instead of standard LM metrics. The benefit is a structured taxonomy of degradation types.
