9:T43d,The framework separates three axes of inference-time scaling. Reasoning scaling spends more tokens before producing an answer. Sampling scaling generates more attempts and selects one. Interaction scaling alternates between proposal, external observation, and revision. The paper defines grounded feedback as observations from an instrument that measures the artifact’s actual behavior or form: running tests, rendering a page and measuring element positions, querying an external source, or measuring a layout. It also defines grounded evaluation as using such observations as the score. A coverage principle ties the two together: feedback helps only for defects that the instrument can observe. This explains why a linter may be grounded but ineffective for behavioral bugs, and why a screenshot-reading VLM can fail as a layout judge. The screenshot may crop away overflow, and small overlaps may be below the judge’s effective visual acuity. The contribution is not another agent loop; it is a sharper account of what kind of feedback and metric make interaction scaling real.a:T411,The experiments cover seven artifact-producing modalities: code, web pages, slides, figures, animations, video edits, and research reports. A dense academic-figure case shows the evaluation problem clearly: a standard screenshot VLM judge marks 14 of 15 single-shot renders as perfect, while direct measurement finds only 3 clean outputs.
