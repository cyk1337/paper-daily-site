17:T55c,The hyper-epoch pretraining (q0) framework consists of three core primitives:

1. **Cyclic Parallel Schedule**: Inspired by Fast Geometric Ensembling (FGE), short training cycles with anti-correlated learning rate and weight decay (LR rises as WD falls and vice versa) collect many model snapshots along a small number of parallel trajectories. Only a few trajectories are trained from scratch with different random initializations; the rest are generated through cyclic exploration, dramatically reducing exploration cost.

2. **Chain Distillation**: Each new snapshot is trained against its predecessor as a teacher, in addition to the standard objective. This allows the population's capability to compound across training, rather than producing models of similar individual quality as in traditional ensembling.

3. **Learned Weighting at Inference**: Weights for each model are learned on a held-out fitness set, replacing simple uniform averaging. The weight assignment exhibits non-trivial behavior: snapshots that are not individually optimal under validation loss but complementary for ensembling receive substantial weight.

Key differences from traditional ensembling: naive ensembling trains every model from scratch (wastes compute), produces models of homogeneous quality, and uses uniform weighting (ignoring individual differences). q0 addresses all
