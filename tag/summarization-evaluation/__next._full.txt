18:T6bc,The main technical contribution is an analysis-ready perturbation framework plus a mechanistic probing pipeline. Existing adversarial summarization datasets are usually too coarse for causal tracing because they modify text at the sentence or document level. The authors instead build paired clean/corrupt summaries with explicit token-level modification maps. They define eight localized perturbation types spanning two quality axes, Readability and Adequacy, while controlling perturbation intensity through a parameter k and preserving exact token positions of edits.

Using this setup, they analyze two judge models: Themis, built on Llama-3-8B, and Prometheus, built on Mistral-7B. The experiments combine causal tracing, logit-lens vocabulary projection, and attention-head knockout. The central finding is a two-stage internal evaluation process. Lower layers, especially attention modules, are mainly involved in defect identification and signal routing. Upper-layer MLPs integrate those signals into a final rating, with the score becoming sharply encoded in the residual stream at a late layer. The paper also shows criterion-specific attention behavior: readability errors spread across nearby syntactic context, while adequacy-related perturbations stay more localized around the modified tokens.

Another useful result is the comparison with the base model. Fine-tuning does not appear to create the evaluation pipeline from scratch. The base Llama-3-8B already exhibits a routing-like substrate and late-layer score crystallization. Judge fine-tuning mainly modifies that substrate by suppressing low-layer MLP contribution at the last position and moving the crystallization point earlier by about two layers.19:T4b2,The most important results are mechanistic. Both judges show a consistent stage split: below roughly layer 15, attention dominates error detection and routing; around layers 25 to 26, the final rating becomes sharply represented. Themis writes the score in a more concentrated top-layer step, while Prometheus distributes the write across mid-to-late layers and applies an opposite-direction correction at the final layer. The comparison to base Llama-3-8B is also notable: the base model already contains much of the routing structure, and judge fine-tuning mainly reshapes information flow rather than inventing a new circuit.

The perturbation analysis further shows that different error types induce different attention patterns. Readability defects recruit broader local context, whereas adequacy defects remain more tightly centered on the perturbed tokens. This suggests that criterion-specific supervision could be a sensible design direction for future evaluator training.
