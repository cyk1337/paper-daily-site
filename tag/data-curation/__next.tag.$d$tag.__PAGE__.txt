The model does not remove full attention entirely; instead, it inserts a small number of standard softmax MHA layers periodically for explicit token-token interaction and global routing. For image-language data, the pipeline filters low-quality images, generates multiple caption candidates, and then selects the best one using relative image-text scoring plus textual informativeness ranking. For video-language data, it decomposes raw videos into compact clips, samples representative frames, filters clips by semantic and motion consistency, and fuses local frame details with global temporal semantics into better captions. The key idea is not just “more cleaning,” but converting noisy image-text and video-text corpora into more grounded and discriminative supervision.

Instead of pretraining at a fixed resolution and later adapting to dynamic resolution, TuringViT is trained with variable resolutions and aspect ratios from the start. To keep this affordable, the first 80% of training caps the maximum image size at 512, and the later stage removes that cap and introduces larger images and video data. The logic is straightforward: learn robust generic visual representations early, then spend the expensive part of the budget on high-resolution and temporal modeling later.
