9:T81e,The method has two main layers. The first is architectural progression. The authors first define Tuna-R: remove the VAE, keep a pretrained representation encoder, encode images into visual tokens, and feed them into an LLM decoder. On the generation side, they move away from latent-space generation and use pixel-space flow matching with an x-prediction objective. Tuna-R is not the final answer; it is a controlled intermediate model that isolates the role of the encoder.

The second layer is Tuna-2 itself. Here the representation encoder is removed as well. Images or videos are split into patches and embedded with simple patch embedding layers, then processed together with text tokens by a single transformer decoder. Both understanding and generation are handled within the same token stream and the same backbone, making the model genuinely end-to-end from raw pixels.

What makes this trainable is the masking-based visual feature learning scheme. Direct pixel-space unified modeling is harder than latent-space modeling, and the model can otherwise overfit to low-level local statistics instead of learning robust visual semantics. The masking mechanism introduces a feature-learning bias that encourages the model to recover or model semantically meaningful visual structure under partial observation. In effect, it injects a masked-visual-modeling style signal into unified pixel-space pretraining, improving stability and representation quality.

Conceptually, Tuna-2 differs from the dominant “strong vision encoder plus LLM” recipe. It is closer to a monolithic decoder-only multimodal model where perception is not outsourced to a separately pretrained encoder. Compared with VAE-latent generation, it avoids the information bottleneck and the mismatch between understanding and generation objectives. Compared with decoupled encoder designs, it removes module boundaries and objective misalignment. Compared with shared-encoder unified models, it goes one step further by discarding external visual priors in exchange for stronger end-to-end optimization.a:T70a,The most useful part of the empirical section is not a single benchmark win, but the controlled comparison between Tuna-R and Tuna-2. The central finding is dynamic: at earlier stages of training, the encoder-based Tuna-R is often easier to optimize because the pretrained visual encoder provides a strong starting representation. But as multimodal pretraining continues, Tuna-2 catches up on generation and consistently surpasses Tuna-R on understanding, with the clearest gains on fine-grained and perception-intensive tasks. That pattern matters more than any one leaderboard number. It suggests that encoder-free models are harder initially, but not fundamentally weaker, and may have a better asymptotic regime for perception.

The paper reports top-tier performance across a broad set of multimodal understanding and generation benchmarks. On the understanding side, the emphasis is on fine-grained perception, local detail recognition, and visually demanding QA. On the generation side, pixel-space flow matching remains competitive even after removing the VAE, which is an important result by itself. The ablations are also substantial. They examine whether the representation encoder is necessary, whether masking-based feature learning is critical, how the two architectures differ in training dynamics, and at what stage the encoder-free model turns from a liability into an advantage.
