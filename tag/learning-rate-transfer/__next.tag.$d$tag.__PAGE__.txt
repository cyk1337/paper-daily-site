5:T9ba,方法核心是：作者不接受 μP 在 nGPT 上默认成立，而是先测量训练中的 weight-activation alignment，再据此构造新的缩放律。全文的逻辑大致分三步。

第一步，诊断原始 nGPT 为什么没有 learning-rate transfer。作者检查了 μP 一类推导通常依赖的矩阵—向量对齐假设，发现 nGPT 中权重与激活既不是完全独立，也不是强对齐，更接近 Everett et al. 提出的“mid alignment”状态。这个判断很关键，因为学习率随宽度如何缩放，本质上取决于更新量、前向信号和梯度在宽度极限下的相对量级；一旦对齐结构变了，μP 的宽度缩放就不再是自然答案。

第二步，在 mid alignment 假设下重新推导宽度方向的参数化。作者基于 alignment exponent 的经验拟合与量纲分析，给出 νGPT 的参数缩放和学习率转移规则。可以把它理解为：保留 nGPT 的归一化训练优点，但把各类权重、残差分支、可训练尺度参数的宽度依赖重新整理，使不同宽度下每层的有效更新幅度更一致。文中 figure 2 是这一部分的关键，展示了不同张量族的对齐指数与所支持的缩放关系。

第三步，把 transfer 从 width 扩展到 depth 和 token horizon。深度方面，作者采用了近期 residual scaling 文献里更偏经验但已较稳定的做法，即使用适合深层 transfer 的 residual-depth parameterization，核心是让每个 block 对主干状态的增量随层数按合适指数衰减，从而避免深层网络在放大时出现前向爆炸、更新失衡或层内线性化过强的问题。文中明确站在 α_depth=1 这一支上，理由不是理论最优，而是对 realistic Transformer block 的可迁移性和稳定性更可靠。token horizon 方面，作者实测最优学习率随训练 token 数按 tokens^{-1/3} 缩放，这与 Bjorck et al. 在非归一化 Transformer 上的观察一致。这个结果的含义是：即便模型结构换成 nGPT，训练预算变长时学习率 annealing 的主导尺度仍然近似遵循同一类 compute-aware law。

从与已有方法的区别看，νGPT 不是简单给 nGPT 加一个 Depth-μP 标签，也不是把 μP 原封不动搬过来。它的关键区别有两点：一是承认并利用 nGPT 中不同于标准 Transformer 的对齐统计；二是把 width、depth、token horizon 的 transfer 规则联合校准，而不是只证明单一维度上的宽度迁移。6:T859,The central move is to reject a blind application of μP and instead measure the alignment structure that actually appears in nGPT training. The paper proceeds in three stages.

First, it diagnoses why vanilla nGPT lacks LR transfer. Standard μP-style derivations rely on specific matrix–vector alignment assumptions. The authors empirically check these assumptions in nGPT and find that the observed regime is neither fully aligned nor fully unaligned. It is closer to the “mid-alignment” regime discussed by Everett et al. This matters because the correct width scaling of updates depends on how forward activations, gradients, and parameter updates co-scale with width.

Second, under this empirically supported mid-alignment assumption, they derive a new width parameterization and LR transfer rule, which they call νGPT. Intuitively, νGPT keeps the optimization benefits of nGPT’s normalization-heavy design, but rescales parameter families and trainable gain terms so that effective update magnitudes remain comparable across widths. Figure 2 is the key technical anchor here: it links measured alignment exponents to the proposed scaling prescription.

Third, they extend transfer beyond width. For depth, they adopt a residual-depth scaling rule aligned with recent work arguing that realistic deep Transformer blocks need a depth parameterization that preserves stability and transfer, especially when blocks contain internal matrix multiplications. The paper sides with the α_depth = 1 branch for practical transfer and stability. For token horizon, they empirically find that the optimal learning rate scales as tokens^{-1/3}, consistent with prior observations in unnormalized Transformers. This suggests that even in normalized models, the dominant LR adjustment with longer training budgets follows a similar compute-aware law.

Relative to prior work, νGPT is not just “μP for nGPT.” Its novelty is twofold: it explicitly adapts to the alignment statistics induced by normalized Transformers, and it jointly calibrates transfer across width, depth, and token horizon rather than validating only one axis.7:T6a9,The main empirical claim is clear: νGPT improves learning-rate transfer across width, depth, and token horizon without sacrificing the performance of a well-tuned nGPT baseline. Figure 1 is the headline result: νGPT matches the original nGPT in final performance while making hyperparameters found at small scale reusable at larger scale. Figure 7 shows that width transfer is more reliable under νGPT than under μP, supporting the mid-alignment-based derivation. Figure 3 focuses on depth and shows improved stability for deeper models under the proposed depth scaling.

The experiments appear to have four important components. First, alignment measurement: the paper does not only theorize, it directly measures alignment exponents during training and uses them to justify the new scaling rules. Second, width transfer: it compares original nGPT, μP-style scaling, and νGPT across multiple hidden dimensions while reusing learning rates tuned on smaller models. The reported pattern is that νGPT keeps the optimal LR region more consistent across widths. Third, depth transfer: the same LR can be reused across different layer counts with better stability under νGPT. Fourth, token-horizon transfer: the best LR follows an approximately tokens^{-1/3} law as training length increases.

Then go directly to Sections 3 and 3.1, where νGPT is defined. Do not just follow the formulas. Track which alignment assumptions are changed relative to μP and why each parameter family gets its specific scaling.

Next, study Figure 2 carefully. It is the empirical foundation of the whole paper. If the measured alignment regime is not convincing, the rest becomes much weaker. Then read Figures 3 and 7 for depth and width transfer. The right question is not only whether νGPT improves the best point, but whether it widens the usable LR region and reduces catastrophic instability under mismatch.

Finish with the token-horizon section and check how robust the tokens^{-1/3} fit is. If time is limited, the main text plus the key figures should take about 45–60 minutes.
