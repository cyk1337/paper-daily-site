18:T7d1,REAL-Q changes both the objective and the solver.

On the objective side, it replaces the standard layer-local reconstruction MSE with an end-to-end-aligned surrogate called Aggregated Fisher MSE. This surrogate is derived from a second-order Taylor expansion of the global KL divergence at the current transformer block output. Unlike grouped approximations that discard cross-channel interactions, REAL-Q keeps the full aggregated Fisher matrix, so output-channel coupling is preserved. To keep the computation manageable, the expansion point is chosen so that backpropagation only needs to traverse at most two adjacent blocks rather than the full network. The method also introduces a loss sliding window to smooth transitions across neighboring blocks, reducing discontinuities in the optimization landscape during sequential block-wise quantization.

On the optimization side, the paper argues that the core limitation of the GPTQ lineage is its dependence on a closed-form column-wise solver. That requirement forces heavy Hessian compression and then freezes the approximation for the whole layer. REAL-Q instead uses Dynamic Block-wise Gradient Descent. It still follows a sequential column-wise quantization pipeline, but after each column block of 128 columns it inserts one Adam update. The gradient is recomputed online against the actual partially quantized model state, so the method can correct accumulated upstream errors and adapt to the shifted loss landscape as quantization progresses. The authors describe this as increasing the correction granularity from per-layer to per-column-block, roughly two orders of magnitude finer. Adam’s diagonal preconditioning provides an implicit second-order effect without explicit Hessian inversion.

Importantly, REAL-Q is not a complete departure from GPTQ-style pipelines. It is a practical modification that keeps the sequential structure while making both the loss and the correction mechanism more faithful to the end-to-end objective.19:T45e,The experiments cover LLaMA-3.1 8B and 70B, and Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 32B. The main metric is KL divergence under weight-only W4A16 quantization, compared against GPTQ and more recent variants. The headline result is that REAL-Q achieves the lowest KL on every evaluated model, with up to about 49% KL reduction on Qwen3-1.7B at W4A16.

That result supports two claims: the end-to-end-aligned Fisher-based objective is better than plain layer-local reconstruction loss, and dynamic online updates help mitigate the error accumulation that appears in sequential quantization.

The most important ablations are likely the contribution of Aggregated Fisher MSE over standard reconstruction losses, the effect of the loss sliding window on stability across block boundaries, and the role of Block-GD frequency and optimizer choice. The excerpt does not include the full numeric tables, but those ablations are where the paper’s argument really stands or falls.
