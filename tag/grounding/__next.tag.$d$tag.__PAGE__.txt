5:T43d,The framework separates three axes of inference-time scaling. Reasoning scaling spends more tokens before producing an answer. Sampling scaling generates more attempts and selects one. Interaction scaling alternates between proposal, external observation, and revision. The paper defines grounded feedback as observations from an instrument that measures the artifact’s actual behavior or form: running tests, rendering a page and measuring element positions, querying an external source, or measuring a layout. It also defines grounded evaluation as using such observations as the score. A coverage principle ties the two together: feedback helps only for defects that the instrument can observe. This explains why a linter may be grounded but ineffective for behavioral bugs, and why a screenshot-reading VLM can fail as a layout judge. The screenshot may crop away overflow, and small overlaps may be below the judge’s effective visual acuity. The contribution is not another agent loop; it is a sharper account of what kind of feedback and metric make interaction scaling real.6:T411,The experiments cover seven artifact-producing modalities: code, web pages, slides, figures, animations, video edits, and research reports. Grounded execution feedback raises two hard coding suites to a 100% pass rate. Grounded geometry feedback removes 40% to 74% of measured layout defects across four visual modalities. A dense academic-figure case shows the evaluation problem clearly: a standard screenshot VLM judge marks 14 of 15 single-shot renders as perfect, while direct measurement finds only 3 clean outputs. Under equal token budgets, reasoning-only and best-of-N saturate; best-of-N saturates even with an oracle verifier, while interaction methods continue improving. The proposer-reviewer harness reaches the gains with lower token cost and zero reported seed variance. Instrument ablations support the grounding thesis: execution feedback beats critique for behavioral bugs, a linter adds little when it observes the wrong property, and a screenshot VLM reviewer worsens slide geometry while a geometry reviewer repairs it.7:T91a,The study combines architecture-aware probing with several mechanistic interventions. The authors analyze two representative VLM families. LLaVA-1.5 serves as a clean baseline because CLIP ViT-L/14 patch tokens are mapped one-to-one into the LLM space through a small MLP. InternVL-3.5 adds two complications: token compression via pixel shuffle and dynamic multi-view processing with a global thumbnail plus local high-resolution tiles. This contrast lets the paper test whether the same localization mechanism survives under compression and multi-view fusion.

The dataset is a curated subset of COCO val, not raw COCO. The authors first correct annotation issues using prior semi-automatic procedures, then filter out tiny or dominant objects, low-resolution images, and ambiguous cases. The final set contains 3,560 images and 6,403 object annotations. They also build auxiliary controls to reduce context-only hallucinated detections. This matters because mechanistic claims are fragile under noisy labels.

The main tools are:
1. Token ablations to identify which visual tokens causally support class prediction and box prediction.
2. Controlled perturbations that preserve object extent while scrambling internal semantic arrangement, or alter context outside the object, to test whether localization depends on internal semantic layout or mainly on token support boundaries.
3. Positional decoding from intermediate representations to see how a 1D token sequence still carries enough signal for the LLM to reconstruct an approximate 2D grid.
4. Attention knockout to rank heads by causal importance for classification and localization.
5. Causal mediation analysis to separate heads that directly affect localization from heads whose effect is mediated through classification.

The central claim is a containerization mechanism: object-aligned tokens define a spatial container, and the semantic arrangement inside that container matters surprisingly little for the predicted box. In InternVL, the global view carries most of the spatial signal, while local crops mainly refine semantics and help small-object classification. The paper also argues for a sequential dependency: localization-critical processing depends on classification-critical heads, even though the dominant heads for the two tasks are largely distinct.8:T692,The most useful results are intervention-based rather than benchmark-based. The analysis covers LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B, and InternVL-3.5 8B, on the curated COCO-derived set with 3,560 images and 6,403 object instances.

The main empirical findings are:
1. Localization information is genuinely encoded in visual tokens. Ablating object-aligned tokens strongly disrupts box prediction, while ablating irrelevant regions has much smaller effect.
2. Scrambling semantics inside an object while preserving its token support leaves localization relatively stable. This is the key evidence for the containerization claim.
3. In InternVL, removing the global thumbnail hurts localization more than removing local high-resolution tiles, whereas local tiles contribute more to fine-grained recognition and small-object classification. This suggests a division of labor: global view for spatial frame, local view for semantic refinement.
4. Positional decoding shows that the LLM can recover an approximate 2D layout from the linearized token stream using residual positional structure and strong corner anchors, even without an explicit preserved 2D grid.
5. Only a very small number of attention heads carry strong causal effects. In LLaVA, these heads appear mostly in early-to-mid layers; in InternVL, more in mid-to-late layers. Classification and localization heads are not the same, but localization causally depends on classification-relevant heads, supporting a staged pipeline of identify first, ground later.

The paper is not about pushing a task metric by a large margin, so the important evidence is the consistency of these trends across interventions, layers, and architectures.9:T487,Read it as a mechanistic case study, not as a new VLM method. Four parts deserve attention.

First, inspect the architecture setup carefully. The contrast between LLaVA and InternVL explains why head locations and view roles differ later.

Second, focus on the token ablation and controlled perturbation figures. These are the core evidence for the containerization hypothesis. Pay attention to how the paper isolates object extent, internal semantic arrangement, and background context.

Third, read the global-versus-local analysis in InternVL if you care about multimodal pretraining design. It gives a concrete answer to what high-resolution tiles are actually buying.

Fourth, study the attention knockout and mediation analysis. The important point is not merely that a few heads matter, but how classification-critical and localization-critical heads are distributed across layers and how the causal dependency is established.

About 30 to 45 minutes is enough for a solid skim. If the goal is pretraining insight, the main text figures and experimental setup are sufficient. The appendix matters mainly for data curation details and robustness checks.a:T4cb,The main contribution is a benchmark and metric suite. On the data side, the authors start from more than 100 million raw PDFs, down-select roughly 250k candidates through stratified sampling, then apply a two-stage MLLM-based annotation process for domain and subcategory classification. The final benchmark contains 711 documents spanning 7 domains, 30 subcategories, and 2 languages, with 1,897 questions and an average document length of 40.6 pages.

For annotation, they build an automated pipeline combining document parsers and MLLMs to generate questions, answers, and fine-grained evidence regions. The goal is scalability and consistency, since manual box-level annotation over long PDFs is expensive and noisy. On the evaluation side, they introduce traceability metrics. The central one is Strict Attributed Accuracy, which only gives credit when both the answer and the cited evidence are correct. Additional metrics include Recall for evidence coverage and Relevance for logical alignment between cited regions and the answer.

They draw from a shared resource pool; if their combined purchases exceed capacity, an overdraw occurs and neither side receives reward. Before each simultaneous purchase decision, agents exchange up to five cheap-talk messages. The game is repeated over four rounds, with conditions that vary whether the partner and project scenario stay fixed or change. This setup separates individual planning from interactive coordination: if an oracle or single-agent planner can identify the optimal allocation but the dialogue pair fails, the bottleneck is likely in grounding and commitment formation.
