18:T585,The paper is organized around three ideas. First is hardware-model co-design. MI350X offers about 1.6x the HBM capacity of B200, and the authors do not treat migration as a same-model, same-batch portability exercise. Instead, they redesign the training setup to exploit the extra memory: larger per-GPU batch sizes, less activation checkpointing, rebalanced data/model parallelism, and efficient embedding re-sharding. Importantly, the extra HBM is also spent on model quality, such as longer inputs, larger hidden sizes, and larger embedding tables, not only on throughput. Second is the analytical performance model Origami. DLRM workloads involve fragmented GEMM shapes, where offline autotuning is expensive and often brittle. Origami predicts tile configurations directly, reportedly in microseconds, and reaches 93% accuracy relative to exhaustive autotuning. The paper emphasizes that the model extends to multi-chiplet architectures, which is particularly relevant for AMD hardware. Third is an agentic workflow for kernel optimization. The authors codify microarchitecture-aware tuning steps such as configuration selection, PID swizzling, occupancy hints, specialized instruction usage, and tree-reduction rewrites for atomic-heavy kernels. The key point is not the use of an agent by itself, but the conversion of expert-only kernel-porting knowledge into a structured and partially automated process.19:T4a4,The experiments are mainly on Meta production DLRMs. End to end, MI350X reaches a geometric-mean throughput of 101% relative to the B200 baseline across a representative set of production models, indicating rough parity after substantial optimization. The hardware-model co-design changes deliver about a 20% training speedup together with more than 0.1% improvement in normalized entropy, showing that larger HBM can be converted into both efficiency and model-quality gains. Origami predicts near-optimal kernel configurations with 93% accuracy relative to exhaustive autotuning, while making decisions in microseconds. The agentic workflow yields roughly 1.6x to 2x speedups across handwritten and compiler-generated kernels, and the tree-reduction approach for atomic bottlenecks reaches up to 4x on custom kernels. The paper is also candid about remaining gaps: MI350X GEMM performance still lags in some cases because of emulated TF32 overhead and the lower maturity of hipBLAS versus cuBLAS on irregular matrix shapes.
