18:T7ec,The paper introduces a unified framework of attention-indexed models, where multi-layer and multi-head attention-only architectures are expressed through finitely many quadratic token interactions of the form 1/sqrt(d) x_i^T S_ij x_k, with S_ij being d-by-d extensive-rank matrices. The technical contribution has two main parts.

First, it separates the static and dynamic descriptions. In the high-dimensional limit d→∞, the population loss is shown to depend on only finitely many trace-type order parameters, even though the original parameter space is Θ(d^2). So the loss landscape admits a finite-dimensional macroscopic description. However, online SGD is richer: for both tied and untied factorizations, the updates generate an infinite hierarchy of joint matrix moments, and no fixed finite set closes the dynamics in general. The authors then prove that the empirical trajectory converges to a deterministic infinite-dimensional ODE, and that finite-order truncations approximate this limiting dynamics with exponentially decaying error. This is the key methodological result.

Second, the paper analyzes parameterization-induced implicit bias by comparing three representations of the same effective attention: direct optimization of S, tied factorization S=WW^T, and untied factorization S=UV^T. Direct optimization suffers from an information-exponent bottleneck: when first-order correlation vanishes, SGD can remain trapped in an uninformative phase on the d^2 log d sample scale. In the tied case, the PSD structure provides an automatic symmetry-breaking mechanism. Under a mild non-degeneracy condition, this enables weak recovery at Θ(d^2 log d) samples. In the untied case, the mechanism is different: there is a fast-slow decomposition. Quantities such as 1/sqrt(d) Tr(UV^T) evolve on a fast timescale and create a boundary layer; the slower matrix-moment dynamics then proceed from the state selected by that fast transient. Weak recovery occurs only if this early shift breaks the relevant symmetry.19:T5e8,The empirical component is mainly numerical validation of the theory rather than large-scale model benchmarking. The important results are threefold.

First, the paper shows that finite-order truncations of the infinite moment hierarchy provide accurate approximations, with error decreasing rapidly as the truncation order increases. This supports the practical tractability of the otherwise infinite-dimensional ODE description.

Second, it compares the three parameterizations under uninformative initialization. Direct optimization of S remains bottlenecked when first-order correlation is absent. The tied PSD factorization achieves weak recovery on the Θ(d^2 log d) sample scale. The untied factorization can either recover or remain uninformative, depending on which symmetry branch is selected during the fast boundary-layer dynamics. The important takeaway is the difference in phase behavior and sample complexity, not a single benchmark number.

Third, numerical trajectories match the theoretical ODE predictions, indicating that the high-dimensional limit is not merely formal. The paper also highlights a sharp distinction from finite-rank sequence-indexed models, where a finite set of order parameters often closes the dynamics; here it does not.

The provided full-text excerpt does not include the complete tables or all numerical settings, so it would be unsafe to quote more exact numbers. The most useful figures are likely the phase diagrams, trajectory plots, and truncation-error curves.1a:T463,For a skim, focus on three things.

First, read the introduction and theorem statements to fix the two central questions: what the correct macroscopic dynamics is, and how parameterization changes learnability. Without that, the formulas are easy to lose track of.

Second, read the model setup and the high-dimensional limit section carefully enough to understand why the population loss is finite-dimensional while online SGD generates an infinite hierarchy of moments. That contrast is the structural core of the paper.

Third, compare the tied and untied sections. In the tied case, the key idea is symmetry breaking induced by the PSD factorization. In the untied case, the key idea is fast-slow dynamics and boundary-layer state selection. Once those two mechanisms are clear, most of the paper’s message is already in hand.

If time is limited, 45–60 minutes on the introduction, setup, main theorems, and the tied/untied conclusion figures should be enough. A deeper read should then go into the well-posedness of the infinite-dimensional ODE, the exponential truncation bound, and the weak-recovery conditions.1b:T467,The baseline objective interpolates standard next-token cross-entropy with a KL term between temperature-scaled teacher and student next-token distributions. The paper studies both forward KL and reverse KL, varying teacher size and the interpolation coefficient. The central analysis uses teacher predictive entropy as a diagnostic. Low-entropy teacher predictions tend to appear on procedural or highly constrained tokens, where the teacher distribution is sharp and informative. High-entropy predictions are common on unstructured web text and factual continuations with many plausible next tokens. In high-entropy regions, KD reduces the relative force of the one-hot ground-truth token compared with NTP, so it can slow the learning of facts that the student has not yet acquired. Switch Distillation follows directly from this analysis: use KD on low-entropy tokens and fall back to NTP on high-entropy tokens. The routing signal comes from the same teacher logits already needed for KD, so the method adds little overhead and differs from global fixes such as tuning alpha, changing KL direction, or scaling the teacher.1c:T466,The controlled experiments use the OLMo-2 ecosystem, with 1B students and larger 7B/13B teachers, including instruction-tuned teachers. In pre-training, forward-KL distillation improves reasoning and factual recall over vanilla NTP. In mid-training, increasing distillation strength generally improves reasoning but hurts factual recall relative to NTP. This pattern is robust across teacher sizes, KL directions, and interpolation coefficients; no conventional KD objective Pareto-dominates NTP in mid-training. The entropy analysis supports the mechanism: teacher entropy varies strongly by data type, lower entropy correlates with higher-quality supervision, and the facts not yet learned at the start of mid-training tend to receive weaker teacher supervision. Switch Distillation improves the tradeoff. With an OLMo-2 7B Instruct teacher, it raises average reasoning by 71% and knowledge/commonsense by 19% over NTP, while reducing factual recall by only about 1 percentage point. After post-training, the gains persist: reasoning remains 32% higher, knowledge tasks improve by 20%, and the factual recall gap disappears.1d:T459,1. **Spectral Probing Analysis**: At checkpoints along real training trajectories, the momentum buffer is decomposed into singular directions, and the loss-optimal step size along each direction is estimated on held-out data. The spectral profile is found to be highly anisotropic and stable: a 'volatile head' at the 'Edge-of-Stability' supports only small step sizes, while a 'tolerant bulk' permits much larger steps.
2. **Unified Perspective**: SGD allocates step size proportional to singular values (opposite to landscape suggestions); Adam's coordinate-wise rescaling mitigates but doesn't solve the mismatch; Muon's whitening to a uniform scale reallocates step mass from the head to the bulk.
3. **SAMuon Algorithm**: To exploit the bulk's capacity for larger steps, Spectral-Aware Muon (SAMuon) is proposed. It imposes a static, head-anchored prior on the whitened update. The full SAMuon fits a log-rank-linear shape via randomized SVD; the simplified SAMuon-lite uses power iteration to pin the head and uniformly amplifies the bulk. Both add no persistent state and negligible computational overhead.1e:T638,The setup has three main ingredients. First, the paper adopts the earlier observation that magnitudes of trained Transformer linear weights are well summarized by a Weibull distribution, with a shape parameter k that stays nearly constant across layers and models, around 1.2. That reduces the training-side movement to the scale parameter λ. Second, on the data side, the authors use bigram conditional entropy D = H(next|prev) as a training-free measure of local next-token predictability. To normalize against a matched randomization baseline, they define a predictability margin (Hr - D), where Hr is the entropy reached by matched-budget shuffling. Third, they fit an empirical law
λ^2 - λ0^2 = C0(η) + C1(η)(Hr - D)^0.59,
where learning rate η only affects the two coefficients C0 and C1. A notable claim is that the exponent 0.59 is not chosen by directly fitting the growth curve; it is tied to an independently measured data-side saturation exponent p ≈ 1.69 through 1/p.

The paper is careful about scope. It does not claim D is a universal currency across corpora. The law is established within a fixed corpus while moving along a corruption gradient that changes the next-token mapping. The authors also extend the analysis from whole-model λ to per-block λ. The Weibull shape k remains nearly uniform, but the scale response is heterogeneous and concentrated in feed-forward blocks. Because k is stable, the second moment <w^2> = λ^2 Γ(1 + 2/k) is approximately additive, which lets pooled λ^2 be approximated by a parameter-count-weighted average of blockwise λ^2.1f:T4b7,The main empirical results are fairly specific. The first derives Muon’s update direction from a constrained response model. The weight update is posed as a response of the parameter matrix to an applied force, with an output-side safety budget limiting how much the update may perturb the model’s outputs. Among all admissible responses, the preferred one is the maximally dissipative direction. Under the paper’s assumptions, this leads to the orthogonal polar factor of the momentum matrix, which is exactly the semi-orthogonalized direction used by Muon. This is a matrix-level geometric argument, not a coordinate-wise adaptive scaling argument. That is the key distinction from AdamW: AdamW relies on per-parameter second-moment preconditioning, while Muon chooses an update shape that is better behaved in spectral terms and less dominated by a few singular directions.

The second component derives a two-timescale momentum from stress relaxation. Standard momentum assumes a single exponential decay, which corresponds to a single relaxation mode. The paper argues that this is too crude for real training dynamics. This yields the Bi-Maxwell optimizer. Importantly, the rest of Muon is kept unchanged: the orthogonalization step stays, and the benchmark recipe is otherwise held fixed. That makes the causal claim cleaner, because the observed gains can be attributed mainly to the memory structure rather than to a bundle of recipe changes.

A central conceptual point is that the optimal memory length should not be constant over training. Early on, gradient directions change quickly, so long memory mixes in stale directions and hurts responsiveness. The theoretical setup is a controlled toy model. Each context is associated with a target distribution that is uniform over a random support set of size k. The support size k controls ambiguity. For real, non-uniform language distributions, ambiguity is measured by effective support size, exp(H(p)), which reduces to k in the uniform-support setting.

The authors then separate the curse into architectural and learning roots. On the architectural side, they show that more ambiguous target distributions require more storage capacity and larger embedding dimensions to be represented to a fixed accuracy. Intuitively, predicting one correct token is geometrically easier than assigning similarly high logits to many plausible tokens while separating them from the rest of the vocabulary. The paper studies both random embeddings and jointly optimized embeddings. The first shows degradation at fixed hidden dimension h as k grows. The second shows that even with optimal embeddings, the required representation dimension still increases with ambiguity.

On the learning side, the key issue is token-sampling noise. Training does not observe the full target distribution p directly. It observes one sampled token from p and updates with cross-entropy. For high-ambiguity contexts, a single sample reveals only one element of a larger support, so the stochastic gradient is a noisier estimator of the full distributional objective. The paper derives that fitting such targets to the same precision requires more optimization steps and more samples. The point is not that entropy makes the loss numerically larger. The point is that token-level supervision becomes a less efficient channel for learning the full conditional distribution as ambiguity increases.

Empirically, the paper proceeds in three stages.
