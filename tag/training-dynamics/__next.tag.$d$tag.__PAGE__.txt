First, it separates the static and dynamic descriptions. In the high-dimensional limit d→∞, the population loss is shown to depend on only finitely many trace-type order parameters, even though the original parameter space is Θ(d^2). So the loss landscape admits a finite-dimensional macroscopic description. However, online SGD is richer: for both tied and untied factorizations, the updates generate an infinite hierarchy of joint matrix moments, and no fixed finite set closes the dynamics in general. The authors then prove that the empirical trajectory converges to a deterministic infinite-dimensional ODE, and that finite-order truncations approximate this limiting dynamics with exponentially decaying error. This is the key methodological result.

Second, the paper analyzes parameterization-induced implicit bias by comparing three representations of the same effective attention: direct optimization of S, tied factorization S=WW^T, and untied factorization S=UV^T. Direct optimization suffers from an information-exponent bottleneck: when first-order correlation vanishes, SGD can remain trapped in an uninformative phase on the d^2 log d sample scale. In the tied case, the PSD structure provides an automatic symmetry-breaking mechanism. Under a mild non-degeneracy condition, this enables weak recovery at Θ(d^2 log d) samples. In the untied case, the mechanism is different: there is a fast-slow decomposition. Quantities such as 1/sqrt(d) Tr(UV^T) evolve on a fast timescale and create a boundary layer; the slower matrix-moment dynamics then proceed from the state selected by that fast transient. If time is short, focus on the main equation, the collapse figure, the held-out prediction result, the blockwise analysis, and the limitations.16:T863,The method has two main components. The first derives Muon’s update direction from a constrained response model. The weight update is posed as a response of the parameter matrix to an applied force, with an output-side safety budget limiting how much the update may perturb the model’s outputs. Among all admissible responses, the preferred one is the maximally dissipative direction. Under the paper’s assumptions, this leads to the orthogonal polar factor of the momentum matrix, which is exactly the semi-orthogonalized direction used by Muon. This is a matrix-level geometric argument, not a coordinate-wise adaptive scaling argument. That is the key distinction from AdamW: AdamW relies on per-parameter second-moment preconditioning, while Muon chooses an update shape that is better behaved in spectral terms and less dominated by a few singular directions.

The second component derives a two-timescale momentum from stress relaxation. Standard momentum assumes a single exponential decay, which corresponds to a single relaxation mode. The paper argues that this is too crude for real training dynamics. Borrowing from Maxwell-type viscoelastic models, it decomposes internal stress into a fast and a slow component, each tracked by its own EMA coefficient, and combines them into the momentum that is later semi-orthogonalized. This yields the Bi-Maxwell optimizer. Importantly, the rest of Muon is kept unchanged: the orthogonalization step stays, and the benchmark recipe is otherwise held fixed. That makes the causal claim cleaner, because the observed gains can be attributed mainly to the memory structure rather than to a bundle of recipe changes.

A central conceptual point is that the optimal memory length should not be constant over training. Early on, gradient directions change quickly, so long memory mixes in stale directions and hurts responsiveness. Later, directions evolve more slowly, so longer memory improves denoising and accumulates consistent signal. Bi-Maxwell does not explicitly schedule beta over time; instead, it uses two fixed timescales to approximate a training-dependent effective memory length.17:T773,The headline result is on the public modded-nanogpt optimizer speedrun benchmark. The absolute gain is modest, but on a benchmark that has already been heavily tuned by the community, a further improvement from changing only the momentum structure is meaningful.

The second important result is a read-only measurement study over 8 independent training trajectories, aimed at estimating the optimal momentum memory length during training. The measured trend matches the theory: the preferred memory is shorter early in training and becomes longer later. The value of this experiment is less about the exact numbers and more about turning a usually opaque hyperparameter into something that can be observed and discussed over the course of optimization.

- Forward sensitivity (transport): define q_t=\partial S_t/\partial \alpha for a source-strength parameter \alpha. The recurrence includes (a) injected source perturbations d_t (teacher-induced gradient changes) and (b) feedback through \partial G/\partial S because past perturbations alter w_t and therefore future gradients.
