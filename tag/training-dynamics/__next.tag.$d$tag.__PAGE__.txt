a:T61a,Training is modeled as a deterministic discrete-time dynamical system over a complete state S_t=(w_t,m_t,v_t,\tau_t), where w are parameters, m the first moment (momentum/Adam first-moment buffer), v the second moment, and \tau auxiliary deterministic state (clock/schedule).
- Forward sensitivity (transport): define q_t=\partial S_t/\partial \alpha for a source-strength parameter \alpha. The recurrence includes (a) injected source perturbations d_t (teacher-induced gradient changes) and (b) feedback through \partial G/\partial S because past perturbations alter w_t and therefore future gradients.
- Backward sensitivity (valuation): define a terminal observer O (in LLMs, a target-minus-reference candidate-string log-likelihood on a frozen prompt bank). Compute the costate p_T=(\partial O/\partial S_T)^\top and propagate it backward to obtain a per-step “future value” \lambda_t capturing how valuable a state perturbation at time t is for the final behavior.
- Exact transport–valuation identity: total behavioral change is expressed as a path integral pairing forward-propagated perturbations with backward-propagated values, making route dependence explicit.
- State surgery for causal identification: by transplanting components of the trainer state at a cut, they show the first moment m is the main carrier that continues to write source ancestry into weights after source removal. 作者把 curse 拆成两类来源。架构根因方面，结论是更高 ambiguity 的分布需要更大的“存储容量”和更高维的 embedding 才能在给定误差下被表示。直观上，k=1 时只需把一个 token 从其余 token 中分离出来；k>1 时需要同时为多个 token 保持相近高 logit，并与大量非 support token 拉开间隔，这对线性可分性和几何表示都更苛刻。论文分析了随机 embedding 与可联合优化 embedding 两种情形：前者展示在固定 h 下误差如何随 k 恶化，后者说明即便允许最优表示，embedding 维度仍需随 ambiguity 增长，否则无法同时容纳足够多彼此不同的高熵目标分布。

学习根因方面，作者分析了梯度和采样噪声。训练时我们并不直接看到目标分布 p，而是从 p 采样一个 token 做交叉熵更新。对高 ambiguity 上下文，单个样本只暴露 support 中一个 token，导致梯度对完整分布的估计方差更大；换句话说，同样一次 SGD 更新，对 k 大的分布提供的信息更少、更噪。论文进一步给出优化步数需求随 ambiguity 增长的理论结果：要把预测分布拟合到同等精度，需要更多样本和更多步数。这里的关键不是 label smoothing 那种人为加熵，而是真实条件分布的熵越高，token-level sampling 对分布学习越低效。

First, it turns an empirical complaint—models are poorly calibrated in uncertain contexts—into a mechanistic object of study with architectural and optimization roots. Second, it suggests that learning difficulty in pretraining is shaped not only by sequence length, knowledge content, or model size, but also by conditional entropy itself. It is a foundational diagnosis of AR LM learning dynamics, useful because it identifies where interventions might matter.d:Tb9e,The paper reframes autoregressive language modeling as context classification. A Transformer backbone maps a context to an embedding e, and the final linear head W plus softmax maps that embedding to a vocabulary distribution. The analysis first isolates this last layer while treating the backbone representations as given. That choice is deliberate: the output head is the only component that directly parameterizes the next-token distribution, and it also shapes the gradients flowing back into the backbone.

The theoretical setup is a controlled toy model. Each context is associated with a target distribution that is uniform over a random support set of size k. The support size k controls ambiguity. For real, non-uniform language distributions, ambiguity is measured by effective support size, exp(H(p)), which reduces to k in the uniform-support setting.

The authors then separate the curse into architectural and learning roots. On the architectural side, they show that more ambiguous target distributions require more storage capacity and larger embedding dimensions to be represented to a fixed accuracy. Intuitively, predicting one correct token is geometrically easier than assigning similarly high logits to many plausible tokens while separating them from the rest of the vocabulary. The paper studies both random embeddings and jointly optimized embeddings. The first shows degradation at fixed hidden dimension h as k grows. The second shows that even with optimal embeddings, the required representation dimension still increases with ambiguity.

On the learning side, the key issue is token-sampling noise. Training does not observe the full target distribution p directly. It observes one sampled token from p and updates with cross-entropy. For high-ambiguity contexts, a single sample reveals only one element of a larger support, so the stochastic gradient is a noisier estimator of the full distributional objective. The paper derives that fitting such targets to the same precision requires more optimization steps and more samples. The point is not that entropy makes the loss numerically larger. The point is that token-level supervision becomes a less efficient channel for learning the full conditional distribution as ambiguity increases.

Empirically, the paper proceeds in three stages. First, controlled synthetic experiments validate the predicted trends in representation error, required dimension, convergence speed, and gradient noise. Second, real-language-model analyses bucket contexts by ambiguity and show analogous signatures in training dynamics and distribution fitting. Third, the authors argue that scaling model size does not remove the phenomenon; it only shifts the regime where it becomes visible. Relative to prior work, the contribution is not a new uncertainty metric. It is the decomposition of the problem into output-layer geometry, sample complexity, and token-sampling noise, tied back to practical LM training.e:T628,The experimental contribution is mostly in the consistency of the observed trends rather than in a single benchmark number. In synthetic settings, the authors vary support size k and show that as k increases: (1) the error between predicted and true distributions rises at fixed head capacity and embedding dimension, (2) maintaining the same error requires larger hidden dimension h, (3) optimization slows down and needs more steps, and (4) sampled-token training induces higher gradient variance. They also compare random embeddings with jointly learned embeddings, showing that the problem is not merely poor representation learning but a genuine increase in geometric and statistical difficulty.

On real data, the paper replaces uniform support size with effective support size exp(H(p)) as the ambiguity measure. Contexts with higher ambiguity exhibit higher loss, slower fitting, and worse approximation of the full next-token distribution. Importantly, the gap is not only in top-1 behavior. It appears in probability allocation across plausible continuations. The same qualitative signatures remain visible in full language models rather than only isolated linear heads, which supports the claim that the simplified analysis captures a real bottleneck.

The quantitative setup explicitly revolves around support size k, hidden dimension h, optimization steps, and effective support size. For close reading, the most informative artifacts are the plots showing error versus k, convergence versus k, and gradient-noise statistics, rather than any single summary table.f:T67e,Read it in the order of problem setup, architectural roots, learning roots, then real-LM validation.

For each layer and checkpoint, an activation decomposition is fitted on a training split, using methods such as SVD, ICA, NMF, SemiNMF, or sparse dictionaries. The decomposition provides codes, a dictionary, and a reconstruction baseline. A conservative candidate width Kmax is estimated from training activations using a two-nearest-neighbor intrinsic-dimension estimate with clipping. On a calibration split, the method learns a sparse soft mask with temperature-controlled gates, optimized with Adam under a preservation objective plus a sparsity penalty. On the test split, the masked reconstruction is inserted back into the original model graph. Using the official Mamba implementation and the public Muon reference code, the authors compare a pure AdamW baseline against three hybrid assignments: Muon on the input projection group only, Muon on the output projection group only, and Muon on both groups, with all remaining parameters still trained by AdamW. This turns the question into a clean assignment study rather than a noisy optimizer bake-off.

Muon works by applying a fixed-step Newton-Schulz orthogonalization to matrix-valued momentum updates, approximating steepest descent under spectral norm geometry. AdamW, in contrast, is coordinate-wise adaptive. The paper goes beyond loss curves and tracks spectral diagnostics of the affected matrices: condition number, effective rank, and spectral norm. The goal is to test whether any optimization gain is accompanied by systematic spectral changes.

The main technical takeaway is that Muon is not uniformly helpful inside Mamba-2. Applying it only to the output projection group works better than applying it only to the input projection group, and better than applying it to both. The authors also test a natural explanation and reject it: although Muon does reduce the condition number of the matrices it touches, that alone does not explain the validation-loss differences. Functional role matters, not just improved conditioning.15:T602,The experiments use Mamba-2 130M on OpenWebText and FineWeb-Edu, with token budgets of 1B, 2.6B, and 50B. This is a sensible design because it checks both early token efficiency and behavior well beyond the usual compute-optimal regime, while also testing whether the effect survives across corpora.

The most consistent result is that Muon on the output projection group gives the best outcome among the tested assignments. The gain is primarily in token efficiency: it reaches a given validation loss faster than pure AdamW, rather than dramatically changing the eventual floor. Muon on the input projection is weaker, and applying Muon to both input and output projections does not improve further. That pattern suggests a localized architectural fit rather than a generic optimizer advantage.

The long-budget runs matter. The advantage persists even when training continues far past the nominal compute-optimal point, which makes the result more credible than a short-horizon comparison. On the diagnostic side, Muon systematically lowers the condition number of the matrices it updates and changes their spectral statistics, but those changes do not map cleanly to validation performance. That is an important negative result: cleaner spectra are not sufficient to explain better language-model training.
