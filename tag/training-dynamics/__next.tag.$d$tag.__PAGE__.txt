Inputs pass through a Prelude, then the same Core blocks are applied recurrently multiple times, with the input representation injected at every iteration, and a Coda plus LM head produces next-token logits. During training, each sequence samples a forward depth T_i from max(1, Poisson(16)); evaluation mainly uses T_eval = 16. The control variable is the backward window k: gradients are retained only through the final min(k, T_i) iterations, while earlier iterations are executed in the forward pass and detached at the boundary. This keeps the forward computation and inference architecture fixed, changing only gradient depth and per-update training cost.

The main contribution is the experimental protocol rather than a new model. At each budget, the authors run two paired comparisons. Equal-token comparisons train different windows on the same amount of data, isolating per-token efficiency. Matched-time comparisons allocate approximately the same training time to each window, allowing the shorter window to take more updates because each update is cheaper. Paired runs share random seeds, and the forward-depth distribution, architecture, and inference iteration count are held fixed. This lets the paper separate the benefit of deeper backprop itself from the benefit of simply training longer.

The primary comparison is K4 versus K8, with additional K6 and FULL controls. An important detail is that “extended training” means a full recipe adjusted to the larger update count, including a rescaled learning-rate schedule, not just naively appending extra steps. Four findings matter most. First, at the smaller training budget, K4 beats K8 in matched-time comparisons across all seeds: shallower backprop plus more updates is the better use of limited time. Second, when the budget is increased by 4x, the K4 advantage shrinks on every seed evaluated at both budgets. The paper presents this as a robust trend, not a one-off fluctuation, and reports the same direction under different learning rates and model configurations. Third, equal-token controls confirm that K8 is genuinely more efficient per token: if token count is fixed, deeper backprop learns more from the same data. Fourth, the shift in matched-time preference comes from both components getting smaller with scale, but extended-training gains shrink more than per-token-efficiency gains. In other words, as budget grows, “doing more updates” becomes less valuable faster than “learning more from each token,” making longer windows relatively more competitive.

Given an anchor checkpoint θ0 and an update direction Δ, the authors consider the KL divergence between the anchor model and the model moved slightly along αΔ. A second-order expansion gives a Fisher quadratic form, Δ^T F Δ, where F is the token-averaged Fisher or Gauss-Newton matrix on a fixed evaluation corpus. Instead of estimating this curvature by finite differences, they compute it exactly with a forward-mode Jacobian-vector product, yielding c(Δ). They then normalize by the average curvature of random directions of the same size at the same anchor, c(null), and define leverage as ρ = c(Δ) / c(null).

This construction has several advantages. It measures behavioral sensitivity relative to a random baseline rather than in absolute KL units. It avoids the cancellation floor that makes tiny finite-difference KL estimates unreliable. And it naturally asks whether an update is aligned with high-sensitivity subspaces of the local Fisher geometry.

The paper also introduces several null models and decompositions. The isotropic null gives the default leverage. The profile null matches tensor-wise norms. The sign-flip null keeps the coordinate magnitudes fixed and randomizes only the signs, which separates the contribution of where the update puts its magnitude from whether the signs are coordinated correctly. The resulting sign-structure leverage, ρsign, is especially useful for understanding sparsification and merge operations such as DARE.

The second layer is the adaptive controller Lare, short for Loss-Adaptive Reweighting under fixed Exposure. Fixed coefficients work, but require expensive sweeps. Lare estimates useful domain weights online from running domain-loss statistics. After learning-rate warmup, it applies a normalized inverse-square-root rule to derive relative coefficients. Intuitively, this avoids letting high-loss domains dominate linearly while still increasing their optimization influence; the square-root form and normalization make the adjustment smoother. The normalization is important: the mixture-averaged coefficient remains one, so the method changes relative optimization scale across domains without globally rescaling the objective. This keeps the intervention focused on domain balance rather than effective learning-rate changes.

Relative to prior work, this is not another data-schedule search method and not a standard curriculum-learning variant. Its key contribution is to separate “what the model sees” from “how hard the model learns from it,” then show empirically that the second axis is independently powerful. It is also distinct from importance sampling or token-level weighting: the intervention is domain-level objective reweighting under fixed exposure.12:T5ad,The experimental design is one of the paper’s strengths. 第一部分是理论分析。作者先分析中间状态 s = αe_i + βz 的解码竞争关系。若用欧氏距离，候选 token 的得分会分解成一个随信号缩小而衰减的对齐项，以及一个与当前状态无关的静态范数项。问题就在这里：当 α 变小，即便 β=0、状态方向仍完全对准源 token，低范数 token 也可能因为静态范数优势而“反超”。这不是语义替换，而是度量退化。于是作者转到方向空间，用 cosine / directional ranking 来定义竞争，得到 pairwise crossing 的阈值条件：候选是否超过源 token，取决于有效噪声是否大于两者的语义分离。这个阈值理论把 token identity metric 从经验统计量还原成了大量 pairwise crossing 的聚合结果。

第二部分是 noising geometry。既然问题来自 norm decay，作者就不再沿普通连续高斯轨迹做 corruption，而是设计了 spherical noising。做法是把状态看成沿球面 geodesic 向一个 mask-biased endpoint 插值。这样可以尽量保持向量范数稳定，把竞争关系限制在方向空间里发生，避免欧氏几何下的伪竞争。这里的关键不是“球面”这个词本身，而是它服务于一个更明确的目标：让 corruption 过程和 token competition 的真实判据一致。

第三部分是时间调度，也就是 Semantic Retention Scheduling。Competition Threshold Theory 说明，大量 identity crossing 只会在接近高噪声端才发生。若仍用常规时间参数化，训练的大部分步数都浪费在身份几乎不变的区间，真正困难的身份转移区间反而预算很少。作者因此按 logarithmic rank decay 重新分配时间步，把更多 denoising budget 放到 identity 快速变化的区间。这不是简单的 loss-aware resampling，而是直接围绕 token rank / identity decay 设计 schedule。

第四部分是模型实例化，即 LLaCO。它基于预训练 backbone 适配到连续表示空间，包含 embedding directional adapter 和 timestep-conditioned modulation。前者负责把离散 token embedding 映射到适合方向竞争的连续空间，后者让 backbone 在不同噪声阶段采用不同调制。推理时，模型做 sequential blockwise denoising，并沿 spherical geodesic 逐步把连续语义轨迹翻译回离散 token。这里仍保留 blockwise 生成而不是一次性全序列并行，说明作者在生成质量与并行性之间做了折中：先把连续扩散的语义轨迹建稳，再逐步释放并行生成潜力。

First, the theory. For an intermediate state s = αe_i + βz, Euclidean decoding can be decomposed into an alignment term that shrinks with the signal and a static norm penalty that does not depend on the current state. This asymmetry creates a degeneracy: as α decreases, a lower-norm token may overtake the source even at zero noise. That is not semantic replacement; it is a metric artifact. The paper therefore switches to directional competition, effectively a cosine-based ranking view, and derives pairwise crossing thresholds. A candidate overtakes the source only when the effective directional noise exceeds their semantic separation. Existing token identity metrics are then reinterpreted as statistical aggregates of many such pairwise crossings.

Second, the noising geometry. Since the pathology comes from norm decay, the paper replaces standard continuous corruption with spherical noising. The trajectory is formulated as a geodesic interpolation toward a mask-biased endpoint, so vector norms are preserved more faithfully and competition happens in directional space rather than being distorted by Euclidean norm effects. This is an important design choice: the corruption process is made consistent with the competition criterion.

Third, the schedule. Competition Threshold Theory implies that in high dimensions, most meaningful identity transitions happen only at very high noise levels. A standard time parameterization therefore wastes much of training on regions where token identity barely changes, while under-allocating budget to the narrow transition zone. The proposed Semantic Retention Scheduling reallocates timesteps according to logarithmic rank decay, explicitly concentrating optimization where identity transitions occur.

Fourth, the model instantiation, LLaCO. A pretrained backbone is adapted to continuous representations using an embedding directional adapter and timestep-conditioned modulation. At inference time, generation proceeds through sequential blockwise denoising along spherical geodesics, translating continuous semantic trajectories back into discrete tokens. Compared with earlier continuous diffusion LMs, the novelty is not just a new schedule. It is the combination of a directional theory of token competition, a norm-preserving corruption path, and a schedule aligned to semantic retention rather than raw time.18:T869,The main empirical message is that LLaCO scales beyond toy settings and performs substantially better than prior continuous diffusion baselines, while becoming competitive with discrete diffusion models at similar scale. The instantiated model is 8B, which is itself notable because many continuous diffusion LM papers stop at much smaller scales.

The most useful way to read the experiments is as a three-level validation stack. First, theory validation: the paper compares Euclidean decoding with directional ranking and shows that Euclidean distance produces premature overtaking, including near-zero-noise artifacts, whereas directional competition better matches actual identity decay. Second, schedule validation: empirical identity metrics and rank decay measured on pretrained embeddings track the theoretical crossing behavior, and standard trajectories indeed compress most identity transitions near the noisy endpoint. Third, system validation: once spherical noising and semantic-retention scheduling are built into the model, LLaCO consistently beats earlier continuous diffusion baselines across a broad benchmark suite and approaches discrete diffusion performance.

The most important ablations are likely the following: Euclidean vs directional decoding, standard vs spherical noising, conventional vs semantic-retention scheduling, and the contribution of the directional adapter plus timestep modulation. If these ablations are strong, the gains are not a fragile artifact of one engineering trick but the result of aligning geometry, scheduling, and model adaptation.
