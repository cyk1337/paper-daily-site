The main contribution is the experimental protocol rather than a new model. At each budget, the authors run two paired comparisons. Equal-token comparisons train different windows on the same amount of data, isolating per-token efficiency. Matched-time comparisons allocate approximately the same training time to each window, allowing the shorter window to take more updates because each update is cheaper. Paired runs share random seeds, and the forward-depth distribution, architecture, and inference iteration count are held fixed. Given an anchor checkpoint θ0 and an update direction Δ, the authors consider the KL divergence between the anchor model and the model moved slightly along αΔ. A second-order expansion gives a Fisher quadratic form, Δ^T F Δ, where F is the token-averaged Fisher or Gauss-Newton matrix on a fixed evaluation corpus. Instead of estimating this curvature by finite differences, they compute it exactly with a forward-mode Jacobian-vector product, yielding c(Δ). They then normalize by the average curvature of random directions of the same size at the same anchor, c(null), and define leverage as ρ = c(Δ) / c(null).

This construction has several advantages. It measures behavioral sensitivity relative to a random baseline rather than in absolute KL units. It avoids the cancellation floor that makes tiny finite-difference KL estimates unreliable. And it naturally asks whether an update is aligned with high-sensitivity subspaces of the local Fisher geometry.

First, the theory. For an intermediate state s = αe_i + βz, Euclidean decoding can be decomposed into an alignment term that shrinks with the signal and a static norm penalty that does not depend on the current state. This asymmetry creates a degeneracy: as α decreases, a lower-norm token may overtake the source even at zero noise. That is not semantic replacement; it is a metric artifact. The paper therefore switches to directional competition, effectively a cosine-based ranking view, and derives pairwise crossing thresholds. A candidate overtakes the source only when the effective directional noise exceeds their semantic separation. Third, the schedule. Competition Threshold Theory implies that in high dimensions, most meaningful identity transitions happen only at very high noise levels. A standard time parameterization therefore wastes much of training on regions where token identity barely changes, while under-allocating budget to the narrow transition zone. 第三部分是从 hidden offset 到 collapse 的因果链。输出 logits 是 hidden state 乘输出层矩阵得到的，所以一个共享 offset 会沿着词表方向投影。如果某个 token 的输出向量与这个 offset 对齐得最好，它的 logit 会被系统性抬高，形成 vocabulary well。作者强调这不要求该 token 是罕见 token 或具有异常大的输出向量范数；在 UltraFeedback 上，塌缩 token 甚至可以是普通 token “Conf”。为了证明因果性，文章做了几类很强的 intervention：
1. 只保留 common component，就能重建 vocabulary well；
2. 把学到的 offset 注入一个未训练的 base model，可以复现 collapse，而注入 norm-matched random direction 不行；
3. 对 rejected-side loss 做 centering，让这个公共偏移对损失“不可见”或被抵消，可以在主设置里阻止 well 形成；
4. 额外显式惩罚 offset，也能在更长训练预算下维持生成稳定。

文章还提出了 demand 这个分析量。直观上，它表示损失在多大 per-token margin 之前仍会继续推动 chosen/rejected 分离。demand 高的目标会持续施压，更容易把公共偏移推大；demand 低的目标较早“满足”，offset 虽然也会长，但不一定和某个词表方向强对齐到形成 well。作者据此解释：常规超参下的 textbook DPO demand 较低，所以通常不容易塌；SimPO 介于中间；PRO ranking term 更强，因而更容易在长训练下出问题。一个重要细节是，reference model 不是保护机制的根源。作者专门测试了带 reference 的 per-token objective，发现只要目标仍保留这种相对排序结构，well 仍可能形成。25:T780,实验相当扎实，重点不只是“看到了 collapse”，而是把 collapse 的前兆、形成过程和可逆性都测了。主实验在 17 个 UltraFeedback 训练 run 上展开，覆盖 14/17 个 base-model lineages，并且既有 LoRA/adapter 类设置，也有 full-parameter fine-tuning。作者报告在这些 lineages 中，大多数都观察到相同的 collapse signature；尤其在主研究目标 PRO ranking term 下，现象相当普遍。

几个关键数字值得记住。第一，common-mode offset 对 chosen 和 rejected 的绝对 score 移动可达 tens of nats，但在一个平均 list 上对 margin 的影响不到 3 nat，这直接解释了为什么 held-out ranking 还能继续改善。第二，在 17 个 UltraFeedback runs 上，ranking 越好的 run，其生成样本被两个公开 reward model 偏好于 base model 的频率反而越低，说明 ranking metric 与真实生成质量已经脱钩。第三，full-parameter SimPO 从 base model 出发、训练到作者基准预算的 3 倍时，也会把 offset 推到 collapse；而从公开 SFT checkpoint 按官方 SimPO recipe 训练时，offset 同样增长，只是未必已经形成 vocabulary well。这个结果很关键，因为它说明 common-mode offset 不是某个极端设置下的偶发现象，而是多种 preference objective 下都存在的潜在动力学，只是强弱不同。

干预实验是全文最强的一部分。保留 common component 就能重建 well，说明 residual 不是主因；把 offset 注入 base model 能复现 collapse，说明它不是伴随现象而是致因变量；对 rejected-side loss 做 centering 或显式 offset penalty 后，在四个 base model 上可以把训练稳定地拉长到基准预算的 3 倍而不塌。作者还比较了 DPO、SimPO、PRO 等目标，发现它们都会增长 offset，只是 demand 不同，导致是否进入 collapse 的速度和概率不同。26:Tc26,The work proceeds in three stages: characterize the failure, identify the mechanism, and validate causality with interventions.

For the training setup, the main objective is a listwise ranking loss equivalent to the ranking term of PRO. Given a prompt and K ranked responses, the model is trained with a sequential Plackett–Luce objective that prefers the best remaining response over the lower-ranked ones at each stage. Response scores are average per-token log-probabilities without a reference model. When K=2, the objective reduces to a pairwise ranking form closely related to reference-free, length-normalized SimPO.

The central analysis focuses on final hidden states. The authors observe that hidden representations acquire a nearly shared directional shift across token positions, prompts, and both chosen and rejected samples. They decompose hidden states into a common component and a residual component, and show that the common-mode offset keeps growing during preference training. Because chosen and rejected representations are translated together, the relative ranking loss barely reacts: both scores move by similar amounts, so the margin changes little. The paper quantifies this directly: the offset can move chosen and rejected scores by tens of nats while changing their margin by less than 3 nat on average.

The next step is to connect this hidden drift to generation collapse. Since logits are obtained by projecting hidden states through the output matrix, a shared offset induces a systematic vocabulary-direction bias. If one token’s output vector aligns best with the offset, that token receives a persistent logit boost, forming a vocabulary well. Once the well is deep enough, decoding collapses onto that token. Importantly, the token need not be rare or have an unusually large output norm; in UltraFeedback, the collapse token can even be an ordinary token such as “Conf”.

The causal evidence is unusually strong. Keeping only the common component reconstructs the vocabulary well. Injecting the learned offset into an untreated base model recreates collapse, while norm-matched random directions do not. Centering the rejected-side loss to hide or cancel the common shift prevents well formation in the flagship setting. Explicitly penalizing the offset also stabilizes training under longer budgets.

A further conceptual contribution is the notion of loss demand: the per-token margin up to which the loss continues to push. High-demand objectives keep applying pressure and are more likely to amplify the common-mode offset until it aligns strongly with a vocabulary direction. Low-demand objectives saturate earlier, so the offset may still grow but is less likely to form a destructive well. This explains why textbook DPO at standard settings is often relatively safe, why SimPO sits in between, and why stronger ranking objectives can become unstable under longer training. The paper also shows that the presence of a reference model is not the real protection mechanism; adding a reference term to a per-token objective does not by itself prevent the well.27:T7a0,The empirical section is strong because it studies not only the existence of collapse, but also its precursors, mechanism, and preventability. Several quantitative findings stand out. First, the common-mode offset shifts chosen and rejected scores by tens of nats, yet changes their margin by less than 3 nat on an average list. Second, across the 17 UltraFeedback runs, the better a run performs on held-out ranking, the less often two public reward models prefer its samples over the base model’s samples.
