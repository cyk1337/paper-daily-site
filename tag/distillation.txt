This dense signal is noisy and asymmetric because many student tokens have low teacher probability, producing mostly negative advantages. RSTG uses three controls. The teacher token’s probability is assigned to that student token, and probabilities mapped to the same student token are aggregated. Probability mass that cannot be matched is assigned to an explicit residual category, which is required to preserve total mass. When byte prefixes do not span multiple teacher tokens, the transformed target exactly recovers the teacher’s byte-prefix marginal. At spanning positions, the method uses a mass-preserving chain-factorized lower bound. Training remains on-policy: the student generates trajectories, the teacher is queried at the corresponding byte-aligned states, and the student minimizes a generalized Jensen-Shannon divergence to the transformed target. Compared with candidate-set methods such as SimCT, BPM avoids dropping mass; compared with rank-based methods such as ULD or GOLD, it avoids assigning probability to byte-unrelated student tokens; compared with SeqKD, it keeps dense next-token supervision. 第一根是统一的 RL / On-Policy Distillation 基础设施。作者把 OPD 深度并入 RL 框架，用一个加权损失同时容纳蒸馏项和策略梯度项，通过调节系数 α、β 在 pure RL、pure OPD、joint RL+OPD 三种模式间切换。这里的关键不是“把两个 loss 相加”这么简单，而是把分布式调度、经验采样、在线 rollout、teacher supervision 放进同一套训练骨架里。这样做的好处是，蒸馏不再是离线、静态、与环境脱节的过程，而是可以直接监督 student 自己 rollout 出来的轨迹。报告里还提到支持多种 distillation objective，不只传统 MSE，还包括更适合分布匹配的 Forward_kl_TopK 等形式。

第二根是多专家并行训练，再用 Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation 融合。作者没有把所有任务混在一个大杂烩 reward 里训单一策略，而是按三条轨道分别训练 domain-specific RL experts：Reasoning 轨覆盖 Math、Code、STEM；General 轨覆盖 Instruction Following、Writing、Safety；Agent 轨覆盖 Tool-Use、DeepSearch、Code Agent、Claw Agent。每个专家配自己的数据合成、可验证奖励和训练策略，例如稀疏结果任务用两阶段 reward curriculum，多轮 agent 任务则放进可执行环境。之后做 MOPD：每个训练样本路由到对应冻结 teacher，student 在自己的 rollout 上接受 token-level reverse-KL 监督。这个设计的重点是并行化专家开发，减少顺序依赖，同时尽量保留各专家能力而不在融合时互相冲掉。

第三根是 HMPO，用于最后一步长度压缩。作者观察到强 reasoning policy 往往“过想”，输出过长。HMPO 的做法不是直接惩罚长度，而是先从正确 rollout 的中位长度导出 group-adaptive budget，再用 correctness-first 的乘性奖励结构，避免模型通过“更短但错误”的方式刷奖励。报告声称这种设计在数学上能防止短错答案的 reward hacking。更有意思的是，虽然 HMPO 只在数学上训练，但长度压缩效果能迁移到代码、科学和通用指令任务。20:T85a,The work has three main components.

First, it builds a unified RL / On-Policy Distillation framework. Instead of running distillation as a separate offline pipeline, the authors integrate OPD into the RL training stack with a weighted objective combining a distillation term and a policy-gradient term. By adjusting coefficients, the same infrastructure can operate in pure RL, pure OPD, or joint mode. The important point is not merely adding two losses, but sharing rollout generation, distributed scheduling, experience handling, and online supervision. The report also mentions support for multiple distillation objectives beyond standard MSE, including distribution-matching forms such as Forward_kl_TopK.

Second, it trains multiple domain specialists and then fuses them through Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation. Rather than optimizing one policy on a heterogeneous reward mixture, the authors train separate RL experts across three tracks: Reasoning (Math, Code, STEM), General (Instruction Following, Writing, Safety), and Agent (Tool-Use, DeepSearch, Code Agent, Claw Agent). Each expert uses domain-specific synthetic data, verifiable rewards, and tailored curricula, such as two-stage reward schedules for sparse-outcome tasks and executable multi-turn environments for agent tasks. Fusion is done by routing each sample to the corresponding frozen teacher and supervising the student on its own rollouts with token-level reverse KL. This is designed to parallelize expert development and reduce the capability see-saw that often appears in mixed-domain RL.

Third, the final stage applies HMPO, a length-compression RL method. The motivation is that strong reasoning models often over-generate. HMPO derives a group-adaptive length budget from the median length of correct rollouts and uses a correctness-first multiplicative reward so that the model cannot gain by being shorter if it is wrong. The report claims this structure prevents short-answer reward hacking. An interesting detail is that HMPO is trained only on mathematics, yet the resulting brevity transfers to code, science, and instruction-following tasks.21:T54f,The model is a 35B-parameter MoE with 3B activated parameters. On the systems side, the report attributes up to 17% end-to-end training speedup on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B to operator-level and communication-level optimizations, including SonicMoE indexed grouped GEMM kernels and segmented shared-expert fusion.

The headline task results reported in the text include AIME’26 at 92.70, LiveCodeBench-V6 at 80.91, IFEval at 94.64, IFBench at 82.82, Content-SafetyBench at 98.20, Behavioral-SafetyBench at 80.74, ClawBench at 84.20, and BFCL-v4 at 75.80. The claim is that this places the model in the range of 120B-class systems while keeping inference activation at 3B parameters. That comparison should be read carefully against evaluation settings and test-time budgets in the tables and appendix.

The more interesting experimental message is about training structure. The authors argue that a single heterogeneous reward mixture causes capability trade-offs, while specialist training followed by MOPD preserves or even improves expert performance in a unified student. HMPO reportedly reduces generation length by 19% to 46% with at most 0.7 percentage points of accuracy loss. If those numbers hold under close reading, they suggest that response compression can be learned as part of policy optimization rather than treated as a post hoc decoding constraint.22:T439,For a skim, focus on three parts. Start with the introduction, which lays out the full pipeline clearly: unified RL/OPD, specialist experts plus MOPD, and HMPO for length compression. Then read the infrastructure section for the weighted objective, the dynamic multi-teacher design, and the systems optimizations. Finally, go to the ablations around expert fusion and HMPO, because those sections carry the real methodological content.

With more time, inspect the agent environments and reward construction, since the quality of those signals determines whether the reported gains are robust or mostly benchmark-specific. While reading, keep three questions in mind: whether comparisons use comparable test-time budgets, whether the fused student truly retains specialist peaks, and whether HMPO’s gains come from a genuine shift in policy length preference rather than simply from extra RL exposure.23:T62b,The setup follows GKD-style multi-teacher on-policy distillation. The student samples continuations from its current policy, and teachers provide next-token distribution targets on those sampled trajectories. Each example is routed by a teacher tag to either a tool-call teacher or a response teacher. Both teachers start from the same base model and are specialized on structured tool-call targets or direct natural-language answers. A small supervised loss is also used as a format anchor for tool-call serialization.

The paper introduces the diagnosis of behavior leverage imbalance. The authors check several plausible aggregate explanations: whether tool-call samples receive more token exposure, whether full-sequence per-token JSD is larger for the tool teacher, and whether a gradient proxy is larger. These explanations do not match the observed behavior; in some runs, response tokens receive more exposure or larger aggregate divergence. The proposed explanation is that local signals near the decision boundary have higher behavioral leverage than average sequence-level signals.

The mitigation is Soft Clamp. For token divergence d_i, the method defines a batch-level threshold C = k * mean(d). Divergences below C are left unchanged. Divergences above C are transformed as d'_i = d_i * C / stopgrad(d_i). The forward contribution is capped around C, while the backward gradient is scaled down rather than removed. This differs from hard clipping, which can erase difficult-token gradients, and from global reweighting, which does not target extreme local
