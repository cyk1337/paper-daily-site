第二步，做 sign-consistent span merging。原始轨迹先被切成 reasoning spans，然后把 process reward 符号一致、相邻的 span 合并成更大的 segment。这样做有两个目的：一是减少对细粒度边界估计的依赖，二是让后续比较发生在更稳定的语义单元上。文中给了理论说明：如果内部边界估计有误差，合并后这些误差在段内会相互抵消；同时对 token-level divergence 做段内平均，也能在弱相关假设下降低局部方差。

第三步，用 ranking conflict 做 reward filtering，而不是 reward replacement。对每个合并后的 segment，作者聚合 token-level reverse-KL distillation loss，得到该段“偏离 teacher 的惩罚强度”；同时用 process reward 给出该段“推进解题的贡献”。如果两个排序冲突，例如 progress 更高的段反而被蒸馏损失惩罚得更重，就说明 teacher guidance 在这一局部可能不可靠。R²-OPD 不把 process reward 加到 loss 里，也不重写 OPD objective，而是把这些冲突段的 distillation reward mask 掉，只保留 progress 与 distillation 一致的部分继续监督。这个选择很关键：它把 process reward 用作校验器而不是训练主信号，降低了高噪声 reward 直接驱动优化的风险。

First, it estimates reasoning progress using teacher-independent process rewards. Instead of relying only on final correctness, it samples continuations from successive intermediate reasoning states and measures how the probability of solving the task changes. This gives a marginal contribution estimate for each reasoning span. The paper emphasizes that raw process rewards are noisy because continuation-based estimation has Monte Carlo variance and because fine-grained reasoning boundaries are themselves unstable.

Second, it performs sign-consistent span merging. The trajectory is segmented into reasoning spans, and adjacent spans with process rewards of the same sign are merged into larger segments. This reduces sensitivity to noisy internal boundaries and creates more stable units for calibration. The theoretical discussion argues that internal boundary estimation errors can cancel within merged segments, and that averaging token-level divergence over a segment reduces local variance under weak dependence assumptions.

Third, it detects local ranking conflicts between progress and distillation penalty, and uses those conflicts for filtering. For each merged segment, token-level reverse-KL distillation losses are aggregated into a segment-level penalty for deviating from the teacher. Process reward gives a segment-level estimate of reasoning advancement. If a higher-progress segment is penalized more heavily than a lower-progress one, the two signals disagree in ranking, indicating that teacher supervision may be unreliable there. Instead of adding process reward into the objective or replacing OPD, R²-OPD masks distillation rewards on the most inconsistent segments and keeps teacher guidance elsewhere.

This design choice is the core idea. Process reward is treated as a reliability probe rather than a primary training signal, which avoids directly optimizing against a high-variance reward. The implementation is built on reverse-KL OPD with support-restricted approximation over a top-H token set, so the method can be layered onto standard practical OPD pipelines.1a:T776,The experimental section is aimed at validating the signal-calibration hypothesis rather than demonstrating a large-scale model jump. Three findings stand out. First, the mismatch is real: the paper provides qualitative examples and aggregate analysis showing that some intermediate spans increase downstream solve probability while receiving stronger distillation penalties because they diverge from the teacher distribution. This is especially visible on harder reasoning problems.

Second, the gains come from removing bad supervision rather than injecting another reward. Compared with directly mixing process rewards into optimization, the filtering-based approach is more stable and more consistently helpful. The explanation is straightforward: raw process rewards are noisy, so using them as direct optimization targets can distort learning, whereas using them only to suppress clearly inconsistent teacher signals is safer.

Third, the ablations support the two main design choices: sign-consistent merging and segment-level averaging. Without merging, conflict detection becomes more brittle because boundary noise leaks into the comparison. With token-level filtering instead of segment-level filtering, lexical fluctuations create more false conflicts. The paper also studies the masking ratio and shows that more masking is not always better; excessive filtering removes useful teacher transfer.
