18:T5fc,The method replaces the embedding domain from the unit sphere S^{d-1} to the probability simplex Δ^{d-1}.
- Start with a frozen multimodal encoder f(·) producing pooled features h_u∈R^d.
- Add a lightweight shared adapter g_θ (linear or 2-layer MLP) to produce logits z_u.
- Two matched-capacity heads:
  (1) Euclidean/spherical head: e_u = z_u / ||z_u||_2, scored by cosine similarity.
  (2) Simplex head: p_u = softmax(z_u) ∈ Δ^{d-1}, scored by negative Total Variation distance: s_TV(u,v)=-(1/2)||p_u-p_v||_1.
- Training remains a symmetric in-batch InfoNCE (DPR-style); the only change is the output projection and the scoring function.
The paper’s argument is geometric: cosine on the sphere is invariant to rotations and sign flips, creating many equivalent solutions where cross-modal matching can be satisfied while allowing global distribution shifts between modalities. Simplex constraints (nonnegativity + unit mass) remove much of this symmetry and force both modalities to allocate probability mass over the same coordinates, effectively calibrating them. Softmax projection also introduces invariance to global logit shifts.
To isolate geometry from capacity, they compare Euclidean vs simplex using identical adapters, and further contrast an Euclidean cross-attention variant (XAttn) against ReTreever-style simplex outputs. They also introduce RelGap, a geometry-adjusted modality-gap metric that normalizes centroid separation by within-modality spread to make comparisons across domains more meaningful.19:T4d4,The authors first introduce Layer-wise Compositional Divergence (LCD), a metric that measures per-layer representation separation between negated and affirmative captions.
