9:T504,The proposed MOSAIC framework formulates MoE architecture-systems co-design as a mixed-integer nonlinear program. First, the authors derive a MoE scaling law that demonstrates the necessity of systems constraints: under model-FLOPs-only constraints, optimal MoE sparsity sits at the feasibility boundary, with loss monotonically decreasing as sparsity increases, meaning no interior optimum exists without explicit systems limits. Second, a calibrated system performance model estimates, for any given MoE architecture on a target cluster, attainable MFU, communication overhead across data/tensor/pipeline/expert parallelism dimensions, memory usage, and the best available parallel layout. Third, instead of solving a continuous unconstrained optimum and snapping to the nearest valid discrete architecture (the standard practice), MOSAIC directly minimizes predicted loss over a discrete grid of valid architecture choices—expert count, active parameters, total parameters, training token count, layer dimensions—to jointly output a cluster-tailored MoE geometry, token budget, and parallel execution plan. 1) Fit a standard Chinchilla loss law on single-epoch runs (no repetition) as a function of model size N and training tokens D.
2) For multi-epoch training, first pretend repeated tokens are fresh: D = U_D · (1 + R_D), and use the single-epoch Chinchilla fit to predict the expected loss.
3) Define the residual (observed minus predicted) as repetition-induced damage. Empirically, this residual follows a power law in repetition count R_D and is superlinear.
4) Study how the residual coefficient varies with configuration: larger N and smaller unique data U_D lead to larger penalties. This motivates a family (“complexity ladder”) of additive penalty forms with 1, 2, and 4 parameters, trading simplicity for fit quality.
5) Use the fitted law to prescribe compute-optimal allocations in the data-constrained regime, solving for (N, R_D) that minimize validation loss under a compute/token budget. The key new prescription is the existence of an optimal repetition level; beyond it, scaling N is better than adding epochs.
