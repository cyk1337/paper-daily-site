9:T42f,The framework explicitly decouples duplicate detection from copy retention.
Detection (scalable and shard-invariant): documents are segmented at natural boundaries; each segment is normalized (formatting/casing/whitespace etc., per the paper’s rules) and exact-hashed; distributed key–value aggregation computes global occurrence counts for each hash, ensuring cross-shard visibility.
Retention (explicit policy): instead of keep-one/keep-k, the method defines a retention function k=f(global_frequency, segment_length). High-frequency segments (likely boilerplate/templates) are aggressively compressed; longer repeated spans get smaller budgets as they more often reflect direct copying or templated reuse; low-frequency repeats are treated conservatively to avoid deleting legitimate quotations or mild reuse. A coherence-preserving deletion step reduces fragmentation when removing repeated spans.
Trade-off vs suffix arrays: less coverage for arbitrary cross-boundary substrings, but far better scalability, shard invariance, and controllable retention behavior.a:T6bd,The method has three layers. The first is empirical diagnosis. The authors show systematically that in ELF, more repetition often coincides with lower Gen-PPL. When repeated spans are stripped out, model rankings can reverse; the smallest model looks best largely because it repeats the most. The second layer is mechanism analysis. They rewrite self-conditioning as a feedback recurrence: the denoiser predicts a clean estimate, and that estimate becomes part of the next-step input. Linearizing this loop around sampling trajectories and inspecting the Jacobian reveals a dominant slowest-contracting mode that is nearly one-dimensional and strongly aligned with repetitive trajectories. They also run direct causal probes: increasing feedback strength alone raises repetition while lowering Gen-PPL, which supports the claim that the loop creates the pathology that the metric then rewards. The third layer is the fix, ACE (Attractor-Contrast-Escape). ACE subtracts a single fixed direction d from the self-conditioning feedback at every step. That direction is estimated without labels, auxiliary models, or retraining: collect feedback states from high-repetition trajectories and low-repetition trajectories, then take the difference of their means. The paper also derives a closed-form usable steering window so the intervention is strong enough to escape the basin but not so strong that it destroys semantics. A key design choice is where to intervene. ACE acts on the latent feedback state, not at token decoding, because the repetition is already formed upstream in continuous space before argmax readout. That makes it fundamentally different from repetition penalties, n-gram blocking, or decode-time heuristics.b:T5ab,Three result groups matter most. First is metric pathology. The empirical study is broad: more than 2,000 training runs, model sizes from 101M to 805M parameters, and several target-data regimes. The paper covers three families of constrained data. For multilingual experiments, German from FineWeb2 is artificially capped at 50M, 100M, 500M, and 1B tokens, with English FineWeb as the abundant generic source; French and Swahili are used to test whether the pattern is language-specific. The main empirical pattern is that repetition has smooth diminishing returns rather than a hard threshold, and the useful repetition range depends jointly on model size, target-data size, and total budget.
