9:T660,Two coupled components: a region-inhomogeneous forward process (training) and a detect–remask–refill sampler (inference).

1) Region-inhomogeneous forward dynamics
- Split positions into holes (to be generated) and user-provided context.
- Holes follow standard absorbing corruption over time t.
- Context follows a uniform-mixing corruption kernel at a small per-sequence rate η, which is equivalent to keeping context at a small, fixed “elapsed time” under uniform mixing while holes traverse absorbing dynamics.
- Training remains a masked-diffusion-style weighted cross-entropy denoising objective, but with modified forward marginals.
- Include an η=0 mixture component so the model still trains on perfectly clean conditioning, aiming to prevent degradation on clean inputs.

2) Detect–remask–refill with trust annealing
- Because context is sometimes corrupted during training, the model outputs a meaningful predictive distribution at every position, including the probability of the observed context token—used as a suspicion score.
- During early reverse steps, low-confidence context tokens are re-noised into the absorbing state (remasked) with an annealed intensity, then refilled by the standard reverse dynamics with bidirectional conditioning.
- After a lock time, context is frozen to avoid late-stage instability.
- The paper discusses confidence bookkeeping to compose this with low-confidence decoding.

Conceptually, absorbing diffusion is framed as one endpoint on a “trust axis” (infinite SNR for known regions); the method moves context to finite SNR so the model can doubt and revise it.a:T465,They evaluate corrupted-context infilling on web text with preregistered hypotheses and decision gates, and disentangle training-time vs inference-time contributions via nine experimental arms.
- Diagnostic: under absorbing dynamics, even uniformly random garbage context is treated as highly reliable; the pretrained model assigns on the order of ~50% probability mass to observed context tokens even when they are random, indicating a lack of a graded uncertainty mechanism.
- Results are strongest for conspicuous/uniform corruptions: finetuning under the new forward process yields better detection/repair/containment with no measurable regression under clean conditioning.
- For more plausible corruptions, detection is partial and infilling gains are small; the preregistered primary F1 gate fails.
- Ablations are not fully clean: the paper notes it does not completely isolate the effects of the η=0 mixture and trust annealing from other coupled changes.

Second, Jurisdiction In-Context Learning (J-ICL). The method keeps the standard few-shot interface and uses only query-only LM loss. In J+ episodes, the demonstrated rule genuinely applies to the query, so the correct answer should follow the context. In J- episodes, the demonstrations still support a coherent rule, but that rule is private, conflicting, or undeployed, while the query requires the reality-grounded or formally valid answer. No explicit authority labels, auxiliary classifier, or rationale supervision is added. The model is expected to infer applicability from natural-language cues and episode structure. This is the key distinction from methods such as MetaICL or Symbol Tuning, which mainly improve rule extraction from demonstrations. J-ICL tries to train both rule induction and rule applicability judgment within the same prompting format.12:T63d,The empirical story has three important parts. First, baseline diagnosis: both proprietary and open-weight models are vulnerable on FakeContext-bench. Larger scale and stronger pretraining do not reliably yield context-authority discrimination. The failure is often not that the model misses the demonstrated pattern, but that it successfully infers the false rule and applies it where it should not.

Second, existing ICL-tuning methods can worsen the problem. The paper reports that prevalent ICL fine-tuning approaches improve conventional few-shot performance but can reduce reality-grounded accuracy by as much as 14.95 percentage points relative to the base model under misleading context. This is the central result, because it shows a systematic objective-level trade-off rather than a mere capability gap.

Third, J-ICL mitigates that trade-off. Training with mixed J+ and J- episodes improves robustness to misleading context while preserving, and in some settings improving, standard ICL ability.
