Every unit receives a terminal disposition. Of these, 110 stop before deterministic claim analysis because required historical evidence or semantic grounding is missing; 103 of these stops are from the outcome-blind census-review path and 7 from the frozen random draw. Only 14 units reach deterministic claim audit. The completed audits show that fine-grained and coarse-grained claims can diverge. In AgentDojo, five grounded readings change displayed scores. The primary family keeps Claude as the winner, yields a unique complete order, and stabilizes all three pairwise relations. Controlled interventions in Appendix L show that changing an omitted semantic coordinate can change the conclusion even with fixed observations and predictions.19:T5cf,The core method is a preregistered multiverse analysis. Instead of choosing one analysis pipeline, the authors enumerate a large grid of defensible specifications, with every setting taken from published implementations rather than invented for the study.

A specification includes multiple analytic axes: discovery objective, ablation operator, corruption distribution, evaluation metric, size threshold, prompt variant, seed, and granularity. The testbed is GPT-2 small on the indirect object identification task, using factorized-graph circuit discovery. Each specification returns a circuit C(s), a subset of model edges.

The key move is that the paper does not stop at graph overlap. It introduces a deterministic claim map φ that converts each discovered circuit into a structured Annex IV-style statement. This shifts the evaluation target from “do the circuits look similar?” to “would the resulting regulatory explanation be the same?” The paper then defines flip rate, modal share π*, and conditional within-group variants, and proposes a filability criterion: evidence is filable at tolerance α only if π* ≥ 1 − α.

A second methodological contribution is statistical. When different specifications produce circuits of different sizes, pooled stability statistics can reverse or mislead. The authors therefore analyze pooled results, size-controlled results, and claim mappings with size information removed. That point generalizes beyond this specific task.1a:T56c,The experimental sweep is large: 15,840 preregistered specifications on GPT-2 small and IOI, of which 7,561 produced a claim through the deterministic map.

The main findings are:
1. Claim instability is high. The reported flip rate is 0.7316. With 9 observed claim classes, the theoretical ceiling is 0.8889, so the observed value is 82.3% of the maximum possible. In practical terms, two defensible specifications often yield different regulatory statements.
2. The modal share is too low to satisfy the proposed filability criterion under reasonable tolerances, meaning current circuit evidence would struggle to qualify as stable filing material.
3. The instability survives aggressive controls. It remains even when one analytic axis is standardized, when circuit size is removed from the claim map, and when circuit size is held fixed.
4. The discovered circuits are not random noise. At fixed circuit size, they are more stable than a size-matched random null: 0.2746 versus 0.4230. But functional instability is still substantial: 0.3972 pooled and 0.4777 for the smallest circuits. Cohen’s kappa is only 0.0146, indicating that circuits found under different specifications are functionally almost uncorrelated.

Taken together, the evidence supports a restrained conclusion: circuit discovery contains signal, but in its current form it does not provide audit-grade stability.1b:T514,Do not read this one from the abstract alone. Start with the introduction and especially the section titled “What this paper is not claiming.” The authors are careful about scope, and that section prevents the common misreading that the paper is claiming mechanistic interpretability is useless.

Then read the formalism closely. Three objects matter most: the specification tuple, the deterministic claim map φ, and the filability criterion. The novelty is less about a new discovery algorithm and more about connecting analytic variation to the stability of downstream explanatory claims.

In the experiments, prioritize:
1. the seven analytic axes and where each setting comes from;
2. flip rate, modal share, and within-group stability metrics;
3. robustness checks with one axis fixed, circuit size controlled, and size removed from claims;
4. comparisons to random nulls and the kappa analysis.

If the goal is only the main message, about 45 minutes is enough. For deeper reading, two questions are worth pursuing: whether the claim map itself amplifies or dampens instability, and whether the same multiverse protocol could be extended to larger models, harder tasks, or other interpretability tools such as activation patching, feature attribution, or sparse autoencoder-based analyses.1c:T499,The work has two components. UniHall is a benchmark organized around a three-way taxonomy: Object, Instruction, and Knowledge hallucinations. Object cases include existence, attributes, and relations; instruction cases include refusal failures and sycophancy; knowledge cases cover fictitious factual claims. The seed data is repurposed from open-domain and domain-specific multimodal benchmarks such as Hallu-PI and BEAF, then normalized into a unified annotation protocol. The text states that the object-level split contains over 1,000 representative instances, with raw sources over-provisioned by 5-10x before filtering. SAMF, or Self-Adaptive Multimodal Fuzzing, is the dynamic component. It uses evolutionary mutation, including reinforcement learning, to generate adversarial image and prompt variants while trying to preserve semantics. Evaluation is handled by structured oracles: symbolic rules, detectors, and LLM verifiers are combined in a coarse-to-fine pipeline rather than relying on a single judge. Compared with POPE or HallusionBench, the main difference is the combination of fine-grained taxonomy, dynamic multimodal mutation, and structured verification.1d:T5eb,Two pillars.
(1) MIST-1000 benchmark: each item is instantiated into four matched variants where the question, answer space/type, gold answer, and a plausible wrong answer are held fixed; only the surrounding context changes: clean (no added context), misleading (context nudges toward the plausible wrong answer), correct-context (context supports the gold), and irrelevant (natural related text that does not contain the answer). The dataset contains 1,000 items (800 adapted from existing QA/math/reasoning benchmarks, 200 newly written longer scenarios) with a two-pass human QA process to control ambiguity, leakage, and plausibility.
(2) Metrics + training:
- SC2W (signal-induced correct-to-wrong): computed on the subset of items the model answers correctly in the clean condition, measuring how often misleading context flips them to wrong—separating susceptibility from base competence.
- SCOPE (Selective Context Preference Optimization): a signal-counterfactual preference framework. It mines the critical failures from a base model: cases where the model is correct in clean but wrong under misleading context. For each, it forms a preference pair (truth-consistent response preferred over signal-following response). The key is not changing DPO’s objective, but balancing the training mixture: reuse the same preference pair across all four context conditions and weight misleading pairs equally against clean/correct/irrelevant controls, preventing the “learn to ignore all context” shortcut.1e:T578,The methodology is built around three complementary diagnostics. Sensitivity is measured by Layer-Wise Representation Divergence (LRD), defined as cosine divergence between mean-pooled hidden states for clean and perturbed inputs at each layer. Two summary statistics, slope across depth and recovery rate from early to late layers, are used to classify models into spike-and-suppress versus late-accumulation regimes. Causality is measured with activation patching: at each layer, the clean hidden state is inserted into the noisy forward pass, and the authors record how often originally failed examples are recovered. To avoid over-interpreting weak patching signals, they introduce an identity-patch gate: only models with at least 80% recovery under all-layer clean patching are treated as confirmed for causal conclusions. Compensatory capacity is measured by training LoRA adapters in different layer windows and comparing repair gains against regressions on clean computation. The proposed explanation for the dissociation is cascade disruption: an adapter placed early may locally counteract perturbation damage, but because it changes the entire downstream computation, it can break parts of the clean-input trajectory that were otherwise intact. The paper supports this mechanism with downstream disruption profiles and controlled layer-placement sweeps across multiple perturbation types.1f:T486,The study covers five models: Phi-3.5, Gemma-2-9B, Llama-3, Mistral, and Qwen2.5-7B, plus within-family scaling scans for Qwen2.5 from 1.5B to 14B and a smaller Llama family check. Two propagation regimes emerge. Phi-3.5 and Gemma-2-9B show spike-and-suppress behavior, where early divergence is partially absorbed later. Llama-3, Mistral, and Qwen2.5-7B show late accumulation, where divergence keeps growing toward the output. On the two models that pass the 80% identity-patch threshold, sensitivity and causality are strongly anti-correlated, with layer-wise correlations from -0.72 to -0.88.
