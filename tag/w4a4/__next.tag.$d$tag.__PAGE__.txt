b:T596,The method has three main parts. First, compensation is moved from block level to individual linear layers. Instead of sharing one compensator across a Transformer block, the paper attaches separate compensators to each of the four linear layers. This matters because quantization error structure differs substantially across layers. The fitting is also sequential rather than one-shot: after compensating one layer, the compensated layer is inserted back into the model, and the next layer is solved using the true downstream input distribution under upstream quantization and compensation. This avoids train-deploy mismatch. Second, the core contribution is a numerically stable solver based on a truncated pseudoinverse. Rather than adding damping or ridge regularization to all directions, the method removes only the collapsed singular directions and preserves the remaining compensable subspace. That is the conceptual difference from prior stabilizers: they shrink everything; QuaSAR tries to delete only the directions that make the solution explode. Third, the compensation itself is compressed for deployment through low-rank factorization and compensation quantization, giving a practical accuracy-size tradeoff. The paper also studies budget allocation and argues that global rank budgeting is more meaningful than fine-grained per-layer allocation, because layer importance does not reliably predict rank sensitivity.c:T506,Several results are especially informative. On ViT-B under W4A4, the paper highlights layer b0.fc2 as a canonical failure case: prior gating reports R² = -68.6 and a compensation norm of 6.2×10^7, so the layer is confidently discarded. Yet it is actually the single most valuable compensator in the model; removing it costs 1.05% top-1 accuracy, more than any other layer. This directly validates the paper’s claim that negative R² can be a sign of numerical collapse rather than low utility. In the main result, the method reaches 81.42% top-1 on ViT-B/W4A4 without additional training, outperforming prior training-free methods and, according to the paper, even a fine-tuning-based QwT variant. With low-rank decomposition and compensation quantization, it obtains a more deployable point at 80.26% top-1 and 54.7 MB, filling the gap between the heavier but stronger QwT and the lighter but weaker QwT-v2. The layer-wise design gives a +3.1% gain over block-wise compensation. Another notable ablation is rank allocation: reversing a “high-rank to important layers” policy changes accuracy by only 0.02%, suggesting that layer importance and rank sensitivity are largely decoupled.
