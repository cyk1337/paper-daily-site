Hypothesis A (functional emotions): linear emotion directions correspond to internal emotion-like variables that causally shape behavior (e.g., ‘desperation’ rising before reward hacking).

Hypothesis B (situational contexts): the model’s internal state is organized around computational features of situations (constraint severity, monitoring likelihood, action-space structure, reversibility, goal persistence). Emotion vectors capture only the component of this space that projects onto human emotion axes, so they correlate with behavior without being the causal driver.

Evidence is drawn from tensions within the system card: different sections show different valence/action relationships; and non-emotional “persona” vectors (rigour/caution/analytical thinking) produce similar behavioral effects to negative-valence emotion vectors, which is more naturally explained by a shared situational mode (careful/risk-aware) than by distinct emotional mechanisms.

Proposed discriminating test: apply emotion probes to the strategic concealment episodes (currently analyzed via SAE features).
