a:T500,The main strength is the controlled setup. Starting from the standard KL-regularized RL objective for LLM fine-tuning, the authors vary the optimized signal while keeping the training machinery fixed. This yields three regimes: pure task-reward optimization, pure distribution sharpening, and a hybrid of the two. The sharpening objective is framed as concentrating mass around outputs already preferred by the reference or base policy, while the reward objective is anchored in external task success. The paper also provides a first-principles analysis of why sharpening can fail: its optimum is tied to the geometry of the base distribution, so if the model already assigns high mass to coherent but wrong solutions, sharpening reinforces that error. The objective can also be unstable because small distributional biases get amplified. By contrast, task reward defines an optimum that is not restricted to the dominant modes of the reference policy. As long as the KL penalty is not too strong, RL can shift mass toward trajectories that were initially unlikely but correct. An important methodological point is that both paradigms are optimized with the same RL pipeline, so the observed differences are attributed to the objective, not to optimizer choice or training recipe.b:T40f,From the provided full text, the experiments focus on mathematical reasoning tasks with varying difficulty, specifically to test how the two objectives behave as base-model success rate drops. The paper also compares training-based sharpening against inference-time sharpening baselines, showing that simply concentrating probability mass is not enough to match explicit task-reward optimization. The key ablations appear to vary KL strength, the mixture between reward and sharpening terms, and task difficulty.
