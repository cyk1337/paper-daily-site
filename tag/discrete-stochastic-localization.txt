19:T9af,方法叫 Discrete Stochastic Localization（DSL）。核心设计是把离散 token 映射到单位球面上的 embedding，然后定义一个 stochastic localization 观测通道，而不是直接用常规 VP/VE Gaussian diffusion。作者先从一个全句共享全局 SNR 的高斯观测通道出发，写出 z_t 与干净 embedding x 的关系，并指出其等价的无条件 SDE 的 drift 是 MMSE denoiser，也就是 E[x | z_t]。到这里还只是连续扩散的常规视角。

关键变化发生在单位球几何上。若 clean token embeddings 都放在 unit sphere 上，并采用作者构造的 localization channel，则 Bayes-optimal clean-token posterior 只依赖 noisy state 本身，而不依赖名义上的 SNR/timestep。也就是说，同一个 noisy token state 已经包含了恢复 clean token 所需的全部信息，模型不需要额外的时间标签来解释“当前噪声有多大”。这就得到一个 time-invariant denoiser。

这个性质带来两个直接后果：
1. 每个 token 位置可以走自己的 SNR path，而不必全句共享同一个全局时间步。于是不同位置可以异步更新，有些位置像 masked refinement 一样从低信息到高信息逐步揭示，有些位置则可以按 random-order 的方式先确定再条件化后续位置。
2. masked diffusion 的端点行为成为 DSL 的特例，而连续有限 SNR 状态则提供了更细粒度的中间修正空间。于是 endpoint revealing、连续去噪、混合采样不再需要不同模型，而是同一个 posterior estimator 的不同查询方式。

为了让这个框架能在标准 Transformer/DiT 上训练，作者还引入了 mixed-support SNR sampling，让模型同时见到端点状态和有限 SNR 状态；并通过 mixture-of-tokens converter，把连续状态上的 posterior 学习转成可训练的离散 token 目标。论文还给出两个 exact NLL 视角来说明训练目标的状态支持是合理的，这一点是它比很多“只是换个噪声参数化”的工作更扎实的地方。

和已有 MDLM 的区别在于：MDLM 主要工作在离散 mask/no-mask 端点或有限离散状态上；DSL 保留了 mask-like 零信息端点，但在中间引入连续状态，同时不要求 denoiser 显式依赖时间。和传统 continuous diffusion LM 的区别在于：DSL 不是把 token embedding 扔进普通高斯扩散，而是专门设计了几何与通道，使 noisy state 自身就足以决定 clean posterior。1a:T974,The method is called Discrete Stochastic Localization (DSL). The core idea is to place clean token embeddings on the unit sphere and define a stochastic-localization observation channel, instead of using a standard VP/VE Gaussian diffusion directly in embedding space. The paper starts from a sentence-level Gaussian observation channel with a global SNR and derives the equivalent unconditional SDE whose drift is the MMSE denoiser, namely the posterior mean E[x | z_t]. Up to that point, the setup still resembles ordinary continuous diffusion.

The key step is the unit-sphere geometry. Under the proposed localization channel, the Bayes-optimal clean-token posterior becomes a function of the noisy state alone and does not depend on the nominal SNR. In other words, the noisy token state already contains all the information needed for clean-token prediction, so the denoiser does not need an explicit timestep embedding to calibrate uncertainty. This yields a time-invariant denoiser.

That property has two major consequences.
1. Different token positions can follow different SNR paths. The model is no longer tied to a single global diffusion time for the whole sequence. Some positions can behave like masked refinement, while others can be revealed in a random-order autoregressive manner.
2. Endpoint masked diffusion becomes a special case of the same framework, while finite-SNR continuous states provide richer intermediate refinement. As a result, endpoint revealing, continuous denoising, and hybrid samplers can all be served by one posterior estimator rather than separate models.

For practical training, the paper introduces mixed-support SNR sampling so the model sees both endpoint states and finite-SNR states during training. A mixture-of-tokens converter maps the continuous-state posterior-learning problem into a trainable objective on a standard Transformer/DiT-style backbone. The paper also provides two exact NLL views to justify the support of the training objective, which makes the proposal more principled than a simple reparameterization trick.

Relative to MDLM, DSL keeps the mask-like zero-information endpoint but adds continuous intermediate states. Relative to standard continuous diffusion LMs, DSL is not just Gaussian noise over token embeddings; it is a deliberately designed geometry-plus-channel pair that makes the noisy state itself sufficient for posterior prediction.1b:T67d,The experiments are mainly on OpenWebText, and the most important empirical claim is that a single checkpoint supports multiple decoding paths. The model is obtained by fine-tuning a pretrained MDLM checkpoint rather than training from scratch.

On masked-refinement decoding, using a ReMDM-family decoder, DSL improves distributional faithfulness measured by MAUVE across a range of step budgets from T=128 to T=1024. That result matters because it shows DSL does not give up the practical strengths of masked diffusion; instead, it uses continuous intermediate states to improve few-step generation.

The same checkpoint also supports random-order autoregressive sampling without retraining. This is more than a convenience result. It is direct evidence for the paper’s central claim that the model learns a clean-token posterior estimator rather than a sampler-specific reverse process.

The paper further presents a hybrid continuous-then-discrete sampler that can produce coherent generations in as few as T=48 steps. For non-autoregressive generation, this suggests a useful division of labor: continuous states for fast coarse refinement early on, followed by discrete revealing later.

The paper also reports competitive likelihood on Text8, although the emphasis appears to be more on generation quality and decoding flexibility than on pushing NLL alone. The provided excerpt does not include the full result tables, so I cannot reliably quote exact MAUVE values, parameter counts, or training-token totals. Then read Section 2.2 carefully. The unit-sphere embedding geometry and the resulting SNR-invariant Bayes-optimal posterior are the conceptual center of the paper. If that point is convincing, the rest of the framework becomes much easier to interpret.

Next, read Section 2.3 on per-token SNR paths. This is where the paper explains why masked refinement, random-order AR, and hybrid decoding can all be seen as different query patterns over the same posterior estimator. The important point is that the unification is model-level, not just a loose combination of decoders.

In the experiments, prioritize three things:
- MAUVE improvements on OpenWebText under few-step masked refinement;
- evidence that the same checkpoint supports random-order AR;
- the T=48 hybrid-sampling results.
