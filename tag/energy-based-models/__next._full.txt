The main task is connectivity-2: given an Erdős–Rényi graph G(N=12, p=0.2), predict its all-pairs reachability matrix. The base model is IRED’s 32-channel GraphEBM, trained for 30k steps with 10 diffusion timesteps and an MSE denoising objective on ±1-scaled targets, with energy-landscape supervision and inner-loop optimization enabled.

Retrieval keys are produced by a 3-layer GIN. The encoder uses a label-ordered readout instead of sum pooling, because the retrieved reachability matrix must preserve the node-label alignment of the query graph. A learned per-position ID embedding is added to avoid collapses in degree-symmetric cases. The key encoder is trained with supervised contrastive learning for 3000 steps; target-nearest neighbors are defined by Hamming agreement over reachability entries.

The main contribution is the five-arm ablation: oracle, best-constant, per-query-random, shuffled, and aligned. The best-constant arm estimates how much can be gained by a favorable fixed initialization. The per-query-random arm tests whether stochastic perturbation alone helps. The shuffled arm keeps the value distribution and retrieval mechanics but permutes key-value pairings. The aligned arm keeps the correct pairing.
