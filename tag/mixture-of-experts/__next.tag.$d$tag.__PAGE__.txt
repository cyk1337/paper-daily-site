9:T62a,The technical story has two parts. The first is regime characterization. Using gpt-oss-20b as the main case, the paper focuses on models trained with an unusually large auxiliary load-balancing coefficient, λ_aux = 0.9, far above the typical 0.001–0.01 range used in systems such as Mixtral or Switch Transformer. This pushes mean routing probabilities toward the uniform prior 1/E. The authors then argue for two consequences. One, pruning can improve local LM metrics because removing low-contribution experts concentrates routing mass onto the remaining experts, which may sharpen token assignment enough to lower perplexity while still harming task performance. Two, any global importance score washes out domain-specific micro-signals: an expert that is mildly useful for math but not code will look average once scores are aggregated over all tokens.

The second part is MESA, Minimax Expert Score Allocation. Instead of assuming a single globally valid expert ranking, MESA computes domain-aware scores and iteratively boosts experts associated with the currently worst-affected domain under the tentative pruning plan. The objective is explicitly minimax: reduce the largest domain-specific degradation rather than optimize average retention. This is the main conceptual difference from frequency-based or activation-aware pruning methods such as REAP. Those methods still rely on a stable global notion of importance; MESA assumes that such a notion may not exist under over-dispersed routing and therefore reallocates score mass to preserve fairness across domains.a:T63a,The main experiments are on gpt-oss-20b, with Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct used as a standard-routing control. The most important result is the perplexity–accuracy disconnect. Across five pruning configurations on gpt-oss-20b, the expected negative correlation between perplexity and downstream accuracy disappears. The paper reports Spearman ρ = 0.70 on GSM8K (with p > 0.1) and ρ = -0.10 on GPQA. A striking example is that random pruning achieves the lowest perplexity, 40.95, yet gives the worst math performance, while REAP has the highest perplexity, 145.04, but preserves much stronger downstream behavior.

A second result is the capability trade-off across pruning signals. Activation-aware pruning, represented by REAP, preserves competition-style math better, reaching 73% on AIME 2025, but loses badly on science QA, with GPQA at 47.72% versus 65.48% for a stronger alternative, an 18-point gap. Frequency-based methods show the opposite tendency on some domains. This supports the paper’s claim that weak domain specialization survives inside an apparently uniform router and different pruning signals damage different capabilities.

A third result is the benefit of MESA on worst-case retention. At pruning ratio r = 0.25, MESA achieves the smallest worst-domain degradation. On a per-benchmark comparison, it beats REAP on 7 of 11 benchmarks, with 5 statistically significant wins. On Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, the same perplexity–accuracy pathology does not appear, which strengthens the argument that the issue is regime-specific rather than a generic property of expert pruning.b:T47d,For a quick read, focus on three things. Third, the side-by-side comparison between gpt-oss-20b and Mixtral, because that is where the paper most clearly shows this is a routing-regime issue rather than random instability.

Training this system is nontrivial because the routing spaces are heterogeneous. Expert routing is a large sparse categorical problem, while attention mode and bit-width are small categorical choices with highly uneven sensitivities. The authors argue, and empirically show, that collapse in one axis can induce collapse in the others: if attention routing becomes overly skip-heavy, the FFN router sees degraded inputs and loses discrimination, which then makes bit allocation less meaningful. To address this, they introduce a coupling-aware balancing objective rather than relying only on standard single-axis load balancing. Third, they define a unified differentiable cost model that combines FLOPs and KV memory, and enforce a target average budget with an online Lagrangian controller.
