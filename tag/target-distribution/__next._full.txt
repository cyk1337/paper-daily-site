18:T4e1,The Q-target framework replaces the one-hot target δ(y_t) with a mixture target Q_t:
Q_t = γ_t · δ(y_t) + (1-γ_t) · \tilde{\pi}_t.
Here γ_t∈[0,1] controls how strongly the observed token is trusted (token-level confidence), and \tilde{\pi}_t specifies how the remaining probability mass is distributed over plausible alternatives (e.g., shaped by a prior/reference distribution rather than uniform smoothing).

The key conceptual move is: many seemingly different SFT losses correspond to different implicit choices of (γ_t, \tilde{\pi}_t). So objective design can be treated as explicit target design.

They propose Target-SFT as an explicit instantiation of this view, constructing Q_t directly instead of relying on implicit effects of reweighting/regularization. A practical distinction from vanilla label smoothing is that \tilde{\pi}_t need not be uniform; it can be aligned with the model prior (or a teacher/reference) to soften supervision where the demonstrated token is uncertain or noisy.
