18:T759,The model continues the Kathleen line. The input side uses a ByteRotate encoder: instead of learning 256 separate byte embeddings, it learns one vector and derives all byte representations through DFT phase rotation. This means the whole byte alphabet is covered with only 128 parameters. Sequence modeling is handled by a multi-scale reverberant state, which replaces attention with three banks of content-gated exponential decay states. The recurrence is s_t = gamma_t s_{t-1} + (1-gamma_t) v_t, where gamma_t depends on content, and the three decay ranges cover short, medium, and long memory. A reverb block is LayerNorm, then reverb in parallel with causal depthwise convolution, then learned gate mixing, then FFN. The trunk has three such blocks with hidden size 128. A notable engineering addition is fast scan: the recurrence is parallelized in chunks of 16 using a cumprod/cumsum closed form, numerically matching the sequential loop to 1e-7 while giving roughly 10–50x wall-clock speedup. That makes the 512 MB training point feasible on free-tier hardware. The baseline is a parameter-matched attention model with the same skeleton, replacing reverb plus convolution by 4-head causal SDPA. Beyond byte-level LM, the paper also uses a 3.08M-parameter word-level variant with a 10K vocabulary for generation studies. Another technical contribution is FORM DISTANCE, a non-parametric, frozen, offline metric for whether sampled text has the statistical form of natural text. It is not a semantic metric. The authors explicitly document one case where the metric was gamed and how they patched it, which makes the evaluation setup more credible than LLM-as-a-judge for this setting. Finally, the decode-time attribution study compares decoding policies, retrieved phrase memory, and several trunk modifications to isolate where generation improvements actually come from.19:T64b,The central experiment is a five-point data-scaling study on WikiText-103 raw UTF-8 bytes, using training subsets of 2, 8, 32, 128, and 512 MB, a fixed 1 MB test slice, 2 epochs, sequence length 256 bytes, and identical AdamW plus cosine recipes for both models. The attention-free reverb model wins at every data point against the parameter-matched transformer baseline. At 512 MB, the reverb model reaches 1.84 bits per byte versus 2.04 for the transformer. At 32 MB, reverb gets 2.011 bpb, while the transformer trained on the full 512 MB still only reaches 2.043 bpb, which the authors summarize as at least a 16x data advantage at equal scale. The log-log slopes are -0.074 for REVERB and -0.107 for ATTN. The paper correctly warns against reading the steeper transformer slope as superiority; it is more consistent with catching up from behind, while both curves are already flattening. Model sizes are 565,888 parameters for the byte-level reverb LM and 463,360 for the attention baseline. The paper also reports replication on a second dataset and a frozen-probe transfer study that supports the same conclusion. The generation-side result is arguably more interesting: at this small scale, text form is driven mainly by decoding policy and retrieved phrases, not by extra architectural additions to the trunk. Four pre-registered trunk additions did not help. That negative result is useful.
