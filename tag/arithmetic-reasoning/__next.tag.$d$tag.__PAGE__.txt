9:T5ab,The paper uses a controlled task: addition of three (or more) 10-digit integers, analyzing residual-stream activations at every generated digit position. It defines column-wise Raw Sum r_p, propagated carry c_p, and ground-truth digit s_p, and introduces model-side latent counterparts (predicted raw sum / predicted carry).

Method components:
- Probing: lightweight probes (logistic regression, small MLPs) decode multiple variables from a single activation vector, establishing “probe versatility.”
- Geometry: UMAP visualizations (primarily final-layer activations) reveal an Iso-Raw-Sum Trajectory (IRST): continuous fibers of constant raw sum threading through a macro-structure of digit “basins,” with micro-structure tied to carry states.
- Mechanistic model: the Noisy Quantization Model posits a continuous Carry Potential internally estimated and then discretized; noise can push it across quantization thresholds, causing “geometric slippages” across digit-basin decision boundaries.
- Validation via intervention: an inference-time dual-stream consistency method decodes local (raw-sum) and global (carry-potential) signals and enforces consistency to self-correct, supporting the claim that correct components exist latently even when output is wrong.

Compared to prior arithmetic-mechanism work, the emphasis is on a continuous-fiber + quantization-threshold explanation rather than only demonstrating probe extractability.a:T865,The setup is deliberately controlled. The authors build synthetic arithmetic datasets with prompts of the form “Please calculate a +/- b =”. They constrain operands and results to be at most 520 so that both GPT-2 XL and GPT-NeoX-20B represent each number as a single token. This design choice is important: if numbers were split into multiple tokens, tokenizer effects would interfere with any interpretation of layerwise behavior.

The analysis has three parts. First, they study when the task is recognized and when the answer is formed. After each attention and MLP update, they project the residual stream through the LM head and inspect the intermediate top predictions. The main pattern is that arithmetic-task awareness appears early, but the correct result token only becomes dominant in the final layers. This suggests early layers set up the task context, while later layers write the answer.

Second, they study how input information is propagated. By tracking intermediate predictions and their relation to input tokens, they argue that in stronger models attention blocks are more involved in carrying operand and operator information forward, whereas MLP blocks are more involved in combining that information into an answer representation. This aligns with prior observations that attention often acts as a routing mechanism and MLPs as transformation/aggregation modules, but here the claim is grounded in arithmetic behavior.

Third, they examine how operands are combined, including both two-operand and three-operand settings. The evidence suggests that the model is not merely recalling frequent operand-result pairs. Instead, one operand often behaves like an argument or conditioning signal, while the other is transformed depending on the operator and that argument. The authors interpret this as evidence for function-like processing beyond pure factual retrieval.

Compared with stronger causal tools such as activation patching or systematic ablations, the paper relies mostly on observational evidence from early decoding. That makes the method simple and interpretable, but also limits the strength of causal claims.b:T6bc,The experiments compare two decoder-only models with clearly different arithmetic ability: GPT-NeoX-20B and GPT-2 XL. For both addition and subtraction, the authors build small and large datasets; the small setting keeps operands and results within 99, and each dataset contains 500 unique queries. Across all settings, numbers are capped at 520 to preserve single-token representations. The main paper focuses on addition and reports subtraction in the appendix, noting that the qualitative patterns are similar.

There are four main findings. First, task recognition happens early, but correct answer generation happens late: intermediate logits indicate arithmetic-related processing in early and middle layers, while the correct result token usually stabilizes only near the top of the network. Second, the difference between the stronger and weaker model is not just final accuracy; it is also a difference in internal organization. GPT-NeoX-20B shows a clearer attention/MLP division of labor, while GPT-2 XL does not. Third, information propagation and result aggregation appear partially separable at the module level: attention updates more often align with input-token propagation, and MLP updates more often move the representation toward the final result. Fourth, in multi-operand settings the model behavior looks more compositional than simple token-level memorization.

This is not a benchmark-heavy paper. The evidence is mostly in layerwise trends, visualizations, and module comparisons rather than large tables of task scores. The most important experimental controls are the zero-shot setup and the single-token number constraint, because they make the interpretation of intermediate states much cleaner.c:T4bf,For a skim, read it as a mechanism paper, not as a math-reasoning benchmark.
