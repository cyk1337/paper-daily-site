Each environment consists of templated articles, a randomly sampled relational world structure, and programmatically generated multi-hop questions. The task is not single-shot QA. The model must issue search actions under a budget, inspect retrieved documents, chain evidence across turns, and answer at the end. Rewards are exactly verifiable because the world is generated by rules.

Several design choices matter. First, the pipeline is fully rule-based, so reward computation is clean and benchmark leakage is avoided. Second, environment complexity is explicitly controllable: universe size, question difficulty, and question budget are tunable. The paper further decomposes question distributions into three types—linear hops, attribute comparison, and attribute constraints—which turns the environment into an analysis tool rather than just a data source. Third, the training setup uses full RL fine-tuning of four off-the-shelf instruct models, making the study about whether the environment itself provides a strong enough optimization signal.

Compared with Search-R1-style training on real Wikipedia, the key difference is not merely synthetic versus real text. It is what behavior the environment rewards. Real-world environments mix two gains: better search policy and exploitation of stored factual knowledge. PhantomEnvironments intentionally remove the second route, concentrating learning pressure on search behavior. The ablations also show that complexity dimensions do not compose automatically. Linear-hop questions drive most of the transfer. Comparison questions help specifically on real comparison tasks. Constraint questions can backfire because rare attributes enable one-shot verbatim retrieval, letting the model bypass decomposition entirely.b:T6a7,The study trains four models with RL in PhantomEnvironments: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and Phi-4-mini-instruct. Evaluation covers six real-world multi-hop search benchmarks: HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue, SynthWorlds-RM, SynthWorlds-SM, and FRAMES.

The headline result is strong transfer from fictional environments to real benchmarks. Relative to the base models, RL-trained agents improve by about 1.7x on the Wikipedia-2018-based benchmarks and about 2.2x on the newer, harder benchmarks. The paper also compares PhantomEnvironments directly against Search-R1-style training in a real Wikipedia environment. The real environment wins on in-domain Wikipedia-2018 evaluation, which is expected because memorized knowledge helps there. But that advantage narrows on benchmarks built from newer Wikipedia snapshots, and for Qwen models it disappears. The cleanest evidence comes from the SynthWorlds real-vs-synthetic mirror pair: training on real-world data preserves or widens the RM/SM gap, while training in PhantomEnvironments closes it, consistent with less reliance on snapshot-specific memorization.

The ablations are especially informative. Linear-hop questions account for most of the transfer. Comparison questions improve performance on corresponding real-world comparison tasks. Constraint questions hurt transfer because they reward a shortcut: retrieving the gold document with a single precise query instead of decomposing the problem.
