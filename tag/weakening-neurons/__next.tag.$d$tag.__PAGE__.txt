9:T4de,The method starts from a residual-stream view of a gated MLP neuron. For a GLU neuron, the model reads from the residual stream through input-side vectors such as w_gate and w_in, and writes back along w_out. The authors compute cosine similarity between the read-side and write-side directions and use this as a functional descriptor. Strongly negative cosine indicates a weakening neuron: when the neuron detects a direction in the residual stream, it tends to write in the opposite direction. Positive cosine corresponds to strengthening-like behavior, with intermediate regions capturing weaker or conditional variants.

A useful part of the setup is the explicit treatment of gated activations. Unlike ReLU neurons, SwiGLU and GEGLU can produce both positive and negative outputs, so activation magnitude alone is not enough to characterize function. A neuron can be “on” in one sense while still writing a suppressive update. The paper then applies this IO taxonomy to nine modern LLMs, examining layerwise distributions of neuron classes, correlations with activation frequency, and causal influence via ablations. This matters because the work does not stop at static weight geometry; it tries to connect geometry to runtime behavior.a:T4a9,There are four main empirical findings. First, the same broad layerwise pattern appears across nine LLMs: early-to-middle layers contain many conditional strengthening neurons, while late layers are enriched with weakening neurons. That suggests the read-write geometry is not random but part of a stable functional organization learned during training. Second, IO functionality correlates strongly with activation frequency. Third, weakening neurons are relatively rare but activate often. Fourth, and most importantly, they have disproportionately large causal influence under ablation. Their effect persists even when their gate value is negative, which argues against simplistic notions of neuron importance based only on positive activation.

From the provided text, the paper’s emphasis is on robust structural patterns and causal trends rather than a single headline metric. The key evidence appears in the layer-distribution plots and ablation figures, especially the ones corresponding to Figs. 2, 3, 5, and 6. This is a mechanistic analysis paper, so the main question is whether the phenomenon is consistent, cross-model, and behaviorally meaningful.
