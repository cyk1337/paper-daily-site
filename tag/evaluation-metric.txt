19:T51b,Proposes the Coherence Under Commitment (CUC) evaluation paradigm with four core designs: (1) Commitment Score: c(φ) = p(φ) + p(¬φ), quantifying total probability mass allocated to decisive outcomes (endorsing either φ or ¬φ); values far below 1 indicate abstention, regardless of apparent coherence. (2) Deterministic Black-Box Elicitation Protocol: Computes p(φ) and p(¬φ) via normalized YES/NO log probabilities without sampling, eliminating sampling variance and requiring only two forward passes per example for high reproducibility and model agnosticism. (3) CUC Frontier Framework: Formalizes the empirical trade-off between coherence and commitment into a measurable frontier curve, enabling fair comparison of models optimized for different preferences on the trade-off and avoiding misclassification of high-abstention models as strong reasoners. (4) Systematic Ablation Methodology: Quantifies sensitivity to threshold selection, elicitation format (YES/NO vs. True/False), model scale, and architectural choices, to derive transferable evaluation best practices. Core differences from prior work include joint measurement of coherence and decisiveness, deterministic elicitation instead of sampling-based consistency checks, and explicit modeling of the coherence-commitment trade-off.1a:T448,The analysis uses two quantization cohorts: 28 GGUF quantizations of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, a mixture-of-experts model, and 41 quantizations of Devstral-Small-2-24B-Instruct-2512, a dense code-oriented model. Fidelity is measured against the corresponding BF16 reference, primarily as KL(p_BF16 || p_quant) over next-token distributions. The study also tests 14 variants, including perplexity ratio, delta log-perplexity, top-1 agreement, response-only scoring, longer contexts, and on-task calibration. Downstream quality is measured on coding, instruction following, tool use, math, visual math, and knowledge benchmarks. The key analytic device is a volume-direction decomposition of score changes. Volume counts how often the quantized model and the reference differ in correctness. Direction separates leapfrogs, where the quantized model is correct and the reference is not, from drops, where the reverse happens. The resulting score identity is score = ref_score + vol · (2f - 1) / N. KLD tracks volume more consistently than direction, which explains why it fails to rank near-baseline models.1b:T411,They build a three-stream comparison setup: Human continuation (H), base-model continuation (B), and instruction-/preference-trained continuation (I), all generated from the same prompts. Prompts come from splitting PubMed abstracts into two halves at a sentence boundary near the midpoint: first half is the prompt, second half is the human gold continuation. For each prompt, they generate B and I with deterministic greedy decoding (T=0, no sampling/top-k/top-p) and standardized preprocessing. The Triangulated Preference Shift (TPS) score is then computed from lexical frequency distributions across H/B/I, designed to flag tokens/words that become overrepresented in I in a way consistent with a preference-stage-induced shift, rather than being inherited from B or simply reflecting human frequency. A key design choice is triangulation: avoiding the common pitfall of only comparing I vs H (which confounds base-model bias with preference-stage effects). Windowing is used to normalize comparisons and reduce length/position effects.1c:T643,The main contribution is a new evaluation metric, Synergistic Faithfulness (F_syn). The paper does not propose a new explainer. Instead, it changes the target of evaluation. Rather than asking whether removing important features from one modality reduces confidence, it asks whether visual and textual features jointly contribute something beyond the sum of their separate effects.

The theoretical grounding comes from cooperative game theory. The authors connect the problem to the Shapley Interaction Index and focus on the 2-way Harsanyi dividend between visual and textual features. This quantity isolates the true interaction term, not just the additive combination of marginal contributions. Exact computation is intractable for modern VLMs because it requires enumerating feature subsets with complexity O(2^(m+n)).

To make the metric practical, the paper introduces a scalable surrogate based on continuous perturbation trajectories. The reported result is a Spearman correlation of 0.92 with exact SII, together with a 24x empirical speedup. The conceptual shift matters more than the exact formula: an explainer that only highlights visually salient regions but fails to capture text-image grounding should not score highly under F_syn.

The evaluation protocol also uses a Linear Mixed-effects Model to separate explainer effects from dataset and model variance. This is an important design choice because explainability benchmarks are often dominated by the particular benchmark or architecture being used. The LMM lets the authors estimate the fixed effect of each explainer more cleanly.1d:T549,The benchmark covers 3 datasets, 3 VLM architectures, and 8 explainers. The tested models include LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL-3.5. The first key empirical result is the failure mode itself: when the same explainers are evaluated on the same data using visual-only versus text-only perturbation metrics, the rankings can strongly disagree. This is the paper’s central evidence that standard unimodal faithfulness metrics collapse in multimodal settings.

For metric validity, F_syn achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.92 with exact Shapley Interaction Index computation while being 24 times faster empirically. That supports its use as a scalable surrogate.

For explainer comparison, attention-based methods perform best. AttnLRP obtains the highest LMM coefficient, with beta = 0.029. The VLM-native explainers highlighted in the paper, including LLaVA-CAM and TAM, are reported around beta = 0.008. The authors interpret this as evidence that many VLM-specific explainers overemphasize visual salience and undercapture true cross-modal interaction.

When reading the full paper, the most important details are not the final rankings but the robustness checks: how the metric behaves under different masking baselines, perturbation schedules, and patch/token granularities. Those choices determine whether the benchmark is actually reliable.1e:T536,1) Benchmark construction (HiL-Bench): starting from SWE-Bench Pro and BIRD tasks, the authors select items that top models can solve with high success when full context is restored, then inject 3–5 human-validated blockers (missing info, ambiguity, contradictions). Hidden information is stored in a blocker registry.
2) Interaction mechanism: agents get an ask_human() tool acting as an oracle; it returns information only when the question precisely targets a registered blocker, making question quality measurable.
3) Progressive discovery: blockers are designed to surface during execution and environment exploration rather than upfront inspection, forcing an iterative loop (work → hit a gap → ask → continue) and preventing degenerate “ask everything at the start” strategies.
