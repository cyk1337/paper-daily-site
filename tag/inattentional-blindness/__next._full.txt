19:T67c,The work has two components. First is the MixRea benchmark. Each example contains a story context with implicit information, a hypothetical question with explicit information, and four candidate event sets. Each candidate consists of two events, and the correct choice must satisfy both the explicit query and the latent contextual cues. The benchmark is built on Possible Stories, which is a good substrate because multiple questions share the same context and answer options, making it easier to recombine explicit and implicit constraints in a controlled way. The authors filter conflicting question pairs, then use GPT-4o for rewriting, pairing, validation, and quality control, producing 2,246 multiple-choice questions across 9 reasoning types with varied explicit/implicit information distributions.

Second is PRCP, Potential Relation Completion Prompting. Instead of asking the model to simply reason longer, PRCP explicitly asks it to surface possible latent relations before making the final choice. The paper’s error analysis suggests that many failures happen early: the model never completes the hidden causal relation needed to connect the context to the task. PRCP is designed to externalize that step. Compared with standard CoT, it is more targeted at relation elicitation; compared with one-shot prompting, it reduces the tendency to answer by shallow pattern matching. The prompt itself is not the main contribution. Its real role is causal diagnosis: if performance improves when latent relation completion is forced, then the bottleneck is likely in cue retrieval and multi-source integration rather than only in final answer selection.1a:T58f,The benchmark is evaluated on 21 models from 7 families, including Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, DeepSeek-R1/V3, GPT-4o/4o-mini, Claude 3.7, Qwen variants, LLaMA 3.x, and Gemma 2/3. The headline result is that performance is low across the board. Even the best model, Gemini 2.5 Pro, reaches only 42.8% consistency, which indicates that explicit-implicit integration remains unreliable even for strong reasoning-oriented models. Reasoning models generally outperform non-reasoning ones, but not by enough to remove the failure mode, suggesting that longer or more polished reasoning traces do not automatically translate into better use of implicit cues.

PRCP outperforms both CoT and one-shot prompting more consistently. The abstract does not expose the full gain table, but the full text presents the improvement as broad rather than model-specific. The authors also run ablations on three additional tasks involving explicit and implicit information, and similar failures persist there, which argues against MixRea being a narrow dataset artifact. The error analysis is the most informative part: models often understand the explicit question and can restate the context, yet fail to connect latent causal cues to the task objective.
