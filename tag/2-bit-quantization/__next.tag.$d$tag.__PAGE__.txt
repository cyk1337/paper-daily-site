9:T72b,RoLaQ combines rotation learning and range calibration. The starting point is an error analysis: if the dequantized latent differs from the original by e, then the induced key and value projection errors are governed by quadratic forms involving W_K and W_V. The authors trace-normalize the two projection energies and sum them into a joint matrix H, which measures how sensitive each latent direction is to shared K/V distortion. Empirically, the leading half of H's eigenvectors captures 62% to 75% of the projection energy across models and layers, so RoLaQ restricts rotation learning to a compact subspace defined by this joint geometry rather than searching over the full latent space.

The second ingredient is coupled threshold-gain learning. RoLaQ does not keep the quantizer range fixed while only optimizing the rotation. Instead, it jointly learns a per-layer threshold whose value also determines the reconstruction gain. This matters because at 2 bits, the rotation changes the latent distribution seen by the quantizer, while the quantization boundaries and dequantization scale jointly determine the effective reconstruction response. Optimizing only one side leaves a mismatch between assignment and readout behavior.

Training uses frozen model weights. Supervision comes from two sources: a prediction-level KL to a BF16 teacher over next-token distributions, and an attention-output reconstruction loss that directly constrains layerwise attention outputs. The latter is important because final-logit supervision alone can hide harmful intermediate distortions. On the systems side, the serving implementation packs the latent cache into INT2, fuses cache writes, optimizes the attention path, and absorbs the read-side rotation into MLA projections so that inference does not pay an extra standalone rotation cost.a:T618,The evaluation covers three MLA models: DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Moonlight-16B-A3B, and MiniCPM3-4B, across six benchmarks. The main result is that RoLaQ achieves the best average accuracy among compared 2-bit methods both with and without token protection. Without token protection, it improves over the strongest baseline by 1.1 to 3.8 points on average, which is a meaningful gain at this compression level.

The long-context result is also important. On RULER retrieval at 8K and 16K, the gains persist beyond the 4K calibration context, suggesting that the method is not merely overfitting a short calibration window but is improving the underlying quantization geometry.

The serving numbers are strong for the target setting. On DeepSeek-V2-Lite with a 256K input on a single A100 80GB, the packed 2-bit cache reaches 3.96x the BF16 cache capacity at the same memory budget and 2.51x decode throughput, while also reducing first-token tail latency. Calibration itself takes about 30 minutes on one A100, so the offline cost is modest.

The ablations are the most informative part. Rotation-only and range-only variants both underperform joint optimization, supporting the claim that error direction and reconstruction scale must be calibrated together. The K/V-guided subspace outperforms less structured rotation search, which validates the MLA-specific geometry argument.
