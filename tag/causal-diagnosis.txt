18:T61c,The framework decomposes each composite multimodal task into unit tasks and represents their dependencies with a structural causal model. Each unit task targets a relatively isolated capability, and directed edges encode prerequisite relations. During evaluation, each prerequisite can be left unassisted, supplied with a verified correct answer, or supplied with a plausible incorrect answer. This yields three capability metrics. NC measures natural performance without assistance. IC measures intrinsic capability when prerequisites are corrected, reducing contamination from upstream cascading errors. RC measures resilience when prerequisites are wrong, showing whether the model follows or resists misleading intermediate information. The paper also defines N-Score and S-Score for causal contribution. N-Score measures how much success is lost when one prerequisite is degraded, corresponding to necessity. S-Score measures how much failure is recovered when one prerequisite is supplied, corresponding to sufficiency. These metrics adapt probabilities of causation to MLLM evaluation without assuming monotonicity, which matters because wrong premises can sometimes improve answers through prompt effects or spurious correlations. The benchmark, CADET, contains 10 composite tasks decomposed into 46 unit tasks and more than 33,000 unit-task questions. It covers perception, spatial, temporal, and cognitive capabilities. Each question has human-created and verified correct answers plus plausible incorrect answers, enabling the three-state interventions.19:T4e6,The paper evaluates 12 frontier MLLMs. The main result is that end-to-end accuracy hides very different failure structures. At the capability level, cognitive tasks have the lowest natural score, NC=0.41, but rise to IC=0.72 when correct prerequisites are supplied, becoming the second strongest category under intervention. This removes 54% of natural cognitive errors, suggesting many apparent reasoning failures originate upstream. Spatial and temporal tasks remain harder: even with correct prerequisites, they retain 72%–75% of their errors. When incorrect prerequisites are injected, perception and spatial tasks degrade across models with RC below NC, while cognitive tasks improve in 10 of 12 models, showing non-monotonic behavior and warning against simple assumptions about prerequisite effects. The N-Score and S-Score analyses show sparse bottlenecks: a small number of critical prerequisites dominate each composite task. The single highest-S-Score prerequisite captures 84% of the gain from supplying all prerequisites, and it rarely coincides with the prerequisite that has the lowest NC.
