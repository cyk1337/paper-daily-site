1b:T51a,The baseline Sinkhorn matrix optimizer repeatedly normalizes the rows and columns of a matrix gradient and applies a scalar update scale. It is attractive because it removes moment state for dense two-dimensional weights. SAGE-like hybrids typically keep lightweight state for vocabulary tensors and use Sinkhorn for dense matrices. The paper shows that this dense-model role assignment breaks for MoE experts. For expert e, the gradient G_{e,t} is drawn from a changing conditional distribution p(x | e is selected by top-k routing), not from the full minibatch distribution. Since Sinkhorn normalization is nonlinear, Sinkhorn(E[G_t]) and E[Sinkhorn(G_t)] can behave differently. MESH addresses this by maintaining a hidden first-moment signal H_t for expert gradients and applying Sinkhorn to H_t rather than to G_t. Non-expert matrices and vocabulary tensors keep the SAGE/Sinkhorn hybrid treatment. The expert momentum is maintained through the gradient-buffer lifecycle instead of being stored as an explicit optimizer-state tensor. MESH-B adds a coarse block/neuron inverse-RMS multiplier, giving a limited form of adaptive scaling.
