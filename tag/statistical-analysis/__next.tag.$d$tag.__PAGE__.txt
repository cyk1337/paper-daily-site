9:T82f,The work consists of two benchmark components plus a unified execution setup. Stat-Closed contains 404 questions spanning 18 statistical topics, with multiple formats: multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, decision-making, and practical application. The practical-application subset is the key design choice: each instance is a triplet of statistical question, dataset, and ground-truth function parameters, so the model must interact with the provided toolkit rather than answer purely in prose.

The toolkit, SAToolKit, exposes 35 callable statistical functions covering descriptive statistics, hypothesis testing, regression, causal inference, survival analysis, high-dimensional analysis, and more. This lets the benchmark evaluate three distinct abilities in a controlled way: selecting the right procedure, specifying valid parameters, and interpreting the returned results.

For data construction, the authors aggregate material from textbooks, prior benchmarks, and public exam-style resources. To mitigate contamination, they use a modify-and-verify pipeline: questions answered correctly by Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct are flagged as potentially leaked, then manually perturbed by rewriting stems, changing distractors, shuffling options, or inverting conditions. Only items that at least one model subsequently fails, or that experts explicitly validate as high quality, are retained. This is not a formal guarantee against leakage, but it is stronger than naive collection from public sources.

Stat-Open contains 30 open-ended tasks adapted from mathematical modeling competitions such as MCM/ICM, CUMCM, and MAS. These require end-to-end data cleaning, exploratory analysis, model building, and structured report generation. Since there is no single gold answer, the authors use structured outputs and an LLM-as-Judge protocol, with claimed human alignment validation. On the execution side, Stat-Closed is run mainly through LangChain MCP with SAToolKit, and the paper also compares several agent frameworks including Qwen Agent, CrewAI, AutoGen, and Smolagents.a:T488,The headline numbers are straightforward. On Stat-Closed, GPT-5.1 reaches only 68.6% accuracy, while the strongest open model reported, Qwen2.5-72B, gets 60.6%. On Stat-Open, the best agent framework achieves an average score of 61.86. The paper also reports a Friedman test with p = 2.87 × 10^-9, indicating that the performance differences across systems are statistically significant.

More interesting than the absolute numbers is the structure of the evaluation. The benchmark separates easier knowledge-centric formats from practical tool-based tasks and then from open-ended modeling. That layered design helps identify where models fail: conceptual recall, procedural execution, or long-horizon analytical planning. The comparison across agent frameworks is also informative, because it shows that the same underlying model can move noticeably depending on orchestration and tool-calling setup.
