18:T9f4,方法分两步。先是理论重写，再是工程化校准。

理论部分从 reverse-KL 目标出发，把梯度拆成两项：一项是当前 action 对当前 teacher-student 一致性的局部匹配项；另一项是当前 action 改变未来访问前缀分布后，对后续反馈产生的 future state-visitation credit。常见 sampled-token OPD 只保留前者。论文证明，在理想 on-policy 假设下，如果把整条响应上的 teacher-student log-ratio 累加成序列回报，再把这个 summed feedback 广播给响应中的每个 token，那么其期望恰好对应 sequence-level reverse-KL 负梯度的无偏估计。这个结论很关键，因为它说明“把后续反馈分给早期 token”不是启发式，而是和原目标一致。

但直接广播原始序列分数在实践里会塌。作者指出有两个原因。其一，越靠后的 token 越处在 student 自己生成出来的偏移轨迹上，teacher feedback 可靠性下降。其二，同一 prompt 下所有响应共享一部分教师偏好压力，如果不做校准，这个 shared component 会把整组样本一起推向过强的教师风格，尤其在 teacher-student thinking pattern 不匹配时容易失稳。

因此 GR-OPD 做了两个校准。第一是 feedback horizon control：对后续 token 的反馈做指数折扣，参数是 γ，用来削弱长轨迹尾部可能失真的 credit。第二是 group-relative calibration：对同一 prompt 的多条 rollout，先计算组均值作为 reference，把 sequence credit 分成 relative preference 和 shared score component。前者表示教师在同 prompt 下更偏好哪条响应；后者表示整组响应共同承受的教师压力。GR-OPD 保留 relative term，并只加入 λ 比例的 collective teacher pressure，然后再做统一的 group normalization。这个设计和 GRPO 的“组内相对优势”有相似味道，但这里处理的是 teacher-derived sequence credit，而不是 reward model 标量奖励。

从机制上看，γ 控制 credit 的时间传播长度，λ 控制教师公共压力进入更新的强度。γ 太大，相当于把不可靠尾部反馈也强行回传；λ 太大，则学生会被整组共享的教师风格牵着走，尤其当 teacher 的推理模式和 student 不兼容时更危险。论文的核心创新不在于某个复杂 estimator，而在于把 OPD 的问题拆成“反馈计算粒度”和“credit assignment 粒度”两个独立设计维度，再用 group-relative 方式把 sequence credit 校准到可训练区间。19:Ta49,The method has two layers: a theoretical reformulation and a practical calibration scheme.

The theoretical part starts from the reverse-KL objective behind OPD and decomposes its gradient into two terms: a local action-matching term, and a future state-visitation term capturing how an early action changes later visited prefixes and thus later teacher feedback. Standard sampled-token OPD keeps only the local term. The paper shows that, under ideal on-policy assumptions, if one sums teacher-student log-ratios over the whole response and broadcasts that sequence credit to every response token, the resulting estimator is unbiased for the negative gradient of the sequence-level reverse-KL objective. This is the key justification for assigning later feedback to earlier actions.

In practice, however, broadcasting raw sequence scores is unstable. The paper identifies two reasons. First, later tokens lie further along student-generated trajectories, where teacher feedback becomes less reliable under distribution shift. Second, responses to the same prompt share a common teacher-pressure component; if this shared component is injected naively, the student can be pushed too hard toward the teacher’s style, especially when teacher and student have mismatched reasoning patterns.

GR-OPD therefore adds two calibrations. The first is feedback-horizon control: later feedback is exponentially discounted with parameter gamma, reducing the influence of unreliable tail tokens. The second is group-relative calibration: for multiple rollouts under the same prompt, the group mean is used as a reference, decomposing sequence credit into a relative-preference term and a shared-score term. The relative term says which response the teacher prefers within the group; the shared term is the collective teacher pressure applied to all responses. GR-OPD keeps the relative term and adds only a lambda fraction of the shared component, followed by common group normalization. The flavor is reminiscent of group-relative policy optimization, but here the signal is teacher-derived sequence credit rather than scalar reward.

Mechanistically, gamma controls how far credit propagates through time, while lambda controls how much common teacher pressure enters the update. Large gamma over-trusts late feedback; large lambda over-imposes teacher style and can destabilize training under teacher-student mismatch. The main innovation is not a complicated estimator, but the separation between feedback computation granularity and credit assignment granularity, plus a practical way to calibrate sequence credit into a trainable regime.1a:T60d,The experiments cover five Qwen3 teacher-student settings and six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, using macro Avg@K as the main metric. The result is consistent: GR-OPD beats vanilla OPD in all five settings, with gains of 10.9 and 11.5 points in two matched-thinking-pattern cases. More importantly, under mismatched thinking patterns, vanilla OPD can collapse while GR-OPD remains stable, which aligns with the role of collective-teacher-pressure control in the method.

The ablations are the most informative part.
