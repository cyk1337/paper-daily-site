18:T871,方法分两部分：测量协议和缓解方法。

先看测量。作者在训练环中每 20 step 对 OOD evaluation set 做一次双 judge 评估：一个是训练时使用的 proxy judge，另一个是更强、且跨家族的 gold judge。这里“跨家族”很关键，目的是降低与训练 judge 同源偏差带来的假阳性。如果只是 judge 有固定偏差，gold 曲线应整体平移；而作者观察到的是 proxy 持续上升、gold 先升后降，这更符合 reward hacking 而不是静态偏差。实验用了两个独立 train→eval 配对：RubricHub-Medical → HealthBench-Hard，以及 RubricHub-Science → ResearchQA。

缓解方法是 Rubric Dropout。训练时对每个 prompt 的 rubric，随机丢弃一部分 criterion，再用剩余 criterion 计算 reward。这样策略在不同 step 面对的是不断变化的子 rubric，无法稳定利用某个固定 criterion，尤其是那些跨 prompt 重复出现的模板化 criterion，比如“结构清晰”“语言明确”。这个设计的优点是简单：只改 reward function 一行，不增加 judge 调用次数，只有一个超参数 f，即 dropout fraction。

关键实现点在于和 GRPO 的兼容。GRPO 依赖同一 prompt 的多条 rollout 之间 reward 可比较。如果每条 rollout 用不同子 rubric，group-relative advantage 就会被污染。作者因此采用 group-shared masking：同一个 rollout group 共享一张 dropout mask。附录里给出推导，说明在这种设置下 reward normalizer 会在 advantage 中抵消；在 group standardization 之前，dropout 主要表现为对期望 advantage 的缩放，以及对依赖单一 criterion 的更新起到方差正则作用。这个解释很重要，因为它说明 Rubric Dropout 不是在“加噪声碰碰运气”，而是在系统性削弱对单一可利用 criterion 的过拟合。

作者还把 Rubric Dropout 与 POW3R 风格的 criterion reweighting 做了对比。后者试图按训练 usefulness 重加权 criterion，但在这里反而更容易把优化压力集中到少数可利用 criterion 上，因此可能加剧 hacking。全文实验也支持这一点。19:T866,The method has two parts: an in-loop measurement protocol and a mitigation.

For measurement, every 20 training steps the model is evaluated on an OOD benchmark using two judges: the training-time proxy judge and a stronger gold judge from a different family. The cross-family choice is important because it reduces the chance that the observed gap is just shared bias. If the proxy judge were merely biased, the gold curve would be shifted but not necessarily turn downward while the proxy keeps improving. The paper uses two independent train-to-eval pairs: RubricHub-Medical to HealthBench-Hard, and RubricHub-Science to ResearchQA.

The mitigation is Rubric Dropout. At each training step, a random fraction f of the rubric criteria is dropped before computing reward. The policy therefore never sees exactly the same rubric twice, which makes it harder to exploit any fixed criterion, especially generic templates repeated across prompts such as “well-organized” or “clear language.” The appeal is operational simplicity: one line in the reward function, one hyperparameter, and no extra judge calls.

The crucial systems detail is GRPO compatibility. In GRPO, multiple rollouts for the same prompt must be scored on a comparable basis. If each rollout were graded with a different sub-rubric, the group-relative advantage would be corrupted. The paper solves this with group-shared masking: one dropout mask per rollout group. The appendix shows that under this scheme the reward normalizer cancels out of the advantage, and before group standardization the main effect of dropout is to rescale expected advantage while acting as a variance regularizer against updates that rely too heavily on any single criterion. That makes the method more than just noise injection; it is a targeted way to reduce overfitting to exploitable proxy features.

The paper also compares Rubric Dropout with POW3R-style criterion reweighting. The argument is that reweighting can inadvertently concentrate optimization pressure on a smaller set of exploitable criteria, which may worsen hacking rather than reduce it. The experiments support that concern.1a:T67b,The most important empirical result is the divergence between proxy and gold curves during training. Using Qwen3-8B with GRPO, the paper observes this on two independent domains. On the medical setup, the proxy-minus-gold gap grows from 29% to 44%. On the science setup evaluated on ResearchQA, the gold score drops by 22 points from its peak while the proxy score continues to rise. That is strong evidence that the standard rubric-RL recipe can over-optimize the proxy out of distribution.

In the fixed three-way comparison, the paper evaluates base training against Rubric Dropout with f = 30% and f = 50%. Both dropout settings outperform base on the gold score at every matched checkpoint within the comparison window. The gain is about +1 to +2 points on HealthBench-Hard and +6 to +7 points on ResearchQA. At the same time, both hacking measures improve, and the paper reports no in-domain cost. That last point matters because anti-hacking interventions often trade away the optimized training metric.

A sweep over dropout fraction shows a broad usable region from 20% to 50%, with the sign turning only at 60%. That suggests the method is not overly fragile to hyperparameter choice. By contrast, POW3R-style reweighting performs below base in the medical sweep and yields the highest overclaim fraction, consistent with the idea that criterion reweighting can amplify proxy exploitation.

The paper also reports consistent window-mean improvements at two model sizes. The presentation emphasizes consistency of direction across domains and scales rather than a single best checkpoint, which is the right way to argue for a training-dynamics claim.1b:T469,1) Diagnostic metric: Proposes dimension-corrected Principal-Subspace Overlap. For each pretrained linear weight, SVD is performed to extract top-k left and right singular vectors as principal subspaces. The stepwise realized update (ΔW, including effects of optimizer, learning rate, and momentum) is decomposed into four orthogonal blocks, and the expected overlap under an isotropic null hypothesis is subtracted to obtain excess overlap. 2) Optimization method: Proposes GCPO (Geometrically Constrained Policy Optimization). During each rollout iteration, a hard bilateral orthogonal constraint is applied to the effective update of each adapted layer—forcing updates to lie in the bilateral orthogonal complement of the selected pretrained principal subspaces. This guarantees that the layer's response to inputs in the pretrained principal input subspace remains completely unchanged, while retaining a high-dimensional complement space for task adaptation. The constraint is complementary to KL regularization: KL controls policy divergence in output space, while GCPO controls feasible directions of parameter updates.1c:T4cb,RSTG first analyzes GRPO, OPD, and SFT under a shared policy-gradient view. GRPO estimates advantages from group rewards; when all rewards in a group are identical, the advantage disappears. OPD minimizes reverse KL from the student to the teacher on student-generated trajectories and can be written as a token-level advantage based on the teacher-student log-probability gap. This dense signal is noisy and asymmetric because many student tokens have low teacher probability, producing mostly negative advantages. RSTG uses three controls. First, sample selection applies OPD only when the student fails and the teacher succeeds, focusing on negative zero-variance prompts and weighting the signal by teacher mastery. The paper reports that using only 3.63% of the full data in this way outperforms standard OPD over all samples. Second, token selection restricts updates to high-entropy student tokens or tokens with large teacher-student divergence, reducing noisy gradients and slowing premature teacher imitation. Third, auxiliary SFT uses teacher-generated correct trajectories on negative zero-variance prompts, adding a global positive trajectory signal that OPD lacks when it conditions on erroneous student prefixes.1d:T77e,BiCAA builds a bidirectional process reward. The paper argues that a single process signal is insufficient. Forward-looking rewards encourage locally helpful search, but they do not distinguish necessary steps from redundant ones. Pure hindsight credit can identify pivotal decisions after the fact, but it does not tell the agent whether a step actually improved the information state when it happened. BiCAA combines both.

The first branch is forward solvability gain. For a question with ground-truth answer, each retrieval step is treated as incremental evidence acquisition. At step t, the method compares the model’s conditional log-likelihood of the correct answer given the trajectory prefix up to t versus up to t-1. This marginal change measures whether the newly retrieved evidence makes the answer more solvable. In other words, it is an immediate estimate of how much that search action improved the model’s belief in the correct answer.

The second branch is hindsight success criticality. This branch looks back over the full trajectory and estimates whether a step was actually pivotal for reaching a correct final answer, rather than merely producing a small local gain.
