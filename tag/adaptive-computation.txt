17:T4d9,SVR makes the model emit, at every turn, a solution, a discrete verdict, and a confidence estimate. The verdict is one of Correct, Incorrect, or Unsure. At inference, the trajectory stops only when the verdict is Correct and the confidence exceeds a threshold; otherwise, the next prompt includes the original problem, the previous solution, and the model’s own self-verification. Correctness labels are used during training to build rewards, but they are never inserted into refinement prompts and are not available to the inference controller. Training uses fixed-horizon trajectories with a GRPO-style objective. Per-turn solve, verification, and format scores are averaged into a trajectory return, while optimization is applied to the final completion tokens rather than treating intermediate turns as separate samples. The confidence reward uses a Brier-style calibration term, penalizes confident mistakes, and encourages recognizing both error states and correct states that are eligible for stopping. Compared with fixed-budget refinement, the main change is not longer reasoning but learned answer retention. Compared with verifier-guided methods, the deployment-time information boundary is reduced to the model’s own outputs.18:T401,The experiments use Qwen3.5-2B on seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including arithmetic search, grade-school word problems, competition math, and multi-step reasoning. This creates a two-level structure: an outer temporal evolution over environment steps, and an inner iterative refinement loop for the next latent state. Instead of using a stack of distinct layers once, the model reuses the same transition operator multiple times.

A central technical ingredient is a spectrally constrained state-retention parameterization, adapted from recent work on stabilizing looped transformers. The intuition is straightforward: part of the recurrent update is written as a contractive linear retention term, combined with input injection and transformer nonlinearities. The goal is not to encode exact physics, but to keep the latent dynamics bounded as the number of inner-loop iterations grows. This is a meaningful departure from standard deep transformers. In ordinary residual stacks, stability is mostly handled implicitly through initialization, normalization, and optimization. In a looped model, the same block is reapplied many times, so the spectral properties of the induced state transition become directly tied to whether deeper recurrence remains stable.

The paper also emphasizes adaptive computation. Different transition steps have different difficulty, so a fixed amount of inner-loop compute is wasteful on easy steps and insufficient on hard ones. LoopWM allows the number of refinement iterations to vary, effectively allocating more compute where the transition is harder. That is a substantial modeling difference from conventional fixed-depth world models.

Conceptually, this is not a diffusion world model and not simply a replacement for RSSM or autoregressive transformer dynamics. It adds a shared-depth computational dimension that is orthogonal to scaling width, data, or context. 第一部分是 FP-MGM，也就是 fixed-point masked generative model。作者把原始 masked denoiser 中间的一部分 Transformer 层替换成一个共享参数的 fixed-point block。这个 block 会被重复应用若干次，直到达到某种平衡态，平衡态表示作为该层输出。这样做有两个直接效果：一是参数量下降，因为多层显式堆叠变成了同一模块反复使用；二是有效深度不再固定，可以通过 solver iteration 数控制。作者把这个思路分别实例化到文本 MDLM 和图像 MaskGIT 上，得到 FP-MDLM 和 FP-MaskGIT。

第三部分是 three-state reuse（3SR）。固定点模型常见的提速方法是 warm-start：用上一步的 fixed-point 解作为下一步求解初值。但在 MGM 里，上一时刻的解并不是对所有 token 都同样可信，因为 token 状态变了。作者把位置分成三类：未变化且可见的 token，仍然被 mask 的 token，以及新揭示出来的 token。3SR 对这三类位置采用不同的复用强度：对未变化可见 token 几乎完全复用上一步解；对仍 mask 的位置部分复用；对新揭示 token 更依赖当前输入注入。这个设计很贴合 masked generation 的离散状态机，而不是把 DEQ 的 warm-start 机械照搬过来。

First, the paper introduces FP-MGMs. The middle part of the masked denoiser is replaced with a shared fixed-point block. Instead of stacking many distinct transformer layers, the model repeatedly applies the same block and uses the equilibrium representation as the output. This reduces parameters and allows the effective depth to be controlled by the number of solver iterations. The idea is instantiated for text with MDLM, producing FP-MDLM, and for images with MaskGIT, producing FP-MaskGIT.

Second, the paper adds a cross-step consistency loss, L_CONS. The motivation is that masked generation has abrupt discrete changes between neighboring denoising steps: some positions stay visible, some remain masked, and some become newly revealed. These abrupt token-state changes induce non-smooth hidden-state transitions, which are especially harmful under low sampling budgets. L_CONS aligns the representation of a noisier student state with that of a cleaner teacher state. The paper frames this as a form of cross-time self-distillation. Its role is to make the denoiser’s temporal evolution smoother and to sharpen masked-token predictions when only limited compute is available.

Third, the paper proposes three-state reuse (3SR) for warm-starting the fixed-point solver. Standard fixed-point models often initialize the next solve from the previous equilibrium. In masked generation, that previous solution is not equally trustworthy for all token positions because the token states have changed. 3SR therefore treats three token categories differently: unchanged visible tokens, still-masked tokens, and newly revealed tokens. Unchanged visible tokens reuse the previous solution strongly, still-masked tokens reuse it partially, and newly revealed tokens rely more on the current input injection. This is a masked-generation-specific adaptation of warm-starting rather than a direct import from DEQ models.

Finally, the paper shows that pretrained MDLM checkpoints can be converted into FP-MDLMs through a short distillation stage, avoiding full retraining. That makes the approach more practical for research iteration.

Relative to prior work, the key distinction is that the paper does not mainly optimize the reveal schedule or skip masked-token computation. The router is a two-layer MLP that reads the current hidden state of each token and emits a sigmoid halting probability. This probability softly gates the residual update of the next block: when the gate is high, the token largely skips that layer’s attention and FFN update; when it is low, the model behaves like a standard Transformer.
