b:T4e8,A three-regime taxonomy is proposed under the thesis that channel location determines auditability. Two key constructs are defined: a screen is any quantity computed before training that predicts transfer magnitude; a carrier is the component whose removal, replacement, or neutralization abolishes transfer. The three regimes are: 1) Initialization-dependent body channel: traits transfer via activation patterns in non-output weights, predictable before training by coverage — the cosine similarity between the student's first distillation gradient step and the teacher's fine-tuning displacement vector; 2) Convergent unembedding / vocabulary geometry channel: single-token traits in pretrained LLMs transfer probability mass via high-cosine neighbours in the output matrix, independent of initialization, so all initialization-alignment screens including coverage fail; 3) A single pretrained model can host both channels, and the active channel is a property of the signal rather than the model — conditional behaviours route through the body channel, while single-token preferences route through readout geometry. A relocation experiment that randomizes the readout restores initialization gating, confirming channel location is signal-dependent.c:T657,The setup is controlled and fairly clean. The authors use two 7B instruction-tuned teachers: Llama-2-7B-Chat and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. They first extract a refusal direction using a contrastive harmful-vs-harmless prompt set, following prior work on linear refusal features in the residual stream. For Llama they select layer 15 as the most separable direction; for Qwen they select layer 18. During generation, they inject the direction across a band of layers: 10-17 for Llama and 9-16 for Qwen. Negative steering coefficients suppress refusal, with α in {-0.05, -0.10, -0.15, -0.20, -0.25}.

To isolate subliminal transfer from direct contamination, they sample 1,000 benign prompts from the normal_benign_train split of the Mechanistic Anomaly Detection dataset. Teachers generate responses to these benign prompts under each steering strength using greedy decoding. Repetitive or failed generations are filtered out, and only benign-looking outputs are retained as distillation data. The student is then trained by imitation on this benign-only teacher data, without ever seeing jailbreak or harmful prompts during training.

Evaluation is performed on 100 JailbreakBench prompts, with GPT-4.1 as the judge. The key metric is a transfer ratio τ that relates the teacher’s behavioral degradation to the student’s degradation. This gives a direct way to ask: for one unit of teacher compromise, how much harmful behavioral tendency is inherited by the student? The strength of the design is the separation of three stages: controlled teacher compromise, benign-only distillation, and independent harmful evaluation.d:T4de,The main result is that transfer is robust but scales differently by model family. Llama-2 shows a threshold-like pattern: once steering passes roughly α≈-0.15, the transfer ratio rises into the τ=0.25-0.32 range. That suggests limited transfer under mild compromise, followed by a more stable leakage regime after the teacher’s refusal behavior is sufficiently weakened. Qwen2.5 shows a smoother and larger transfer curve, with τ reaching as high as 0.61.

The experimental ingredients are: 148 harmful and 165 harmless prompts for refusal-direction extraction, 1,000 benign prompts for teacher-data generation, and 100 JailbreakBench prompts for downstream safety evaluation. Based on the provided full-text excerpt, the strongest evidence is about the qualitative scaling shape and the magnitude range of τ, rather than fine-grained absolute calibration. The paper appears more focused on characterizing transfer behavior than on exhaustive training ablations.

In practical terms, the result says that benign-only distillation does not guarantee benign-only behavioral inheritance. The leakage is neither zero nor a universal constant.
