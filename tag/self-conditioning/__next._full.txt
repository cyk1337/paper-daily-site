19:T6bd,The method has three layers. The first is empirical diagnosis. The authors show systematically that in ELF, more repetition often coincides with lower Gen-PPL. When repeated spans are stripped out, model rankings can reverse; the smallest model looks best largely because it repeats the most. The second layer is mechanism analysis. They rewrite self-conditioning as a feedback recurrence: the denoiser predicts a clean estimate, and that estimate becomes part of the next-step input. Linearizing this loop around sampling trajectories and inspecting the Jacobian reveals a dominant slowest-contracting mode that is nearly one-dimensional and strongly aligned with repetitive trajectories. They also run direct causal probes: increasing feedback strength alone raises repetition while lowering Gen-PPL, which supports the claim that the loop creates the pathology that the metric then rewards. The third layer is the fix, ACE (Attractor-Contrast-Escape). ACE subtracts a single fixed direction d from the self-conditioning feedback at every step. That direction is estimated without labels, auxiliary models, or retraining: collect feedback states from high-repetition trajectories and low-repetition trajectories, then take the difference of their means. The paper also derives a closed-form usable steering window so the intervention is strong enough to escape the basin but not so strong that it destroys semantics. A key design choice is where to intervene. ACE acts on the latent feedback state, not at token decoding, because the repetition is already formed upstream in continuous space before argmax readout. That makes it fundamentally different from repetition penalties, n-gram blocking, or decode-time heuristics.1a:T5ab,Three result groups matter most. First is metric pathology. ELF-B reports a Gen-PPL of 19.5, but after removing repetition the score rises to 27.7, enough to change the apparent ranking against larger models. Sample-level analyses also show that more repetitive generations receive better GPT-2-based perplexity scores, confirming that the scorer favors the degenerate outputs. Second is mechanism validation. Increasing self-conditioning feedback strength increases repetition and further lowers Gen-PPL, which supports the proposed causal chain. The Jacobian analysis shows that the problem is dominated by a single slow-contracting direction, and sharper sampling settings deepen the basin. Third is ACE performance. A direction estimated once on the smallest model transfers well across inference settings and model scales; its cosine similarity to per-configuration re-estimated directions is reported between 0.82 and 0.96. Under the paper’s defect-controlled evaluation, ACE reaches human-clean text at 1.5x to 5x lower compute cost while maintaining competitive quality on accepted samples. The same direction-estimation recipe also recovers useful controls on Plaid and LangFlow, suggesting the issue is not ELF-specific but tied to self-conditioned continuous DLMs more broadly. The training procedure uses two forward passes through the same denoiser. In the first pass, the model takes the current masked/noisy sequence and predicts a clean-state distribution. In the second pass, that prediction is detached and fed back as a self-conditioning signal together with the same current input, producing a refined prediction. The loss is applied only to the second-pass output. Several design choices matter. First, there is no recurrent latent-state pathway and no auxiliary reference model, so the architectural change is small. Second, detaching the first-pass prediction is important: it turns the first pass into a fixed draft for refinement rather than a shortcut gradient path. Third, the paper argues that post-training is a distinct regime from training from scratch. In continuous diffusion, self-conditioning is often dropped stochastically so the model learns both conditioned and unconditioned behavior. Here, once a pretrained MDM already produces informative clean estimates, dropping the self-conditioning signal becomes counterproductive because it mixes refinement updates with weaker unconditional ones. The paper therefore advocates full self-conditioning during adaptation. Relative to prior discrete self-conditioning approaches, the claimed differences are: no extra architectural modules, no auxiliary model, and direct adaptation from an existing pretrained MDM checkpoint.1d:T4de,The evaluation spans four discrete-generation domains: natural language, molecules, genomics, and discretized images.
