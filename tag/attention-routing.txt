18:T48d,Routing Policy Optimization (RPO) reframes attention visibility as the exploration action.
1) Action definition (routing): visual tokens are grouped into spatial blocks. At a small set of denoising timesteps, each query block either keeps dense attention or samples additional key blocks according to the model’s native dense attention mass, producing a sparse attention route (mask).
2) Likelihood for score-function updates: routing changes the predicted velocity field v_θ(x^t,t,c). The method assigns a Gaussian likelihood to the routed velocity around the dense velocity, yielding a differentiable surrogate that scores routed transitions while updating the dense velocity network parameters.
3) Controlled two-rollout estimator: two trajectories share all non-routing sources of variation and differ only in sampled routes; the signed reward difference provides a leave-one-out advantage, reducing variance. Route proposal is non-differentiable (no gradient through the sampling pass); gradients flow through the likelihood term into the dense model. At inference, routing masks are removed and the original dense attention + deterministic sampler is used.19:T4ab,The method is called Declarative Attention (DA). The model is prompted to emit parseable control tags inside its chain-of-thought, declaring the attention scope it needs at each phase of generation. An inference-side state machine reads these tags online and constructs the attention mask dynamically. DA defines three modes. <global> allows access to the full context and is used when the model needs to survey or search broadly. <focus> commits the model to a specific region of the context, restricting attention to that region. <local> limits attention to recent generated tokens, avoiding long-context reads altogether. This is the key departure from prior sparse-attention methods: the selection signal comes from the model’s own generated reasoning trace, not from hidden-state proxy scorers, recency heuristics, historical attention statistics, or an auxiliary scan over the cache. The paper also includes a practical implementation in vLLM using block-aligned, in-place KV-cache masking compatible with FlashAttention. Another notable point is that the protocol works zero-shot on off-the-shelf models, without task-specific fine-tuning or hand-designed partitions for each benchmark.1a:T4cc,The evaluation covers 15 long-context tasks, with main results reported on Gemma-4-31B and Qwen-3.6-27B. The central finding is a favorable cost-accuracy trade-off. DA reduces average decoding attention cost by 52.0% on Gemma-4-31B with only a 1.27 percentage-point accuracy drop, and by 31.1% on Qwen-3.6-27B with a 2.75-point drop. The paper also shows positive scaling behavior: as model size increases from 4B to 31B, the accuracy gap between DA and full attention narrows, suggesting that self-declared attention depends on model capability. Absolute savings grow with context length, reaching up to 21 million attended tokens saved per response. The ablations are important. Relative to a maskless ablation, the DA-induced dynamic mask cuts attended tokens by as much as 71.1%, indicating that the savings are driven by the masking mechanism itself rather than by prompt formatting alone. On the systems side, a roofline-style analysis estimates decode wall-clock cost reductions to 0.71× of vanilla for Gemma-4-31B and 0.77× for Qwen-3.6-27B on a well-optimized serving stack. The paper is careful not to equate token savings with end-to-end speedup directly; it grounds the estimate in memory-bandwidth considerations.1b:T491,Begin with the introduction, especially the discussion of why long-context decoding is bandwidth-bound due to repeated KV-cache reads. Then read the DA protocol section closely to understand the semantics of <global>, <focus>, and <local>, and how the inference-side state machine turns generated tags into masks. In the experiments, start with the main 15-task results to get the overall cost-accuracy picture, then read the scaling section because it reveals whether the protocol improves with model capability. After that, focus on the ablations, especially the maskless comparison and any analyses of mode transitions, since those are the best tests of whether the gains come from dynamic masking rather than just extra CoT structure. Finally, read the vLLM implementation and roofline analysis to judge practical viability. Three questions are worth tracking while reading: how well declared focus regions align with actual useful context; whether the protocol can induce premature narrowing of attention; and how much additional gain might be unlocked by explicit post-training for DA. A careful pass through the core sections should take around 50 to 70 minutes.1c:T4b5,CHIAR-Former is a 4-layer hybrid transformer. For each token, the model computes spectral entropy from the DCT power spectrum of its representation and routes the token to one of three operators: DCT spectral mixing, RBF kernel mixing, or full self-attention. Low-entropy tokens are treated as smooth and sent to DCT mixing. High-entropy tokens are treated as complex and sent to attention. Mid-range tokens are initially assigned to an RBF kernel branch implemented with random Fourier features.

The paper provides a theoretical motivation for this partition: low-entropy tokens admit bounded DCT truncation error, medium-entropy tokens can be handled by kernel approximation, and high-entropy tokens benefit from dynamic cross-token projection. Relative to FNet, the key difference is selective rather than universal spectral mixing. Relative to Mixture-of-Depths or LayerSkip, the model does not skip computation but changes the operator type while preserving depth. The most important technical outcome, however, is that learned routing collapses: the router repeatedly rejects the RBF branch. The authors then embrace that result and build a DCT+Attention-only variant as the effective architecture.1d:T410,The headline number is on WikiText-103: the DCT+Attention variant reaches validation perplexity 36.54 versus 66.6 for a full-attention baseline, while using 62.5% fewer attention FLOPs. If the setup is fully matched, that is a large gain. Because the gain is so large, the first thing to verify is baseline fairness: same parameter budget, same training schedule, same context length, same optimizer, and same data exposure.

The broader experimental claim is more interesting than the single best result. The paper reports evaluations on four datasets and finds a clear operating regime: spectral routing helps more on larger, naturalistic text corpora, but loses to full attention on small datasets and synthetic pattern-matching tasks.
