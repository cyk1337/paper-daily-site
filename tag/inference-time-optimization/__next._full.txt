1b:T641,The method has three stages. First, the authors define two pooled representations for each query q. The input embedding e_in(q) is obtained by running the prompt once and mean-pooling the final-layer hidden states over the prompt tokens. The thinking embedding e_th(q) is obtained by generating a reasoning trace and mean-pooling the final-layer hidden states over the <think>...</think> span. Second, they analyze the geometry of these two sets. Across models and benchmarks, both sets show extreme conicity: vectors cluster tightly around a single mean direction. However, the mean input direction mu_in and the mean thinking direction mu_th are not aligned; the paper reports an average cosine of about 0.66 across tested settings, while within-space alignment is much closer to 1. This combination of near-one-dimensional structure plus cross-space misalignment motivates an orthogonal mapping. Third, they fit an orthogonal Procrustes transform R on a small set of correctly solved examples, minimizing the discrepancy between R e_in and e_th. At inference time, they compute e_in for a new query, form a synthetic thinking vector e_hat_th = R e_in, and inject it as a single embedding position between <think> and </think> through the inputs_embeds interface. Two design choices matter here. They restrict the map to be orthogonal rather than affine or nonlinear, which keeps the hypothesis sharp and avoids hiding capacity in an adapter. And they fit R on held-out solved examples rather than target-benchmark data, aiming to capture model geometry rather than task-specific prompting patterns.1c:T521,The study spans four model families, roughly 4B to 31B parameters, and benchmarks in math, science, and code reasoning. On the geometry side, the fitted rotation reconstructs thinking embeddings with cosine similarity above 0.96 and mean squared error below 0.05 across tested settings, which is strong evidence that the prompt-to-thinking transition is well approximated by a single matrix. On downstream accuracy, Rotate2Think improves 30 out of 32 model-benchmark combinations, indicating that the effect is not confined to one architecture or one task family. The most striking transfer result is cross-modal: a rotation fitted only on text reasoning transfers zero-shot to visual math reasoning on MATH-Vision (testmini). For Gemma-4 E4B, Base+R2T reaches 38.1%, exceeding the model’s full reasoning-mode accuracy of 34.9% on a modality never seen during fitting. That result supports the claim that the learned transform reflects a model-internal reasoning geometry rather than a dataset-specific textual template. Token-level confidence is defined using the KL divergence between the next-token distribution and the uniform distribution, so sharper distributions correspond to higher confidence. Each reasoning trace is partitioned into position-normalized bins. The method compares the average confidence near the tail with the average confidence near the head, producing a tail-minus-head confidence gain. During answer aggregation, CDG combines three signals: the number of traces supporting an answer, the mean confidence of those traces, and the dynamic confidence gain. This differs from prior confidence-weighted voting methods, which usually compress an entire trace into a static confidence score.
