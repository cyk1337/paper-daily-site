17:Tb06,技术方案围绕三个模块展开：系统化探索、基于探索轨迹的任务合成、以及更可靠的轨迹生成与建模。

第一部分是 systematic breadth-first exploration。与 Explorer 这类从 homepage 提任务再向外扩展的方法不同，AutoSurfer 直接把网站探索建模成一个带队列的 BFS 过程。队列里维护已发现页面及到达这些页面的 action trace，因此每个新页面都带着“它是怎么被发现的”这一因果路径。这样做有两个好处：一是覆盖更全，能逐层深入网站结构，而不是停留在首页可见功能；二是后续任务合成时可以直接利用到达路径，而不是只看孤立页面截图或 DOM 片段。作者还强调 knowledge propagation：探索过程中从先前页面抽取的网站知识会传递给后续页面，用来理解导航结构、避免重复探索、识别哪些元素属于同一功能簇。

第二部分是对静态与动态元素做差异化处理。静态功能，如菜单、导航栏、dropdown、设置项，适合系统遍历，因为它们定义了网站的功能骨架；动态内容，如商品列表、推荐流、搜索结果、用户生成内容，如果也做穷举，成本会迅速爆炸且收益有限。因此 AutoSurfer 对动态区域采用代表性采样，而不是完全展开。这是一个很关键的设计取舍：作者不是追求状态空间穷举，而是追求“功能覆盖最大化 / 冗余最小化”。这比 random walk 更稳，因为 random walk 容易在动态内容里浪费大量步数，却漏掉真正重要的结构性入口。

第三部分是 task synthesis with exploration trajectory。现有方法常从最终页面或局部交互反推任务，容易产生 hallucinated、模糊或不符合用户意图的任务描述。AutoSurfer 利用 BFS 过程中保留下来的完整探索轨迹，把中间状态、页面语义和最终发现的能力一起输入任务合成器。这样生成的任务不只是“这个页面能做什么”，而是“用户如何通过一串合理步骤完成什么目标”。这一步把任务 grounding 从静态页面内容提升到了过程级别。

第四部分是 trajectory generation 和 wLLM 建模。作者不仅生成任务，还基于更扎实的任务定义去生成执行轨迹，并用这些数据训练 website-specific LLM。这里的隐含假设是：对于复杂网站，通用 MLLM 的能力瓶颈部分来自对站点局部语义、导航习惯和功能组合的不熟悉；如果用系统探索得到的高质量站点数据做针对性训练，小模型也能获得明显收益。与 AgentTrek、OS-Genesis、SynthAgent 相比，AutoSurfer 的区别不在单个 prompting 技巧，而在于它把探索、任务发现、任务表述和训练数据构造连成了一个一致流程。18:T5c3,For pretraining and mid-training research, this paper matters more than a typical agent data-engineering paper. The reason is not a new model architecture, but a serious treatment of coverage in interactive data generation. In text pretraining, people already think in terms of data mixture, deduplication, and quality filtering. Web-agent data adds another axis: functional-space coverage. If the exploration stage misses deep menus, form branches, dynamic widgets, or cross-page dependencies, then later task synthesis is operating on an incomplete state space no matter how polished the prompting is. AutoSurfer’s main contribution is to elevate exploration from random walks or homepage-centered heuristics into an explicit, knowledge-carrying, website-structured process.

A second reason the paper is important is that it does not stop at generating demonstrations. It uses the generated data to train a website-specific LLM. That makes it relevant to the interface between data generation and targeted pretraining or continual training. The broader lesson is that in interactive environments, data quality is not just about whether a trajectory succeeds. It also depends on whether the task is properly grounded, whether exploration is systematic, and whether the collected transitions cover the right capability boundaries. The contribution is best viewed as data infrastructure for web agents, but one that directly touches how the training distribution is formed.19:Tb94,The method has three main components: systematic exploration, trajectory-grounded task synthesis, and more reliable trajectory generation for downstream modeling.

The first component is systematic breadth-first exploration. Unlike homepage-based methods such as Explorer, AutoSurfer models website exploration as a queue-based BFS process. The queue stores discovered pages together with the action traces used to reach them, so each page is paired with its causal access path. This has two benefits. It improves coverage by progressively expanding into deeper site structure rather than staying near the homepage, and it preserves process information that can later be used for task synthesis instead of relying on isolated screenshots or DOM fragments. The paper also emphasizes knowledge propagation: information extracted from earlier pages is passed forward to help interpret later pages, recognize navigation structure, avoid redundant exploration, and group related functionality.

The second component is differentiated handling of static and dynamic elements. Static functionality—menus, navigation bars, dropdowns, settings—is traversed systematically because it defines the structural backbone of the site. Dynamic content—product lists, recommendation feeds, search results, user-generated content—is sampled selectively rather than exhaustively. This is a key design choice. The goal is not full state-space enumeration, but high functional coverage with low redundancy. That is a better fit than random walks, which can waste many steps in dynamic regions while still missing important structural entry points.

The third component is task synthesis with exploration trajectories. Prior methods often infer tasks from a final page or a local interaction snippet, which can lead to hallucinated, ambiguous, or weakly user-aligned tasks. AutoSurfer uses the full exploration trajectory produced by BFS, including intermediate states, page semantics, and the final discovered capability, to synthesize tasks. This moves task grounding from static page content to process-level evidence. The resulting task is not merely “what this page can do,” but “what a user can accomplish through a plausible sequence of actions.”

Finally, the paper connects data generation to modeling by using the resulting tasks and trajectories to train a website-specific LLM. The underlying hypothesis is that part of the failure of general-purpose multimodal LLMs on complex websites comes from weak familiarity with site-local semantics, navigation conventions, and capability composition. If systematic exploration can produce high-quality site-specific data, then a smaller model may gain a lot from targeted training. Compared with AgentTrek, OS-Genesis, and SynthAgent, the novelty is less about a single prompting trick and more about turning exploration, task discovery, task formulation, and training-data construction into one consistent pipeline.1a:Ta1f,The most important part of the evaluation is not a single benchmark score, but whether the paper demonstrates that better exploration actually becomes better training data and then better models. First, exploration quality. AutoSurfer is compared against homepage-based and random-walk-based methods, likely using metrics such as page coverage, action coverage, functionality coverage, and redundant exploration rate. The paper’s claim is that BFS plus knowledge propagation discovers deeper site functionality while reducing repeated or wasteful traversal. The static-systematic plus dynamic-selective strategy is intended to improve the coverage-cost trade-off.

Second, task and trajectory quality. Since the paper explicitly criticizes prior methods for hallucinated or ambiguous tasks, the evaluation should include either human or model-based judgments of whether tasks are grounded, clear, and aligned with actual website capabilities, as well as whether generated trajectories are executable and complete. The expected advantage of AutoSurfer is more specific tasks, clearer preconditions, and higher trajectory reliability.

Third, and most important, is training value. The paper explicitly claims that AutoSurfer-generated data can meaningfully train a website-specific LLM. That means the work goes beyond dataset construction and includes actual model training, with comparisons against untuned general models or models trained on data from weaker generators. This is the section to inspect most carefully: model size, number of trajectories or tokens, training setup, and gains on in-site task execution. If the paper shows that a smaller wLLM approaches or surpasses a larger general model on site-specific tasks, that would be the strongest result because it would show that high-quality site data can partly substitute for generic scale.
