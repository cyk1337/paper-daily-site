9:T458,The labeling scheme is built from Monte Carlo rollouts. For selected prefixes of a generated reasoning trace, the model samples continuations, and the fraction that reach a correct answer estimates the continuation value. If the initial problem state is already unrecoverable, the trace is labeled a capability gap. If an incorrect trace remains recoverable at all evaluated prefixes, it is a near miss. If it first becomes unrecoverable partway through, it is a pit, and that transition point defines the pit step. The experiments use Qwen3 4B and 8B in thinking mode on hard math problems from DeepMath-103k and the MATH training split. Reasoning traces are split by blank lines and merged so each step has at least 400 tokens. The probe family includes a confidence probe trained on final labels, combined/value probes trained on rollout rates, a failure-type classifier reading transcoder activations, and pointer-style probes trained to predict pit steps. Baselines that use no hidden states include classifiers based on trace/problem variables and a length rule that predicts a late step from trace length.a:T468,The results show limited failure-type signal and weak localization. On incorrect 8B traces, the confidence probe remains calibrated for final correctness at the final step, but at a 0.5 cutoff it misses 81% of pits and 47% of near misses. After controlling for four trace/problem variables, the pit-versus-near-miss difference exceeds the prespecified 0.08 effect margin at 8B; at 4B it is nonzero but below the margin. A combined probe trained on rollout rates changes the failure-type ordering: for 8B, the confidence probe assigns a higher final-step score to a random pit than to a random near miss with probability 0.76, while the combined probe gives 0.40. A classifier on transcoder activations separates capability gaps from pits better than the strongest non-hidden-state baseline by 0.055 AUROC at 8B, meeting the 0.05 margin; the 4B gain is 0.049 and does not meet the margin.
