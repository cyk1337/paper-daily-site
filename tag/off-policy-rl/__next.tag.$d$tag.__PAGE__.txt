Instead of deriving another trust-region surrogate, it asks what target policy these objectives actually optimize toward when the data come from an older behavior policy. The induced target is shown to be Lambert-tempered rather than the usual exponential target from KL-regularized policy optimization. This matters because Lambert tempering can damp high-advantage samples, preventing a few stale completions from dominating the update. The behavior depends on the advantage baseline and normalization. In the positive regime, the target is pessimistic and conservative; at the boundary, it recovers the exponential update; in the negative regime, it becomes more aggressive than the exponential update and is more prone to entropy collapse.
