17:T8c6,Chronicle is a 324M-parameter decoder-only transformer trained from scratch. The architecture is intentionally minimal. Text tokens and time-series patches are placed into a single autoregressive sequence and processed by a fully shared transformer backbone. Modality-specific components are limited to the interfaces: a 131,072-entry BPE embedding table and tied LM head for text, plus a patch projection layer for time series and a quantile forecasting head with Q=21 output levels. In other words, almost all parameters are updated by both modalities, and cross-modal interaction comes from ordinary causal self-attention rather than cross-attention modules or dual encoders.

For time-series preprocessing, the model follows the practical TSFM recipe used by systems such as Chronos-2 and PatchTST-FM: standardization using only visible values, followed by an inverse hyperbolic sine transform to compress heavy-tailed amplitudes, then patching. This matters because raw numeric scale can destabilize training when the backbone is shared with text.

Training is done in two stages. In the main pretraining stage, most batches are unimodal: each micro-batch contains either only text or only time-series patches. This is a deliberate choice. It lets the shared backbone first learn stable within-modality statistics while still coupling the modalities through shared parameter updates. A short second stage at longer context length introduces a small fraction of interleaved text-and-time-series sequences for explicit cross-modal alignment. That recipe is one of the most useful takeaways: instead of forcing heavy mixed-modality sequences throughout training, the model first uses shared-parameter co-training and then adds a light alignment stage. For unified pretraining, that is often more stable and less dependent on large amounts of paired data.

The paper is also careful about scope. It is not trying to build an instruction-following temporal chat model. The target is general-purpose representation learning for forecasting, classification, embedding extraction, and language understanding. That distinction matters because conversational ability about time series is not the same thing as learning strong temporal representations.18:T76f,The experiments are the strongest part of the paper because the authors compare against serious unimodal baselines. On the language side, Chronicle is evaluated on 19 NLU tasks against scale-matched models from GPT-2 up to LLaMA-3.2-1B, and the reported result is that it matches or approaches Gemma-3-270M-PT on language understanding. That suggests joint training does not significantly damage text capability. On the time-series side, it is evaluated on GIFT-Eval against public TS foundation models, and on 14 UCR/UEA classification datasets against supervised models and frozen TSFM embeddings. The paper claims a new strong result for frozen-backbone time-series classification and reports that Chronicle beats all supervised fusion baselines on Time-MMD. For a 324M shared model, that is meaningful evidence that the temporal side is not being sacrificed either.

The more important question the experiments answer is whether joint training is actually necessary. If Chronicle merely survives on both modalities, the result would be weak. But the reported pattern is stronger: near-scale-matched language performance plus competitive time-series performance indicates that catastrophic interference is not severe at this scale. If the full paper provides exact training ratios, token and patch counts, context lengths, and the fraction of interleaved sequences in stage two, those numbers are worth extracting carefully because they are the parts most likely to transfer to speech, vision tokens, or other modalities.

The ablations are also important: single-modality training, removing the interleaving stage, varying patch design, changing loss weighting, and testing modality imbalance. For unified pretraining, those details matter more than a single headline score because they reveal whether the shared backbone is learning transferable structure or simply being dominated by one
