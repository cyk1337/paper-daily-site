9:T885,方法名是 TreeOPSD，核心由两步组成。

第一步是 tree-aware credit construction。作者利用多轮交互天然存在的前缀分叉结构：对同一个历史前缀，采样多个后续 continuation，形成 rollout tree。这样可以把“某个 turn 之后的选择是否有帮助”与此前历史解耦。然后在树上做 recursive backup，把叶子节点的终局 verifier reward 回传成各个 turn 的 backed-up credit。这里的关键不是简单把终局 reward 平均分摊到所有 token，而是利用共享前缀下不同分支的结果差异，得到更细粒度的 turn-level 相对信用。

第二步是 posterior self-distillation。作者不是直接拿 backed-up credit 做 REINFORCE/PPO 权重，而是相对于冻结的行为策略 π_old，通过一个仿射映射把每个 turn 的 credit 变成一个有界的 teacher distribution。这个 teacher 是“后验”意义上的目标分布：高 credit 的动作概率被抬高，低 credit 的动作概率被压低，但整体仍 anchored 在行为策略上，因此不会失控。随后训练当前策略 π_θ 去最小化与 teacher 的 Forward-KL。作者强调两个性质：1）在 π_θ = π_old 的切点处，这个 distillation objective 的梯度与 tree policy gradient 一致；2）随着当前策略接近 teacher，梯度会自然衰减，到达目标后为零，若超过目标则反向，因而比固定 advantage 权重的多轮 mini-batch 更新更稳定。

Existing methods usually address one side at a time: critics or process reward models provide denser supervision but add training complexity and their own failure modes, while PPO-style importance weighting and clipping control drift without defining a finite target that the optimization can converge to. The paper aims to derive turn-level supervision directly from rollout trees and terminal rewards, then turn the RL update into a posterior distillation problem.b:T9fa,The method, TreeOPSD, has two main components.

The first is tree-aware credit construction. Multi-turn interaction naturally exposes branching prefixes: from the same history prefix, one can sample multiple alternative continuations, forming a rollout tree. This isolates the effect of decisions made after a given turn while holding the preceding context fixed. Terminal verifier rewards at the leaves are then recursively backed up through the tree to produce turn-level credit. The point is not to spread a final reward uniformly across all tokens, but to exploit outcome differences among sibling branches under a shared prefix to obtain more informative relative credit for each turn.

The second is posterior self-distillation. Instead of plugging the backed-up credit directly into REINFORCE or PPO weights, the method maps turn-level credit through an affine transformation relative to the frozen behavior policy π_old, producing a bounded teacher distribution for each turn. This teacher can be interpreted as a posterior target: actions with higher backed-up credit get their probability increased, lower-credit actions get suppressed, but the target remains anchored to the behavior policy. The current policy π_θ is then trained to minimize a Forward-KL divergence to this teacher.

Two properties are central. First, at the tangent point where π_θ equals π_old, the gradient of the distillation objective recovers the exact tree policy-gradient direction. Second, unlike a fixed collection-time advantage weight, the student-teacher residual shrinks as the student approaches the target, becomes zero at the target, and reverses sign after overshoot. That gives the optimization a finite destination and is the paper’s answer to sequential mini-batch staleness.

The paper also claims a structural result: because the affine map from credit to target distribution commutes with tree backup, matching a backed-up parent target is gradient-equivalent to aggregating descendant distillation losses. This is important because it ties the target construction to the recursive tree credit mechanism rather than treating it as an ad hoc heuristic.

Relative to PPO, the difference is conceptual and practical: PPO remains a ratio-weighted policy-gradient method with clipping, whereas TreeOPSD turns the update into distribution matching against a frozen target. 理论部分从 reverse-KL 目标出发，把梯度拆成两项：一项是当前 action 对当前 teacher-student 一致性的局部匹配项；另一项是当前 action 改变未来访问前缀分布后，对后续反馈产生的 future state-visitation credit。常见 sampled-token OPD 只保留前者。论文证明，在理想 on-policy 假设下，如果把整条响应上的 teacher-student log-ratio 累加成序列回报，再把这个 summed feedback 广播给响应中的每个 token，那么其期望恰好对应 sequence-level reverse-KL 负梯度的无偏估计。这个结论很关键，因为它说明“把后续反馈分给早期 token”不是启发式，而是和原目标一致。

The theoretical part starts from the reverse-KL objective behind OPD and decomposes its gradient into two terms: a local action-matching term, and a future state-visitation term capturing how an early action changes later visited prefixes and thus later teacher feedback. Standard sampled-token OPD keeps only the local term. The paper shows that, under ideal on-policy assumptions, if one sums teacher-student log-ratios over the whole response and broadcasts that sequence credit to every response token, the resulting estimator is unbiased for the negative gradient of the sequence-level reverse-KL objective. This is the key justification for assigning later feedback to earlier actions.

In practice, however, broadcasting raw sequence scores is unstable. The paper identifies two reasons. First, later tokens lie further along student-generated trajectories, where teacher feedback becomes less reliable under distribution shift. Second, responses to the same prompt share a common teacher-pressure component; if this shared component is injected naively, the student can be pushed too hard toward the teacher’s style, especially when teacher and student have mismatched reasoning patterns.

GR-OPD therefore adds two calibrations. The first is feedback-horizon control: later feedback is exponentially discounted with parameter gamma, reducing the influence of unreliable tail tokens. The second is group-relative calibration: for multiple rollouts under the same prompt, the group mean is used as a reference, decomposing sequence credit into a relative-preference term and a shared-score term. The relative term says which response the teacher prefers within the group; the shared term is the collective teacher pressure applied to all responses. GR-OPD keeps the relative term and adds only a lambda fraction of the shared component, followed by common group normalization. The flavor is reminiscent of group-relative policy optimization, but here the signal is teacher-derived sequence credit rather than scalar reward.

Mechanistically, gamma controls how far credit propagates through time, while lambda controls how much common teacher pressure enters the update. Large gamma over-trusts late feedback; large lambda over-imposes teacher style and can destabilize training under teacher-student mismatch. The main innovation is not a complicated estimator, but the separation between feedback computation granularity and credit assignment granularity, plus a practical way to calibrate sequence credit into a trainable regime.10:T60d,The experiments cover five Qwen3 teacher-student settings and six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, using macro Avg@K as the main metric. 第一层是 session-aware trajectory-level advantage estimation。普通 broadcasting 做法是先按任务内 rollout group 的 outcome reward 做 GRPO 式标准化，再把同一个 trajectory advantage 广播给所有 token。Mosaic 认为这样忽略了 session 使用效率，于是在“相同 outcome”内部再看 session count 的相对排序。直观上，若两条轨迹都成功，使用更少 session 的那条应得到更高 advantage；若两条都失败，反而不应简单惩罚 session 多的轨迹，因为失败样本仍需要探索空间。论文因此设计了一个有界调制项，用 session 数在同 reward 组内的 rank 去修正 trajectory-level advantage。这里有两个关键约束：一是调制不能打乱由 outcome reward 决定的主排序，二是调制幅度要受控，避免 session 偏好压过任务成败本身。作者强调它的作用不是替代 outcome signal，而是拉开 homogeneous-reward group 内部的 advantage 间距，提高学习信号分辨率。

第二层是 session-level advantage shaping。作者把每个 session 对最终 outcome 的边际贡献视作一种 immediate reward。做法上，是比较包含该 session 与不包含该 session 时的 outcome 增益，得到 session-level marginal gain；然后按 session type 分开做 group-relative normalization，再把这个 shaping signal 与上面的 trajectory-level advantage 结合。之所以要按 session type 分开归一化，是因为不同类型 session 的边际收益分布不同，例如压缩 session、主 agent session、fork 出来的子 agent session，其长度、功能和对最终成功率的贡献尺度都不一样，混在一起标准化会让某些类型的信号被系统性放大或压小。

整体上，Mosaic 形成了一个层级目标：trajectory-level signal 负责长期、全局、结果导向的优化；session-level shaping 负责局部、短程、边际贡献导向的优化。并且整个方法保持 critic-free，仍沿用 group-relative 的思路，而不是额外训练 value model。这是它和 PPO-style token/value advantage 路线的主要区别。14:T45f,This is not a pretraining paper in the narrow sense, but it matters for the pretrain-to-post-train interface, especially for long-context agents, tool-using systems, and reasoning models with explicit context management. That is important because modern agent execution increasingly departs from the single-context assumption used during RL training. The first component is session-aware trajectory-level advantage estimation. In the standard broadcasting setup, one computes a GRPO-style normalized trajectory advantage from outcome rewards within a rollout group, then assigns that same advantage to all tokens across all sessions. Mosaic argues that this ignores session efficiency. It therefore refines the trajectory-level signal using the relative ranking of session counts within trajectories that share the same outcome. Intuitively, if two trajectories both succeed, the one using fewer sessions should receive a larger advantage. If both fail, however, extra sessions should not be penalized in the same way because failed samples still serve exploration. The paper implements this through a bounded modulation term based on session-count ranking inside homogeneous-reward groups. Two design constraints are central: the modulation must not overturn the primary ordering induced by outcome reward, and its magnitude must remain controlled so that session preference does not dominate task success. The point is not to replace outcome supervision, but to increase advantage resolution within reward-tied groups.

The second component is session-level advantage shaping. Each session is assigned an immediate shaping signal based on its marginal contribution to the final outcome. Concretely, the method estimates a session-level marginal outcome gain, normalizes it with group-relative statistics separately for each session type, and combines this shaping term with the trajectory-level advantage. The type-conditioned normalization is important because different session types have different gain distributions. A compaction session, a main-agent session, and a forked subagent session differ in length, function, and contribution scale. Pooling them into one normalization scheme would distort the signal.

Together, these two components form a hierarchical objective: the trajectory-level term provides a global, long-horizon, outcome-oriented signal, while the session-level shaping term captures local marginal effects. 2. Different session types indeed exhibit different marginal-gain distributions, validating the need for type-conditioned normalization.
3. Training changes the agent’s session usage pattern toward fewer but more useful sessions, suggesting that the objective shapes reasoning structure rather than merely adjusting token probabilities.
