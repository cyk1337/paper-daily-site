This already differs from outcome-supervised RL because criterion-choice tokens now receive direct local supervision shaped by the rationale.

The main technical contribution is the entropy-shift analysis. For each position, the paper measures the change in entropy between the teacher and student next-token distributions after conditioning the teacher on feedback. This yields two regimes. In context sharpening, entropy drops sharply and the teacher concentrates probability on a narrow expression aligned with the feedback. In context spreading, entropy increases or lies in the lower tail of the per-sequence entropy-shift distribution, meaning the teacher preserves multiple feedback-consistent alternatives. The paper interprets sharpening as encouraging memorization of a particular phrasing, while spreading better preserves semantic understanding of the criterion.

Based on this, the authors propose masking positions for distillation using entropy shift. They keep the lower tail of the entropy-shift distribution within each generation and remove the highest-shift positions from the distillation loss. In the propose stage, an LLM-based judge compares successful and failed trajectories from the same GRPO group and selects a segment in a successful trajectory that may have caused the divergence in outcome. The judge is used only for localization, not for direct reward assignment. This is an important design choice: it leverages model-based pattern recognition without trusting model judgment as the final training signal.

In the verify stage, the method restores the environment states immediately before and after the proposed segment, then samples fresh continuations from the current policy from both boundary states. The difference in empirical terminal success rates estimates the segment’s advantage. Intuitively, if the post-segment state is much easier for the current policy to complete successfully than the pre-segment state, then the segment likely contributed real causal progress.

In the credit stage, only positively verified segment advantages are added to the original GRPO advantages, and only for policy tokens inside the proposed segment. So ProVer does not replace GRPO. It augments GRPO with sparse, localized, outcome-grounded bonus credit.

Relative to prior approaches, the distinction is clear. It avoids a separately trained value model, so it reduces dependence on critic generalization. It avoids directly optimizing against an LLM evaluator’s intermediate scores, so it is less exposed to evaluator gaming. 第一种是 PROBE-JS，属于 black-box 方案。做法是在每个 boundary 插入一个很短的 probe，类似“所以你的最终答案是什么”，采样得到 answer distribution，然后计算相邻两个 boundary 的 Jensen-Shannon divergence。JS 大说明模型在这一步前后对最终答案的分布发生了明显变化，可能是关键推理步，也可能是犯错点。它的优点是只需要 sampling access，不依赖内部状态；缺点是每个 boundary 都要额外生成一小段 probe answer。

第二种是 LENS-SHIFT，属于 white-box 方案。它不让模型显式回答，而是用 logit lens 读取各层对最终答案的承诺强度，形成一个 depth profile，再比较相邻 boundary 的 profile 变化幅度。这个设计的意思是：belief shift 不一定只体现在最后一层 logits，也可能体现在整条深度方向上的表征重排。相比 probe，它没有额外 generation cost，但需要访问 hidden states，并且依赖已知 final answer 作为 readout target。

第三种是 BSV（Belief-Shift Vector）。作者先离线收集高置信与犹豫状态的 activation，对比拟合出一个“confidence shift”方向，之后只需把 boundary hidden state 投影到这个方向上即可打分。它更像一个 amortized readout，用很低成本近似直接测量 belief shift。论文里它主要用于 pre-RL validation，验证如果能更直接地读到 belief shift，是否会更贴近 Monte-Carlo ground-truth value curve。也就是说，BSV 更像分析工具，而不是主训练时的标准配置。

For a generated chain, define candidate boundaries. At each boundary, read the model’s current belief about the final answer. Score each segment by the amount of belief change between consecutive boundaries, and fork at the highest-scoring location. Two details matter.

First, the paper uses fork-before attribution. If belief changes sharply over segment (t-, t], the causal step is that segment, so the score is assigned to t- and sibling continuations are re-rolled from just before the belief-moving step. This aligns the counterfactual branch with the step whose contribution is being estimated.

Second, the paper instantiates belief readout at three access levels.

PROBE-JS is the black-box version. At each boundary, the model is interrupted with a short probe such as “so what is your final answer?”, and the resulting answer distribution is sampled. The score is the Jensen–Shannon divergence between consecutive boundary distributions. Large JS indicates that the segment caused a meaningful shift in answer belief. The advantage is that it only requires sampling access; the downside is extra generation at every boundary.

LENS-SHIFT is the white-box version. Instead of making the model answer explicitly, it uses a logit-lens style readout to measure how strongly each layer already commits to the final answer, producing a depth profile at each boundary. The score is the change in this profile across adjacent boundaries. The motivation is that belief shifts may appear as representational reorganization across depth, not only as a change in final logits. This avoids extra generation but requires hidden-state access and a target final answer for readout.

BSV, or Belief-Shift Vector, is an amortized readout. The authors fit an activation direction offline by contrasting high-confidence and doubtful states, then score a boundary by projecting its hidden state onto that direction. In the paper, BSV is mainly used for pre-RL validation: if one could measure belief shift more directly, would it align better with the Monte-Carlo value curve? So BSV functions more as an analysis instrument than the default online training method.

The distinction from prior selectors is sharp. Fixed grids, midpoints, and delimiter-based rules ignore whether a step actually changes the outcome. Entropy or surprisal tracks local token uncertainty, which is often inflated by paraphrase freedom, stylistic variation, or surface branching in code, without implying any change in final answer.
