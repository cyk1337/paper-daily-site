On the dataset side, the authors consolidate six public Bangla safety resources into a unified four-level taxonomy: Safe, Offensive, Harmful, and Severe. They preserve source metadata and use deterministic source-aware mapping rules, which is important because the original datasets differ in label semantics. To address the common shortage of benign examples in safety corpora, they convert negative Bangla e-commerce reviews into first-person customer-support requests and use them as Safe prompts. The key design choice is the three-way aligned rendering of each prompt: native Bangla script, romanized Bangla, and Bangla code-mixed with English, Hindi, or Urdu. All three versions share the same label and split, so differences in model behavior can be attributed much more directly to writing form rather than content drift. Romanization is produced with an Indic transliteration model, while code-mixed prompts are generated with controlled language proportions and alignment-based substitution. On the modeling side, BanglaShield is a MuRIL-based encoder fine-tuned for four-way classification. This is a sensible choice for a lightweight guard because MuRIL is built for Indian-language multilingual text and is better suited to discriminative filtering than prompting a generative LLM. Third, the fine-tuned MuRIL-based BanglaShield outperforms all evaluated LLMs across all three writing forms while showing much smaller cross-script variance.
