The paper identifies usable symmetries in attention and MLP blocks. Value vectors in attention admit an SO(d_k) rotation, with corresponding transformations of W_V and W_O preserving the exact output; under GQA, each KV head group shares its rotation. Query/key rotations are constrained by RoPE: they must commute with the RoPE matrix, reducing the available symmetry to U(1)^{d_k/2}, which the paper does not exploit. In the SwiGLU MLP, the down-projection boundary also admits an invariance, but the rotation cannot be fully absorbed upstream because SiLU and the element-wise product do not commute with a general dense rotation. As a result, H·R remains a runtime operation while R^T can be fused into W_down. Instead of simulating quantization, the method adds a LogSumExp penalty on rotated boundary activations. LogSumExp acts as a smooth dense proxy for the infinity norm, which matters because the uniform quantization step size is driven by the largest outlier. A stop-gradient operator ensures that this auxiliary term updates only the rotation matrices, while the base model parameters are still trained only by cross-entropy. Compared with SpinQuant-style post-training rotation search, GaugeQuant does not require calibration data, a frozen model, or a non-differentiable quantization operator in the optimization loop.19:T698,The paper has three main technical components. First, it characterizes the native discrete gauge of transformer residual streams under coordinate-preserving parameterizations. For LayerNorm, the residual chart has permutation symmetry up to a global sign. For RMSNorm with generic per-channel gain, the correct group is the hyperoctahedral group B_d = S_d ⋉ {±1}^d, meaning arbitrary coordinate permutations plus independent sign flips. The paper also clarifies an important distinction: broader orthogonal freedom can be recovered only after absorbing the RMSNorm gain into adjacent weights, but that changes the coordinate system and no longer preserves sparse coordinate identity. Second, it proposes sign-marginalized activation matching. Instead of matching coordinates by signed correlation, it matches by absolute correlation |<h_i, h'_j>| and recovers the sign afterward from the matched pair. This is the right objective because the true counterpart of a coordinate may be maximally anti-correlated under a negative gauge sign. The authors prove a sharp population-level failure mode for signed-correlation matching in a decorrelated setting: if half of the true signs are negative, the correct entries become the most negative ones in their rows, so maximizing signed correlation systematically avoids them even with unlimited probes. Third, it introduces transport via a checkpoint atlas. Rather than matching only two endpoints, the method composes local B_d gauges saved along a training trajectory.
