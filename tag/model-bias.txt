18:T580,The work first documents three forms of model-specific bias. Generation bias is measured under repeated sampling: when self-consistency fails, wrong samples often collapse onto the same dominant wrong answer rather than spreading across alternatives. The paper quantifies this with C_q, the fraction of incorrect samples assigned to the most frequent wrong answer, under SC@20 on benchmarks such as MMLU-Pro and GPQA-Diamond. Initial-answer persistence bias is measured by asking the model for a direct answer first and then asking it to reason before answering; the post-reasoning answer remains strongly tied to the initial prediction, especially when the initial prediction is wrong. Longer-reasoning preference bias appears in self-evaluation: when the model judges its own earlier and later answers, it overwhelmingly prefers the later, more elaborate one. In disagreement cases, the later-stage answer is selected more than 95% of the time, often with high confidence even when it is wrong. Dia, the proposed method, uses these observations directly. It compares an intermediate-stage prediction with the final-stage prediction. If they agree, the final answer is returned. If they disagree, a small heterogeneous solver panel answers the question independently without seeing either candidate.
