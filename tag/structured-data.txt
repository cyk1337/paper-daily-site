The key stress-test variable is job, which has 494 categories with a very long tail; even the most frequent class is only 0.75% of the data. That makes the task hard to solve through semantic generalization alone and forces the model to deal with distributional adaptation. The authors evaluate Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 under a unified schema prompt, comparing zero-shot, 1/5/10-shot ICL, and LoRA finetuning. Records are generated one by one as JSON with strict validation and regeneration until 10,000 valid samples are collected. Evaluation has three parts: structural reliability via invalid generation rate, statistical fidelity via per-feature TVD between real and synthetic marginals, and memorization risk via DCR ratio plus exact duplication rate. They also track whether the rare fraud base rate is reproduced and inspect how marginal failures propagate into joint structure. The main empirical pattern is that more in-context examples steadily improve numerical fidelity, while categorical fidelity quickly plateaus, especially on high-cardinality long-tail variables. Prompting changes do not fix it.
