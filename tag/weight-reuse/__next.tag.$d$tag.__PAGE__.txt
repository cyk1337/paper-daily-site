a:T53c,LOTUS inserts K learnable padded latent blocks, each with c latent tokens, between the question and the answer. The whole sequence is processed R times by the same Transformer backbone, reusing weights across iterations. This recurrent-depth design converts a length-N explicit CoT into R rounds of parallel dense computation, with R much smaller than N.

The central training signal is parallel cross-entropy supervision on the post-loop latents. Each latent position is read through the base model’s own LM head and trained to predict the corresponding gold CoT token. This gives the latent trace a direct token-level target. The design differs from autoregressive latent methods such as Coconut, CODI, and SIM-CoT because it does not generate latent thoughts one by one. It also differs from methods such as PCCoT and KaVa, which reduce sequential bottlenecks but rely on more indirect grounding signals such as teacher hidden states or compressed KV-cache targets.

The paper also introduces LOTUS-aux, where the same supervision is routed through an auxiliary decoder conditioned on all latents and trained with teacher forcing. The auxiliary decoder is used only during training. It can match the direct route at the 3B scale, but it degrades on smaller backbones, which makes the direct LM-head route the more robust design choice.b:T443,The main model is Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct. On GSM8K, LOTUS comes within 1 point of explicit CoT. On the out-of-domain math average, it surpasses explicit CoT while reducing thought-phase latency by 2.5x in the math-expression setting. On a more verbose natural-language CoT stress test, it matches explicit CoT accuracy and reduces thought-phase latency by 6.9x.

The ablations support two key claims. First, direct parallel supervision is important; without CoT-token grounding, the latent trace is less reliable as a reasoning carrier. Second, the looped architecture needs enough block width and loop depth. The benefit is not obtained simply by inserting latent tokens into a standard forward pass.

The interpretability analysis is also notable. Reading post-loop latents through the base LM head recovers most gold intermediate tokens, and sometimes assigns probability mass to alternative valid reasoning steps that were not used as training targets.
