18:Tb25,技术上，作者先把自回归 LM 重新表述成“context embedding 到 next-token distribution 的分类问题”。Transformer backbone 产出上下文向量 e，最后一层线性 head W 加 softmax 生成词表分布。论文刻意先把 backbone 固定，只分析最后线性层，因为它是唯一直接把表示映射到词表分布的部件，也决定了回传到 backbone 的梯度形态。理论部分采用一个可控 toy setting：每个上下文对应一个大小为 k 的随机 support，目标分布在 support 上均匀，k 用来控制 ambiguity；真实语料中的非均匀分布则用 effective support size，也就是 exp(entropy)，来度量。

作者把 curse 拆成两类来源。架构根因方面，结论是更高 ambiguity 的分布需要更大的“存储容量”和更高维的 embedding 才能在给定误差下被表示。直观上，k=1 时只需把一个 token 从其余 token 中分离出来；k>1 时需要同时为多个 token 保持相近高 logit，并与大量非 support token 拉开间隔，这对线性可分性和几何表示都更苛刻。论文分析了随机 embedding 与可联合优化 embedding 两种情形：前者展示在固定 h 下误差如何随 k 恶化，后者说明即便允许最优表示，embedding 维度仍需随 ambiguity 增长，否则无法同时容纳足够多彼此不同的高熵目标分布。

学习根因方面，作者分析了梯度和采样噪声。训练时我们并不直接看到目标分布 p，而是从 p 采样一个 token 做交叉熵更新。对高 ambiguity 上下文，单个样本只暴露 support 中一个 token，导致梯度对完整分布的估计方差更大；换句话说，同样一次 SGD 更新，对 k 大的分布提供的信息更少、更噪。论文进一步给出优化步数需求随 ambiguity 增长的理论结果：要把预测分布拟合到同等精度，需要更多样本和更多步数。这里的关键不是 label smoothing 那种人为加熵，而是真实条件分布的熵越高，token-level sampling 对分布学习越低效。

实证部分分三层推进。第一层是严格受控的合成实验，单独训练 logistic head 或小网络，验证表示误差、所需维度、收敛速度、梯度噪声都随 k 变差。第二层是在真实语言模型上做上下文分桶，按下一词分布的 effective support 或 entropy 分析 loss、拟合误差、训练动态，观察到与理论一致的 signature。第三层是规模外推：作者论证这些现象在更大模型上仍存在，不会因为参数变大而自动消失，只是阈值后移。与已有工作相比，这篇论文的区别不在于提出新的 uncertainty metric，而在于把“高熵目标更难学”具体落实到最后层几何、样本复杂度和 token sampling 噪声三个机制上，并把它们连到 LM 训练闭环。19:T45a,Its value for pretraining research is threefold. First, it turns an empirical complaint—models are poorly calibrated in uncertain contexts—into a mechanistic object of study with architectural and optimization roots. Second, it suggests that learning difficulty in pretraining is shaped not only by sequence length, knowledge content, or model size, but also by conditional entropy itself. Two contexts with the same token count can impose very different learning burdens depending on how diffuse the target distribution is. That has implications for scaling laws, data curricula, loss weighting, tokenizer design, and synthetic data generation. Third, it clarifies why several post-training methods hit a ceiling: RL can only reinforce sampled trajectories, search-based inference needs probability mass to be spread over viable branches, and self-generated corpora become fragile if the model collapses too early onto a narrow set of continuations. This is not a new architecture paper. It is a foundational diagnosis of AR LM learning dynamics, useful because it identifies where interventions might matter.1a:Tb9e,The paper reframes autoregressive language modeling as context classification. A Transformer backbone maps a context to an embedding e, and the final linear head W plus softmax maps that embedding to a vocabulary distribution. The analysis first isolates this last layer while treating the backbone representations as given. That choice is deliberate: the output head is the only component that directly parameterizes the next-token distribution, and it also shapes the gradients flowing back into the backbone.

The theoretical setup is a controlled toy model. Each context is associated with a target distribution that is uniform over a random support set of size k. The support size k controls ambiguity. For real, non-uniform language distributions, ambiguity is measured by effective support size, exp(H(p)), which reduces to k in the uniform-support setting.

The authors then separate the curse into architectural and learning roots. On the architectural side, they show that more ambiguous target distributions require more storage capacity and larger embedding dimensions to be represented to a fixed accuracy. Intuitively, predicting one correct token is geometrically easier than assigning similarly high logits to many plausible tokens while separating them from the rest of the vocabulary. The paper studies both random embeddings and jointly optimized embeddings. The first shows degradation at fixed hidden dimension h as k grows. The second shows that even with optimal embeddings, the required representation dimension still increases with ambiguity.

On the learning side, the key issue is token-sampling noise. Training does not observe the full target distribution p directly. It observes one sampled token from p and updates with cross-entropy. For high-ambiguity contexts, a single sample reveals only one element of a larger support, so the stochastic gradient is a noisier estimator of the full distributional objective. The paper derives that fitting such targets to the same precision requires more optimization steps and more samples. The point is not that entropy makes the loss numerically larger. The point is that token-level supervision becomes a less efficient channel for learning the full conditional distribution as ambiguity increases.

Empirically, the paper proceeds in three stages. First, controlled synthetic experiments validate the predicted trends in representation error, required dimension, convergence speed, and gradient noise. Second, real-language-model analyses bucket contexts by ambiguity and show analogous signatures in training dynamics and distribution fitting. Third, the authors argue that scaling model size does not remove the phenomenon; it only shifts the regime where it becomes visible. Relative to prior work, the contribution is not a new uncertainty metric. It is the decomposition of the problem into output-layer geometry, sample complexity, and token-sampling noise, tied back to practical LM training.1b:T628,The experimental contribution is mostly in the consistency of the observed trends rather than in a single benchmark number. In synthetic settings, the authors vary support size k and show that as k increases: (1) the error between predicted and true distributions rises at fixed head capacity and embedding dimension, (2) maintaining the same error requires larger hidden dimension h, (3) optimization slows down and needs more steps, and (4) sampled-token training induces higher gradient variance. They also compare random embeddings with jointly learned embeddings, showing that the problem is not merely poor representation learning but a genuine increase in geometric and statistical difficulty.

On real data, the paper replaces uniform support size with effective support size exp(H(p)) as the ambiguity measure. Contexts with higher ambiguity exhibit higher loss, slower fitting, and worse approximation of the full next-token distribution. Importantly, the gap is not only in top-1 behavior. It appears in probability allocation across plausible continuations. The same qualitative signatures remain visible in full language models rather than only isolated linear heads, which supports the claim that the simplified analysis captures a real bottleneck.

Second, it develops theory for sampler separations. Under perfect conditionals, sequential one-coordinate unmasking is exact and order-invariant because it follows the chain rule. - Key differences: unlike speculative/blockwise decoding, BitLM changes the output interface rather than accelerating softmax sampling; unlike fully non-causal diffusion LMs, it keeps a causal backbone and block-level causality; unlike AR multi-token heads, jointness comes from coupled bit denoising rather than independent per-position classification.32:T868,方法由两个模块组成。第一阶段是非循环 backbone，文中实现为 Transformer，它根据上下文先产生每个位置的初始输出 embedding，相当于对下一个 token 的隐表示做一次快速猜测。第二阶段是 attractor module，一个通常更小的循环网络，它不直接输出 token，而是接收 backbone 的初始 embedding，并通过迭代映射把该 embedding 推向一个固定点。最终在这个平衡态 embedding 上做解码，得到词表分布。

关键改动在于训练方式。传统 looped LM 会把 recurrent block 展开 T 步，然后做 BPTT，训练内存和计算都随 T 增长。Attractor Model 则把 refinement 写成 z*=f(z*; x, h0) 这样的 fixed-point equation，其中 h0 是 backbone 的初始提议。前向时用求解器迭代到收敛，反向时不对所有中间步做显式回传，而是用 implicit differentiation 计算梯度，因此训练显存不再线性依赖迭代步数。这一点直接借鉴 DEQ，但论文的设计不是把整层网络都做成 equilibrium，而是只在输出 embedding 空间上求平衡，降低了优化难度，也保留了标准 Transformer backbone 的表达和并行优势。

Second, it brings DEQ-style implicit layers back into language modeling in a more practical form. That has direct implications for latent reasoning, train-time versus test-time compute, and how slow internal computation may be absorbed into a fast path over training.34:T841,The architecture has two stages. A non-recurrent backbone, implemented as a Transformer, first proposes an initial output embedding for each token position. This is a fast latent guess for the next-token representation. A separate attractor module, typically smaller and recurrent, then refines that embedding by iterating toward a fixed point. The final token distribution is decoded from the equilibrium embedding rather than from the backbone proposal alone.

The key change is in how the recurrent part is trained. Standard looped language models explicitly unroll the recurrent block for T steps and backpropagate through all steps, which makes memory and optimization cost scale with T. Attractor Models instead formulate refinement as a fixed-point equation, roughly of the form z* = f(z*; x, h0), where h0 is the backbone proposal. The forward pass runs a solver until convergence, and the backward pass uses implicit differentiation rather than storing and differentiating through every intermediate iterate. This is the main source of constant-memory behavior with respect to refinement depth.

A second important design choice is the two-stage decomposition. The backbone is responsible for producing a good initialization, while the attractor performs local correction. This is more stable and cheaper than asking a recurrent module to do all the work from scratch. The model also supports adaptive refinement naturally: different tokens can require different numbers of solver iterations at train and test time, depending on convergence. That is a cleaner route to variable test-time compute than fixed-depth looped Transformers.

The paper also identifies equilibrium internalization. During training, the backbone’s initial prediction moves closer to the attractor’s fixed point, so the solver needs fewer iterations to reach approximate equilibrium. This happens without any explicit distillation loss. The constant-memory claim with respect to effective refinement depth is one of the central empirical results.
