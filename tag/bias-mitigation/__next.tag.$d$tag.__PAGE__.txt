b:T490,The authors formalize RLHF as a single-turn contextual bandit with prompts drawn from a fixed context distribution, responses sampled from a policy, a true reward, and a learned proxy reward. Under KL-regularized RLHF, the optimal policy can be expressed as a softmax reweighting of the reference policy. This makes clear why changing one reward component can shift probability mass toward responses with other correlated proxies. The paper separates the diagnostic or audit distribution from the policy-induced distribution and builds a taxonomy of mitigation outcomes: R0 for successful mitigation, R0_cont for a contaminated success case, R1 for bias substitution, R2 for overcorrection, R3 for silent non-operation, and R4 for audit-distribution sensitivity. The main theoretical result is an impossibility-sufficiency pair. No functional over audit-distribution observables can distinguish successful mitigation from substitution or overcorrection, even with oracle access to true reward.
