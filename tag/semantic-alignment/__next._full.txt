The first diagnosis is that action trajectories contain verb information beyond visual state changes. The authors decompose verb grounding into action goals, represented by visual changes between episode endpoints, and motion dynamics, represented by the 7-DoF action trajectory. Their analysis shows that each carries verb-relevant information not fully captured by the other. This means that visual outcomes alone are insufficient; the action process itself contains language-relevant structure.

The second diagnosis is that existing discrete action representations erase this structure. The paper compares Bin tokenization, VQ-VAE tokenizers, and FAST, and measures the mutual information between verbs and tokenized action representations. Reconstruction-only tokenizers consistently reduce verb-related information, with stronger compression causing larger losses. Importantly, downstream language-conditioned policy training does not fully recover the missing structure, indicating that the bottleneck is created at the tokenizer interface rather than by an underpowered policy head.

SALT—Semantically ALigned action Tokenizer—is then built on top of a VQ-VAE-style tokenizer. During tokenizer training, the quantized action latents are fed into a frozen pretrained VLM, which is asked to generate the corresponding episode instruction. This adds a language-alignment objective to the usual reconstruction, commitment, and codebook losses. The point is not to classify verbs explicitly, but to force the codebook to preserve information that makes the original instruction recoverable. Actions described by similar language are therefore encouraged to occupy nearby or related discrete representations while maintaining control-space fidelity.

The key modeling difference is that SALT treats the tokenizer as a cross-modal bottleneck. Compared with per-dimension binning, it learns a compositional discrete vocabulary. Compared with reconstruction-only VQ-VAE, it explicitly constrains the codebook to retain language-recoverable structure. Compared with FAST-style frequency compression plus BPE, it prioritizes semantic preservation over compression alone.
