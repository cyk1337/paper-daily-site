The paper operationalizes a strong hypothesis: a nontrivial fraction of reasoning ability may already be formed during pretraining, while post-training often reorganizes or exposes that ability rather than creating it from scratch. Darwin is useful as an experimental probe for that hypothesis. Three implications matter. First, reasoning-relevant computation appears unevenly distributed across depth and tensor groups, which is relevant to selective updates, layerwise scaling, and mid-training interventions. Second, if merging specialized checkpoints works, then different post-training paths may be extracting complementary behaviors from a shared pretrained substrate rather than learning entirely separate capabilities. Third, cross-architecture breeding touches a deeper question about whether different model families converge to alignable functional decompositions. In contribution scale, this is better viewed as an interesting post-training systems paper with pretraining implications, not as a direct change to the pretraining paradigm.1a:T7d3,The method has four main pieces. First is the parameter decomposition view. Two parent models A and B are assumed to share a common base model, and their specialization is represented as deltas on top of that base. Darwin merges those deltas tensor-wise instead of averaging full weights, which is already more selective than model soups. Second is MRI, Model-layer Response Importance. For each tensor T, MRI combines static statistics and probe-based functional responses. The static term aggregates normalized entropy, variance, and capped L2 norm. The probe term uses a small calibration set and measures cosine distance between reasoning-conditioned activations and generic activations, aiming to estimate how sensitive that tensor is to reasoning behavior. The two are mixed with a fixed alpha of 0.5. MRI is used as a soft prior, not a hard merge rule. Third is a low-dimensional adaptive merge genome optimized by evolutionary search. Rather than assigning an independent coefficient to every tensor, Darwin encodes block-level and component-level merge decisions in a 14-dimensional genome and searches that space without gradients. This keeps the search tractable while still allowing nonuniform recombination. Fourth is MRI-Trust Fusion. Instead of fully trusting diagnostics or fully trusting search, Darwin learns a trust parameter that fuses MRI-derived ratios with genome-derived ratios into final tensor-wise merge coefficients. The motivation is straightforward: diagnostics are biased, but unconstrained search is sample-inefficient and unstable. The paper also introduces an Architecture Mapper for heterogeneous parents. It establishes tensor correspondences using tensor type, shape compatibility, and depth alignment before numerical recombination.
