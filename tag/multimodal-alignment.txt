18:T745,The paper has two methodological layers. The first is task construction. The authors build a synthetic shape-counting task where a scene of rectangles and trapeziums can be specified either as text or as an image, with exact semantic equivalence between the two forms. The text form is Python-like code describing edges, coordinates, positions, and rotations; executing it renders the image. A key design choice is to shuffle the order of edges in the code, which prevents the text from being locally readable and forces the model to compose distant pieces into shapes. This gives a controlled setting where the same underlying problem is much easier in visual space than in textual form.

The second layer is representation diagnosis and intervention. They use model families that provide clean minimal pairs: the same decoder before and after VL adaptation, namely Qwen 2.5 vs Qwen 2.5-VL, and Mistral Nemo vs Pixtral. They first compare behavior. The VL models are much stronger on the image version, but show essentially no gain on the text version relative to their text-only counterparts. They then probe decoder hidden states with linear and contrastive probes to test whether text inputs induce more recoverable visual structure after VL alignment. The answer is again no: the decoder adapted for vision does not produce more vision-aligned text representations.

To show that stronger multimodality is at least learnable, they introduce a self-distillation objective. Instead of supervising the final answer, the model is trained on paired text/image examples so that, from text alone, it predicts the embedding it would have produced if given the image. This is the key shift: the target is an internal representation from the image pathway, not a task label. In effect, the method is representation distillation aimed directly at text-side alignment.19:T64a,The experimental story is clean. First, the task itself is validated: the same VLM performs much better on the image version than on the text version, showing that visual input genuinely makes the problem easier and that the synthetic setup is not simply out of distribution. Second, in minimal-pair comparisons, Qwen2.5-VL provides almost no improvement over the corresponding Qwen2.5 text model on the text-only shape-counting task; Pixtral shows the same pattern relative to Mistral Nemo 12B. So the gain from VL alignment stays confined to image-conditioned use.

Representation analysis reinforces the behavioral result. Both linear probes and contrastive probes indicate that visual structure remains poorly recoverable from decoder states when the input is text, and the gap between the text-only LM and the VL-adapted decoder is small. This rules out the easy interpretation that the model may have learned better visual representations internally but failed to express them behaviorally.

The final experiment is a proof of concept for the proposed self-distillation objective. Without explicit task supervision, the method yields substantial gains on the text version across difficulty levels. The important point is less the absolute score and more the mechanism: once the training signal directly targets text-side representations, the desired capability can be induced. When reading the paper, the most informative results are the difficulty-bucket breakdowns and the before/after probe results, because together they suggest that the improvement is representational rather than a narrow shortcut.1a:T4cc,This is a skim paper, but several sections are worth reading carefully. Start with the introduction: the weak-versus-strong multimodality distinction is useful well beyond this paper. Then read the task construction section, especially why shuffled edges matter and why exact text-image equivalence is necessary. After that, focus on the section showing that current VLMs use poor visual representations for text. The key question is whether the authors really isolate the effect of VL alignment, and the minimal-pair setup is central there. Finally, read the self-distillation section for the change in objective: from answer supervision to representation matching.

If time is limited, prioritize three result types: image-versus-text performance, probe results for VL decoders versus base LMs under text input, and the difficulty-stratified gains from self-distillation. Expected reading time is about 25–35 minutes. The deeper questions worth following up are whether the failure comes from VQA-style alignment specifically or from the broader decoder-centric multimodal interface, and whether this kind of representation distillation could scale into native multimodal pretraining rather than remain a synthetic-task fix.1b:T435,The experiments build on the VA-VAE implementation. The autoencoder and the diffusion prior use the same architecture and training budget across arms, with only the distillation objective changed. The autoencoder is trained from scratch on 256×256 ImageNet for 50 epochs with batch size 256, followed by a LightningDiT-XL prior trained for 64 epochs with batch size 1024. The comparisons include no distillation, position-wise distillation, first-order pooled distillation, and relational distillation, across latent shapes and teacher modalities. Pool-Align performs as well as or slightly better than position-wise alignment in settings where position-wise alignment is applicable, and it also works for 1D token-sequence latents. The cross-modal setting shows that distilling a text encoder into an image autoencoder can still improve diffusability, suggesting that the useful signal is not limited to visual patch correspondence. The relational objectives test a weaker form of geometry transfer; the most reliable variant appears to be direct first-order pooled matching.1c:T44d,The paper evaluates on MMAU, MMAR, MMSU, and MMAU-Pro. It reports that the alignment-only model can match or exceed more heavily post-trained open-source audio-language models while using substantially less data. On the instruction-following split of MMAU-Pro, freezing the LLM helps preserve its original instruction-following behavior. The ablations are more informative than the aggregate scores: scaling alignment data mainly helps open-ended reasoning tasks; closed-set recognition saturates quickly when the encoder lacks the needed information. Encoder sweeps show that benchmark performance tracks the capability emphasized during encoder pretraining.
