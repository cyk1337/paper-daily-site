The authors then isolate a second issue: the evaluated image lacks the LMCache CUDA-ops extension and falls back to a PyTorch path. In that path, the raw-pointer lmcache_memcpy_async branch uses cudaMemcpy with cudaMemcpyDefault but without dependence on the current CUDA stream. Because the stream pool uses cudaStreamNonBlocking, the copy does not inherit the producer-consumer ordering of the preceding gather/scatter work. Activations remain in bf16 throughout. Hardware, vLLM version, engine flags, and sampling settings are held fixed within each model, and the same seed set is reused across precision tiers. Instead of relying on existing benchmarks, the paper builds two custom prompt batteries. The cultural battery contains 218 country-eliciting prompts across 11 domains plus 80 style-control prompts, each annotated at construction time with a country-pull label (none, weak, strong). 1. At 8B, int4 reduces diversity. In practical terms, the model becomes more likely to repeat default answers.

2. This content-level concentration largely disappears by 14B and remains absent at 32B. Larger models show stylistic drift rather than a stable collapse in country or brand choice. This scale dependence is the central empirical result.

3. There is no evidence of stereotype amplification. The dedicated stereotype-direction analyses do not show quantization pushing outputs more strongly toward predefined stereotype sets. The effect is therefore not “more biased” in the directional sense tested here; it is “more concentrated” within the valid answer space.

4. Token entropy rises while semantic entropy falls. This is one of the most interesting results. If one only inspects token-level variability, quantized outputs may appear more random. But at the semantic level—entity choice and meaning-level alternatives—the distribution becomes narrower. The authors interpret this as a shift from semantic alternatives to surface variation, not simple sharpening of the same distribution.

5. The analysis follows a declare-then-run protocol. Wave 1 was pre-specified before generation; waves 2 and 3 were added after seeing the 14B results but before generating the 8B and 32B arms, with the repository history preserving the sequence. That matters because this topic is especially vulnerable to exploratory overfitting.
