18:T420,The study uses a counterfactual fairness setup. The authors take 120 real emergency department cases from MIMIC-IV and create demographic swaps while keeping the clinical content fixed. They run a full factorial design over five model families and four aggregation topologies, yielding 20 model-by-topology cells. The analysis compares independent agent outputs with the final committed team decision. The central mechanism is called disparity laundering: the team optimizes within-version quantities such as agreement, coherence, and clinical plausibility, but the fairness property lives between paired counterfactual versions of the same patient. Since no agent observes both versions, the deliberation process cannot distinguish a harmful dissent from an error to be smoothed out. The paper tests late-stage interventions such as fairness instructions, demographic masking, and per-agent self-debiasing. It then introduces a Diverse-Perspective Ensemble that makes counterfactual versions visible inside the team and pools them into the final decision.19:T473,The framework has two components. GEAR, or Geometric Agreement for Latent Reasoning, constructs the next continuous input from candidate token embeddings. Starting from the sender logits, it applies temperature scaling and candidate filtering, then reweights candidates using both their probabilities and their embedding-space agreement with other candidates. This differs from Soft Thinking, which simply averages embeddings under the predictive distribution. A candidate receives more weight when it is probable and geometrically supported by other probable candidates. The second component is the H+J communication interface. The H channel maps the sender’s consumed feedback distribution into the receiver’s embedding table, producing receiver-side continuous inputs. The J channel uses a Jacobian lens to read selected vocabulary-indexed coordinates from an intermediate sender state. The sender transmits token IDs and signed readouts; the receiver reconstructs a norm-scaled residual update using its own Jacobian-lens dictionary during prefill. This keeps each model’s KV cache local and supports different hidden dimensions.1a:T5bc,The paper casts collaboration as finite block optimization over x=(x1…xm) with a global loss F(x) accessible via an exact verifier. Each agent b controls block xb and has a cheap local objective Lb that ignores interactions. Define the true conditional change fb(u;x)=F(x with xb←u)-F(x). The key construct is a correction term Rb capturing the interaction effects missing from Lb; agents instead optimize a corrected surrogate \hat{f}_b(u;x)=Lb(u)+\hat{R}_b(u;x), where \hat{R}_b approximates Rb and is anchored so \hat{R}_b(xb;x)=0.
Protocol: (1) parallel private search—each agent proposes an update u_b by optimizing \hat{f}_b with other blocks frozen; (2) exact verification—coordinator evaluates true global deltas via F; (3) compatibility-based merging—build a dependency graph over proposals and select a non-interfering subset (via coloring) to merge, guaranteeing non-increasing F. Extension: even interfering proposals may be merged if the coordinator verifies that their joint application yields a larger decrease; the convergence rate then depends on the realized joint improvement rather than worst-case interference.
Theory: linear convergence up to an explicit error floor determined by correction approximation error; the floor vanishes with exact corrections. The appendices also discuss learning reusable corrections from global evaluations and provide a joint convergence characterization for both the solution and the learned correction.1b:T55d,The work has two layers. First, it operationalizes IFs and builds an annotated benchmark. The authors collect 1,828 traces from 9 MAS-benchmark configurations: 1,048 from MAST and 780 manually curated traces from WebWalker / MrlX on WebWalkerQA and BrowseComp-Plus. They use Gemini 3.1 Pro as an LLM judge to inspect traces step by step and flag candidate IFs, then validate annotation quality with human audits and cross-model re-annotation. On a stratified sample, 87.7% of reviewed IF annotations were independently retained by both human annotators, and none were jointly rejected; among IFs identified by at least 3 of 4 judges, Gemini covered 90.2%. Second, they turn IFs into actionable signals. They derive taxonomies of IF error types and absorption mechanisms through open coding, then run matched counterfactual replay: repair an IF while holding the rest of the trajectory as comparable as possible, and test whether the final outcome changes. Finally, they use IFs in two downstream ways: IF-based scoring for best-of-N trace selection, and IF-GRPO, a variant of Group Relative Policy Optimization that incorporates IF cost into the learning objective. The key distinction from standard outcome rewards or PRMs is that IF-GRPO explicitly optimizes against locally harmful-but-absorbed errors, exposing the trade-off between task completion and process quality.1c:T50b,There are four results worth retaining. First, IFs are common and not reducible to final failure. Across the full corpus, 54.3% of successful traces and 75.2% of failed traces contain at least one IF; conversely, 24.8% of failed traces contain none. This directly shows that outcome labels miss substantial process variation. Second, the annotation pipeline is reasonably stable, though not perfect. In the human audit, 64 of 73 reviewed IF annotations were retained by both annotators, and none were rejected by both. In cross-judge analysis over 90 traces, 174 IFs were identified by at least 3 of 4 judges, and Gemini recovered 157 of them. Third, counterfactual replay suggests IFs are not merely descriptive artifacts: repairing an IF improves failure rescue by up to 46.2 percentage points over no-op replay. That is the strongest evidence that IFs correspond to actionable process states. Fourth, IF-based scoring improves best-of-N trace selection over existing process reward models, and IF-GRPO improves accuracy in 6 of 7 configurations while reducing IF cost per task in 3.
