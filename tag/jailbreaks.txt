18:T459,The paper uses targeted weight pruning as a causal probe: identify a small set of parameters most responsible for harmful generations and remove them while preserving benign capabilities. Unlike activation/attribution methods that require choosing intervention sites and counterfactual activations, pruning is a direct parameter-level causal intervention. Main claims:
1) Compactness: harmful generation depends on an extremely small fraction of weights (~0.0005%).
2) Unified cross-domain mechanism: weights found from one harm category (e.g., malware) also control other harms (e.g., hate speech, physical harm instructions).
3) Alignment-induced compression/separation: aligned models show stronger separation between harm-generation weights and benign-capability weights than unaligned models; this separation strengthens with scale.
4) Generation vs knowledge dissociation: pruning disrupts producing harmful content, not recognizing/explaining harmfulness.
5) EM link: pruning substantially reduces emergent misalignment, even when pruning data come from a different harm domain than the fine-tuning domain.19:T83a,must_read (deep technical analysis + close-reading guide)

  1) Introduction: extract the three motivating failures (jailbreaks/EM/heuristics) and the core contributions (0.0005% weights, cross-domain unity, alignment-induced compression).
  2) Method: understand precisely how harmful weights are scored/selected, the pruning granularity, and how benign capability is protected/evaluated. Write down: the signal used to rank weights, constraints/selection to avoid capability loss, and evaluation protocol.

     - Figure 1 (mentioned): end-to-end pipeline (harm data → identify weights → prune → cross-domain + capability eval).

  5) EM experiments: scrutinize fine-tuning setup, inducing vs evaluation domains, and the evidence that mismatched pruning domains still mitigate EM.

  1) Elevating pruning into a mechanistic, causal interpretability tool;
  2) Empirically demonstrating a compact, unified weight mechanism for harmful generation;
  3) Proposing alignment-induced compression and connecting it to EM.
