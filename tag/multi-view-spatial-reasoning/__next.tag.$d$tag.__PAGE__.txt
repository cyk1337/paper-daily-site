9:T41d,For pretraining-oriented readers, the main value is not a new objective function but a stronger argument that capability structure in data matters. Some multimodal abilities, especially 3D consistency and multi-view reasoning, do not seem to emerge from scaling generic image-text data alone. This work contributes a concrete recipe: define a capability hierarchy, generate high-level tasks first, decompose them into prerequisite sub-questions, and use the resulting structure as both supervision and reasoning traces. That has three useful implications. First, data organization can be more important than raw volume for sparse capabilities. Second, chain-of-thought supervision can be induced from task decomposition rather than only from teacher-model distillation. Third, the paper treats SFT and RL as stages along the same capability curriculum, which is relevant to the pretrain-to-post-train interface. The contribution is best read as a solid data-and-training design paper for multimodal reasoning, not as a new foundation-model architecture.a:T534,The method has three coupled pieces. First, capability decomposition. The authors define three levels: Level I for single-view spatial perception, Level II for cross-view scene understanding, and Level III for complex contextual spatial reasoning. The key point is not difficulty ranking but dependency: Level III tasks are constructed so they require the lower-level capabilities and cannot be solved through single-view shortcuts. Second, top-down data generation. Using 3D-grounded assets and multi-view scene sources, the pipeline starts from complex Level III tasks and then decomposes them into prerequisite Level I and II sub-questions. This is important because the lower-level questions are not sampled independently; they are generated as supporting steps for a coherent spatial cognition problem group. The ordered answers to these sub-questions naturally become chain-of-thought annotations. Third, capability-aligned training. From the available text, the training recipe appears to use the hierarchy in a staged way, spanning supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, with the goal of keeping model reasoning aligned with the dependency structure encoded in the dataset.
