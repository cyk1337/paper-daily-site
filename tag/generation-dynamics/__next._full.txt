18:T6bd,The method has three layers. The first is empirical diagnosis. The authors show systematically that in ELF, more repetition often coincides with lower Gen-PPL. When repeated spans are stripped out, model rankings can reverse; the smallest model looks best largely because it repeats the most. The second layer is mechanism analysis. They rewrite self-conditioning as a feedback recurrence: the denoiser predicts a clean estimate, and that estimate becomes part of the next-step input. Linearizing this loop around sampling trajectories and inspecting the Jacobian reveals a dominant slowest-contracting mode that is nearly one-dimensional and strongly aligned with repetitive trajectories. They also run direct causal probes: increasing feedback strength alone raises repetition while lowering Gen-PPL, which supports the claim that the loop creates the pathology that the metric then rewards. The third layer is the fix, ACE (Attractor-Contrast-Escape). ACE subtracts a single fixed direction d from the self-conditioning feedback at every step. That direction is estimated without labels, auxiliary models, or retraining: collect feedback states from high-repetition trajectories and low-repetition trajectories, then take the difference of their means. The paper also derives a closed-form usable steering window so the intervention is strong enough to escape the basin but not so strong that it destroys semantics. A key design choice is where to intervene. ACE acts on the latent feedback state, not at token decoding, because the repetition is already formed upstream in continuous space before argmax readout. That makes it fundamentally different from repetition penalties, n-gram blocking, or decode-time heuristics.19:T5ab,Three result groups matter most. First is metric pathology. ELF-B reports a Gen-PPL of 19.5, but after removing repetition the score rises to 27.7, enough to change the apparent ranking against larger models. Sample-level analyses also show that more repetitive generations receive better GPT-2-based perplexity scores, confirming that the scorer favors the degenerate outputs. Second is mechanism validation. Increasing self-conditioning feedback strength increases repetition and further lowers Gen-PPL, which supports the proposed causal chain. The Jacobian analysis shows that the problem is dominated by a single slow-contracting direction, and sharper sampling settings deepen the basin. Third is ACE performance. A direction estimated once on the smallest model transfers well across inference settings and model scales; its cosine similarity to per-configuration re-estimated directions is reported between 0.82 and 0.96. Under the paper’s defect-controlled evaluation, ACE reaches human-clean text at 1.5x to 5x lower compute cost while maintaining competitive quality on accepted samples. The same direction-estimation recipe also recovers useful controls on Plaid and LangFlow, suggesting the issue is not ELF-specific but tied to self-conditioned continuous DLMs more broadly. 局限也很明显。样本规模不大，prompt 分类和正确/错误判定依赖 substring matching，模型只有一个 1.5B 开源模型，且 patching 只在 residual stream post-attention、最后 token 位置进行。因此结论更适合作为机制假设和因果证据雏形，而不是关于所有 LLM hallucination 的一般定律。1c:T5f0,The method has two parts. First, the authors introduce same-prompt bifurcation. Instead of comparing different prompts that happen to yield correct versus hallucinated outputs, they repeatedly sample the exact same prompt at nonzero temperature and keep prompts for which both outcomes occur. This is meant to isolate trajectory-level divergence from prompt-level semantic confounds.

Second, they run bidirectional activation patching on those bifurcating prompts. For each prompt, they cache full hidden states from multiple correct runs and multiple hallucinated runs, using the residual stream after attention across all 28 layers and generation steps. At a chosen layer and step, they replace the target run’s activation with the source run’s activation and continue autoregressive decoding. Two main directions are tested: hallucinated-to-correct (can a bad trajectory be repaired?) and correct-to-hallucinated (can a good trajectory be corrupted?). They also include controls: patching from a different prompt, patching hallucinated-to-hallucinated, and an unpatched baseline.

The technical novelty is not activation patching itself, but the causal asymmetry revealed by patching. Prior work often shows that hallucination is detectable in hidden states. This paper asks a stronger question: are those states sufficient to redirect the generation trajectory? The answer appears asymmetric: hallucinated states can derail correct trajectories much more easily than correct states can rescue hallucinated ones.1d:T744,All experiments are run on Qwen2.5-1.5B, a 28-layer transformer with d_model = 1536. The prompt set contains 61 prompts across six categories: factual, false premise, confabulation, leading, multi-hop, and math. Each prompt is sampled 20 times at temperature 0.7. A prompt is marked as bifurcating if it yields at least two correct completions and at least two hallucinated completions. By this criterion, 27 of 61 prompts, or 44.3%, bifurcate.

The paper reports that divergence often happens extremely early. For many bifurcating prompts, the KL divergence between the mean output distributions of correct and hallucinated runs is zero at step 0 and already exceeds 1.0 at step 1. That supports the claim that the trajectory split is an early commitment, not a late drift. Hidden-state separation heatmaps based on Cohen’s d further show strong class separation in mid-to-late layers.
