1b:T432,The study tests the hypothesis in both pretraining and supervised fine-tuning. In pretraining, the token budget, data, model configuration, and optimization setup are fixed, while the training context window is varied. Coherent document span length serves as a proxy for the amount of available relevant information. This design separates longer-context learning from simply seeing more tokens. In supervised fine-tuning, the context length is fixed and the amount of task-relevant context is directly controlled, isolating relevance from length. The theoretical account treats language modeling as compression with two predictive channels: information stored in parameters and information supplied in context. When context offers a lower-complexity solution, less task information needs to be encoded in the weights to reach the same risk threshold.
