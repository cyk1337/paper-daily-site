a:T776,The setup is simple but careful. The authors freeze model weights and intervene only at inference time. They study Qwen 3.5-2B (24 layers, d=2048) and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (16 layers, d=2048).

The language axis is built layer by layer. First, they collect hidden states from 20k held-out bilingual sentence pairs and subtract the layerwise mean. Second, they run PCA on the centered activations and keep the top 150 components. Third, instead of taking PC1, they score each principal component by Cohen’s d between the two language distributions and select the component with the largest absolute separation, orienting it so the positive direction corresponds to the target language. This gives one unit vector per layer. Compared with a raw mean-difference vector or a probe weight, this procedure constrains the search to high-variance subspaces and then explicitly selects the component that best separates the language pair.

They then perform two intervention types. Steering adds a scaled offset along the language axis during the forward pass. Ablation removes the projection onto that axis. Steering tests sufficiency: can the model be pushed into another language? Ablation tests necessity: does removing the axis impair the original language behavior? The paper also includes strong controls, especially equal-magnitude random perturbations, to rule out the trivial explanation that any large perturbation can flip the output.

Two technical findings stand out. First, the geometry is pair-specific rather than a single universal “language direction”: English-Chinese and English-Spanish require distinct axes. Second, the effect is strongly layer-dependent. Mid layers often resist steering, while later layers are more sensitive, suggesting that language realization is not decided only at the final logits, but surface-form commitment becomes much easier to alter in later representations.b:T58f,The experimental sweep is fairly large for an activation intervention paper. The main evaluation uses FLORES-200, with 500 English-Chinese pairs and 100 English-Spanish pairs. Axis calibration uses 20k held-out sentence pairs from MultiUN. In total, the study reports 1.26 million generations.

The main results are qualitative but consistent. Steering along the PCA+Cohen’s d language axis reliably induces language switching in both cross-script and same-script settings, while random perturbations of the same magnitude generally do not. That supports specificity rather than generic disruption. Ablation weakens or disrupts language-consistent generation, which supports the claim that the axis is not only decodable but causally used. Layer sweeps reveal a non-uniform control profile: some mid layers are relatively resistant even under stronger steering, whereas later layers are much easier to redirect. The paper also characterizes a boundary between coherent steering and generation collapse, showing that useful control exists only within a finite intervention range.

Second, they introduce geometry-aware steering. The baseline is standard linear steering, which adds a vector in activation space and scales its magnitude. The motivation is to keep interventions near the support of natural activations.

One result is a scaled isometry relation: geodesic distances on Mh align closely with geodesic distances on My, while Euclidean distances do not.
