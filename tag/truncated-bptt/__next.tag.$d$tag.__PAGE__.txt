9:T5c9,The study covers nine looped language models: seven published models and two converted ones, with sixteen pre-registered studies. The main findings are specific. On trained models, the lost-gradient curve is largely determined near the output; once the total number of loops is well above k, increasing the loop count changes the loss of gradient little, within about 0.03 from 32 to 64 loops for k≤8 in the tested setting. A geometric proxy ρ^k approximates the decay within roughly 20% at moderate loop counts, but it overestimates the lost gradient at longer loop counts because the fitted ρ drifts upward as farther, slowly fading loops enter the fit. A two-mode model explains this drift and its extrapolation to 96 and 128 loops held on Parcae-140m. Ouro is an exception: its backward signal grows, and keeping only one or two of four loops loses 92% or 81% of the final-output gradient; keeping two still loses 85% under its own objective without the entropy term. During fine-tuning, the lost gradient falls: after 300 full-backprop steps it drops by 0.07–0.22 per sequence on three of four models. Fine-tuning through only the last two loops also reduces it by 61–98% as much. In 600-step fine-tunes, keeping one of 32 loops in Parcae costs about 0.04 nats, while blended settings cost 0.005–0.016 nats, compared with a 0.18-nat benefit from training the loop at all.
