c:T9b3,方法叫 WebGraphMix。核心设计很朴素：先在 Common Crawl 的 host-level hyperlink graph 上给每个 host 算结构中心性分数，再把属于这些 host 的文档映射回预训练语料，最后按 central / peripheral 的比例构造训练 mixture。

图的定义是 host 级而不是 page 级。每个节点是一个 host，例如 wikipedia.org；有向边表示 host 间超链接。作者用的是 Common Crawl 的 host graph，规模 13.9M nodes、439.6M directed edges。之所以选 host-level，而不是 page-level，一是规模可控，二是 host 级位置更稳定，也更适合和大规模语料中的文档来源对齐。

中心性主要试了两类：Betweenness centrality 和 Katz centrality。Betweenness 强调一个节点是否位于大量最短路径上，适合刻画“桥接不同社区”的 host；Katz centrality 则考虑来自邻居及更远节点的影响衰减，偏向刻画全局连接中的重要性。作者也试了 PageRank，但没有看到提升，这一点和 DCLM 里 PageRank 信号效果一般的观察一致。这个负结果其实很有信息量：对预训练数据来说，‘被很多节点指向’未必等于‘提供更有用的学习信号’，而桥接性和结构位置可能更关键。

拿到中心性分数后，作者把语料按 host 分桶，构造几种 mixture：只采 central、只采 peripheral、以及 central/peripheral 的混合，尤其关注 50/50 这种简单配比。这里的关键不是做复杂的连续权重优化，而是先用可解释的结构分区，观察能力变化方向。

更有意思的是它和内容质量信号的结合方式。论文把 centrality 与 DCLM-fasttext 质量分类器做 multiplicative 和 divisive 组合打分。虽然全文细节公式需要结合方法节看，但直觉上是在问：如果一个文档既来自结构中心 host，又被内容分类器判为高质量，是否更值得采样；反过来，是否可以通过除法或重加权避免某一类信号完全主导。实验结果表明这种组合优于单独使用任一信号，说明 web graph topology 与文本内容质量确实捕捉到不同维度。

从方法取舍看，这篇工作刻意避免训练额外模型，也不做下游驱动的 mixture search。优点是轻、稳、可迁移；代价是 centrality 只是 host 级粗粒度信号，无法区分同一 host 内部页面质量差异，也可能把大型聚合站点的结构优势和真实文本价值混在一起。d:T85a,实验设置比较扎实，且控制得干净。作者把 WebGraphMix 接进标准化的 DCLM pipeline，训练了 400M 和 1B 两个规模的模型，分别用 8B 和 28B tokens。tokenizer、shuffle、优化器、训练流程都保持一致，尽量把变量压到“数据选择”本身。评测用的是 DCLM CORE v2 的 23 个任务，覆盖较广。

关键结果有几组。

1. 单独看 central vs peripheral 的能力偏置：
在 1B 模型上，采样结构更中心的 host 文档，相比 uniform sampling，在 Symbolic & Algorithmic Reasoning 上提升 +1.4%。而采样更边缘的 host，则在 Science & Factual Knowledge 和 Commonsense & Reasoning 上更好。这个结果很重要，因为它说明图位置不是单调的‘越中心越好’，而是不同区域承载不同能力相关信号。

2. mixture 优于单边采样：
用 betweenness centrality 做 50% central + 50% peripheral 的混合，在 23 个任务平均分上达到 41.4%，而 uniform sampling 是 39.8%。这是 +1.6 个点的平均提升。对预训练数据配方来说，这个幅度不小，尤其考虑到方法几乎不增加训练时成本。

3. 与内容质量信号结合进一步提升：
把 centrality 和 DCLM-fasttext 质量分类器结合后，平均分提升到 43.8%。也就是说，相比 uniform 的 39.8%，总提升达到 +4.0 个点；相比只用 graph mixture 的 41.4%，还有 +2.4 个点增益。这是全文最关键的结果之一，因为它直接支持“图结构信号与内容质量信号大体互补”。

4. 计算成本：
完整 Common Crawl host graph 的 centrality 计算总共不到 9 GPU 小时。这个数字很关键，它说明方法不是理论上可行、工程上很重，而是真的可以作为预训练前处理步骤。

Its contribution is not a more elaborate scorer, but a new selection axis: web-graph position. That matters for three reasons.

First, it provides a signal that appears complementary to content quality. When graph centrality is combined with the DCLM-fasttext quality classifier, performance improves beyond either signal alone. That suggests topology is not just a noisy proxy for existing quality scores.

Second, it sharpens the discussion around data mixtures and capability allocation. Central hosts seem to favor reusable abstractions and bridge-like information, while peripheral hosts contribute long-tail and specialized knowledge. The result is not 'higher quality is better' in a generic sense, but 'different structural regions induce different capability trade-offs.'

Third, the method is lightweight enough to matter in practice. Computing centrality on a 13.9M-node, 439.6M-edge host graph takes under 9 GPU hours and can be reused across many training runs. That is a much lower barrier than training multiple proxy models.

Overall, this looks like a credible addition to the pretraining data toolbox rather than a narrow benchmark trick.f:T938,The method, WebGraphMix, is simple by design. It computes structural centrality scores on the Common Crawl host-level hyperlink graph, maps those host scores back to documents in the pretraining corpus, and constructs mixtures that emphasize central hosts, peripheral hosts, or combinations of the two.

The graph is defined at the host level rather than the page level. Each node is a host such as wikipedia.org, and each directed edge indicates that one host links to another. The graph contains 13.9 million nodes and 439.6 million edges. This choice is important: host-level graphs are much more tractable than page-level graphs, and host identity aligns naturally with large-scale corpus provenance.

The paper mainly studies betweenness centrality and Katz centrality. Betweenness captures whether a host lies on many shortest paths and therefore acts as a bridge across otherwise weakly connected regions. Katz centrality measures global influence with distance decay. The authors also tried PageRank but did not observe gains, which is itself informative. For pretraining, being heavily linked may not be the same as providing broadly useful learning signals; bridge structure may matter more than raw popularity.

Once centrality scores are computed, documents are partitioned by host score and sampled into different mixtures: central-only, peripheral-only, and mixed settings such as 50/50. The point is not to solve a continuous optimization problem, but to create interpretable structural slices and observe how they affect learned capabilities.

A particularly useful part of the paper is the combination with content-based quality signals. The authors combine centrality with the DCLM-fasttext quality classifier using multiplicative and divisive scoring. The exact formulas matter less than the design intent: reward documents that are strong under both signals, while preventing one signal from completely dominating the other. The gains from this combination indicate that graph topology and textual quality capture different aspects of pretraining value.

The trade-off is clear. The method is cheap, unsupervised, and portable, but host-level centrality is coarse. It cannot distinguish high-value and low-value pages within the same host, and it may conflate structural prominence with actual textual usefulness for large aggregator sites.10:T87d,The experimental setup is strong and well controlled. WebGraphMix is integrated into the standardized DCLM pipeline. Models are trained at 400M and 1B scales with 8B and 28B tokens respectively. Tokenization, shuffling, optimization, and training procedures are held fixed so that the main variable is data selection. Evaluation uses the 23-task DCLM CORE v2 benchmark.

Several results stand out.

1. Central and peripheral regions favor different capabilities.
At 1B scale, sampling from structurally central hosts improves Symbolic and Algorithmic Reasoning by +1.4% over uniform sampling. In contrast, sampling from peripheral hosts improves Science and Factual Knowledge as well as Commonsense and Reasoning. This is one of the paper's most important findings: graph position is not a monotonic quality ranking. Different parts of the web graph support different capability profiles.

2. Mixed sampling beats one-sided selection.
Using betweenness centrality, a 50% central plus 50% peripheral mixture reaches 41.4% average across the 23 tasks, compared with 39.8% for uniform sampling. A +1.6 point average gain is substantial for a data-selection intervention with almost no training-time overhead.

3. Graph structure and content quality are complementary.
Combining centrality with the DCLM-fasttext quality classifier raises the average to 43.8%. That is +4.0 points over uniform sampling and +2.4 points over the graph-only 50/50 mixture. This is the strongest evidence that topology provides information largely orthogonal to content-based quality scoring.

4. The preprocessing cost is low.
Computing centrality over the full host graph takes fewer than 9 GPU hours in total. This matters because it makes the method operationally plausible for real pretraining pipelines.

The most informative ablations are not about tiny differences between centrality metrics. They are: PageRank does not help, betweenness is stable and interpretable, and central-peripheral mixtures outperform either extreme alone.
