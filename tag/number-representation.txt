19:T647,The approach has three pillars.
1) Metrics and setup: focus on embeddings e(n) for number tokens n in 0..999 (aligned with common tokenizers where these are single tokens). Compute a DFT along the token index and measure power at frequencies 1/T to identify spikes. Separately, train a linear probe (T-way logistic regression) to predict n mod T from e(n), operationalizing “geometric separability.”
2) Theory: prove that Fourier-domain sparsity/spikes are necessary but not sufficient for mod-T linear separability (Theorem 1). Intuitively, a periodic component in the sequence of embeddings does not guarantee that residue classes form linearly separable clusters; two systems can share near-identical spectra yet differ drastically in the geometry relevant to linear decoding.
3) Controlled attribution experiments: train ~300M-parameter models on matched data while perturbing one factor at a time. Data-side perturbations progressively remove (i) text–number co-occurrence, (ii) cross-number interaction signals, and (iii) long-context dependencies. Architecture comparisons include Transformers vs linear RNNs vs LSTMs under identical data. Optimizer comparisons test whether optimization path affects geometric convergence even when spectral convergence is stable. Tokenizer effects are probed via training-from-scratch addition tasks: multi-token numbers force modular subproblems (e.g., digit/block-wise modulo constraints), making circular/periodic representations more stable; single-token numbers admit multiple solution strategies, making learned representations more optimizer-dependent.1a:T665,The experimental story is built around contrasts and ablations rather than a single headline benchmark.
- Universality: T=2/5/10 spikes are observed across many released LMs and non-Transformer sequence models, plus GloVe/FastText; strikingly, the corpus-level frequency distribution of number tokens already exhibits a similar periodic spectrum.
- Two-tier outcome: spectral convergence is near-universal; geometric convergence is not. A key controlled contrast: Transformers and linear RNNs (in large-token training settings) yield high mod-T probe accuracy, while LSTMs trained on the same data can show even stronger spikes yet remain near chance on mod-T probing.
- Data ablations: removing co-occurrence, cross-number interactions, or reducing context length degrades mod-T probe accuracy substantially, while Fourier spikes persist—supporting the claim that spikes are largely data/tokenization-driven, whereas linear decodability requires additional structured signals.
- Architecture/optimizer: architecture is decisive for geometric convergence; optimizer can affect whether separable structure emerges in some settings, but does not eliminate the spectral spikes.
- Tokenizer/task constraint: in addition-from-scratch, multi-token number representations more reliably induce modular/circular features due to forced modulo subproblems; single-token setups allow alternative shortcuts, leading to higher variance and optimizer dependence.
