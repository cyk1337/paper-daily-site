In language-model experiments, the construction exhibits a sharp absorbing-state transition measurable to three decimal places, but the paper attributes it to the probe rather than the model. The mechanism is an attracting fixed point of the argmax map, and it appears only for radii r∈{1,2}. A masked-LM control, whose map lacks the same fixed point, shows no such transition. The paper also reports four retracted conclusions caught by known-answer systems, motivating estimator gating for black-box LM dynamics.b:T80d,The setup is straightforward. For an input x, a latent CoT model produces a sequence of hidden states z_1, z_2, ..., z_T. The paper treats this sequence as a trajectory in high-dimensional representation space and studies it with both quantitative metrics and low-dimensional visualizations.

The quantitative side focuses on three families of signals. First, step-to-step change measures how much the hidden state moves between adjacent latent steps, which helps distinguish active iterative computation from early collapse. Second, direction consistency measures whether successive updates point in similar directions, indicating smooth progression versus oscillatory behavior. Third, stability-related metrics include Lyapunov sensitivity and Dynamic Mode Decomposition. Lyapunov-style analysis estimates sensitivity to small perturbations, while DMD models the latent sequence as a discrete-time dynamical system, extracting dominant modes and eigenvalues from snapshot pairs to characterize convergence or instability.

For visualization, the paper uses t-SNE, UMAP, PHATE, and DMD-mode projections. Importantly, it states that all quantitative metrics are computed in the original hidden space, with dimensionality reduction used only for visualization. That is a sensible choice because low-dimensional embeddings can easily create misleading geometric impressions.

The experiments use CODI, COCONUT, and SIM-CoT variants on GSM8K. All models are GPT-2-small scale: 124M parameters, 12 layers, hidden size 768, and an extended vocabulary of 50,260 with three latent special tokens. Each model runs a fixed T=6 latent reasoning steps per input. The dataset is stratified into seven math concept categories, allowing the authors to compare trajectory structure across task types.

Methodologically, this is closer to trajectory analysis than to mechanistic interpretability in the causal sense. It does not localize circuits or prove semantic roles for individual latent steps. It studies geometry and stability statistics of the latent reasoning process.c:T536,The experimental emphasis is descriptive rather than benchmark-driven. The paper argues that latent CoT trajectories are structured and non-random: they show measurable movement across steps, some degree of directional consistency, and differences in stability and geometry across training paradigms. CODI, COCONUT, and their SIM-CoT variants are compared to show that training choices affect smoothness, clustering patterns, and perturbation sensitivity of latent trajectories.

The concrete setup is clear from the paper: GSM8K train and test combined, 8,792 examples total, stratified into seven concept categories; GPT-2-small backbones with 124M parameters; six latent reasoning steps per sample; greedy decoding with max length 512; fixed seed 42. The paper also makes the important methodological point that quantitative conclusions are drawn from the original representation space rather than from projected plots.

From a pretraining research perspective, however, the evidence remains exploratory. The work is stronger as phenomenology than as mechanism. Without stronger intervention studies, such as perturbing specific latent steps and measuring downstream effects, or showing that the dynamical metrics predict accuracy or training efficiency, the reported geometric patterns are hard to turn into concrete design rules.d:T461,For a skim, start with the framework figure and the experimental setup, then read one or two pairs of quantitative and visualization results together.
