9:T759,The model continues the Kathleen line. The input side uses a ByteRotate encoder: instead of learning 256 separate byte embeddings, it learns one vector and derives all byte representations through DFT phase rotation. This means the whole byte alphabet is covered with only 128 parameters. Sequence modeling is handled by a multi-scale reverberant state, which replaces attention with three banks of content-gated exponential decay states. The recurrence is s_t = gamma_t s_{t-1} + (1-gamma_t) v_t, where gamma_t depends on content, and the three decay ranges cover short, medium, and long memory. A reverb block is LayerNorm, then reverb in parallel with causal depthwise convolution, then learned gate mixing, then FFN. The trunk has three such blocks with hidden size 128. A notable engineering addition is fast scan: the recurrence is parallelized in chunks of 16 using a cumprod/cumsum closed form, numerically matching the sequential loop to 1e-7 while giving roughly 10–50x wall-clock speedup. That makes the 512 MB training point feasible on free-tier hardware. The baseline is a parameter-matched attention model with the same skeleton, replacing reverb plus convolution by 4-head causal SDPA. Beyond byte-level LM, the paper also uses a 3.08M-parameter word-level variant with a 10K vocabulary for generation studies. Another technical contribution is FORM DISTANCE, a non-parametric, frozen, offline metric for whether sampled text has the statistical form of natural text. It is not a semantic metric. The authors explicitly document one case where the metric was gamed and how they patched it, which makes the evaluation setup more credible than LLM-as-a-judge for this setting. Finally, the decode-time attribution study compares decoding policies, retrieved phrase memory, and several trunk modifications to isolate where generation improvements actually come from.a:T64b,The central experiment is a five-point data-scaling study on WikiText-103 raw UTF-8 bytes, using training subsets of 2, 8, 32, 128, and 512 MB, a fixed 1 MB test slice, 2 epochs, sequence length 256 bytes, and identical AdamW plus cosine recipes for both models. The attention-free reverb model wins at every data point against the parameter-matched transformer baseline. At 512 MB, the reverb model reaches 1.84 bits per byte versus 2.04 for the transformer. At 32 MB, reverb gets 2.011 bpb, while the transformer trained on the full 512 MB still only reaches 2.043 bpb, which the authors summarize as at least a 16x data advantage at equal scale. The log-log slopes are -0.074 for REVERB and -0.107 for ATTN. The paper correctly warns against reading the steeper transformer slope as superiority; it is more consistent with catching up from behind, while both curves are already flattening. The first is Causal History Routing (CHR). Streaming AR video generation proceeds block by block, and each new block depends on a historical KV cache. The authors argue that meaningful exploration should happen through alternative uses of historical context rather than through noise injection in latent space. In CHR, the sink cache is kept fixed to preserve global anchors, while the local cache is stochastically routed to create branch-specific contexts. The most recent frames remain fixed, while earlier local-memory slots are selectively varied across branches. This is intended to produce semantically different but causally grounded continuations. The key claim is that such exploration stays on-manifold because it does not alter the generator into a stochastic diffusion process.

The second component is an ODE-native surrogate policy. Prior ODE-based GRPO variants such as NeighborGRPO approximate the surrogate policy using Euclidean distances between generated samples. The authors argue that this assumes an overly uniform geometry in latent space and may not reflect the model’s actual preference structure. They instead define Trajectory Velocity Energy (TVE) in the flow-matching velocity-field space, measuring how likely the current policy is to reproduce a branch trajectory under the native ODE dynamics. A Gibbs-form surrogate policy is then built from TVE, and combined with group-relative rewards to form a reward-weighted contrastive flow-matching objective. The main design choice is clear: preference optimization is embedded directly into the model’s learned velocity field rather than imposed through external sample-space geometry or SDE transition kernels.

Methodologically, the paper is internally consistent. If the base model is a few-step distilled AR video generator with causal attention and KV caching, then both exploration and optimization are designed to exploit exactly those structures.d:T4ad,The experiments are conducted on multiple distilled AR video generators, covering both single-prompt short-video and multi-prompt long-video settings. The qualitative claim is that CHR produces branch diversity at the semantic and narrative level, while noise injection mostly changes texture or local appearance. First, multi-noise training. Standard TARFlow trains by maximum likelihood on noisy inputs and derives a score for denoising from the gradient of the learned log-density with respect to the input. iTARFlow no longer commits to one fixed noise level. Instead, it trains the same Transformer autoregressive flow across a range of noise scales, so the model explicitly learns the noisy data distribution at multiple corruption levels. The motivation is straightforward: high noise supports coarse global synthesis, while lower-noise scores are better suited for recovering fine detail. A single noise level forces the model to compromise between the two.

Second, iterative inference. Sampling starts from a high-noise autoregressive generation step, preserving the sequential generation structure of the flow. Then, instead of applying a single Tweedie-style cleanup, the model performs iterative denoising across the trained noise levels. The refinement updates are driven by scores obtained implicitly from the likelihood parameterization. This is the key distinction from diffusion. Diffusion models are trained to predict noise or score directly and sample by integrating a reverse stochastic or deterministic process from pure noise. iTARFlow keeps end-to-end exact likelihood training throughout; score-based denoising appears only as an inference-time refinement mechanism derived from the density model.

Architecturally, the paper is effectively splitting generation into two roles: autoregressive flow for globally coherent coarse synthesis, and iterative parallel denoising for local cleanup. Compared with discrete autoregressive image models, it avoids tokenizer-induced information loss. Compared with diffusion, it preserves a likelihood-based training objective. Compared with classical one-shot flow sampling, it accepts that a single inverse pass is not enough for visual quality and adds a controlled refinement stage.f:T674,The experimental story appears to center on three points. The abstract states competitive performance across ImageNet resolutions and substantial gains over previous TARFlow-based models, while narrowing the gap to diffusion and discrete autoregressive generators.
