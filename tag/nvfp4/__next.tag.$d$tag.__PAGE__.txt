9:T6e4,The method, QUADS, stands for quantization-error alignment across dual sides. The central idea is to treat trainer-side and rollout-side errors differently instead of forcing a single quantization scheme over both. The diagnostic step is crucial: the authors separately quantize weights and activations during rollout and compare W4A16 against W16A4. W16A4 closely reproduces the failure mode of full W4A4, while W4A16 is much more stable, showing that activation quantization dominates the instability and that weight and activation errors are roughly additive. Based on this, QUADS has two components. First, trainer-side asymmetric QAT: weights are fake-quantized to NVFP4, but activations remain in BF16. The rationale is that weights are synchronized across rollout and trainer engines, so their quantization–dequantization path can be aligned; activations are recomputed online during rollout and cannot be faithfully matched by symmetric W4A4 fake quantization on the learner side. Second, rollout-side residual activation compensation: since activation error is the main problem, the rollout engine performs a second-pass quantization on high-residual channels to reduce activation-induced log-prob bias while preserving native W4A4 GEMMs. The implementation uses fused kernels so that most of the throughput benefit of FP4 Tensor Cores is retained. This differs from prior work in an important way. Weight-only NVFP4 methods such as QeRL never enter the true W4A4 regime because activations stay in BF16, and FP8/INT8 RL systems operate on a much denser numerical grid where mismatch can often be controlled by clipping or truncated importance sampling. QUADS is designed specifically for full-parameter MoE GRPO with native FP4 activation quantization.a:T480,The empirical story is straightforward. Naive NVFP4 rollout with W4A4 inference and BF16 training collapses after roughly 150 RL steps: reward and held-out performance drop, while the per-token rollout–trainer log-probability gap grows monotonically and pushes importance ratios outside the trust region. The weight-vs-activation ablation then identifies the source: W16A4 largely matches the instability of full W4A4, whereas W4A16 remains much closer to stable behavior, supporting the claim that activation error is the dominant driver. In the final results, QUADS reaches 72.86% average pass@1 across four held-out benchmarks, essentially matching the BF16 baseline at 73.15% and far outperforming naive NVFP4 at 51.37%. At the same time, it improves rollout throughput by 16% over FP8.
