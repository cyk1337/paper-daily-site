b:T780,MechaRule has two main stages.

First, it performs task-local rule extraction. Given a dataset and a success metric, the method splits examples into success and failure cases. It then builds an interpretable feature space using an LLM-based agent that inspects contrastive examples and proposes candidate predicates, such as divisibility conditions or output-range conditions. These automatically proposed predicates are combined with a small set of manual seed features, and sparse if-then rules are extracted with RuleSHAP. The goal is not a universal global explanation, but a compact symbolic hypothesis for a specific behavior.

Second, it performs neuron localization through contrastive hierarchical ablation. Instead of exhaustive single-neuron probing, the method relies on two empirical hypotheses: sparse gating, meaning a behavior is often controlled by a small set of neurons, and regime-conditional dominance/overlap, meaning intervention effects can be monotone, saturating, and partially masked by dominant neurons. The paper calls this masking pattern overtopping.

Based on these assumptions, candidate neurons are organized into a hierarchy of nested groups. The method ablates groups first and only descends into branches whose group-level effect remains sufficiently large. The pruning criterion is not additive decomposition, but a high-confidence below-threshold group effect. This is important because intervention effects need not be linear or independent. The result is a more efficient search procedure that can still recover causally important neurons under overlap and saturation.

Finally, the extracted rules are anchored to the localized neurons. The rules predict when a neuron should matter; interventions test whether that neuron actually controls the behavior. That is the central novelty: symbolic rules are tied to causal internal units rather than treated as standalone surrogates.c:T405,From the provided full-text excerpt, the experiments are designed to validate the pipeline rather than to maximize benchmark performance. The main empirical claims are:
- a small number of agonist neurons can strongly disrupt rule-related behavior when neutralized, supporting the sparse-gating hypothesis;
- group-level intervention effects show approximate monotonicity and saturation within a fixed regime, which justifies hierarchical group ablation;
- dominant neurons and broad antagonistic effects can mask weaker but behavior-specific neurons, making adaptive descent preferable to flat exhaustive search;
- the extracted symbolic rules can predict which examples are controlled by particular neurons, suggesting a nontrivial alignment between symbolic behavior descriptions and internal causal structure.
