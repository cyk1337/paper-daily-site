18:T448,The core metric is Token Economy Score, TES(Mr, Mb, T) = [Acc(Mr,T) - Acc(Mb,T)] / [GenTok(Mr,T) / GenTok(Mb,T)]. Mr is the reasoning-enabled model, Mb is the non-reasoning baseline, and GenTok is reasoning tokens plus final answer tokens. Input tokens are deliberately excluded because they are mostly determined by the benchmark prompt and evaluation harness rather than by the model’s reasoning behavior. TES is therefore a marginal metric, unlike absolute accuracy-per-token metrics such as Per-Token Intelligence. TES greater than 1 means the accuracy gain in percentage points exceeds the generated-token multiplier; values between 0 and 1 indicate positive but weak marginal efficiency; values at or below 0 indicate that reasoning is wasteful or damaging. The paper also defines deployment-facing quantities, including Reasoning Cost Share and Deployment Cost Multiplier, to connect token behavior with cloud and on-premises cost profiles. A key caveat is explicit: TES is a practical convention, not a universal utility function, and should be read with baseline and absolute accuracy.19:T87f,方法分三步：generation、routing、refinement。先从同一个生成模型独立采样 n 条完整轨迹，再用 reward model 对整条轨迹打分。与直接按绝对分数阈值决策不同，SRD 采用 rank-normalized score，把候选按 reward 排序后映射到 [0,1] 区间，再用两个阈值 θ_low 和 θ_high 做三路路由：高分样本 Keep，中间样本 Refine，低分样本 Discard。这样做的动机是减少不同题目、不同 reward 尺度下绝对分数不稳定的问题。

真正的创新在 refinement。对进入 Refine 的边界样本，算法不会整条重采，而是沿轨迹按固定步长 m 检查 prefix-level reward 变化，寻找最早出现“相对前缀退化”的位置 j，把 j 之后的 suffix 截掉并重新生成。重生成时提高采样温度，目的是增加局部探索，避免原轨迹在同一错误模式附近反复采样。新 suffix 接回原 prefix 后，再重新打分并与其他活跃候选一起排序，只保留最终进入 Keep 的样本。

论文还给了理论分析。核心结论是，在温和假设下，SRD 相比 rejection sampling 有严格更高的期望最优轨迹质量，并带来约 1.28–1.36 倍的 sample efficiency 提升，且收益会随候选池增大而上升。文中把收益写成与 (1 + ρ·p_M/p_H) 相关的形式，其中 p_M、p_H 对应中等质量可修复样本与高质量样本的概率质量，ρ 则刻画 refinement 成功率。这个表达很重要，因为它说明 SRD 不是无条件占优：只有当“边界样本里确实存在可救前缀”且 reward 能识别退化位置时，局部重写才有意义。

和已有方法相比，Best-of-N 只会在末端选择，完全不回收中间结构；speculative rejection 会更早停掉低质前缀，但一旦拒绝就不可逆；Controlled Decoding 依赖 prefix-aware value function 在 token 级引导，而 SRD 不需要额外训练 value function，也不需要更大 teacher，只需生成模型加 reward model。代价是它仍然依赖完整轨迹采样和额外 reward 评估，且边界定位目前较粗，主要靠固定间隔扫描，不是细粒度 token-level attribution。1a:T95d,SRD has three stages: generation, routing, and refinement. It first samples n complete trajectories from the same generator, then scores them with a reward model. Instead of using an absolute reward threshold, it converts rewards into rank-normalized scores in [0,1], and applies two thresholds, theta_low and theta_high, to route candidates into Keep, Refine, or Discard. The motivation is practical: absolute reward scales are often unstable across tasks and prompts, while rank is more robust.

The main novelty is the refinement stage. For borderline candidates routed to Refine, SRD does not resample the whole trajectory. It scans the trajectory at fixed intervals m, evaluates prefix-level reward behavior, and finds the earliest point j where quality appears to degrade relative to the preceding prefix. It then truncates the suffix after j and regenerates only that suffix, using a higher sampling temperature to encourage local exploration and avoid repeating the same failure mode. The repaired trajectory is rescored and reranked jointly with the remaining candidates, and is kept only if it survives the final routing.

The paper also provides a theoretical analysis. Under mild assumptions, SRD achieves strictly higher expected best-trajectory quality than rejection sampling and improves sample efficiency by about 1.28–1.36x, with gains increasing as the candidate pool grows. The gain is characterized by a term of the form (1 + rho * p_M / p_H), where p_M and p_H capture the mass of repairable medium-quality samples and already-high-quality samples, and rho reflects refinement success. This matters because it clarifies when SRD should help: only when there is a meaningful population of salvageable trajectories and the reward signal can identify degradation reliably.

Relative to prior work, best-of-N only selects at the end and never recovers partial structure; speculative rejection can stop early but is irreversible once a prefix is rejected; Controlled Decoding uses a learned prefix-aware value function for token-level steering, whereas SRD needs only a generator and a reward model, with no larger teacher and no separately trained value function. The trade-off is that SRD still requires full-trajectory sampling up front and extra reward evaluations, and its boundary detection is fairly coarse, based on interval scanning rather than token-level attribution.1b:T6b0,The experiments cover MATH500, GPQA, HotpotQA, and AlpacaEval, spanning mathematical reasoning, science QA, multi-hop QA, and instruction following. The main empirical claim is about the accuracy-compute frontier rather than a single accuracy number: at matched or near-matched quality, SRD uses fewer generated tokens; under low compute budgets, it tends to outperform speculative rejection; under larger budgets, it approaches best-of-N quality at lower cost.

The clearest number stated in the paper is the theoretical 1.28–1.36x sample-efficiency gain. The full text indicates that the gain becomes more visible with larger candidate pools, because more borderline trajectories become available for repair. The ablations focus on two issues. First, reward calibration: when rewards are stable and ranking is reliable, conservative global rerouting works better because it identifies worthwhile candidates more consistently; when rewards are noisy, local self-comparison is more robust because it reduces misrouting from cross-sample score instability. Second, refinement boundary choice: truncating too early destroys useful context, while truncating too late leaves too little room for repair, so the interval size m and routing thresholds matter.

A useful experimental detail is that the authors test multiple generator–reward model pairings rather than tying the method to one specific model stack. That suggests SRD is a search-layer improvement rather than an artifact of a single base model. Still, many results are presented as tradeoff curves and relative gains, so readers who care about exact token costs and absolute per-benchmark deltas should inspect the main tables and appendix carefully.1c:T5a4,For a quick read, start with the introduction and the main method figure. The key idea is simple: SRD does not just sample more trajectories and pick one; it repairs near-miss trajectories by preserving good prefixes. Then read the routing rule and the regeneration-boundary definition. The use of rank-normalized reward, rather than absolute reward, is an important design choice because it determines how stable the method is across tasks.

After that, skim the theory section for the main proposition rather than every proof detail. Focus on the assumptions behind the sample-efficiency gain: the prevalence of repairable borderline samples, the success rate of suffix regeneration, and the reliability of reward ranking. Those assumptions define the regime where SRD should work. First, it formalises the collaboration tax using team-decentralisation and cooperative-game language. Second, the authors build a 32-task suite grouped into Spatial, Relational, and CSP families. Third, the paper develops a mechanistic account of failure. It identifies a four-stage conversational cascade: grounding, querying, integration, and re-derivation. Grounding failures occur when agents introduce facts supported by neither private view. Querying failures occur when they do not ask the partner for missing information. Integration failures occur when they fail to combine both views before committing. Re-derivation failures occur when the receiver accepts an answer without recomputing it. In Stage 1, the model samples K full solution traces. The purpose is to compress a long reasoning trace into a small set of high-information, checkable propositions. In Stage 2, the same model is not asked to solve the problem again.
