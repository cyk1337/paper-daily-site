19:T78a,The core idea is WriteSAE. A standard SAE uses vector decoder atoms, which is appropriate for reconstructing residual activations. But in Gated DeltaNet-like models, the native per-token write is a rank-1 outer product k_t v_t^T into a matrix state S_t. One can still train an SAE on vec(S_t), but a generic vector atom does not correspond to a valid native write event. WriteSAE therefore constrains each decoder atom to factor as a rank-1 outer product v_i w_i^T, matching the architecture’s write shape. The model is trained as a TopK SAE on mean-centered state vectors with reconstruction loss plus a dead-feature revival term. This makes each atom interpretable as a cache-slot write primitive that can be swapped in for a native write.

The paper then builds three validation mechanisms. First is cache-slot substitution: replace one native write with a learned atom at matched Frobenius norm, continue the forward pass, and compare the resulting output KL against a matched-norm ablation baseline. If the atom beats ablation, it is doing more than simply deleting the write. Second is a closed-form logit-shift approximation. The authors derive a three-factor expression in which the downstream effect of a write perturbation factors into a gate-decay term, a read-query term, and an unembedding term. This is useful because it links a local recurrent-state intervention to final token logits using quantities observable from a single forward pass. Third are direct interventions—erase, install, and generation probes—to test whether the learned atoms can causally steer model behavior.

The paper also distinguishes between register-like and bundle-like atoms, depending on whether the write direction is recoverable from the cache or dispersed across it. This is used to compare architectures by an effective write-rank notion, with GDN, RWKV-7, and Mamba-2 exhibiting different degrees of low-rank write structure.1a:T718,The main experiments are run on Qwen3.5-0.8B at a specific layer/head location, with transfer tests on Mamba-2-370M. The central substitution test is performed at Qwen3.5-0.8B L9 H4. Across 4,851 atom firings, replacing the native write with the learned atom beats matched-norm ablation in 92.4% of cases. At the population level over 87 atoms, the success rate is 89.8%. This is the strongest evidence that the learned atoms capture functional structure of the native write rather than merely approximating deletion.

The closed-form logit-shift validation is also strong in the reported regime. Across 200 atom-by-epsilon cells, the median R^2 between predicted and measured logit shifts is 0.98. If robust, this suggests that in Gated DeltaNet-style gated recurrences, the effect of a single write can be tracked with a relatively simple interpretable expression.

The intervention results are more causal. Erasing one example atom (F412) on its native firings reduces the promoted token’s log-prob by 0.116 nats on average, with n=150 and p=1.07e-6. Single-position predictive installs get the sign right in 84.6% of 2,000 atom-token-context triples. Sustained three-position installs at 3x strength raise target-in-continuation from 33.3% to 100% under greedy decoding over 300 contexts, with +1.27 nats of first-step support. Transfer to Mamba-2-370M L24 H0 yields an 88.08% substitution-over-ablation win rate.

Importantly, the paper also reports several nulls or boundary cases, including failures or weaker results for 4B firing-level tests, Mamba-2 closed-form validation, top-1-matched rank-2 settings, and Mamba-2 generation interventions.
