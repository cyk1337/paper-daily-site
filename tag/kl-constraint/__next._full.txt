18:T5c5,The method starts from a unified gradient view. For a group of responses under the same prompt, positive weights increase the likelihood of preferred responses, negative weights suppress dispreferred ones, and magnitudes determine update strength. GVPO uses the analytical solution of KL-constrained reward maximization to design these weights. The obstacle is the partition function in the closed-form solution, which would require an intractable expectation over all possible responses. The key condition is that if the assigned weights within a prompt group sum to zero, the partition function cancels across compared responses. GVPO then defines the response weight as the difference between two centered distances: one from implicit rewards induced by the current and reference policies, and one from actual rewards. The authors provide two interpretations. First, the GVPO gradient matches the gradient of a mean-squared-error objective between implicit-reward and actual-reward centered distances. Second, the loss can be decomposed into a group-relative advantage term, a covariance term between current and reference policies that constrains excessive policy drift, and a current-policy variance term that encourages moderate entropy. Compared with GRPO, it avoids uncontrolled importance-sampling ratios.
