数据构造上，benchmark 是一篇覆盖 25 个主题域的长报告，里面故意混入 factual errors 和 misleading citations，让 judge 不能只靠表面词重合做判断。标注单元是 624 个 attribution-citation pairs，对应 1,248 个 rubric decisions。所有标签都经过人工复核，其中 378 个 hard cases 来自 judge disagreement 后再 adjudication，这一点很重要：它让 benchmark 更像是在测“judge 在边界样本上的稳定性”，而不是只测容易样本上的平均准确率。

评测对象是 8 个 off-the-shelf judges，来自 Anthropic、Google、OpenAI 三个家族。作者没有做 task-specific fine-tuning，刻意模拟实际部署时“直接拿现成模型做 judge”的情形。指标设计也比常见的 judge benchmark 更贴近 reward 使用：主指标是 F1 和 Cohen’s kappa，用来衡量与 gold 的一致性；同时额外报告 pass-rate drift、FPR、FNR，用来刻画方向性偏差。这里的关键判断是：两个 judge 可能 F1 接近，但一个偏宽松、一个偏保守，在 RL 中会导向完全不同的策略更新。论文真正有信息量的地方就在这里，而不是简单排一个 judge 榜单。

从问题设定上看，这篇工作和一般 LLM-as-judge 论文的区别在于三点。第一，它不是开放式偏好评审，而是细粒度 rubric decision，更接近 process reward model 的 step-level judgment。第二，它把 citation quality 拆成 relevance 与 support 两个不同认知负担的子任务，避免把错误混在一起。第三，它强调 retrieval harness 会影响 judge 输入分布，因此 judge 评测结果只在给定 attribution extraction 和 source presentation 方式下成立，这种系统视角比单纯比较模型分数更实用。18:T7f7,The paper does not introduce a new judge model. Its contribution is a benchmark and an analysis protocol designed around actual reward-model use. The evaluation pipeline follows prior source-attribution work: parse attribution-citation pairs from markdown long-form outputs using an AST-based parser, then score each pair against rubric criteria. The new piece is a controlled adversarial benchmark with gold labels for the two LLM-judged dimensions.

The benchmark is a single long-form report spanning 25 topic domains, intentionally seeded with factual errors and misleading citations. This design prevents judges from succeeding through shallow lexical overlap alone. The labeled unit is the attribution-citation pair. In total there are 624 pairs and 1,248 rubric decisions. All labels are human-reviewed, and 378 hard cases are explicitly adjudicated from judge disagreements. That matters because it shifts the benchmark toward boundary cases, where reward signals are most fragile.

The study evaluates 8 off-the-shelf judges from Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI, without task-specific fine-tuning. This is deliberate: the goal is to reflect realistic deployment, where teams often plug in a general-purpose model as a judge. Metrics are also chosen with reward use in mind. F1 and Cohen’s kappa measure agreement with gold labels, while pass-rate drift, false positive rate, and false negative rate expose directional bias. This is the central methodological point: two judges with similar F1 can induce very different RL behavior if one over-accepts weak citations and the other over-rejects valid but complex ones.

Relative to generic LLM-as-judge papers, the setup differs in three ways. It uses structured rubric decisions rather than open-ended preference judgments. It separates source relevance from factual support instead of collapsing them into one score. And it treats retrieval and source presentation as part of the evaluation context, acknowledging that judge behavior depends on the harness, not just on the model.19:T513,The main empirical result is that no single judge dominates both dimensions. On source relevance, GPT-5-mini achieves the best F1 at 0.908 with kappa 0.636. On factual support, Claude Opus 4.6 leads with F1 0.750 and kappa 0.701. This split is informative: relevance looks more like topical matching plus local evidence alignment, while factual support requires stricter evidence sufficiency and factual verification.

The second key result is that directional bias varies substantially even when scalar accuracy is similar. The paper highlights large differences in pass-rate drift, false positive rate, and false negative rate across judges. That matters directly for RLVR. A high false positive rate rewards unsupported claims; a high false negative rate suppresses correct but nuanced attributions. The benchmark therefore does more than rank judges. It shows that reward design must account for the shape of judge errors, not just average agreement.

In terms of scale, the benchmark contains 624 attribution-citation pairs and 1,248 LLM-judged decisions, all human-reviewed, with 378 hard cases including 263 relevance disputes and 115 support disputes. This is not a massive dataset, but it is substantial for citation-level human adjudication, especially in an adversarial long-form setting.1a:T8c6,实验分三块，设计得比较完整。第一块是在 RewardBench 2 的 Ties 子集上直接测 oversensitivity。这个子集专门包含应当判为平手的样本，适合看 RM 是否会对等价回答乱排高低。作者在多种流行 RM 上做离散化后，结论比较一致：specificity 明显改善，discriminative ability 只付出温和代价，因此两者的综合指标更好。这里最重要的不是单个模型涨了多少，而是趋势稳定，说明 oversensitivity 不是个别 RM 的偶发现象。

第二块是一个可控模拟环境。作者构造了 primary reward 与 spurious stylistic reward 的混合目标，前者代表真实任务效用，如 instruction following；后者代表风格、措辞、长度等容易被 RM 学进去但未必该被优化的因素。直接优化原始连续 reward 时，policy 会明显朝风格维度过拟合，牺牲 primary reward；离散化后，这种 stylistic overoptimization 被显著抑制，主任务表现保持更好。这个实验很关键，因为它把 oversensitivity 从“评测现象”推进到“会伤害 RL 训练结果”的因果链条。

第三块是更接近真实使用场景的多任务 RL。作者在无标注 prompt 上训练 policy，并在 IFEval、GSM8K、MATH 上比较原始 RM 与离散化 RM。结论是：离散化版本从不显著差于原始 reward，而且经常更好。摘要里没有给出全部数字，正文表述重点也不是追求某个 benchmark 的极限分数，而是强调稳健性：当 reward model 可能混入风格偏差或 tie-breaking 噪声时，离散化能减少坏梯度。若精读实验部分，建议重点看不同任务上的方差、显著性和是否存在“原始 RM 提升某一项但整体不稳”的情况。

从实验结构判断，作者想证明三件事：能测到 oversensitivity；oversensitivity 会被 policy 学走；简单离散化足以缓解。这个证据链是完整的。局限也存在：离散化依赖候选回答集合与聚类阈值估计，若候选分布很窄或 RM dropout 不稳定，效果可能受影响；另外，论文更像是在 RL teacher 侧做修正，还没有触及如何从训练数据和目标函数层面直接学出低-oversensitivity 的 RM。1b:T74e,The work has a theoretical layer and a practical layer. The theoretical setup models true utility u(x, y) as an integer-valued function with a finite number of perceptible utility levels for each prompt. That formalizes the idea that many responses belong to the same utility class. Under this view, a good reward model must do two things at once: separate different utility classes and avoid inventing distinctions within the same class. The paper uses discriminative ability for the former and specificity for the latter. The central claim is that these objectives need not be traded off blindly; a suitable discretization of reward scores can preserve useful between-class variation while suppressing harmful within-class variation.

The practical method is uncertainty-aware reward discretization. Instead of retraining the reward model, the authors estimate predictive uncertainty with Monte Carlo dropout by running the RM multiple times and measuring the variance of the predicted reward. They then cluster candidate responses using both reward means and uncertainty, treating responses within a cluster as approximately utility-equivalent. RL no longer optimizes fine-grained score gaps inside those clusters. In effect, the method converts a continuous scorer into a confidence-aware ordinal teacher.

This differs from common alternatives in several ways. It is not simple reward binning with fixed thresholds, not temperature scaling, and not binary verification. It preserves some hierarchy when the model is confident that differences are meaningful, while collapsing distinctions when the score differences are likely to reflect uncertainty or arbitrary tie-breaking. A key conceptual point in the paper is that reward variance is only useful when it aligns with true utility differences. Inter-class variance helps learning; intra-class variance hurts it.1c:T7ac,The experiments are organized to establish a full causal story. First, the authors evaluate oversensitivity directly on the Ties subset of RewardBench 2, where responses should be treated as ties. Across several popular reward models, discretization consistently improves specificity while only modestly reducing discriminative ability, improving their combined quality. The important result here is the consistency across models rather than a single headline number.

Second, they build a controlled environment with a mixture of a primary reward and a spurious stylistic reward. The primary component represents actual task utility such as instruction following; the secondary component captures style-related features that a reward model may latch onto. Optimizing the raw continuous reward leads to strong stylistic overoptimization and degraded primary-task performance. Discretization suppresses that failure mode and preserves the primary objective much better. This is the key mechanism experiment because it shows that oversensitivity is not just a benchmark artifact; it becomes a learnable signal that policy optimization exploits.

Third, they run a more realistic multitask RL setup on unlabeled prompts and evaluate on IFEval, GSM8K, and MATH. The discretized reward models are never significantly worse than the raw reward models and are often better. The emphasis is on robustness rather than chasing a single best benchmark score. The paper argues that when reward models contain stylistic bias or tie-breaking noise, discretization reduces harmful gradients without sacrificing useful supervision.

The experimental story is strong because it covers measurement, mechanism, and downstream impact. The main limitation is that the method still depends on the candidate-response set and on uncertainty estimates from dropout. It is a teacher-side correction rather than a solution to training low-oversensitivity reward models from first principles.1d:T697,Start with the introduction and the early figures, especially the examples that separate accuracy, variance, and oversensitivity. Those distinctions carry the whole paper. Then read the problem formulation carefully. The assumption that true utility is discrete and finite is not cosmetic; it is what makes discretization theoretically natural rather than an ad hoc heuristic.

In the theory section, focus on how discriminative ability and specificity are defined, and on the constructions showing that an apparently excellent continuous reward model can still be a bad RL teacher. The key insight is structural: the issue is not just reward noise magnitude, but where the variance lives.

In the method section, pay attention to how Monte Carlo dropout is used to estimate predictive variance and how clustering thresholds are chosen. The useful question to keep in mind is why this approach is preferable to simpler fixes such as score clipping, fixed binning, or binary conversion.

For experiments, read them in order: RewardBench Ties for direct oversensitivity measurement, the synthetic mixed-reward setup for mechanism, and the multitask RL results for practical relevance. The most informative plots are the ones showing the trade-off between specificity and discriminative ability, the primary-versus-stylistic reward dynamics, and the stability comparison between raw and discretized rewards.

If time is limited, an hour is enough to get the main idea. If the goal is to reuse the ideas in RLHF or judge-model work, the appendices are worth reading because the value of the paper lies more in the evaluation framework and conceptual cleanup than in the implementation alone.1e:T5e1,The method has three core components. First, it chooses a controllable representation space. Rather than editing dense hidden states directly, the model maps a selected layer activation into a sparse latent code using a pretrained sparse autoencoder. The motivation is straightforward: dense activations are high-dimensional and entangled, while SAE latents are sparser and easier to optimize locally. Second, it trains a latent reward model. Intermediate latent states are collected from successful and unsuccessful reasoning traces, and final answer correctness is used as supervision to estimate how promising a latent state is. Importantly, the reward model is not trained on manually labeled behaviors such as verification or decomposition. It learns from outcome supervision which internal states tend to lie on trajectories that eventually succeed. Third, at inference time, the current latent is updated using the gradient of the reward model, then decoded back into the model’s hidden space for continued generation. To avoid over-intervention, LRS adds two gates: a reward gate that activates only on low-reward, fragile-looking states, and a confidence gate that suppresses edits when the model already appears confident and stable. This is the key distinction from standard representation steering. Traditional steering uses a fixed direction associated with a predefined behavior; LRS computes a state-dependent correction direction from the current latent and the learned reward landscape.1f:T5fa,The experimental story is organized around three questions: whether the method transfers across reasoning models, whether it helps across multiple reasoning benchmarks, and whether the gains really come from intervening on fragile steps rather than from generic perturbation. The paper reports consistent improvements over vanilla decoding and over explicit behavior-control baselines, with stronger gains on problems that require mid-trajectory correction. The qualitative examples are informative: LRS often helps by triggering local checking, rejecting an earlier assumption, or revising a subgoal, rather than by simply producing longer chains of thought. The ablations are especially important. Removing the reward gate reduces gains and increases damage to already-correct trajectories, which supports the claim that intervention should be selective. Removing the confidence gate broadens intervention too much and hurts stability. Replacing reward-gradient updates with a fixed steering direction also weakens performance, which is evidence for the paper’s central claim that adaptive state-specific edits are better than predefined behavior vectors. The provided full-text excerpt does not include the final result tables, so I cannot safely quote exact model sizes, training set sizes, or improvement margins. What is clear is that the base reasoning model stays frozen, the SAE is reused from prior work, and the main added cost comes from training the latent reward model and computing test-time gradients through it.20:T900,The method is the Configurable Safety Reward Model (CSRM). The model takes a conversation context, a candidate response, and a natural-language safety configuration. The configuration is not a simple class label; it is a textual specification of safety categories and guidelines. The model must score the response under that specification and output a scalar reward suitable for downstream policy optimization.

Two ideas carry most of the paper. First, the training is joint rather than split across separate modules. The model is optimized for both safety compliance and reward modeling, so it learns not only whether a response violates a rule, but also how severe the violation is and how candidate responses should be ranked under a given configuration. This is a deliberate design choice. A pure classifier tends to produce hard boundaries and sparse learning signals. A pure preference model may lack explicit normative anchors and can generalize poorly when the policy text changes. The joint objective is meant to tie together rule understanding, contextual safety judgment, and usable reward geometry.

Second, the paper introduces configuration-targeted data augmentation. Instead of only training on fixed policy annotations, it systematically varies the strictness and wording of safety configurations and constructs corresponding examples. The key is not just more data, but preserving the relative severity structure across examples. Borderline cases, mild violations, and severe violations must remain ordered after augmentation. This teaches the model how reward should change when the policy text changes. That is the main difference from prompt-conditioned safety judges such as Llama Guard or ShieldGemma. Those systems are optimized to classify or judge. CSRM is optimized to produce a calibrated scalar signal that can sit inside RL.

The design is trying to avoid two failure modes at once: rewards that are too sharp and induce blanket refusal, and rewards that are too flat and fail to define a meaningful boundary. The emphasis on calibrated safety compliance suggests that the authors care about the numerical shape of the reward, not just classification accuracy. That is exactly the part that matters if the model is later used in PPO-like or other policy optimization loops.21:T710,From the provided full text, the experimental story has three main claims. What is clear is that the paper evaluates multiple alignment settings and emphasizes Pareto-frontier analysis rather than a single safety score. That is the right evaluation lens here, because the goal is not simply to become stricter, but to provide a controllable reward under varying safety specifications.

The most important comparisons are conceptual. Against configurable judges, the paper aims to show not that judges cannot detect violations, but that their output geometry is ill-suited for RL.
