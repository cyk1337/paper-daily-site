18:T732,The method has three main components. First, the paper rewrites sparse decoding error in terms of dropped probability mass δ and proves that the output error is bounded by 2Bδ, where B bounds the value vectors. Under softmax, δ remains strictly positive for any finite truncation budget. Under entmax, once the candidate set covers the true support, δ becomes zero and the output is exact. This reframes the objective from preserving large weights to avoiding support misses.

Second, the KV cache is partitioned into pages, and each page stores lightweight metadata rather than requiring full key/value loading. At decode time, the query interacts with page metadata to produce page-level scores or bounds; only the most promising pages are fetched, and α-entmax attention is computed over the selected entries. The point is not generic top-k pruning, but page-level support recovery with reduced HBM traffic.

Third, the paper introduces a Gaussian-aware entmax selector. Using lightweight statistics of score distributions within pages, it estimates the entmax threshold τ and predicts whether a page may contain tokens satisfying the support condition (α−1)s_i > τ. Unlike softmax-oriented sparse decoding methods that rely on max-score heuristics, block bounds, or generic thresholds, this selector is designed around the entmax support criterion itself. It also adapts the candidate budget to the score distribution instead of using a fixed number of retained pages.

Relative to prior sparse decoding work, the differences are clear. The target is not to approximate softmax outputs, but to recover entmax support. The selection rule is not based only on token contribution magnitude, but on whether a page may cross the entmax threshold. That leads to different behavior in support retention, dropped mass, and output error.19:T5a1,The experiments focus on three metrics: support retention, dropped mass δ, and final attention/output error, along with long-sequence decoding speed. The main empirical result is that EntmaxKV preserves more true support tokens than softmax-based sparse decoding under the same page budget, which leads to lower dropped mass. As the budget increases, the entmax error curve exhibits a clear zero-error transition once the support is fully recovered, matching the theory; softmax truncation does not show such a regime. The paper also reports wall-clock speedups over full-cache softmax and full-cache entmax in long-context decoding.

The most useful empirical patterns are:
1. As the candidate budget grows, entmax error drops to zero after a distinct support-recovery point, whereas softmax error decays smoothly but never vanishes.
2. The Gaussian-aware selector is more robust than simpler page-ranking baselines, especially when score distributions are sharp or sparsity varies across heads/layers.
3. The speed gains come primarily from reduced KV-page reads rather than reduced attention FLOPs, which is consistent with the paper’s memory-bound decoding premise.
