9:T6eb,The method, SpaRLax, has two main ingredients.

First, it builds stochastic but differentiable soft reasoning states. At each reasoning step, the model produces vocabulary logits, applies Gumbel reparameterization, and obtains a relaxed token distribution that can still support backpropagation. The key distinction from prior Gumbel-based soft-CoT methods is that the authors explicitly use the differentiability of the relaxation to restore gradient flow across reasoning steps. A soft token is therefore not treated as an opaque RL action; it becomes an internal node in the computation graph, allowing later losses and rewards to shape earlier soft reasoning choices through cross-step credit assignment.

Second, it introduces sparse relaxation. With a large LLM vocabulary, a dense softmax mixture spreads probability mass over many low-probability tokens, which injects long-tail noise into the mixed embedding and weakens reasoning focus. To address this, the paper replaces dense softmax with entmax on top of the Gumbel perturbation. Entmax can assign exact zeros to low-scoring tokens while preserving a continuous mixture over a small candidate set. This gives a better trade-off: it keeps the superposition capacity of soft reasoning but avoids the semantic blur of dense mixtures. The paper also argues that entmax adapts the degree of sparsity to uncertainty at each step, which is more principled than a hard top-k truncation.

Training is done in a controlled GRPO setup, using only final-answer rewards rather than supervised intermediate CoT labels. Evaluation is performed under both soft-CoT inference and standard hard-CoT inference. First, the paper rewrites sparse decoding error in terms of dropped probability mass δ and proves that the output error is bounded by 2Bδ, where B bounds the value vectors. Under softmax, δ remains strictly positive for any finite truncation budget. Under entmax, once the candidate set covers the true support, δ becomes zero and the output is exact. This reframes the objective from preserving large weights to avoiding support misses.

Second, the KV cache is partitioned into pages, and each page stores lightweight metadata rather than requiring full key/value loading. At decode time, the query interacts with page metadata to produce page-level scores or bounds; only the most promising pages are fetched, and α-entmax attention is computed over the selected entries. The point is not generic top-k pruning, but page-level support recovery with reduced HBM traffic.

Third, the paper introduces a Gaussian-aware entmax selector. Using lightweight statistics of score distributions within pages, it estimates the entmax threshold τ and predicts whether a page may contain tokens satisfying the support condition (α−1)s_i > τ. Unlike softmax-oriented sparse decoding methods that rely on max-score heuristics, block bounds, or generic thresholds, this selector is designed around the entmax support criterion itself. It also adapts the candidate budget to the score distribution instead of using a fixed number of retained pages.
