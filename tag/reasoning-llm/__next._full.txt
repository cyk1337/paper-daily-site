18:T49f,The central formulation treats test-time scaling as budgeted inference over the implicit prefix tree of an autoregressive model. Nodes are partial prefixes, complete paths are traces, and an inference algorithm specifies how to expand, score, stop, and aggregate under a budget. The work separates three regimes. Single-trajectory sequential scaling spends extra compute on one evolving solution path. Sampled inference generates completed candidates and then applies voting, reranking, verifier selection, judge selection, or minimum-Bayes-risk style reduction. Partial-state search allocates compute before completion, expanding and pruning unfinished prefixes. A useful distinction is made between evidence computed per trace and decisions made over a completed candidate pool. Causal stopping is modeled separately because it changes generation cost, not just selection behavior. For evaluation, the work distinguishes shared-bank comparisons, which isolate reducers or verifiers on the same candidate pool, from end-to-end comparisons, which evaluate the full inference system. Candidate-bank diagnostics include repeated-sampling metrics that separate discovery from stability.19:T426,Two desirable properties for group-based RL optimizers are first formalized: (P1) Gradient unbiasedness — the gradient estimator is an unbiased estimate of the true policy gradient; (P2) Length invariance — each trajectory's total contribution to gradient updates is independent of its length. An impossibility theorem is then proven: under outcome rewards (scalar reward assigned only at generation end, e.g. 0/1 for correctness) and group-relative baselines, no length-based weighting function can satisfy both P1 and P2 simultaneously. A parametric family of weighting functions f_α(L) = L^(α-1) (α ∈ [0,1]) characterizes the full tradeoff spectrum: α=0 recovers GRPO (length-normalized, length-invariant but gradient-biased), α=1 recovers Dr. GRPO (no length normalization, gradient-unbiased but length-biased). Quantitative analysis shows that for two trajectories with length ratio r, the longer trajectory captures r/(1+r) of the total gradient signal under Dr. GRPO, meaning length bias can be substantial even for moderate length differences.1a:T58d,The paper derives a first-order analysis of token-level entropy dynamics under GRPO. The entropy change of a token decomposes into the product of the trajectory-level advantage and a local entropy sensitivity function determined by the next-token distribution. This yields an advantage-surprisal quadrant view: positive-advantage low-surprisal tokens tend to decrease entropy, positive-advantage high-surprisal tokens tend to increase it, and the signs are reversed under negative advantage. Since low-surprisal tokens occur much more often, the realized update is biased toward entropy reduction. The authors also identify a near-criticality property: a mild perturbation to the weights of specific tokens can flip the direction of entropy evolution; beyond the critical threshold, the weight mostly controls magnitude rather than sign. STARE uses this observation in three steps. It selects entropy-critical tokens using batch-internal surprisal quantiles, amplifies the effective advantage of positive-advantage high-surprisal tokens, attenuates that of negative-advantage high-surprisal tokens, and activates the intervention only when batch-averaged entropy falls below a target. Compared with DAPO-style clip-higher, it remains active even when importance ratios stay close to one in near on-policy training. The architecture changes are:
- CCA (Compressed Convolutional Attention): performs sequence mixing in a compressed latent space via a lightweight convolutional down-projector, aiming to reduce training and prefill FLOPs and shrink KV-cache pressure for long context.
- ZAYA1 router: replaces the standard linear MoE router with a multi-layer MLP router to increase routing expressiveness; positioned as a small-parameter component that controls a large expert parameter pool, improving load balancing stability and quality.
- Learned residual scaling: learnable scaling on both the residual stream and layer input to control residual-norm growth with negligible overhead.
Training is “reasoning from scratch”:
- Reasoning/CoT data is included already in pretraining and midtraining. To fit long traces into limited context, AP-trimming truncates the tail of the reasoning while preserving the final answer; samples are dropped if even the answer cannot fit. This is a data-construction-time length control, not an inference-time or rollout-time constraint.
- Post-training uses a 4-stage RL cascade: (i) reasoning warmup on math/puzzles, (ii) a 400-task adaptive curriculum on RLVE-Gym, (iii) math+code verifiable RL with test-time-compute traces, and (iv) a final behavioral RL stage. Implementation details include asynchronous PipelineRL, DPPO Binary-TV trust-region masking, Dr-GRPO sequence-level aggregation, MaxRL advantage estimation, and explicitly no KL term in the reward (stability is handled via verifiers/precision/data curation).
