c:T990,论文围绕 WINO 做系统复评。WINO 的思路是：先用 confidence-based adaptive unmasking 逐步生成 token，再根据已生成 token 在当前上下文下的置信度，把低置信 token 重新 mask，给模型一次改写机会。作者没有提出新 remasking 规则，而是把问题拆成三个层次来检查。

第一层是公平比较。作者指出，早期 WINO 结果缺少一个关键 baseline：强 confidence-based unmasking 本身，例如 Fast-dLLM。若不和这种强基线比较，很容易把“更聪明地决定先解哪些 token”带来的收益误记到 remasking 头上。于是论文在相同模型、相同解码预算下，直接比较 Fast-dLLM 与 Fast-dLLM+WINO，考察 remasking 的边际贡献。

第二层是机制诊断。作者追问：为什么 remasking 常常没用？他们的解释不是 remasking 选错了位置，而是更深一层——模型在这些被挑出来重做的位置上，往往提不出比原 token 更好的候选。也就是说，confidence 能发现“不太确定”，却不等于模型具备“重新采样后会更好”的能力。对于 absorbing masked diffusion，这个问题尤其自然，因为训练过程只学习从部分 mask 状态恢复原 token，并没有显式训练“撤销已作决定并在更完整上下文下重选”的行为。

第三层是解码条件依赖性。作者把评估扩展到 stochastic generation，考察温度采样、不同 block size、以及 learned Bernoulli unmasking policy。这里的核心观察是：remasking 的效果高度依赖 unmasking policy。若底层 unmasking 已经是偏确定性的 confidence policy，remasking 的空间很小；若 unmasking policy 本身更随机、更容易引入局部错误，remasking 才更可能修正这些错误。相应地，WINO 在 learned Bernoulli policy 上的收益明显大于在 deterministic Fast-dLLM 上的收益。

这篇论文还有一个重要点是把质量和多样性一起看。作者指出，confidence-based unmasking 已被发现会导致 diversity collapse，而 remasking 在 stochastic setting 下虽然能提高 pass@1，减少部分随机错误，但会进一步压缩样本多样性。也就是说，它改善的是单样本命中率，不一定改善采样分布质量。这个取舍对 diffusion LM 很关键，因为并行生成的一个卖点就是在给定延迟下提供更灵活的采样，而不是把输出进一步推向近似贪心。d:T883,实验部分的主线很清楚：重新比较 WINO 与强基线，并把评估从标准 greedy 扩展到 stochastic decoding。

在标准采样设置下，也就是短 block length、greedy decoding 或接近确定性的解码条件，WINO 与 Fast-dLLM 基本持平。作者的结论不是“完全无效”，而是“边际收益可以忽略，尤其一旦把 remasking 带来的额外 latency 算进去”。这点很重要，因为 diffusion LM 的核心卖点之一就是吞吐和并行度；如果一个修正机制带来额外步数，却没有换来稳定质量提升，那它在系统层面就站不住。

机制实验进一步支持上述判断。作者检查了被 remask 的位置在下一轮是否真的会被替换成更优 token，发现很多情况下模型并不能提出更好的替代项。这说明瓶颈不在 confidence estimator，而在底层 reverse model 的条件建模能力。换句话说，remasking 只是在暴露模型不会 revision，而不是自动赋予 revision 能力。

在 stochastic generation 下，结果更复杂。使用非零温度时，WINO 可以修复一部分采样噪声导致的错误，因此 pass@1 有提升；但与此同时，多样性下降更明显，延续并加重了 confidence-based unmasking 已知的 diversity collapse。这个现象说明 remasking 更像一个“去随机化”装置：它能把明显偏离高概率模式的 token 拉回来，但代价是样本分布更窄。

当 WINO 与 learned Bernoulli unmasking policy 结合时，收益明显大于与 deterministic Fast-dLLM 结合时。作者据此认为，remasking 的有效性不能脱离 unmasking policy 单独讨论。若前者是后验修正，后者决定了前面到底制造了多少可修正错误。

The value of the paper is not a new algorithm but a correction to an emerging narrative: masked dLLMs do not become reliably self-correcting just by adding a training-free remasking heuristic. The key point is that remasking only helps if the underlying model can actually propose a better replacement token after the position is masked again. If the reverse model lacks that conditional revision ability, remasking mostly adds steps without adding quality. For pretraining research, the implication is direct: if we want diffusion LMs to support robust revision, that ability may need to be built into the training objective, the forward process, the decoding policy, or post-training, rather than assumed to emerge from a post-hoc heuristic.

The contribution is mainly careful re-evaluation and mechanism clarification. It is not a paradigm shift by itself, but it should change how future papers choose baselines, report latency-quality tradeoffs, and reason about whether revision should be learned or decoded heuristically.f:Ta49,The paper performs a systematic re-evaluation of WINO. WINO works by combining confidence-based adaptive unmasking with a confidence-based remasking step: after some tokens have been generated, low-confidence decoded tokens are masked again so the model gets another chance to fill them in. The authors do not propose a new remasking rule. Instead, they decompose the question into three layers.

First, they enforce a fair comparison. They argue that earlier WINO evaluations missed the most important baseline: strong confidence-based unmasking on its own, such as Fast-dLLM. Without that baseline, gains from better unmasking order can be mistakenly attributed to remasking. So the paper compares Fast-dLLM directly against Fast-dLLM+WINO under matched model and decoding budgets, isolating the marginal value of remasking.

Second, they diagnose the mechanism. Why does remasking often fail to help? Their answer is not merely that the wrong positions are selected. The deeper issue is that, at many remasked positions, the model cannot produce a better alternative token than the one it already chose. Confidence can identify uncertainty, but uncertainty does not imply that the model has a superior replacement available. This is especially natural in absorbing masked diffusion, where training teaches the model to reconstruct original tokens from partially masked inputs, but does not explicitly train it to revoke previous decisions and re-decide under richer context.

Third, they study dependence on decoding conditions. The evaluation is extended to stochastic generation, varying temperature, block size, and unmasking policy, including learned Bernoulli unmasking. The central observation is that remasking effectiveness is highly policy-dependent. If the base unmasking policy is already deterministic and confidence-driven, there may be little room left for remasking to improve anything. If the unmasking policy is more stochastic and introduces more local errors, remasking has more opportunities to repair them. Consistent with that view, WINO helps much more on top of learned Bernoulli policies than on top of deterministic Fast-dLLM.

A further strength of the paper is that it evaluates quality and diversity together. Confidence-based unmasking has already been linked to diversity collapse, and the paper shows that remasking can intensify this effect. Under stochastic decoding, WINO may improve pass@1 by correcting some sampling-induced errors, but it also narrows the sample distribution further. So the gain is not a free quality improvement; it is often a trade of diversity for single-sample accuracy.10:T9a7,The experimental story is clean: re-compare WINO against a strong baseline, then extend the evaluation from standard greedy decoding to stochastic generation.

Under standard sampling settings, meaning short block lengths and greedy or near-deterministic decoding, WINO performs roughly on par with Fast-dLLM. The paper does not claim remasking is universally useless; the claim is narrower and more important: its marginal gain is often negligible once the extra latency from remasking is included. That matters because one of the main motivations for diffusion LMs is throughput and parallelism. If a correction mechanism adds decoding steps without delivering stable quality gains, it weakens the systems case for using it.

The mechanism analysis supports this conclusion. The authors inspect whether remasked positions are actually replaced by better tokens in subsequent steps and find that this often does not happen. That points to a limitation of the underlying reverse model rather than the confidence estimator. In other words, remasking exposes the absence of revision ability; it does not create that ability.

Under stochastic generation, the picture becomes more nuanced. At non-zero temperature, WINO can repair some errors introduced by sampling noise, so pass@1 improves. But diversity drops further, extending and worsening the diversity collapse already known for confidence-based unmasking. This suggests that remasking often acts as a de-randomization mechanism: it pulls samples back toward high-probability modes, which helps single-sample accuracy but narrows the output distribution.

When WINO is paired with learned Bernoulli unmasking policies, the gains are meaningfully larger than when it is paired with deterministic Fast-dLLM. The paper therefore argues that remasking cannot be evaluated in isolation from the unmasking policy. The latter determines how many correctable mistakes are created upstream.
