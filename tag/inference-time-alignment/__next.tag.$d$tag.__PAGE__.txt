9:T44c,GRAS combines two changes. First, it improves the guided proposal. Prior GILC-style methods correct clean-token logits with a straight-through reward gradient, but the gradient is often estimated from a single noisy sample. For differentiable rewards, GRAS uses a Gumbel-Rao / Rao-Blackwellized reveal estimator to analytically average part of the reveal randomness and reduce variance. For non-differentiable rewards, it uses a leave-one-out baseline to reduce particle-level reward noise. These proposal changes add no denoiser calls. Second, it changes the search stage. Standard SMC/TDS-like resampling often uses a fixed temperature, although the spread of values differs across denoising steps. GRAS introduces AMC, an adaptive-temperature resampling rule that standardizes per-step values into group-relative advantages and lets a single scale parameter control selection pressure. Compared with simply stacking gradient guidance and search, the useful contribution is the accounting framework: variance reduction, adaptive selection, diversity cost, and denoiser budget are analyzed together.a:T841,技术上分成两个层次。第一层是 PrivBoN。标准 BoN 是从基模型采样 n 个候选，用奖励模型打分后取 argmax。作者在每个候选分数上加入独立 Gumbel 噪声，尺度设为 sigma=2Δr/ε，其中 Δr 是奖励模型对相邻偏好数据集的敏感度。这样做有两个效果：从隐私角度，它等价于指数机制，直接给出 ε-DP；从优化角度，带 Gumbel 噪声的 argmax 在大样本极限下对应 softmax 选择，也就是求解一个 KL 正则化的最优策略。论文进一步把这个 regularization 强度与 Huang 等人关于 BoN regret skyline 的理论接起来，证明当隐私预算超过阈值 ε*(x) 时，DP 所要求的噪声强度正好落在 regret 最优区间，因此不会额外损伤对齐。

第二层是 PrivITP。PrivBoN 的问题在于“隐私免费”的阈值依赖未知的 coverage coefficient C^{π*}，实际系统无法验证。为此作者提出 private inference-time pessimism：先用 χ² 正则化的 rejection sampling 构造更保守的选择分布，再用两阶段 Gaussian 机制实现近似 DP。这里的关键不是简单加高斯噪声，而是把基于 ReLU 的拒绝采样重写成 randomized-threshold mechanism，从而得到一个与候选数 n 无关、只依赖实际 halting time 的 ex-post DP 保证。理论分析里还有一个重要工具：smoothed-weight policy comparison lemma，用策略权重函数的 ℓ∞ 距离控制 value gap，于是 regret 可以分解成奖励模型误差项和隐私噪声项，形式上是 O(sqrt(C^{π*}(ε_RM^2 + R_max σ)))。这比直接分析 noisy argmax 更细，因为它把 privacy cost 单独拆了出来，也解释了为什么 PrivITP 在未知 coverage 情况下更稳。

The first is Private Best-of-N (PrivBoN). Standard BoN samples n candidates from a base policy, scores them with a reward model, and returns the argmax. PrivBoN adds i.i.d. Gumbel noise to each reward score with scale sigma = 2Δr/ε, where Δr is the reward-model sensitivity under neighboring preference datasets. This gives ε-differential privacy because noisy argmax with Gumbel noise is equivalent to the exponential mechanism. More interestingly, in the large-n limit the same mechanism implements a KL-regularized alignment optimum: the induced selection rule becomes a softmax policy over rewards. The paper then connects this regularization strength to the regret skyline from prior BoN theory, showing that when the privacy budget exceeds a critical threshold ε*(x), the privacy-imposed noise is exactly in the regret-optimal range. In that regime, privacy is effectively free from an alignment-regret perspective.

The second is Private Inference-Time Pessimism (PrivITP). PrivBoN depends on an unknown coverage coefficient C^{π*}, so the favorable threshold cannot be verified in practice. PrivITP addresses this by combining χ²-regularized rejection sampling with a two-phase Gaussian mechanism. The key move is not simply adding Gaussian noise, but reformulating a ReLU-based rejection sampler as a randomized-threshold mechanism. That yields an ex-post approximate-DP guarantee whose bound is independent of the candidate count n and depends only on the realized halting time. The regret analysis uses a smoothed-weight policy comparison lemma that controls value gaps through the ℓ∞ distance between policy weight functions, leading to a bound of the form O(sqrt(C^{π*}(ε_RM^2 + R_max σ))). This cleanly separates reward-model error from privacy cost and explains why PrivITP is more robust when coverage is unknown.

A notable design choice is that the paper does not solve the problem by retraining the reward model with DP or by using heuristic smoothing. It treats inference-time selection itself as the object of study and chooses mechanisms whose stochasticity is analytically tied to both privacy and alignment.c:T5b6,The empirical study spans four reward models, two language models of different sizes, and three benchmark families: GSM8K, MMLU, and MATH. The main findings are consistent with the theory.

1. Standard BoN is not scaling-monotonic under reward-model error. As N increases, performance can peak and then degrade, which is the practical signature of reward hacking.
2. PrivBoN mitigates this failure mode. With theoretically calibrated Gumbel noise, the performance curve as a function of N becomes much more stable, and the method preserves the benefit of test-time compute without over-amplifying reward-model error.
3. PrivITP consistently matches or outperforms PrivBoN at comparable privacy levels, especially in regimes where large candidate sets make BoN more brittle.
4. In multi-query deployment, the ex-post composition structure matters. When combined with the FSRC accounting framework, PrivITP can answer substantially more queries under a fixed privacy budget than standard worst-case composition.

The most informative part of the experiments is not any single headline number, but the shape of the scaling curves: whether BoN degrades at large N, whether the private mechanisms restore monotonicity or at least suppress the pathology, and whether the observed privacy-performance tradeoff matches the threshold story from the theory.
