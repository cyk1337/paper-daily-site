9:T4f3,The analysis is built on “marginal utility + flip-event tracking.” (1) Budget control via budget forcing: if the model tries to stop early, append ‘Wait’-style tokens; once the budget is reached, force-decode the end-of-thinking delimiter. Budgets are swept from 500 to 16,000 tokens in 500-token steps. (2) Marginal utility MU(t)=Acc(t+Δt)−Acc(t) with Δt=500; MU>0 means extra thinking helps, MU<0 indicates harmful overthinking. (3) Per-problem trajectory tracking: record predicted answer ŷ_i(t) at each budget; define flips between consecutive budgets and classify them as positive (incorrect→correct) or negative (correct→incorrect). The flip ratio (negative/positive) diagnoses whether additional compute is more likely to hurt than help at a given budget (ratio>1 suggests overthinking dominates). (4) Overthinking indicators from traces: hesitation markers (“wait”, “but”, “actually”, “reconsider”), answer oscillations (intermediate conclusion changes), and confidence trajectories derived from token-level log-probabilities—intended as signals for early stopping. 1) Step-wise state detection: monitor local information entropy to detect hesitation/uncertainty; distinguish it from confident-but-overlong redundancy.
2) Dynamic strategy switching:
- Knowledge-guided compression: when uncertainty or potential bias is detected, retrieve external evidence (RAG) and apply contrastive decoding to steer generation toward evidence-supported shorter reasoning, aiming to both correct and compress.

3) Answer-distribution early stopping: stop further verification once the answer distribution converges, suppressing redundant self-check loops.
4) Online long–short contrastive sampling: treat the original chain as the long sample; generate a short sample online via step-wise segmentation and the above compression strategies.
5) Training: build on DPO-style contrastive preference learning while leveraging reward differences (highlighted as combining PPO-like reward modeling with lightweight DPO).
