17:T6a4,The proposed method is Hidden-state Driven Margin Intervention (HDMI). At a chosen layer and position, the method perturbs a hidden state so that the model shifts from a source continuation to a target continuation. Prior causal probing methods often train a property-specific probe and then move the hidden state along the probe gradient. HDMI removes that extra component and directly optimizes a margin objective defined on the LM's own logits: increase the target-token logit while decreasing the source-token logit.

Because the intervention is defined through the native output head, the resulting gradient is aligned with the model's predictive geometry rather than with an externally learned probe boundary. The paper further notes that this gradient can be computed efficiently as a closed-form matrix-vector product involving the unembedding, which makes the method lightweight. HDMI also extends beyond single-token flips to settings with multiple acceptable continuations. A second contribution is Lookahead HDMI, designed for text editing: given an edited target sequence, it uses a softmax embedding transition so that future edit constraints can influence earlier decoding steps, allowing hidden-state interventions throughout generation.

The central methodological claim is sensible: if the purpose of causal probing is to test whether a property is functionally used by the model, the intervention should be defined in the model's own output space. The likely limitation is scope. Margin-based local interventions are naturally suited to agreement, lexical choice, and constrained editing, but may be less informative for diffuse semantic concepts or long-horizon planning behavior.18:T41e,The evaluation follows the framework of Canby et al. and includes LGD agreement tasks and the CausalGym suite. The reported pattern is that HDMI performs strongly on completeness and selectivity: it can change the target property while avoiding excessive collateral changes to unrelated properties. This is the right metric pair for causal probing, since a method that changes everything is not a useful intervention. The paper also emphasizes the practical advantage that HDMI does not require training a separate probe for each property.

Lookahead HDMI is evaluated in text-editing settings, where the model is steered toward a user-provided edited continuation while maintaining fluency. The most informative parts of the experiments are not the raw scores alone, but the comparisons against probe-based baselines, the layer-wise intervention behavior, and the completeness-selectivity trade-off. The provided content does not include the full result tables, so the paper is better read for its intervention design than for a single benchmark number.19:T4ca,Perturbation Probing is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (ranking) computes a signed importance score for every FFN neuron from two ingredients: a structural coupling term c_n derived from weights (capturing how the neuron couples into the residual/output stream) and a perturbation response Δa_n (how the neuron’s activation changes under a behavior-targeted input perturbation). Their product yields a task-specific, signed hypothesis: neurons that causally push toward the behavior vs away from it. This stage costs two forward passes per prompt.
Stage 2 is a one-time intervention sweep over the top-ranked neurons to causally validate the hypotheses; the sweep cost is amortized across all discovered neurons (reported on the order of ~150 forward passes).
The method also outputs an FFN/Skip diagnostic that predicts whether the behavior is controlled by an FFN-centric “opposition circuit” (amenable to ablation/amplification) or by an attention/residual “routing circuit” (better addressed via layer-targeted direction injection). The paper further argues that architectural choices (e.g., post-norm) change the accessibility of these circuits, yielding different “safety topologies” across model families.1a:T4a7,Evaluated on 13 models across four architecture families and eight behavioral circuits. Replicated on 200 HarmBench prompts with extremely small p-values (p < 1e-74).
