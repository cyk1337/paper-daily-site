在此基础上，作者证明了一个针对 time-inhomogeneous Fokker-Planck 的定量绝热定理。误差用 χ² divergence 或 total variation 控制，上界由两部分组成：初始条件记忆项，按有效谱隙指数衰减；以及有限速率造成的 lag 项，正比于 schedule 速度 |ṫ| 和 ||∂_t logρ_t||，并被有效谱隙 Γ 抑制。这里 Γ 不是裸谱隙 Δ，而是扣除了时间变化项后的 effective gap。

随后作者把这个框架用于 learned score 不精确的 diffusion model。定义在真实分布 ρ_t 和模型分布 ρ_t^θ 下的 L2 score error，推导终态误差上界，并给出匹配下界。核心结论是：score error 的影响受谱隙调制，终态存在约 ε_score / √Δ 的误差地板。也就是说，分布几何越差、混合越慢、Poincaré 常数越大，训练误差越难在采样时被“洗掉”。

从方法区别看，已有 diffusion 理论常从 SDE 离散化误差、概率流 ODE、或 log-Sobolev / mixing 分析切入；这篇文章的特点是把 learned score 直接编码进一个可谱分析的 Hamiltonian，并把采样解释成 ground-state tracking。这种表述更适合讨论谱结构、激发态、以及 schedule 设计。a:T85e,The core construction maps a score field to a Schrödinger operator. For a conservative score S = grad log rho, the Score Hamiltonian is defined as H-hat = -Delta + Q_minus(rho), where Q_minus = (Delta sqrt rho) / sqrt rho. When rho is strictly positive, this can be written directly in terms of the score as -Delta + one-half div S + one-quarter norm(S)^2. This operator has three key properties. First, sqrt rho is its ground state with eigenvalue zero. Second, after the ground-state transform Psi_tau = mu_tau / sqrt(rho_ttau), the time-inhomogeneous Fokker-Planck equation becomes a simple imaginary-time evolution with a gauge term: partial_tau Psi = -H_t Psi - (dot t / 2) partial_t log rho_t Psi. Third, this turns diffusion sampling into a ground-state tracking problem.

Using that representation, the paper proves a quantitative adiabatic theorem for time-varying Fokker-Planck dynamics. The tracking error, measured in chi-squared divergence or total variation, has two components: a memory term from the initial condition that decays exponentially with an effective spectral gap, and a finite-rate lag term controlled by the schedule speed and the L2 norm of partial_t log rho_t, again suppressed by the effective gap. The effective gap is not just the raw spectral gap Delta; it is reduced by time-variation terms.

The framework is then extended to imperfectly learned scores. The authors define score errors under both the true interpolation density and the model density, and derive upper and matching lower bounds on the final discrepancy between the sampler and the target law. The main message is that score error is amplified according to the spectral geometry, yielding a terminal floor on the order of epsilon_score / sqrt(Delta). So poor mixing or narrow spectral gaps make training error harder to wash out at sampling time.

Compared with prior diffusion analyses based on discretization error, probability-flow ODEs, or generic mixing inequalities, the distinctive move here is to encode the learned score directly into a spectrally analyzable Hamiltonian and interpret sampling as adiabatic ground-state transport.b:T40f,The empirical section is mainly there to illustrate and validate the theory, not to benchmark generative quality at scale. The point is not image quality or likelihood, but that the Hamiltonian spectrum captures meaningful geometric structure of the learned distribution.
