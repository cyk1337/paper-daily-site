a:T65b,The method has three layers. First, starting from the decoupled AdamW update, the paper derives a leading-order evolution equation for the squared weight norm and decomposes it into three forces: an alignment force, an injection force, and a decay force. The alignment force measures correlation between the current weights and the adaptive update direction u_hat; if updates point partly along the weight vector, the norm grows. The injection force comes from the second-order contribution of the adaptive step magnitude itself. The decay force is the linear pullback induced by decoupled weight decay. Second, the paper builds a bridge from norm dynamics to the Weibull scale λ. This relies on the previously observed k-lock phenomenon: for a class of transformer components, the Weibull shape parameter k stays close to its initialization anchor during training. Under approximate k-lock, RMS and λ differ by an almost fixed multiplicative factor sqrt(Gamma(1+2/k)), so norm dynamics can be mapped to λ dynamics up to a measurable reconstruction offset. Third, for the realistic case where Adam moments are missing, the authors introduce a spline displacement method based on the AdamW displacement identity. Instead of using a crude two-checkpoint difference, they fit sparse checkpoint trajectories with splines to better recover local displacement directions and then estimate the alignment force. Relative to prior work, the novelty is not in another terminal norm law or another descriptive fit of weight distributions, but in explicitly tying optimizer-level force decomposition to a higher-level Weibull statistic.b:T470,The main experiments use self-trained Pythia-70M models with ground-truth optimizer moments retained, which allows direct measurement of the three forces. The key findings are: (1) during the rise phase, alignment dominates the absolute force budget, contributing about 88% to 94% across four random seeds; this argues that early λ growth is driven much more by update-weight alignment than by the raw magnitude injection term; (2) near saturation and during the overshoot-to-relaxation transition, alignment and decay move toward balance, matching the point where λ stops increasing and begins to relax; and (3) the result remains stable after super-weight removal, suggesting the conclusion is not an artifact of a few extreme weights. For checkpoint-only recovery, the spline displacement method recovers roughly 92% to 94% of the alignment force on self-trained models, about 2x the naive two-point baseline.
