The authors set the classification prior's max_classes to two and keep the rest of the TabICLv2 recipe unchanged, including optimizer settings, learning rate, weight decay, dropout, sequence-length distribution, and the regression prior configuration. The proposed explanation is that classification is implemented as an in-context comparison operation. Labels from the training rows are encoded and injected into the context, and predictions are obtained by comparing query-row representations against class-conditioned representations or prototypes derived from the context. Under a shared label encoder, the model is not tied to a fixed class-specific embedding table, so binary match/mismatch supervision can train an operator that accepts more than two candidate classes at inference. The paper also separates three notions that are often conflated: the class count sampled by the prior, the per-forward comparison cap, and the number of classes the system can express.
