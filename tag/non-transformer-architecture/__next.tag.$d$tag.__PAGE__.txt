9:T416,The contribution is best read as an existence proof for changing the compute-to-performance tradeoff, not as a complete replacement for large-scale Transformer pretraining. It is relevant to pretraining research for three reasons. First, it revisits recurrent deep computation as a way to increase computation depth without increasing parameter count in the same way as stacking Transformer blocks. Second, it treats instruction-response training from scratch as a pretraining objective rather than as a post-training stage. Third, the reported comparisons against open models such as Llama, Qwen, Gemma, OLMo, Ouro, and Huginn show comparable benchmark neighborhoods with 100–900x fewer training tokens and 96–432x less estimated training compute. The main caveat is that instruction-response data, response-only likelihood, and common evaluation benchmarks are closely aligned. This makes the result highly informative for sample-efficient conditional generation, but not a clean apples-to-apples replacement for raw-text language modeling.a:T609,HRM-Text uses a hierarchical recurrent model with two timescales. A high-level state z_H is initialized from token embeddings and is meant to carry slower semantic and task-level context. A low-level state z_L is initialized in a fixed way and performs faster local refinement. The forward pass runs two high-level cycles. Each cycle applies three fast L-module updates followed by one slow H-module update. Token logits are produced from the final H state through a linear head. This design adds iterative computation through recurrence rather than by simply adding more independent Transformer layers. The difficult part is optimization. Reusing the same transformation across recurrent steps can amplify activation and gradient pathologies. The paper introduces MagicNorm to stabilize the residual-normalization behavior under recurrence, and uses warmup deep credit assignment: training initially backpropagates through only the last two recurrent steps, then expands to the last five steps. The modules also use parameterless RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, and sigmoid-gated self-attention. The objective departs from raw-text autoregressive pretraining. HRM-Text is trained from scratch on instruction-response pairs and computes NLL only on the response tokens, optimizing P(answer | instruction). A PrefixLM attention mask allows bidirectional attention over the instruction while preserving causal generation over the response. This avoids spending loss on predicting the prompt and lets the prompt act as a fully readable conditioning context.b:T41f,The reported training budget is 40B unique tokens. The paper compares HRM-Text with contemporary open models including Llama, Qwen, Gemma, OLMo, Ouro, and Huginn. According to Figure 1 and Table 4, HRM-Text reaches a similar performance neighborhood on most benchmarks while using about 100–900x fewer training tokens and 96–432x less estimated training compute. The main experimental signal is the efficiency curve rather than a single benchmark number. The important ablations to inspect are the hierarchical recurrence versus a standard Transformer baseline, MagicNorm versus conventional normalization choices, warmup credit assignment versus full or shallow backpropagation through recurrence, and PrefixLM plus response-only loss versus a standard causal LM objective. The provided text gives the training scale, recurrence schedule, and relative token/compute claims, but not the full benchmark table or ablation numbers.
