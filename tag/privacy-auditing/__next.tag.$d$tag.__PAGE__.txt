First, canaries are generated by prompting an instruction-tuned LLM, typically from the same model family as the audited model, with high-temperature sampling. In the reported setup, Gemma3 12B is used with top_p=1.0 and temperatures at or above 0.8, targeting Enron-style email data. Increasing temperature flattens the sampling distribution, producing text that is unusual enough to create a measurable gradient signal but still syntactically and semantically coherent. This differs from random canaries, which can be too far from the model’s language manifold, and from private-data-derived canaries, which complicate the privacy accounting. Second, the paper introduces a proxy audit for synthetic data. The released synthetic dataset is used to fine-tune an auxiliary attack LLM.
