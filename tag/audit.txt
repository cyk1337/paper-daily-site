17:T7cc,TSFMAudit is built on a simple hypothesis: if an audited dataset was seen, or nearly seen, during pretraining, then a short probe fine-tuning run on that dataset should look unusually efficient. Efficiency here is not just low initial loss. It means faster loss reduction under a small update budget and smaller movement of the backbone parameters. Intuitively, contaminated data are already close to the model’s internal representation, so only limited adjustment is needed to fit them well.

The procedure has three stages. First, the candidate TSFM is subjected to a controlled probe fine-tuning run on the audited dataset, with a deliberately small training budget. The goal is not to optimize forecasting performance, but to expose comparable adaptation dynamics. Second, the method extracts probe-time signals such as loss reduction and parameter displacement, along with related dynamic features described in the paper. Third, these features are combined into a contamination-risk score for the model–dataset pair.

A notable design choice is that the audit target is defined at the dataset level rather than the sample level. This matches the practical structure of forecasting benchmarks, where many samples are windows derived from the same underlying source series. The paper formalizes contamination in terms of whether the source time series behind an audited sample appears in the pretraining corpus, and then defines a dataset-level contamination ratio.

Relative to LLM-style baselines, the method deliberately avoids two difficult dependencies. It does not rely on explicit overlap detection, because near-duplicates in time series are often transformed rather than literally repeated. It also avoids large-scale shadow-model calibration, which would be expensive for TSFMs. Probe dynamics are used because they capture both prior familiarity and local plasticity, making them better suited than static loss to distinguish contamination from inherently easy-to-forecast data.18:T4fb,The evaluation covers 6 TSFMs and uses their reported pretraining-corpus information together with GIFT-Eval and the TIME benchmark, for a total of 187 datasets. The comparison includes 10 baselines adapted from the LLM contamination-auditing literature. The main result is that TSFMAudit delivers consistently stronger detection performance across models and benchmarks.

Several experimental points matter. First, the paper shows that contaminated datasets tend to exhibit faster loss reduction and smaller backbone movement during probe fine-tuning. This observation is both the empirical motivation and the basis for feature design. Second, the feature ablations indicate that adaptation dynamics carry more discriminative signal than static loss alone, and parameter-displacement features appear to add useful information beyond fit quality. Third, the method is evaluated across multiple TSFMs rather than being tied to one architecture.
