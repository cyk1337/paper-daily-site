It partitions the cache into 64-token blocks and assigns each block to a monotone precision ladder: exact 16-bit storage, 8/4/2-bit scalar quantization, or a 1-bit centroid-like representation. Block importance is tracked through a causal EMA of observed attention mass, so allocation decisions depend only on the prefix. For GQA models, allocation is performed once per physical KV head by aggregating masses across the associated query heads.

The allocator is framed as a monotone rate-distortion problem. After a block leaves the recent window, it may be demoted but not promoted, since the dense copy is no longer kept. DynaRule injects encoded external rules directly into the LLM’s KV cache and makes re-retrieval part of decoding. The intuition is that this layer exhibits the sharpest and most decisive rule selection. Emitting <search> triggers re-attention over the rule memory, selects a new subset of relevant rules, and replaces outdated entries in the KV cache.

This differs from standard RAG, where retrieval is front-loaded and retrieval errors propagate through the whole generation, and from static memory injection, where the model can get stuck with early-selected rules even when later reasoning steps require different ones.
