a:T6b8,The method has two parts: diagnosis and correction. For diagnosis, the authors project hidden states from each layer into output probabilities and track how answer preference evolves with depth. They define Modal Dominance Ratio (MDR) to quantify whether a layer prefers the image-supported answer w_vis or the text-supported answer w_text. This yields a layerwise trajectory of modal preference. In many conflict-incorrect examples, MDR is positive in intermediate layers, indicating visual preference, and turns negative only in later layers, indicating a shift toward text. The authors further show that the mere existence of a late shift is not enough to predict failure, because successful examples also shift. What matters is the direction of the shift: failures usually move toward text, while successes usually move toward vision. For correction, they propose Conflict-Aware Layer Reference Decoding (CALRD). The method identifies the transition layer where the output distribution changes most sharply and treats it as an anchor layer that still contains the pre-override prediction. It then computes two signals: anchor confidence, which measures how certain the anchor-layer prediction is, and prediction retention, which measures whether that prediction survives to the final layer. When the anchor is confident but poorly retained, CALRD interprets this as harmful override and blends anchor-layer logits back into the final distribution with adaptive strength. When late-layer processing appears beneficial, it leaves the output mostly unchanged. This is more selective than naive multi-layer ensembling because it aims to correct only harmful overwriting rather than weakening deep computation in general.b:T4e2,The paper introduces Conflict-VQA, a diagnostic benchmark with 5,969 samples and explicit annotations for both the image-supported answer and the text-supported answer. Each image-question pair is paired with both consistent and conflicting contexts. Images come from VrR-VG and questions from TDIUC, covering six categories: object presence, color, attribute, counting, spatial reasoning, and activity recognition. Two headline mechanistic numbers are central: among failures, 85% of prediction shifts move toward text, while among successes, 89% move toward vision. This shows that shift direction is more informative than shift occurrence. The evaluation spans five MLLMs, from InstructBLIP to Qwen3-VL, suggesting that the phenomenon is not architecture-specific. CALRD yields up to 9.4% improvement on conflict tasks while largely preserving standard performance on non-conflict settings.
