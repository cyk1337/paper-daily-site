1b:T55a,The core design is to hold the single-shot solver fixed and vary one memory choice at a time. The paper decomposes future value into three settings. First, exact-query replay: if the same question returns, can the system cheaply replay the stored verified SQL? Second, cross-question retention: remove a question’s own card from the bank and test whether the remaining cards still help answer it, isolating reuse across questions rather than direct memorization. Third, held-out same-database transfer: evaluate whether memory helps new questions from the same database. To make transfer interpretable, the authors define CR, the crystallization ratio, as held-out transfer lift divided by the on-demand repair headroom on those same held-out questions. This measures how much of the gain that would otherwise require fresh repair can be recovered from memory at first attempt. In the main instantiation, execution-guided repair generates episodes, which are then stored in a database-scoped verified card bank. The paper runs controlled interventions around three questions: where future value appears, what information actually carries transfer, and which choices in collection, verification, formatting, and retrieval materially affect outcomes. An important detail is that the card writer never sees gold SQL, which avoids leaking supervision into the memory bank.1c:T52f,The main experiments are on BIRD, with three paired seeds and analysis that accounts for variation across both databases and questions. This gain corresponds to 44.4% of the on-demand repair headroom on the same held-out questions.
