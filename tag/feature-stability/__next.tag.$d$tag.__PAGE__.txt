9:T827,The central methodological contribution is a per-feature notion of stability rather than a single dictionary-level matching score. The setup is simple: train multiple SAEs on the same activations with identical hyperparameters but different random seeds, choose one run as an anchor, and for each anchor feature match its decoder vector to the most similar decoder vector in each other run using cosine similarity. If the similarity exceeds a threshold, typically 0.7, the feature is counted as having reappeared in that run. Averaging across runs yields a reappearance probability for each feature. This many-to-one matching is much cheaper than one-to-one Hungarian matching, and the paper shows that it produces nearly the same matched feature sets, with IoU 0.978 ± 0.001.

Using this signal, the authors compare stable and unstable features across several axes: reconstruction contribution, marginal predictive utility, activation statistics, token triggers, automatic explanations, and decoder-space geometry. The first major result is a functional asymmetry. Stable features account for most of the reconstruction- and prediction-relevant signal, whereas unstable features tend to have weak marginal impact and are dominated by low-frequency surface-form triggers. The second major result is more subtle: unstable features are often not reproducible as individual latents, but they do recover reproducible lower-rank subspaces. In other words, the basis is unstable while the represented region of activation space is stable.

The paper strengthens this claim with a synthetic experiment where the ground truth is known. Even when the true latent structure is low-rank, SAEs can recover the correct subspace without recovering the same individual latent directions. Finally, the authors build deduplicated feature pools from multiple runs and reconstruct SAEs using stable cross-seed features. In that construction setting, higher explained variance can coincide with higher mean feature probability, suggesting that stability and reconstruction quality do not have to trade off.a:T5db,The empirical study is broad: multiple models, layers, dictionary sizes, and five SAE variants are included, namely Vanilla ReLU+L1, TopK, BatchTopK, HierarchicalTopK, and JumpReLU. First, the proposed many-to-one matching closely agrees with Hungarian matching, with IoU 0.978 ± 0.001, which makes the per-feature stability analysis computationally practical. Second, stable features consistently carry most of the reconstruction and prediction signal, while unstable features contribute much less on the margin. The abstract does not list every number, but the body emphasizes that this pattern is robust across architectures, layers, and SAE variants.

The qualitative analyses are equally important. Automatic explanations and activation statistics show that unstable features are disproportionately associated with low-frequency surface-form triggers rather than semantically or functionally robust abstractions. Decoder-space geometry reveals that these unstable features often cluster into reproducible low-rank regions. The synthetic low-rank experiment confirms that this is not merely an artifact of messy natural-language data: basis non-identifiability with subspace recovery appears even when the ground truth is controlled.
