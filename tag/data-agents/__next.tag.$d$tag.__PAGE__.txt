9:T8c4,The core system is a multi-agent synthesis engine called Data World. A world is formalized as a four-tuple: dependency skeleton G, canonical state X, observed environment E, and recomputable phenomena I. G is a DAG whose nodes are variable clusters and whose edges encode generative relations such as monotonic, threshold, saturation, lagged, or interaction effects. This is not just a graph template; the edge annotations define how the world is generated. X is the latent canonical state, the objective world state that actually governs the phenomena of interest. E is produced by an observation map O that projects X into analyst-visible artifacts such as CSVs, spreadsheets, and logs, with imperfect observability, aggregation, missingness, and noise. I contains target phenomena that can be recomputed and checked from the generated world and, crucially, are intended to remain recoverable from E.

A key design choice is the staged multi-agent pipeline. Separate agent sessions handle world specification, state generation, file projection, task and rubric synthesis, and auditing, while exchanging read-only artifacts. This is meant to preserve consistency without letting later stages directly exploit hidden latent variables. The auditor is central: it verifies both that the intended relationships were instantiated and that the target phenomena remain recoverable from imperfect observations. That addresses two common failure modes in synthetic environments: latent mechanisms that disappear after projection, and tasks that become trivial because the signal is too directly exposed.

Task generation is paired with fine-grained rubrics rather than single gold answers. The rubrics distinguish correct computation from correct analytical insight and evidence use. This gives structured rewards for evaluation and RL. The training setup is world-disjoint, so train and test tasks come from disjoint worlds rather than different prompts over the same latent template. The paper then uses these rubric rewards for RL-zero training, without distilling trajectories from stronger teacher models, to produce DataWorld-35B-A3B. That makes the work especially relevant to current interest in verifier- or environment-based training signals.a:T647,The engine produces two main assets. DataWorld-Bench contains 100 tasks from 50 held-out worlds across 16 domains, with human-preference-based curation for question realism. The RL training set contains 2,135 tasks from 1,037 worlds that are disjoint from the benchmark worlds. The scale is moderate, but the emphasis is on world-level diversity and split hygiene rather than raw task count.

On evaluation, the authors test 10 frontier models and consistently observe a computation-versus-insight gap. Models can often perform local calculations, filtering, and aggregation, yet struggle more on identifying the underlying drivers, explaining why an outcome changed, or tracing downstream implications. This is an informative result because it shows that the bottleneck in data agents is not only tool use or table manipulation, but mechanism recovery under partial observation.

For training, rubric-reward RL-zero yields DataWorld-35B-A3B, which improves over its base model on held-out worlds and also transfers to three external benchmarks spanning data analysis and broader professional work. The abstract does not expose the full numeric table, but the paper’s framing suggests the important result is not a single benchmark spike; it is the combination of gains on unseen worlds plus gains on external benchmarks, which argues against pure benchmark memorization.
