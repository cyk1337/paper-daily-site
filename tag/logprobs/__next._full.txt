18:T638,Each reasoning trajectory induces a K-dimensional probability vector θ over candidate answers. In the black-box setting, one only observes a sampled label from θ. In the grey-box setting, one observes θ itself, along with the sampled answer. The goal is sequential sampling with a stopping rule that returns the true modal answer with probability at least 1-δ at any stopping time. The theory first derives asymptotic lower bounds on expected stopping time under different observation models. A key result is that grey-box feedback is never worse than black-box feedback, and its benefit depends on the structure of the trajectory-level answer distributions. When trajectories are effectively one-hot, grey-box reduces to black-box and the complexity is of order Θ(Δ^-2). When trajectories expose stable beliefs across candidates, the complexity can improve to Θ(Δ^-1), where Δ is the frequency gap between the top answer and the runner-up. The proposed algorithm, ASC-D, is the grey-box analogue of adaptive self-consistency. Instead of counting answer labels, it accumulates pairwise evidence using probability differences θ_i-θ_j and stops when one candidate has enough certified advantage over every alternative. The design is distribution-free in the sense that it does not assume prior knowledge of the trajectory distribution, yet it provides anytime δ-correctness and asymptotically matches the lower bound. Relative to prior adaptive SC methods, the real shift is the observation model: the method operates on distribution-valued feedback rather than answer-only samples.19:T447,The empirical section includes both synthetic and real evaluations. The synthetic experiments validate the theory: as the modal gap Δ shrinks, the advantage of grey-box feedback becomes larger, and the observed stopping-time behavior falls between the predicted Δ^-1 and Δ^-2 regimes depending on how informative the trajectory-level probability vectors are. The real benchmark is MMLU-Redux with multiple-choice questions, where the final-token probabilities over answer options can be extracted directly. Models include Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen3-1.7B. On these tasks, ASC-D uses 46.4% to 95.6% fewer trajectories than answer-only baselines when the modal gap is small. Under a fixed maximum budget of N=128, it also achieves the best correct-certification rate across all tested confidence levels, which matters because the gain is not merely earlier stopping but more reliable certified decisions under the same compute cap. The appendices provide additional implementation details on extracting option probabilities and extra ablations on synthetic and real settings.1a:T84d,作者提出了一个 surrogate fidelity 的层级框架，按从弱到强、从观测到干预再到机制的顺序，定义四类一致性：prediction fidelity、attribution fidelity、representation fidelity、cross-level fidelity。这里最巧的一点，是他们把 binary classification 场景下的 log-odds 作为 API-compatible 的表示读出量。因为对正负类 token 的 log-prob 做差时，softmax 的归一化常数会抵消，所以 log-odds 可以写成 residual representation 在一个 readout direction 上的线性投影。这样一来，即便拿不到完整 logits 或内部激活，只要 API 提供 top-K logprobs，仍然可以构造与表示几何直接相关的标量。

在此基础上，prediction fidelity 衡量 surrogate 与 target 在输出层面的预测一致性；attribution fidelity 则看输入扰动后，两个模型的输出变化是否一致，作者主要用 leave-one-out ablations 来做因果归因；representation fidelity 需要白盒访问，比较模型内部表示对同样干预的响应；cross-level fidelity 则更进一步，问 surrogate 的内部表示能否预测 target 的 attribution。这个层级设计有两个优点。其一，它遵循“最小权限原则”：要验证什么层级的主张，就只要求与之匹配的访问权限，而不是默认必须白盒。其二，它把很多过去混在一起的“模型相似性”拆开了：输出像，不等于归因像；归因像，也不等于内部表示真的同构。

全文还有一个值得注意的分析点：作者讨论了 access–validity inversion。某些高访问权限下很容易得到的白盒信号，比如 attention pattern 相似性、扰动幅度等，看起来信息很多，但未必能预测更有因果意义的 black-box attribution agreement。也就是说，能看到更多内部量，不代表你测到的东西更接近真正可迁移的解释。这个判断对 mechanistic interpretability 社区是有针对性的，因为很多常用分析工具默认“白盒信号更可信”，而作者的结果表明至少在跨模型迁移上不能这么简单看。1b:T7c7,The paper proposes a hierarchy of surrogate fidelity metrics: prediction fidelity, attribution fidelity, representation fidelity, and cross-level fidelity. The setup is instantiated for binary classification tasks. A key technical observation is that binary log-odds can be recovered from API-accessible log-probabilities and are exactly linear in the model representation. Because the softmax normalization constant cancels when taking the difference between the positive and negative class log-probabilities, the resulting log-odds correspond to a projection of the residual representation onto a readout direction. This gives the framework a clean geometric interpretation while remaining compatible with restricted APIs.

On top of that, prediction fidelity measures agreement at the output level. Attribution fidelity measures whether the two models respond similarly to causal input interventions, implemented through leave-one-out ablations. Representation fidelity requires white-box access and compares how internal representations respond to the same interventions. Cross-level fidelity asks whether the surrogate’s internal representations can predict the target’s attributions. The hierarchy is well designed for two reasons. First, it follows a least-privilege principle: each claim should require only the level of access needed to test it. Second, it separates forms of similarity that are often conflated. Similar predictions do not imply similar attributions, and similar attributions do not automatically imply shared internal mechanisms.

A notable conceptual point is the access–validity inversion discussed in the paper. Some high-access white-box signals, such as attention similarity or perturbation magnitude, may look rich but fail to predict the more causally relevant black-box attribution agreement. That is a pointed critique of a common assumption in interpretability work: more internal access does not automatically mean a more transferable explanation.1c:T45d,The evaluation spans 11 models from four families—Llama, Qwen, GPT, and Gemini—covering scales from 0.5B to 70B, and uses three binary classification benchmarks. This design matters because it includes both open and closed models, within-family scaling comparisons, and cross-family comparisons.

The central empirical result is a dissociation between prediction fidelity and attribution fidelity.
