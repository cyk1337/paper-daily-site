1b:T42f,The framework explicitly decouples duplicate detection from copy retention.
Detection (scalable and shard-invariant): documents are segmented at natural boundaries; each segment is normalized (formatting/casing/whitespace etc., per the paper’s rules) and exact-hashed; distributed key–value aggregation computes global occurrence counts for each hash, ensuring cross-shard visibility.
Retention (explicit policy): instead of keep-one/keep-k, the method defines a retention function k=f(global_frequency, segment_length). High-frequency segments (likely boilerplate/templates) are aggressively compressed; longer repeated spans get smaller budgets as they more often reflect direct copying or templated reuse; low-frequency repeats are treated conservatively to avoid deleting legitimate quotations or mild reuse. A coherence-preserving deletion step reduces fragmentation when removing repeated spans.
