18:T59e,The method has three parts. First, the authors recover the prior direction. Given the unembedding matrix W and an empirical unigram distribution p_unigram, they solve a linear least-squares problem over the frequent-token subset to find a vector d_prior such that softmax(W d_prior) reconstructs the unigram distribution as closely as possible. This requires only the output weights and token counts from a corpus, with no model forward passes. They argue that the recovered direction is unique under the fitting procedure and show that it is absent at random initialization but emerges through training. Second, they derive a tempered Bayesian interpretation. For any final prediction state y_pred, they decompose it into λ d_prior plus an orthogonal component. Plugging this into the softmax yields a factorization of the predictive distribution into a unigram prior raised to the power λ times a context-dependent likelihood term. This gives λ a clean probabilistic meaning: λ=0 removes the prior, 0<λ<1 tempers it, λ>1 sharpens it, and λ<0 inverts it. Third, they validate the mechanism dynamically and causally. They vary contextual informativeness through coherent context, shuffled tokens, short contexts, and ambiguous prompts, and track λ. They also intervene directly on the prediction state along d_prior and compare against norm-matched random-direction perturbations to test whether this axis is functionally special.19:T59b,The experiments cover Llama, Qwen, Gemma, and Pythia, from 0.4B to 405B parameters. Four results matter most. First, existence and universality: a single direction reconstructs the unigram prior with low KL error across all tested families, suggesting this is not an artifact of one architecture or recipe. The paper also shows that the direction is not present at initialization and appears only after training. Second, context dynamics: λ decreases as coherent information accumulates, but not merely when token count grows. Shuffled or semantically weak contexts do not reduce λ in the same way, which supports the claim that λ tracks information rather than length or position. Third, causal intervention: increasing the projection along d_prior pushes predictions toward the unigram prior, while decreasing it makes predictions rely more on contextual evidence. Random directions with matched magnitude do not produce the same effect, so d_prior is not just a post hoc statistical fit. Fourth, scaling behavior: the paper reports that in the high-context regime, λ often decreases with hidden dimension across families, hinting that larger models suppress default priors more effectively when context is informative. This part is more observational than fully explained.
