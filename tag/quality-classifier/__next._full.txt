First, the positive anchor pool is expanded from MKC+ to MKC-e, adding more instruction-style, synthetic, and encyclopedic multilingual sources such as Tagengo, the Wikipedia subset of MURI-IT, EuroBlocks-SFT-Synthetic, and WikiQA. This broadens the notion of “high quality” beyond a single genre. Second, the representation and classifier remain intentionally simple: 768-dimensional document embeddings from XLM-RoBERTa, followed by a lightweight MLP with one 256-d hidden layer, ReLU, 20% dropout, and a sigmoid output. Keeping the classifier small helps isolate the effect of data construction and transfer rather than model capacity. Third, the paper studies negative sampling carefully. Besides standard random negatives from raw FineWeb2, it introduces a Q3 hard-negative strategy that samples documents from the 50th–75th percentile of an existing quality-score distribution. The motivation is important: random negatives are often too easy and encourage the classifier to rely on shallow cues such as boilerplate or formatting noise, whereas Q3 negatives are fluent but low-utility, forcing the model to learn a sharper boundary around information density and usefulness. Training uses 100k positive documents per language, with up to 3x positive upsampling for low-resource languages, and balanced positive/negative classes.
