9:T6bf,The paper proceeds in three steps. First, it formalizes the issue. An explanation function E maps features to strings, and the collision cluster of a feature is the set of all features sharing the same explanation. The authors then define descriptive resolution as the mutual information I(F; A) between a uniformly sampled feature F and its assigned explanation A = E(F). This is a useful move because it turns a vague concern about label reuse into an information bottleneck statement: if the explanation space is much smaller than the feature space, ambiguity is structurally unavoidable. Second, the paper performs a reanalysis of a public human-annotated SAE feature corpus rather than generating new labels. This choice is deliberate. Human labels are a cleaner, higher-variance descriptive source than current LLM explainers, so showing severe collision there strengthens the claim that machine-generated labels should be at least as collision-prone. Third, the paper critiques existing evaluation. It proves that detection-style explanation scores are invariant under substitution among collision-equivalent features, meaning a high score cannot establish that the explanation identifies the intended feature rather than another feature in the same collision class. To address this, the authors propose two corrective directions: discrimination scoring, which asks whether an explanation distinguishes the target feature from colliding alternatives, and collision-adjusted detection, which modifies standard detection-style evaluation to account for ambiguity. The paper also provides pseudocode and discusses computational cost. The main conceptual shift is from explanation correctness to explanation identifiability.a:T494,The empirical section is a statistical reanalysis rather than a new large-scale training study. The dataset comes from the public human-annotated corpus associated with Sparse Feature Circuits. After deduplication, it contains 722 annotated features: 470 from Gemma 2 2B SAEs and 277 from Pythia 70M SAEs. The headline numbers are strong. Those 722 features correspond to only 235 distinct annotation strings. The mean annotation string is reused across 3.07 features. 82.1% of features share their annotation with at least one other feature. The most frequent label, “plural nouns,” is assigned to 101 distinct features spanning both model families, 18 layers, and four model components. This makes collision look structural rather than anecdotal. On the theoretical side, the paper proves a proposition showing that detection-style scoring cannot distinguish among features within the same collision cluster, so high explanation scores do not imply successful feature identification.
