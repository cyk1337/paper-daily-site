5:T429,Its value for foundation-model research is diagnostic rather than merely competitive. First, it separates local perception from global spatial integration, which many existing video benchmarks do not. That matters for embodied pretraining, world modeling, and native multimodal training. Second, it shows that failure modes differ by model family: strong proprietary models improve sharply when the task is reduced to local perception, suggesting that their main weakness is cross-frame spatial integration; many open models do not improve reliably even then, indicating deficits in both perception and reasoning. Third, the paper goes beyond evaluation by introducing GST-Train and showing that targeted training data can substantially close the gap. Qwen3-VL-8B improves from 25.89 to 53.52 after fine-tuning, surpassing all zero-shot proprietary models in their setup. That is a useful signal for pretraining researchers: global spatial competence is not something to assume will emerge automatically from scale alone; data construction and task pressure matter.6:T5fc,The contribution is centered on benchmark and data design rather than a new VLM architecture. The pipeline is built on OmniGibson with BEHAVIOR-1K scenes. For each of 50 indoor environments, the system generates diverse egocentric camera trajectories and additional query viewpoints outside the trajectory. It records 3D coordinates of objects and viewpoints, projects them onto top-down maps, and automatically creates QA pairs. A key filtering rule removes any sample where the target object is visible from the query viewpoint, ensuring that object-localization questions cannot be solved from a single frame.

The task taxonomy is organized around three embodied questions: Where am I? Where is the target? What does the scene look like? These are instantiated as 12 subtasks covering self-localization, object localization, and scene-structure understanding. The top-down image is not cosmetic; it acts as an explicit probe of whether the model can map long-horizon egocentric evidence into a global scene representation.

A particularly strong part of the design is the controlled local variant. By making the target visible from the query viewpoint, the authors convert the task into one that can be handled largely by local perception. This creates a clean diagnostic split between failures caused by weak single-image understanding and failures caused by missing cross-view spatial integration. That makes GST-Bench more than a leaderboard asset; it becomes a tool for identifying what kind of spatial competence is absent.7:T584,The main evaluation covers 22 VLMs, including open-source, proprietary, and embodied-oriented models. The strongest zero-shot model, Gemini-3-Pro, reaches 42.68, far below the human score of 79.08. Open models are substantially weaker; the best reported open model, InternVL3.5-38B, stays around the low-30 range, and many models are close to random guessing. This gap is large enough that it is hard to dismiss as benchmark noise.

The local-variant analysis is one of the most informative results. Proprietary models can gain up to 39 points when the task is reduced to local perception, which strongly suggests that they already have decent local visual understanding but fail to maintain and use a global spatial state across frames. Open models, in contrast, do not improve consistently even in the local setting, pointing to a compound bottleneck involving both perception and reasoning.

The training-data experiment is equally important. Using the same generation pipeline, the authors build GST-Train from BEHAVIOR-1K, ArtVIP, HyperSim, and internal simulation scenes. Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on GST-Train raises its GST-Bench score from 25.89 to 53.52, a gain of 27.63 points, and pushes it above all proprietary models under zero-shot evaluation.
