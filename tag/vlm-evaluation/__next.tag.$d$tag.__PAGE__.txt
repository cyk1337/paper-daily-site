9:T4cf,ExBind is built around a representation-independent latent binding space and deterministic compilation/evaluation:
1) Sample a latent binding instance containing entities, relations, hierarchy, attributes, ordered roles (e.g., directed edge endpoints), and a unique canonical target.
2) Compile the same latent instance into heterogeneous executable surfaces (SVG, DOM, canvas, tree, graph, table), each with its own surface-specific executable identifiers.
3) The model outputs only the reference required by the environment. The evaluator maps the prediction back into the latent space deterministically, then reports:
- validity (is the output a valid candidate under the declared vocabulary/format?)
- exact binding correctness (does it match the gold target?)
- which structural constraints are violated; for families with an ordered constraint decomposition, a concise first-violation diagnosis.
4) The benchmark explicitly distinguishes outcome stability from diagnostic-type stability via candidate-order perturbations.

This minimal-pair construction is the key idea. It suppresses screenshot salience, centrality bias, and fixed-answer shortcuts: a model that always clicks the more salient element can only get one item in the pair correct.

Errors are split into two types: selecting the contrastive wrong candidate versus predicting a point outside both candidate regions. This distinction matters because it changes the diagnosis. A low score does not necessarily mean the model misunderstood the relation word; it may simply have failed to localize either candidate reliably.

The paper also evaluates three training-free interventions: Set-of-Mark prompting, primitive-aware chain-of-thought, and activation steering. SoM explicitly marks the two candidates and therefore acts as an oracle upper bound on candidate discrimination rather than a deployable solution. CoT tests whether explicit reasoning helps.
