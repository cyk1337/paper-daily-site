This immediately reduces both KV cache and attention compute, but it also removes a large amount of head diversity. VHA restores that diversity through two lightweight linear modules.

The first is Q Premix. Within each KV group, Premix applies a near-identity linear transformation to the reduced set of physical query heads, effectively expanding them into a richer set of virtual query heads. The near-identity design is important. It keeps the model close to a stable GQA-2 parameterization early in training, while allowing the network to gradually learn structured specialization among heads that share the same KV pair. The authors’ matrix inspection suggests that this is exactly what happens: Premix learns group-conditioned query specialization rather than arbitrary mixing.

The second is Linear Postmix. Even with better within-group query diversity, using only 2 KV heads still limits cross-group interaction. Postmix therefore applies a low-rank identity-residual transformation, I + AB^T, to the attention output. The rank is small, for example r = Hq'/2 and r = 4 at the 1.7B scale. This gives the model a cheap way to fuse information across heads and groups after attention, without increasing head dimension or introducing nonlinear runtime structure. The paper interprets this as structured low-rank cross-head communication.

A major engineering advantage is that both modules are purely linear. Premix can be folded into the query projection matrix WQ, and Postmix into the output projection WO at inference time. After folding, the deployed model is just a standard GQA-2 model. This means VHA inherits existing inference kernels and serving optimizations from frameworks such as vLLM and SGLang with zero additional runtime overhead.

Relative to prior methods, the distinctions are clean. Compared with standard GQA, VHA adds learnable compensation before and after shared-KV attention. Compared with MLA, it avoids a latent KV bottleneck. If you are considering implementation or adaptation, pay attention to the appendix details on FLOP accounting, head configurations at different scales, and the exact MoE settings.12:T7f3,技术上分三层。第一层是机制定位。作者从 softmax backward 的标准形式 dS = P ⊙ (dP - δ) 出发，其中每一行的 δ_i = Σ_j P_ij dP_ij，本来保证 dS 每行求和为 0，这对应 softmax 对行平移不变。FP8 attention 的常见实现为了省算，会用保存下来的输出 O 来近似这个 row correction，即 δ_i^stale = <dO_i, Ô_i>。在高精度或前后向操作数一致时，这个捷径成立；但在 FP8 下，forward 用的是量化后的 P、V 得到 Ô，backward 又用重新量化后的 dO、V 得到 dP̂，两边的量化误差来源不同，导致 δ_i^stale 不再等于真正与 dP̂ 匹配的 correction。

第二层是误差如何影响训练。作者推导出 stale delta 会让 dS 的每一行出现非零残差 ε_i = Σ_j dS_ij^stale。这个残差不是无害的，因为它等价于在 matched gradient 上额外加了 ε_i P_ij 这一项。若 ε_i<0，会推动该行 logits 变尖；若 ε_i>0，会推动其变平。表面上看，不同行的残差可能互相抵消，但映射到 channel 维度后并不会自然抵消。作者观察到某些被“偏爱”的通道会持续受到同向压力，尤其体现在 key 侧 RMSNorm gain 被系统性推高，随后形成选择性放大，训练越久越明显。

第三层是修复方法 Delta-Matching。核心思想不是简单提高某个张量精度，而是让 backward row correction 使用与 backward 真实乘法路径一致的量。作者从未量化的 softmax 概率 P 和由 FP8 backward operands 在 FP32 中相乘得到的 dP̂ 出发，重新构造匹配的 δ，使 softmax gradient 的零行和不变量在给定数值假设下恢复。文中还特别说明，只把 dO 保留成 BF16 并不够，因为概率量化带来的不一致仍在。Delta-Matching 的价值在于它不依赖额外保存一个高精度 forward output，也不要求改 attention 结构；它针对的是 backward correction 这个具体失配点，因此可以直接嵌入 native block-scaled FP8 attention kernel。13:T822,实验设计比较扎实，重点不只是“最终分数更高”，而是把失效边界画出来了。首先在机制验证上，作者拿 1.67B GDN/GQA 训练末期 checkpoint 的 BF16 重算激活，去探测 FP8 kernel 在 6 个 attention layer、32 个训练窗口上的行为。结果显示，在 dS 量化之前，stale delta 造成的归一化 row-sum residual 中位数大约在 1.1e-2 到 4.7e-2，这已经不是可忽略的舍入噪声量级。

规模实验覆盖 569M、1.67B、5.29B。569M 上，stale-delta hybrid 只表现为较温和的 validation loss gap，下游任务大体还能保住；这解释了为什么一些早期工作在 sub-billion 规模上看起来“FP8 attention 也能训”。但到 1.67B 和 5.29B，训练前期还能跟住 BF16/FP32 mixed-precision reference，之后 loss 开始持续抬升，并带来下游性能下降，而且 5.29B 更早出现拐点，符合“误差积累且随规模加速”的判断。

作者还测试了多种可能的缓解手段。QK normalization 能减轻甚至避免某些配置下的灾难性崩溃，但仍留有明显性能差距；NoPE 在保留位置敏感 recurrent 层时只能延后退化；head dimension 从 128 提到 256 没有根治作用。这些结果说明问题不是某个架构细节偶然触发，而是 attention backward 数值约束被破坏后的普遍现象。

在架构泛化上，作者不只测标准 attention，还测了 Gated DeltaNet + MLA 的 hybrid，以及 KDA + GQA 的组合，stale-delta failure 都存在。在线性层精度、模型结构、训练阶段变化下，Delta-Matching 都能把训练 loss 和整体下游表现拉回到 BF16/FP32 mixed-precision attention reference 附近。

长上下文实验也很关键。按 Kimi K2 / DeepSeek-V3 风格 recipe，从 8K 扩到 64K，并把学习率降下来后，stale-delta baseline 相对 BF16/FP32 的 training-loss gap 要到大约 19k optimizer steps、约 20B tokens 后才超过 0.01。这个结果很有信息量：如果只做短程 context extension 验证，很容易误判为“方法没问题”。14:T7df,The technical story has three parts. First, the paper localizes the failure. Softmax backward has the form dS = P ⊙ (dP - δ), where δ_i = Σ_j P_ij dP_ij for each row. In exact arithmetic this guarantees that each row of dS sums to zero, reflecting softmax shift invariance. Many efficient implementations reuse the saved forward output O to compute the row correction, using δ_i^stale = <dO_i, O_hat_i>. This shortcut is valid when the forward and backward quantities are numerically consistent. Under FP8, however, O_hat comes from quantized forward operands, while dP_hat is formed from separately quantized backward operands. The two paths no longer match, so the shortcut becomes stale.

Second, the paper analyzes how this mismatch affects optimization. The stale correction induces a nonzero row residual ε_i = Σ_j dS_ij^stale. That residual adds an extra term ε_i P_ij to the matched gradient. Negative ε_i sharpens a row of logits; positive ε_i flattens it. Even if row-wise residuals nearly cancel globally, they do not cancel channel-wise after projection through Q/K/V. The authors show persistent pressure on selected channels, especially visible as systematic upward drift in key-side RMSNorm gains. This creates selective amplification over training rather than zero-mean noise.

Third, the proposed fix is Delta-Matching. The key idea is to compute the backward row correction from quantities that are actually consistent with the backward path: unquantized softmax probabilities P and FP32 products formed from the FP8 backward operands. Under the stated numerical assumptions, this restores the zero-row-sum invariant. The paper also shows that simply keeping dO in BF16 is insufficient, because the mismatch caused by probability quantization remains. Delta-Matching is attractive because it does not require storing an auxiliary high-precision forward output and does not depend on architectural changes. It directly patches the numerical inconsistency at the backward correction step.15:T81e,The experiments are strong because they map out the failure regime instead of only reporting end metrics. For mechanism validation, the authors probe the FP8 kernel using BF16-recomputed activations from final 1.67B GDN/GQA checkpoints, across six attention layers and 32 training windows. Before dS quantization, stale delta produces normalized row-sum residuals around 1.1e-2 to 4.7e-2 on average row medians over nonzero-gradient rows. That is far above negligible rounding noise.

The scale study covers 569M, 1.67B, and 5.29B models. At 569M, stale-delta hybrid training shows only a modest validation-loss gap and largely preserves downstream quality. This helps explain why earlier small-scale studies could appear encouraging. At 1.67B and 5.29B, however, stale-delta runs initially track BF16/FP32 references, then diverge later with rising loss and degraded downstream performance. The onset is earlier at 5.29B, consistent with cumulative error that accelerates with scale.

The paper also tests several mitigations. QK normalization can prevent catastrophic collapse in some settings, but it still leaves a substantial performance gap. NoPE delays degradation but does not remove it. Increasing head dimension from 128 to 256 does not solve the problem either. This is useful because it shows the issue is not an artifact of one architecture choice.

Generality is tested beyond a single attention design. The stale-delta failure appears in hybrids combining Gated DeltaNet with MLA, and KDA with GQA. Across architectures, training stages, and linear-layer precision settings, Delta-Matching recovers training loss and overall downstream performance close to BF16/FP32 mixed-precision attention references.

The long-context extension result is especially informative. Using a Kimi K2 / DeepSeek-V3 style recipe to extend from 8K to 64K with a lower learning rate, the stale-delta baseline does not exceed a 0.01 training-loss gap over BF16/FP32 until roughly 19k optimizer steps, about 20B tokens. This shows why short extension runs can miss the problem entirely.16:T559,Start with Sections 3.2 to 3.4. That is a broader lesson for low-precision training: local precision patches often fail if forward and backward are still inconsistent at the algorithmic level. Second, the mitigation results for QK norm and NoPE are useful because they separate error sensitivity from error source. They make training less fragile, but they do not repair the broken invariant.
