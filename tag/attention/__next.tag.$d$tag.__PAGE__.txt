It starts from the usual token-wise base score for each masked position, typically the confidence of the top predicted token. Instead of sorting positions independently by that score, ADAS builds the subset greedily. Once some positions have already been selected, each remaining candidate is discounted according to how strongly it attends to those selected positions and how uncertain those selected positions still are. Intuitively, if candidate i depends heavily on an already selected but still fragile position j, then the marginal value of committing i in the same step should be reduced.

The paper frames this as greedy construction under a non-additive subset utility: confidence acts as an individual utility proxy, while attention acts as a pairwise compatibility proxy. This is the key distinction from prior samplers. Top-k, Fast-dLLM, and EB-Sampler differ mostly in their budget constraints: fixed cardinality, confidence threshold, or entropy budget. They do not make the value of adding one position depend on which other positions are already in the subset. ADAS does exactly that, while remaining plug-compatible with all three.
