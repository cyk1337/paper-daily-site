9:T5f7,The core mechanism is Expert Folding. Instead of dropping an excluded source expert or rerouting it to a similar target expert without correction, ExFold projects the source contribution onto a retained target expert. The method is motivated by two empirical findings: many experts produce directionally aligned outputs on the same inputs, but their output magnitudes differ substantially. Directional alignment makes substitution plausible; magnitude mismatch makes naive rerouting inaccurate. Based on this, each layer is calibrated with two matrices from a single frozen forward pass on unlabeled text. The scalar-projector matrix stores directed source-to-target scaling factors. The projection-loss matrix measures reconstruction loss for folding one expert into another. At inference time, retained experts are selected according to the phase budget: K_pre dominant experts per token for prefill, and D dominant experts per batch for decode. Every excluded expert is then assigned to the minimum-loss retained target, and its contribution is folded into that target by absorbing the scalar projector into the router weight. This gives one shared folding operator across both phases. Compared with token-wise sparsification, the method does not only reduce executed experts; compared with expert-set consolidation, it does not bake compression into a fixed submodel; compared with similarity-based rerouting, it explicitly corrects scale mismatch. The implementation is integrated into vLLM with a lightweight CUDA kernel.a:T4a9,The evaluation covers multiple MoE architectures and real vLLM serving workloads, focusing on TTFT, TPOT, and quality retention. The larger decode gain is consistent with the method’s focus on reducing the active expert set under memory-bound conditions. The more informative part is the comparison structure. Against pruning or skipping baselines, ExFold degrades quality more slowly at the same budget, which supports the claim that explicit reconstruction matters. Against static merging methods, it remains adaptive to token-level or batch-level budgets instead of committing to one permanently compressed model. Against similarity-based rerouting, the directed scalar projector improves approximation when experts are aligned in direction but not in scale. The ablations indicate that both components—the loss-based target assignment and the scalar correction—are necessary.
