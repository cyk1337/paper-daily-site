5:T46c,Pyligent implements a validated-search view of reasoning inspired by the Diligent Learner formulation. It maintains a ChainTree of partial reasoning states. The model emits one action at a time: <node> appends an intermediate step, <done> proposes a final answer, and <backtrack> returns control to an earlier node. Training has three stages. SFT-A teaches the action format and gives the model a nonzero probability of producing valid next steps. Validator-guided exploration samples continuations from selected prefixes and labels both successful continuations and failed leaves. SFT-B then trains on examples extracted from the resulting tree: success pairs, failure pairs with validator-derived backtrack targets, and optional continuation-after-trace pairs. A reverse curriculum starts exploration near the end of a gold chain and gradually moves toward the root. At inference time, the runtime validates backtrack targets and truncates the context accordingly. Traced recovery adds a compact trace after truncation, summarizing the abandoned branch and validator reason, so the model is less likely to repeat the same mistake.6:T4c2,The approach is a controlled benchmark suite with deterministic validators:
- Task families: nine classical puzzles/search problems (SAT, cryptarithmetic, graph coloring, river crossing, Tower of Hanoi, water jug, checker jumping, Sudoku, Rubik’s cube), each parameterized so complexity can be increased monotonically while preserving semantics.
- Formalization: reasoning is defined over (S, A, T, V) with a validity predicate; a solution is only accepted if every step is valid and the final state reaches the goal.
- Collapse profiling: for each model/task, measure success probability as a function of complexity and identify sharp drop-offs (“collapse thresholds”), plus trace-level failure modes (invalid intermediate states, constraint violations, premature termination, reduced effort at higher complexity despite available token budget).
- Cross-domain generalization: compare collapse behavior across structurally different task families to test whether improvements transfer (algorithmic skill) or remain task-specific (heuristics/pattern matching).
Overall, the novelty is turning recent qualitative observations about reasoning limits into a reproducible, complexity-controlled measurement
