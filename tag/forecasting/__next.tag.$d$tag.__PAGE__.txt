b:T60b,WorldReasoner turns each resolved event question into a reproducible historical forecasting task by freezing the information environment at a simulated date. Each instance includes the question, a forecast window (earliest forecastable date t0, simulated date tsim, resolution date tres), the resolved outcome y, and a timestamped evidence repository A. A Temporal Gateway exposes only evidence items with timestamps <= tsim, enforcing a strict temporal boundary while keeping evaluation reproducible.

Agents submit: a predicted answer with probability p, cited/accessed sources C, an optional natural-language rationale, and—when enabled—a forecast-time causal event graph GF. After resolution, a Hindsight Agent constructs reference evidence CH and a reference causal graph GH using evidence up to tres.

Scoring is explicitly multi-axis:
1) Outcome quality: accuracy plus proper scoring rules (Brier/log score) to evaluate probabilistic forecasts.
2) Evidence quality: checks time validity (no post-tsim sources) and relevance/precision of cited or accessed evidence, separating “right for the wrong reasons” from grounded forecasting.
3) Reasoning quality: compares GF against GH to measure key-event recovery and structural alignment, aiming to quantify whether the agent’s causal story covers hindsight-validated drivers.

The paper also describes a scalable construction pipeline sourcing questions from prediction markets/news streams, collecting timestamped evidence, and auto-building hindsight graphs to expand the benchmark.c:T5ad,The methodology has three main components. First, benchmark construction. ForecastBench-Sim is built from AI-vs-AI FreeCiv rollouts. A game state is frozen, converted into a natural-language world report, and the model is asked to forecast future outcomes at multiple horizons. This gives long-horizon uncertainty, partial observability, and occasional shocks, while avoiding contamination from real-world text corpora. Second, mechanism isolation. The authors move beyond benchmark correlation by using a synthetic SIR process to separate 'superlinear growth followed by regime change' from a matched 'linear growth with the same crash structure'. Inverse scaling appears only in the former, which supports the claim that the trigger is not merely the existence of a crash but the model being drawn into aggressive extrapolation during accelerating growth. Third, attribution. They use cross-family capability ranking via ECI, provider fixed effects, within-lineage replication, and a controlled Llama-3.1 {70B, 405B} × {base, instruct} design to disentangle capability, provider effects, and post-training. Forecasts are elicited as five quantiles, converted into a piecewise-linear CDF, scored with CRPS, and decomposed with pinball loss to localize the error to the upper tail. Prompt interventions include a generic uncertainty cue and domain-identity cues; neither provides a stable fix, which suggests the issue is deeper than surface prompting.d:T575,The empirical section is strong. On FBSim, pooling six question templates, the capability-performance correlation at long horizon H7 is ρ=-0.42 under CRPS with N=28 models, meaning stronger models are worse at full-distribution forecasting. When reasoning is enabled, the inversion becomes stronger; the appendix reports ρ=-0.56 at H7. The metric reversal is especially important: on the same benchmark, Brier yields ρ=+0.45 with N=27, and even thresholded Brier derived from the same continuous forecasts can flip the sign. That rules out task-format confounds and shows that single-threshold evaluation misses the upper-tail failure. In the mechanism study, synthetic SIR exhibits inverse scaling while the matched linear control does not, supporting the superlinear-growth trigger. In the Llama-3.1 2×2 study, 405B overshoots more than 70B, instruct more than base, and the combination is worst. Real-world replication covers COVID-19 incidence, pre-vaccine US measles, the 2003–2006 US housing bubble, and 12 hyperinflation episodes; the paper reports domain-wise Spearman correlations and 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, all excluding zero. Influenza serves as a useful negative control: similar domain, insufficient overshoot, no inversion.
