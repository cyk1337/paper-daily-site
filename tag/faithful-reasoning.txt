18:T6a4,The core artifact is the GRACE benchmark, together with a data-driven error taxonomy. The benchmark covers chains of thought from ten models over four source datasets spanning two reasoning settings: evidence-grounded reasoning and deductive reasoning. Each step in a trace is annotated with three pieces of information: a faithfulness label, an error category, and a natural-language explanation.

The taxonomy is discovered bottom-up rather than imposed manually. The authors first collect free-form critiques of many unfaithful steps using a strong LLM judge. These critiques are then embedded and clustered, yielding two empirically distinct error tracks: GRACE-Inference and GRACE-Grounding. The former captures deductive failures, logical leaps, and constraint violations. The latter captures factual contradiction, fabricated evidence, and extraction errors. This is a sensible design choice because it lets the taxonomy emerge from observed failure patterns rather than from a predefined label set.

For data construction, the paper uses multiple LLM judges to scale annotation. Cases with unanimous judge agreement form a large training set. Hard disagreement cases are sent to human annotators and become the high-quality evaluation set. This creates a practical split: scalable supervision for training, and more reliable, more difficult data for evaluation. The benchmark is then used in two ways. First, the authors train step-level evaluators and show that smaller models fine-tuned on the training set can approach larger ones. Second, they inject context-faithfulness signals into RL for downstream reasoning tasks and study the effect on both task accuracy and reasoning reliability.19:T698,The most important findings are qualitative and structural rather than a single headline number. First, the GRACE evaluation set reveals clear headroom for current models. A nontrivial fraction of traces that end with the correct answer still contain unfaithful intermediate steps, while some fully faithful traces still end in the wrong answer. This shows that answer accuracy is not a sufficient proxy for reasoning faithfulness.

Second, the taxonomy appears empirically meaningful. By clustering critiques of unfaithful steps, the authors find that inference-related failures and grounding-related failures separate into distinct groups rather than collapsing into one generic error label. That makes the benchmark useful not only for binary detection but also for diagnosis.

Third, there are two training-related results. Fine-tuning smaller evaluator models on the GRACE training set can bring them close to larger models, suggesting that step-level faithfulness judgment is learnable and can be distilled. In addition, incorporating step-level context-faithfulness signals into RL improves both downstream task accuracy and reasoning reliability. The introduction does not expose all the detailed numbers, so the main tables in the full paper are worth checking. The key point is not just the absolute gain, but that the reward appears to improve reliability without simply pushing the model toward shorter or overly cautious responses.

One caveat is that the paper is centered on benchmark construction and evaluator training, not on a new reasoning architecture. For a foundation-model reader, the main value lies in how it defines a trainable process-level supervision signal.1a:T865,方法核心是把 AtMan 从一个事后解释工具，改造成训练时可优化的 saliency 估计器。标准 transformer 的注意力是对 pre-softmax score H 做 softmax，AtMan 在 H 上加一个可控掩码 H^AtMan。与直接删 token、改 embedding 或做输入扰动不同，这种做法不改变 token 序列本身，也尽量避免引入明显的分布偏移；它只是在注意力分配上抑制或增强某些 token 的影响。

作者把这个掩码限制在 CoT token 范围内，不动 prompt token，也不动 final answer token。原因很直接：prompt 不是模型可控输出，不适合作为 reward shaping 对象；而作者关心的是“答案是否依赖推理链”，所以要只操作 reasoning trace。初始化时，所有 CoT 相关 mask 设为负常数 c=-0.4，相当于先统一压低模型对推理 token 的注意力。然后固定模型参数，只优化 mask，使 teacher forcing 下正确答案 token 的交叉熵损失尽量恢复。直观上，如果某些 reasoning token 对答案很关键，那么要恢复答案概率，就必须把这些位置的 mask 往回调；最终学到的 mask 大小就可视为 token saliency 的近似。

在得到 mask 后，作者把其归一化，并对 CoT 区域做平均，构造一个 saliency reward。这个奖励与 outcome-based reward 一起放进 GRPO 训练中，目标是同时优化答题正确性和推理链的“有效性”。论文的关键设计不在 RL 框架本身，而在 reward construction：不是奖励更长的 CoT，也不是奖励某种模板化步骤，而是奖励那些在注意力层面被证明对答案恢复有帮助的 reasoning token。

这个方法与常见 process supervision 的区别在于，它不需要人工标注每一步是否正确；与纯 outcome RL 的区别在于，它不把所有正确答案都视为等价，而是偏好那些答案更依赖自身推理文本的轨迹。它的潜在问题也很明显：attention saliency 只是 faithfulness 的代理变量，而且只覆盖“通过注意力路径影响答案”的部分，不一定能完整反映残差流、MLP 变换或更深层的内部计算。1b:T86b,The key idea is to turn AtMan, originally a post-hoc interpretability tool, into a differentiable saliency estimator used during training. In a transformer, attention is computed from pre-softmax scores H. AtMan adds a mask to H before the softmax. Compared with deleting tokens, perturbing embeddings, or masking inputs directly, this is a relatively controlled intervention: the token sequence stays unchanged, and the manipulation acts through attention allocation rather than through a large input-distribution shift.

The mask is applied only to CoT tokens, not to prompt tokens or final answer tokens. This design follows the training objective: the prompt is fixed and not under model control, while the final answer should depend on the generated reasoning trace. The mask is initialized with a negative constant c = -0.4 over CoT-related entries, which uniformly suppresses attention to reasoning tokens. Then, with model parameters frozen, the mask is optimized to restore the likelihood of the correct answer under teacher forcing. Intuitively, if some reasoning tokens are important for producing the answer, the optimization must relax suppression on those positions. The learned mask values are then used as a proxy for token saliency.

After normalization, the mask values over the CoT region are aggregated into a saliency reward. This reward is combined with standard outcome-based reward inside GRPO. The novelty is therefore not in the RL algorithm itself, but in reward construction: the model is encouraged not simply to be correct, and not simply to produce longer reasoning, but to produce reasoning whose tokens measurably support the answer under attention-based intervention.

Relative to process supervision, the method avoids manual step annotations. Relative to pure outcome RL, it distinguishes between correct trajectories that are more or less dependent on their own reasoning text. The main limitation is also clear: attention saliency is only a proxy. It does not fully capture all causal pathways in the residual stream, MLP computations, or other internal mechanisms, so it should not be equated with full faithfulness.1c:T564,Experiments are run on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, with GSM8K and MMLU as the main evaluation tasks. The claimed result is not a large accuracy jump. Instead, the paper argues that combining the saliency reward with GRPO preserves answer quality while reducing extraneous reasoning. In other words, the generated CoT becomes shorter and more focused without a major drop in task performance.

The experiments are designed to test three things. First, whether the learnable attention mask can identify CoT tokens that matter for answer prediction. Second, whether using this signal as a reward changes the model’s reasoning behavior rather than merely shrinking output length. Third, whether the resulting training hurts final accuracy. The reported conclusion is that AtManRL reduces unnecessary reasoning while keeping performance broadly intact.

From a foundation-model perspective, the evidence is still early-stage. The model is only 3B, and the evaluation is limited to standard reasoning benchmarks. The available text does not indicate a deep scaling study or a broad stability analysis across mask initialization, optimization steps, layer choices, or reward coefficients. For larger-scale adoption, the missing questions are practical ones: training overhead, variance, reward hacking, and whether the saliency reward is genuinely different from a length-control heuristic.1d:T5b4,For a skim, focus on definitions first, then on reward construction, and only then on the empirical claims. Start with the introduction and make sure the saliency-versus-faithfulness distinction is clear. The paper does not claim to solve faithful reasoning in full; it proposes saliency as an optimizable prerequisite.
