9:T574,The proposed method, STS (Structure-to-Semantics), is a training-free two-stage pruning framework. Stage one operates before visual tokens enter the LLM. Instead of ranking tokens only by attention, it formulates token selection with a potential-energy or repulsion-inspired objective: tokens are treated like charged particles in feature space, and semantically similar tokens repel each other more strongly. This encourages a selected subset that is spatially and semantically diverse, preserving global structure. Stage two operates inside an intermediate LLM layer and uses instruction-aware cross-attention to remove task-irrelevant visual tokens. The design is motivated by empirical analysis of representation dynamics. In deeper vision layers, feature-similar tokens receive increasingly similar attention scores, so attention loses discriminative power within local semantic neighborhoods. In the LLM decoder, earlier layers aggregate broad context while deeper layers become more task-focused. The paper also introduces a KNN Consistency Score to quantify how strongly feature similarity aligns with attention similarity, serving as a diagnostic for attention collapse.
