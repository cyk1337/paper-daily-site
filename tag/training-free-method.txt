18:T6f3,The paper first diagnoses the mechanism, then proposes a training-free fix. The diagnosis centers on the claim that standard dual-encoder similarity s(I,T)=<f(I),g(T)> behaves like soft pooling over atomic concept evidence rather than logical execution. Using one- and two-concept queries on COCO, images are partitioned into four quadrants: Both, A-only, B-only, and Neither. The authors then inspect mean scores and rankings under different logical forms. Anti-monotone queries such as ¬A∧¬B systematically prefer the Both quadrant, which should be the least preferred one. This shows that negation does not actually alter the ranking mechanism. The paper further analyzes the text embedding into dominant concept-related directions and weaker operator-related residuals, arguing that operator information exists but is attenuated or poorly aligned with image evidence under dot-product scoring.

Based on that diagnosis, the paper proposes factored inference and LCSE. The idea is to separate evidence extraction from constraint execution. A frozen VLM is used to score each atomic concept independently, producing concept-level evidence. Boolean operators are then executed outside the model using the parsed query structure. To avoid discarding useful non-logical information captured by the original similarity score, LCSE performs logic-constrained score editing rather than replacing the score entirely. It adaptively edits the holistic similarity only when the query requires non-mean aggregation. This preserves standard retrieval behavior where no logical constraint is present. The trade-off is clear: no retraining and better semantic control, at the cost of requiring query parsing and currently focusing on Boolean compositions over one or two visual concepts.19:T549,The experiments have three main parts. First, the paper shows on COCO val2017 that CLIP-like models can detect whether a concept is present, yet fail to execute negation in ranking. A representative result reported in the text is that concept detection reaches about AUC 0.88, while the rankings induced by “a X” and “no X” have Spearman correlation around 0.94. So the model changes the text embedding, but not enough to change the ordering. Second, the paper introduces FACTOR-Bench, built from operator-contrastive caption pairs with validated concept presence and absence labels, to test whether a model follows Boolean semantics rather than merely tracking mentioned concepts. Third, LCSE is evaluated on FACTOR-Bench, NegBench, and retrieval-retention settings against standard CLIP, SigLIP, and fine-tuned variants. The reported pattern is that LCSE improves constraint-sensitive retrieval while preserving ordinary retrieval better than training-based fixes. The paper also argues that fine-tuning on negation-heavy data often harms standard retrieval because it tries to repair an interface problem at the representation level.
