9:T7d4,The proposed framework is TTRL-CoCoV, short for test-time reinforcement learning with confidence-conditioned verification. The paper begins with an empirical diagnosis of three obstacles. One is noisy exploration signals: Pass@k optimization naturally pushes toward low-confidence regions, but in a label-free setting those are exactly where pseudo-labels are least reliable. The second is length collapse: directly importing Pass@k objectives does not prevent the standard deviation of response lengths from shrinking, and that shrinkage acts as a leading indicator of premature stagnation. The third is formulation failure: advantage designs that work in supervised RLVR do not actually expand useful exploration in TTRL.

To address this, CoCoV has two main components. The first is Confidence-Conditioned Classifying. Samples are handled differently depending on confidence. High-confidence samples are used to bootstrap the verifier because they are more likely to be genuine successes and can improve verifier quality. Low-confidence samples are then filtered more aggressively by the verifier to suppress spurious pseudo-labels and bad trajectories. Medium-confidence samples are treated more conservatively to avoid amplifying ambiguous signals. The key idea is not simple confidence weighting, but co-evolution of generator and verifier under a shared optimization process.

The second component is an exploration-enhancing reward, R_div. Instead of encouraging diversity everywhere, the method rewards solution-strategy diversity only on high-confidence problems. The rationale is important: pushing diversity on low-confidence samples would expand the search space with noisy pseudo-labels, which is harmful in a label-free setting. Encouraging diversity on high-confidence problems is closer to broadening coverage within regions that are already likely solvable.
