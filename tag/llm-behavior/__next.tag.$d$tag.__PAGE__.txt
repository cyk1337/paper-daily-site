a:T425,The paper uses a prompt-template framework with multiple slots; each slot has a set of candidate fragments (often meaning-preserving paraphrases, or content designed to be irrelevant to the target question). For binary-choice settings, the response is the model’s output log-odds. They fit an additive model where the overall log-odds is approximated as a sum of per-slot fragment effects; the fitted coefficients are cue scores.
To induce “model hypnosis,” they estimate cue scores by sampling thousands of random prompt configurations on a source model, then select and stack fragments whose cue scores align toward the desired answer, pushing the log-odds into a regime where the model responds with near certainty.
They also probe limits: stability of additivity under prompt-context changes, the magnitude of higher-order interactions beyond first-order additivity, and behavior under repeated cues / out-of-distribution combinations.
