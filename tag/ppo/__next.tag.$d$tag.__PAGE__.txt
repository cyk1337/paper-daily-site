Compared with heuristics such as increasing the upper clip bound or using asymmetric clipping, the key difference is per-state-action adaptation based on local policy geometry.10:T6ee,PS-PPO replaces full-trajectory updates with stochastic prefix updates. In standard critic-free PPO for language models, a completion of length T receives a broadcast advantage, and the PPO objective sums token-level terms across all timesteps. The authors observe that on reasoning tasks, the probability of eventual success conditioned on the current prefix often saturates before the sequence ends. This suggests that much of the suffix may be redundant for learning.

The method therefore defines a prompt-conditioned cutoff distribution and samples a cutoff c for each rollout. Only the prefix up to c is used in the policy-gradient computation; tokens after c are skipped for that update. The main technical issue is bias: naively truncating the sequence would change the objective because the original update sums over all tokens. To fix this, the paper derives an inclusion-probability reweighting estimator. Each retained timestep is weighted by the inverse of its probability of being included under the cutoff distribution, so that the expectation over cutoff sampling recovers the corresponding full broadcast update. In other words, PS-PPO does not redefine the target objective; it constructs a cheaper stochastic estimator of the same critic-free update.

Another important design choice is that the cutoff distribution is prompt-conditioned rather than fixed. It is driven by a prefix uncertainty signal: examples that appear effectively decided early can be truncated more aggressively, while uncertain examples keep longer prefixes. The paper explicitly acknowledges late recovery and self-correction, so it uses stochastic truncation instead of deterministically discarding all suffix tokens. That is the main robustness-efficiency tradeoff in the design.11:T4ca,The experiments support two claims. The first is a theoretical analysis of clipped policy-gradient updates in RLVR. Starting from the GRPO-style objective, the authors write token-level updates as a function of token probability, advantage, importance ratio, and clipping. The key quantity is the conditional expectation of the effective gradient after clipping, not the raw unclipped gradient. They then connect off-policy degree to the variance of the importance ratio: with more gradient steps per rollout, policy drift increases; low-probability tokens develop more dispersed ratios and are clipped more often. This yields the central phenomenon of gradient dominance reversal. In near on-policy regimes, low-probability tokens can dominate because of stronger amplification. In sufficiently off-policy regimes, those same tokens are disproportionately clipped, and higher-probability tokens end up dominating the surviving update. This is the mechanism used to reconcile prior empirical heuristics.

The second layer analyzes not only update magnitude but also directional bias. The paper decomposes how token probability, advantage sign and magnitude, and asymmetric clipping interact. Positive- and negative-advantage tokens are affected differently by upper and lower clipping bounds. Because ratio dispersion differs across token groups, a single global clipping interval over-regularizes some groups and under-regularizes others. The result is not just uneven gradient magnitude, but also a rotation of the update direction relative to the on-policy gradient.

Motivated by this, the authors propose ACPO, Adaptive Clip Policy Optimization. The main idea is simple but targeted: replace one global clipping range with bin-wise adaptive thresholds. Bins are constructed to reflect heterogeneous token groups, and the clipping thresholds are adjusted according to ratio dispersion. The goal is to reduce uneven clipping and mitigate clipping-induced gradient bias. Compared with methods such as DAPO or CISPO, ACPO does not hard-code a preference for high-probability or high-entropy tokens; it adapts clipping to the observed ratio statistics.16:T655,The empirical section is more about validating the mechanism than chasing a single benchmark number.
