18:T87f,The proposed method, QuantWM, is a training-free 2-bit KV-cache quantization framework. Its goal is not merely to reduce Key/Value reconstruction error, but to explicitly preserve attention logits and temporal consistency. It builds on the centroid-residual representation used in QVG: each Key is assigned to a BF16 centroid, and only the residual is quantized to low bitwidth. The paper argues that QVG is limited because centroid selection is driven mainly by K-means geometry, which optimizes feature-space distance rather than downstream impact on QK^T.

The first component is QSAC, quantization-sensitivity-aware clustering. Instead of selecting centroids purely by Key-space clustering quality, QSAC jointly considers Query sensitivity and residual dynamic range, aiming to reduce the effect of INT2 residual quantization on attention logits. Intuitively, some Key directions may have small Euclidean error but still be highly dangerous if they align with Query-sensitive directions and alter token ranking. QSAC tries to avoid such residual structures already at the clustering stage.

The second component is PSAC, principal-subspace attention compensation. Even with better clustering, Key quantization error can still be amplified along dominant Query directions and distort logits. PSAC extracts the principal Query subspace and uses low-rank projections to represent and compensate the Key error components along those sensitive directions during inference. The idea is not to fully reconstruct Keys, which is unrealistic at 2 bits, but to correct the error components that matter most for attention allocation. This is a different philosophy from simply increasing quantization fidelity: it accepts that full preservation is impossible and spends the available budget on the subspace that most affects retrieval behavior.

The overall design is coherent: QSAC makes Keys more quantization-friendly before compression, and PSAC compensates the most harmful attention shifts after compression. Because the method is training-free, it is intended as a practical drop-in replacement for existing KV-cache compression pipelines in video generation and world models.19:T53a,The experiments cover several representative models, including LingBot-World-v2, Matrix-Game-2, LongCat-Video, HY-World 1.5, and Causal-Forcing, which suggests the authors are aiming for architecture-agnostic evidence rather than a single-model result. The headline outcome is that QuantWM substantially reduces temporal flickering, blur, and artifacts under 2-bit KV quantization while maintaining strong benchmark performance on metrics such as VBench. It also achieves up to 6.20x KV-cache memory compression with limited inference overhead.

The most important evidence comes from the diagnostic ablations. The paper separates Key-only and Value-only quantization and shows that most degradation comes from Keys. It then compares reconstruction errors and finds that Keys can have smaller numerical error than Values despite causing worse output quality. That result supports the paper's central claim: KV quantization should not be judged mainly by reconstruction magnitude, but by its effect on attention logits and token ranking. The method components are built directly around that diagnosis. The God of Concepts provides high-level intent in text or images. The Creator, implemented with an LLM, recursively expands that intent into a world blueprint with themes, shared rules, hidden states, and system constraints. Executors are coding agents that translate the blueprint into executable entities, behaviors, and subsystems. Artists are video generation models that render observations from visual conditions derived from the executable world. Travelers act inside the world and receive observations from their own viewpoints.

Two design ideas carry most of the technical weight. The first is Logical Spaces. A world is decomposed into heterogeneous subsystems such as transportation, construction, resources, and social relations. Each subsystem has local mechanisms, but all must obey shared global constraints. Logical Spaces provides a modular structure for collaborative world construction, so different agents can implement different subsystems while remaining consistent through shared interfaces and rules. This is more controllable than asking one model to generate an entire world monolithically.

The second is Observation as World Registration. Generated observations are not treated as disposable outputs. They are registered back into a persistent world representation, so information obtained from one viewpoint or one interaction can be retained and reused later. This directly targets a weakness of standard video world models, where past frames are consumed as context but are not written back into a structured memory.

The division of labor is deliberate. CoDeR does not ask the diffusion video model to infer rules, track hidden variables, or maintain long-term state, because those capabilities are hard to learn reliably from pixel supervision alone. Instead, the LLM-plus-code layer handles explicit world logic, and the video model focuses on conditional rendering.
