9:T902,作者先给出一个统一的 distributional distillation 公式：把 student 生成的样本经过 embedding 和 Gaussian noising，得到 noised student distribution；再与真实数据对应的 noised data distribution 做某种 discrepancy matching。这个框架可以覆盖已有一些方法，也能自然扩展到 multi-step generation。论文随后把 discrepancy 特化为 reverse KL，并在同一 student 架构下推导出两条路线。

第一条是 Simplex-DMD。student 在每个位置直接输出词表上的概率向量，把它视作 simplex 上的连续 token relaxation，再映射到 embedding 空间并加噪。由于整个路径是连续可导的，可以使用 pathwise gradients。为了估计 noised student distribution 的 score，方法引入一个 auxiliary denoiser，思路延续了 Diff-Instruct / DMD 一类 continuous-data distribution matching 方法，但这里适配的是离散文本经 simplex relaxation 后的连续表示。它的优点是低步数下训练和采样都更稳定，缺点是 relaxation 可能带来偏差，尤其在高质量、低熵文本区域会受限。

第二条是 Reinforce-DMD。这里 student 仍输出 categorical distribution，但训练时从中采样离散 token，再 embedding、加噪，并通过 REINFORCE 估计梯度。为了构造 reverse-KL 所需的密度比，作者训练一个 discriminator 来估计 noised student 与 noised data 之间的 density ratio。这样做避免了 simplex relaxation 的连续近似偏差，更贴近真正的离散生成分布，但代价是方差更高、训练更难，需要更仔细的稳定化设计。论文还把两种方法都扩展到 multi-step generation，而不是只做 one-step distillation，这一点很关键，因为语言生成质量通常对少量中间 refinement step 很敏感。

和已有 trajectory-based distillation 不同，这里不要求 student 追随 teacher 的具体去噪轨迹，而是直接匹配最终分布。这种做法给 student 更大自由度，也更适合研究不同输出参数化下的质量-多样性权衡。论文的一个重要观察是：同样是 reverse-KL matching，不同参数化在不同 NFE 区间各有优势，说明“最佳蒸馏方法”并不存在统一答案，而是取决于你想把采样预算压到多低。a:T918,The paper starts from a unified distributional distillation view. Student samples are embedded and noised, producing a noised student distribution, which is then matched to the corresponding noised data distribution through a generic discrepancy. This formulation subsumes prior distribution-matching approaches and extends naturally to multi-step generation. The authors then specialize the discrepancy to reverse KL and derive two methods under the same student architecture.

Simplex-DMD keeps the student outputs as probability vectors over the vocabulary. These are treated as continuous simplex-valued token relaxations, embedded, and corrupted with Gaussian noise. Because the whole path is differentiable, training can use pathwise gradients. To estimate the score of the noised student distribution, the method introduces an auxiliary denoiser, in the spirit of DMD and Diff-Instruct, but adapted to discrete text represented through continuous simplex relaxations. This route is attractive at very low sampling budgets because it is relatively stable and efficient, though it may suffer from approximation bias due to the relaxation.

Reinforce-DMD instead samples categorical tokens from the student distributions, then embeds and noises those discrete samples. Gradients are estimated with REINFORCE, and a discriminator is trained to estimate the density ratio between the noised student and noised data distributions, which is needed for the reverse-KL objective. This avoids the bias introduced by continuous relaxations and stays closer to the true discrete generation process, but it pays with higher variance and more delicate optimization. Importantly, both methods are extended to multi-step generation rather than only one-step distillation, which matters because language quality is often sensitive to a small number of refinement steps.

A notable design choice is the use of distribution matching rather than trajectory matching. The student is not forced to imitate the teacher’s denoising path. It only needs to match the target distribution. That gives the student more freedom and makes the comparison between parameterizations cleaner. One of the paper’s main findings is exactly that there is no universally best parameterization: the preferred method depends strongly on the target NFE regime.b:T5c8,The experiments distill the same 170M-parameter LangFlow teacher on OpenWebText and evaluate 1,024-token generation. Instead of reporting only a single perplexity number, the paper uses generative perplexity–entropy frontiers, which is the right choice here because it exposes the quality-diversity trade-off rather than rewarding collapsed distributions.

The main empirical result is a clear complementarity between the two methods. Simplex-DMD is stronger in the low-NFE regime, especially at 2–16 network evaluations. A representative number is at 4 NFEs and unigram entropy 5.44, where Simplex-DMD achieves Gen PPL 45.6 versus 90.2 for ReDi. Reinforce-DMD becomes stronger at higher budgets. This pattern aligns with the method design: continuous relaxations help when the budget is extremely tight, while discrete sampling with REINFORCE better preserves the true discrete distribution when more refinement steps are available.

The paper also studies multi-step training choices and sampling decisions, framing the results in terms of quality-diversity trade-offs rather than a single scalar score. Even from the available content, the key message is clear: the student output parameterization is not a minor implementation detail.
