17:T4b8,Family-expert CPT plus five layer-aware parameter alignment strategies.
- Setup: start from a shared Gemma-3-4B initialization; train one expert per language family (Indic, Austronesian, Germanic, Romance, Slavic) on family-specific data, enabling parallel training and incremental addition of new families.
- Data/compute: continue pretraining on MADLAD-400, up to 5B tokens per family.
- Hypothesis: forgetting correlates with excessive weight drift, with middle transformer layers carrying more language-agnostic/general knowledge; therefore constrain middle-layer updates while allowing early/late layers to adapt for language acquisition.
- Alignment suite (layer-aware variants): hard layer freezing; soft L2-style regularization toward the base weights; post-hoc weight reversion of selected layers back to the base after training; and weight-space model merging/interpolation to trade off acquisition vs retention without retraining (the paper counts five strategies total, with multiple layer-granularity variants under these umbrellas).
- Baselines: two unregularized CPT baselines (dense CPT and vanilla family experts) to separate “family grouping helps” from “alignment is still needed.”18:T41d,Coverage: 32 training languages across 5 families plus held-out related languages.
Evaluation axes: Belebele reading comprehension, Global-PIQA physical reasoning, FLORES-200 translation (ChrF), and held-out perplexity as an acquisition proxy.
Key results reported in the paper:
- Unconstrained dense CPT causes sizable comprehension drops (6.6–12.3 percentage points).
- Alignment methods substantially reduce forgetting with minimal acquisition cost and improve generalization to held-out related languages.
- Strategy is task-dependent:
  - Middle-layer freezing improves Belebele even over the base model (avg +1.7pp).
  - Post-hoc layer reversion best preserves translation quality, with FLORES averaging +20.6 ChrF over the base.
  - L2 regularization is consistently strong for held-out perplexity.
- Layer/interpolation analyses indicate middle-layer drift is the main driver for comprehension degradation, while translation exhibits a different layer sensitivity profile—implying no single
