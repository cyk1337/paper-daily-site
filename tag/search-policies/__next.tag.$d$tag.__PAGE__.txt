9:T4f9,The work has two parts. First, it re-evaluates prior GuidedSampling. The authors use Qwen2.5-Instruct models at 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B, and 32B as both concept generators and answer generators, covering all 25 size pairs. They keep the original iterative concept-generation procedure and prompts, but change the answer-generator sampling parameters. With temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, and top-k disabled, concept guidance no longer improves over repeated sampling on MATH500.

The second part turns concept generation into a learned search policy. Instead of generating concepts one by one, the model emits many detailed, problem-specific concepts in a single autoregressive trajectory. These concepts are then used to condition the frozen answer generator, with the answer budget allocated across them. The concept generator is trained with reinforcement learning: the sampled concept trajectory is the action, and the reward is the downstream success of the answer generator. In the main setup, a Qwen2.5-7B concept generator is trained to steer a frozen Qwen2.5-32B answer generator. The key design choice is that the small model is not optimized to solve the problem directly; it is optimized to propose semantic directions that make the larger model more likely to solve it.a:T48c,The baseline study on MATH500 uses 100 answer rollouts per problem and reports pass@50. Repeated sampling spends all 100 rollouts directly from the answer generator, while concept-guided sampling uses the same answer-generation budget split across parsed concepts. Under more exploratory decoding, the previously reported gains of GuidedSampling largely disappear and the method often slightly underperforms repeated sampling.

The main result is on 1,000 held-out hard DeepMath problems. At the same answer-generation allocation, naive repeated sampling with the frozen Qwen2.5-32B answer generator reaches 19.0% pass@128. The RL-trained Qwen2.5-7B concept generator raises this to 39.2%. The trained 7B generator also outperforms an untuned 32B concept generator, suggesting the gain is not just model scale. It transfers without retraining to a Llama-3.3-70B answer generator from a different family, and the improvement persists on Omni-MATH 2.
