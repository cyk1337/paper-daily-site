17:T5b6,The paper frames Adversarial Concept Search (ACS): given a set of concepts, extract a linear direction/encoding for each atomic concept from the model, then use pairwise geometry (primarily angular separation) to predict whether composing those concepts will succeed. The mechanism hypothesis is “lossy superposition causes interference”: while sparse feature activations can in principle allow lossless decoding even with non-orthogonal features, real models decode imperfectly; when multiple task-relevant features are simultaneously active and their directions are too aligned, they interfere and compositional generalization fails.
Operationally, the paper instantiates ‘atomic representations’ differently per task:
- In SCAN, atomic primitives are cleanly defined, enabling direct measurement of concept-pair interactions and angles.
- In multi-hop QA, a two-hop question is decomposed into two single-hop components; the angle between the two single-hop representations predicts whether the composed two-hop query will be answered correctly.
- In multilingual factual recall, geometry between fact representations and language subspaces/directions is used to predict recall accuracy for fact×language combinations.
The key distinction from standard error prediction/active learning is that ACS aims to work without running the model on candidate composed inputs; it tries to rank failure-prone scenarios “upstream”
