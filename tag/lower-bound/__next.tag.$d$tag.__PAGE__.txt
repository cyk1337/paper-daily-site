5:T4af,They formalize diffusion samplers in an oracle model: an algorithm A adaptively queries an oracle returning an estimated smoothed score \(\widehat{s}_\sigma(x)\) for chosen noise level σ and point x, and outputs \(\widehat{X}\) whose law approximates the target \(\pi\) (in TV distance).
The lower bound is proved under two strong assumptions (thus making the bound stronger):
(1) Target class (Assumption 1): \(\pi\) is a Gaussian smoothing of a bounded-support distribution (“bounded-plus-noise”), which implies strong moment control and smoothness enabling TV analysis.
(2) Score quality (Assumption 2): for all σ, the score estimator is polynomially accurate in an \(L^p\) sense (with \(\varepsilon_{err}=1/\mathrm{poly}(d)\), covering the common p=2 setting aligned with score matching).
Main result: any sampling algorithm requires \(\widetilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d})\) adaptive score queries. Moreover, the proof implies any sampler must probe \(\widetilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d})\) distinct noise levels, giving a formal explanation for the necessity of multiscale diffusion.
