5:T683,The proposal has two stages. First, before both training and inference, text is preprocessed by semantic and discourse analyzers that extract intra-sentence and inter-sentence relations. These relations are encoded as additional symbols, expanding the original vocabulary V into a larger V'. The added symbols are meant to explicitly represent entities, predicates, argument roles, and contextual markers. The intended role is similar to a structured working memory or a 'Mind’s Eye' in which relevant items and relations are made explicit.

Second, the model is trained over this expanded representation rather than relying solely on a standard transformer over the raw token stream. The paper argues that this recoding should provide a cheaper and more targeted way to process interactions among distant parts of text. The conceptual framing comes from Robust Logic and Knowledge Infusion: learned knowledge should be stored in a form that supports computationally feasible reasoning.

That said, the technical proposal remains high level. The paper explains why explicit relation symbols might be preferable to scaling up generic attention, but it does not provide the kind of concrete training objective, architecture specification, data recipe, optimization details, or scaling analysis expected in modern pretraining work. In practical terms, the idea is to offload part of the structure induction burden from the model to external semantic/discourse analyzers, then train the language model on a richer symbolic stream.
