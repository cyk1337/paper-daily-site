18:T59e,Cognitive Pairwise Training (CPT) trains the policy model on pairwise comparisons of reasoning traces.

- Supervision format: input u = (problem x, reference answer a*, trace τA, trace τB). The model outputs a short comparative rationale plus a final label indicating which trace is more trustworthy.
- Key design choice: pairwise preference is used instead of pointwise scoring, motivated by higher label stability and reduced biases (length/style/template effects) that often contaminate single-trace grading.
- Data pipeline (as described at a high level): construct trace pairs from existing rollouts; use a strong teacher/judge to label which trace is more reliable; mid-train the target model on this comparison task.
- No external reward model: the “discriminator” is absorbed into the policy via supervised training, reducing RM–policy mismatch and system complexity.
- Placement before RL: the intent is to shape an internal reliability criterion first, then run downstream RL (e.g., RLVR) without immediately drifting toward overconfident policies driven by outcome-only rewards.

Relative to alternatives:
- Versus refusal SFT / abstention-aware RL: CPT targets internal reasoning-quality discrimination rather than explicit refusal behaviors.
- Versus reward-model training: CPT distills judgments directly into the policy, trading off flexibility for simplicity and potentially better generalization of the criterion.19:T48d,Reported experiments span 3B/4B/8B/14B/32B across LLaMA, Qwen, and Olmo families, evaluated on seven math reasoning benchmarks plus AbstentionBench, with controlled compute/data budgets against strong baselines (SFT, standard SFT+RL, abstention-focused methods).

The headline claim is Pareto improvement: across 4B–32B, CPT variants more consistently sit on the frontier of (math reasoning score, abstention quality). A concrete number given in the introduction: at 14B, CPT+RL improves math reasoning by +2.2 and abstention by +5.6 over a math-optimized standard SFT+RL pipeline.

Additional evidence mentioned: better-quality reasoning traces (§6.2), generalization to RAG-style uncertainty handling (Table 4), and robustness/scalability analyses in appendices. The paper also includes judge-pipeline validation (Appendix E) and dataset audits (Appendix G), which are crucial given the reliance on LLM-as-a-judge labels.
