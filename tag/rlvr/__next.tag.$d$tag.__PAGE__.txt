第一步是 tree-aware credit construction。作者利用多轮交互天然存在的前缀分叉结构：对同一个历史前缀，采样多个后续 continuation，形成 rollout tree。这样可以把“某个 turn 之后的选择是否有帮助”与此前历史解耦。然后在树上做 recursive backup，把叶子节点的终局 verifier reward 回传成各个 turn 的 backed-up credit。这里的关键不是简单把终局 reward 平均分摊到所有 token，而是利用共享前缀下不同分支的结果差异，得到更细粒度的 turn-level 相对信用。

第二步是 posterior self-distillation。作者不是直接拿 backed-up credit 做 REINFORCE/PPO 权重，而是相对于冻结的行为策略 π_old，通过一个仿射映射把每个 turn 的 credit 变成一个有界的 teacher distribution。这个 teacher 是“后验”意义上的目标分布：高 credit 的动作概率被抬高，低 credit 的动作概率被压低，但整体仍 anchored 在行为策略上，因此不会失控。随后训练当前策略 π_θ 去最小化与 teacher 的 Forward-KL。作者强调两个性质：1）在 π_θ = π_old 的切点处，这个 distillation objective 的梯度与 tree policy gradient 一致；2）随着当前策略接近 teacher，梯度会自然衰减，到达目标后为零，若超过目标则反向，因而比固定 advantage 权重的多轮 mini-batch 更新更稳定。

First, when supervision comes only from a terminal verifier reward, intermediate turns receive extremely sparse and noisy credit. Broadcasting one trajectory-level advantage across the whole rollout over-credits irrelevant actions in successful runs and under-credits useful actions in failed ones. Second, when a fixed rollout batch is reused across sequential mini-batch updates, the current policy drifts away from the behavior policy that generated the data, creating policy staleness. Existing methods usually address one side at a time: critics or process reward models provide denser supervision but add training complexity and their own failure modes, while PPO-style importance weighting and clipping control drift without defining a finite target that the optimization can converge to. The paper aims to derive turn-level supervision directly from rollout trees and terminal rewards, then turn the RL update into a posterior distillation problem.12:T9fa,The method, TreeOPSD, has two main components.

The first is tree-aware credit construction. Multi-turn interaction naturally exposes branching prefixes: from the same history prefix, one can sample multiple alternative continuations, forming a rollout tree. This isolates the effect of decisions made after a given turn while holding the preceding context fixed. Terminal verifier rewards at the leaves are then recursively backed up through the tree to produce turn-level credit. The point is not to spread a final reward uniformly across all tokens, but to exploit outcome differences among sibling branches under a shared prefix to obtain more informative relative credit for each turn.

The second is posterior self-distillation. Instead of plugging the backed-up credit directly into REINFORCE or PPO weights, the method maps turn-level credit through an affine transformation relative to the frozen behavior policy π_old, producing a bounded teacher distribution for each turn. This teacher can be interpreted as a posterior target: actions with higher backed-up credit get their probability increased, lower-credit actions get suppressed, but the target remains anchored to the behavior policy. The current policy π_θ is then trained to minimize a Forward-KL divergence to this teacher.

Two properties are central. First, at the tangent point where π_θ equals π_old, the gradient of the distillation objective recovers the exact tree policy-gradient direction. Second, unlike a fixed collection-time advantage weight, the student-teacher residual shrinks as the student approaches the target, becomes zero at the target, and reverses sign after overshoot. That gives the optimization a finite destination and is the paper’s answer to sequential mini-batch staleness.

The paper also claims a structural result: because the affine map from credit to target distribution commutes with tree backup, matching a backed-up parent target is gradient-equivalent to aggregating descendant distillation losses. This is important because it ties the target construction to the recursive tree credit mechanism rather than treating it as an ad hoc heuristic.

Relative to PPO, the difference is conceptual and practical: PPO remains a ratio-weighted policy-gradient method with clipping, whereas TreeOPSD turns the update into distribution matching against a frozen target. Relative to OPD or OPSD, the teacher is not supplied by an external stronger model or privileged context; it is constructed directly from the rollout tree and terminal rewards.13:T645,From the available full text, the empirical story centers on improved agentic performance across competitive benchmarks and across model scales, plus evidence that the method induces longer interaction chains in a Deep Research setting. Given a reference policy and temperature, there is a Gibbs target under which the KL-adjusted reward is constant on the support of the target distribution. GGPO does not estimate this target policy explicitly, and it does not apply learner-to-behavior importance weights. Instead, it uses groups of responses sampled from the behavior policy for the same prompt, and measures how far the within-group KL-adjusted rewards are from satisfying that constancy condition.
The resulting objective minimizes a log-mean-exp contrast of KL-adjusted rewards inside each response group. This is different from GRPO-style relative advantage updates, and also different from AGRO, which minimizes the variance of the same adjusted reward. The distinction matters: GGPO and AGRO share the same regularized optimum under common support, but they induce different optimization geometry and different finite-group guarantees.
The paper makes two theoretical moves. First, in the on-policy population setting, the GGPO objective is exactly the KL-regularized optimality gap. That gives the loss a clean interpretation: it is not merely a heuristic group contrast, but a direct measure of suboptimality under KL regularization. Second, in the off-policy setting, the analysis does not try to recover an unbiased estimate of that population quantity. Instead, it proves a direct reward comparison: under bounded coverage, the raw finite-group loss controls the gap between the learner’s expected reward from a single sample and the behavior policy’s best-of-K reward, up to explicit terms for regularization, reference-behavior mismatch, group size, and finite-sample error. This is the central conceptual shift of the paper.
A second major component is periodic reference refresh. With a fixed reference, GGPO optimizes toward a single Gibbs target. Refreshing the reference to the current learner changes the target across rounds. The paper shows that if each round exactly fits its Gibbs target, then T refresh rounds at temperature beta are equivalent to one Gibbs update at temperature beta/T. This explains why refresh can compound improvement rather than merely refine a fixed target. For imperfect updates, the paper derives finite-data guarantees in terms of aggregate tokenwise mismatch and inner optimization error. Under binary rewards and local reference control, the final policy’s single-sample reward can approach the best-of-K reward of the initial policy.17:T5ca,The empirical study is organized around three mismatch regimes: training-inference mismatch, controlled policy staleness, and offline training. The note writer never sees the reference answer. These notes are not treated as supervision; they are prompts for targeted re-exploration. Second, the model re-solves the same problem with the note in context, generating fresh replay trajectories that are scored by the same final-answer verifier. A note is accepted only if its replayed attempts outperform the original group. This replay test is the key safeguard. It filters out plausible but misleading self-diagnoses. The paper reports that about one in nine notes explicitly states the answer, and roughly three in five fail the replay test, which makes the filtering step essential rather than optional. Importantly, this distillation branch is kept separate from GRPO’s own reward normalization. That separation avoids mixing two different signals: relative reward-based policy improvement and imitation of verified better behavior. Compared with higher-temperature retries, RSHD conditions exploration on a diagnosis of a specific failure. Compared with hindsight methods that reveal the answer, it avoids train-test leakage. Compared with direct self-reflection supervision, it requires external verification.
