A wiggle is defined as a verdict flip on binary scales, and on Likert 1–5 scales as a move of two or more points (crossing the midpoint, or jumping from the midpoint to an extreme), excluding minor numerical drift. Stability is decomposed into three dimensions. Mechanical Consistency tests verdict retention under information-free perturbations: 10 identical decoding runs, random-seed strings appended to the system prompt, and swapped argument order. Single-turn Conviction tests whether the verdict survives one substantive challenge immediately after the decision. Multi-turn Persistence tests stability under sustained, adaptive pushback over multiple turns. Each wiggle is further labeled as corrective (toward ground truth) or corrupting (away from ground truth), separating stability from accuracy direction.
