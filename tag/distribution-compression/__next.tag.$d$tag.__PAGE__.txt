9:T601,The main strength is the controlled design. The authors build a matched story-continuation setup: they truncate human stories at controlled prefix lengths and ask four checkpoints from the same model lineage to continue them. The human corpora are chosen to span distinct writing domains: StoryStar as public-platform fiction with low editorial filtering, TMAS as prompt-guided story writing, and The New Yorker as professionally edited literary fiction. This is not just a dataset choice; it is a way to test whether post-training adapts to domain-specific narrative texture or instead pulls all continuations toward a single default regime.

Measurement is also more structured than standard diversity metrics. Rather than relying on preference judgments or lexical variety alone, the paper decomposes “flatness” into three sentence-level dynamic facets. Thematic motion tracks how topics move through a story, including whether the text alternates between dwelling and abrupt shifts or progresses in a more regularized way. Affective prevalence measures the distribution of emotional states, especially whether high-arousal states such as surprise, curiosity, and conflict are suppressed. Linguistic diversity captures stylistic range and cross-domain differentiation. The authors are careful about the scope of inference: the design provides stage-wise geometric evidence about where post-trained checkpoints move in distributional space, but it does not identify which exact data mixture or reward component caused the movement.a:T656,The key experimental asset is the four-checkpoint OLMo 32B path: Base, SFT, DPO, and RLVR. Because they share the same pretrained backbone, observed differences can be attributed much more cleanly to post-training. The main findings are threefold. First, variation contracts monotonically across all three measured dimensions as post-training progresses. Thematic progression becomes more uniform, with within-story topic-jump variance falling below human levels by RLVR. Affectively, high-arousal states are reduced while neutral affect becomes more prevalent. Stylistically, outputs collapse toward a narrower region of language space. Second, this contraction is directionally stable across all three human corpora but differs in magnitude. The New Yorker is compressed the most, because its human baseline is farther from the post-trained model’s default continuation regime. StoryStar and TMAS show smaller gaps, not because the model is especially good at those domains, but because those domains already sit closer to the post-training attractor. Third, post-trained endpoints converge strongly across corpora. One of the clearest numbers reported is that cross-domain style divergence drops by more than 90% from the human level to RLVR. That suggests post-training is not learning domain-sensitive continuation behavior; it is imposing a common writing mode.

The paper’s contribution is therefore diagnostic rather than benchmark-driven. It does not offer a better fiction model, but it makes visible how the standard SFT→DPO→RLVR pipeline can progressively trade away variation in open-ended generation.b:T4d1,For a skim, start with the Introduction because the definition of narrative flattening is unusually clear and sets up the rest of the paper well.
