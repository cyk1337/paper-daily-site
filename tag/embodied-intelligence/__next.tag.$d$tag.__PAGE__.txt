9:T87f,The method has three main pieces. The first is a task-unified single-stream diffusion transformer. Text conditions, image conditions, and visual latent patches are projected into the same hidden space and concatenated into one token sequence. T2I, T2V, and I2V are all handled in one framework, with image generation treated as the special case of a one-frame video. To separate condition tokens from visual tokens without introducing task-specific branches, the model uses 3D MM-RoPE with non-overlapping temporal coordinate ranges. The motivation for the single-stream design is practical and clear: better parameter reuse, denser cross-modal interaction at every layer, larger and cleaner GEMMs, and less split/concat overhead than dual-stream architectures, especially under distributed sequence-parallel training.

The second piece is sparse MoE scaling. The provided text does not include the full routing details, but the intent is clear: MoE is used to scale spatiotemporal modeling capacity without paying dense-compute cost everywhere. This mirrors the logic of MoE in LLMs, but the video setting is harder because routing has to coexist with spatial, temporal, and multimodal structure. Stability, load balancing, and temporal consistency are likely more delicate here. Choosing a single-stream backbone before adding MoE is a sensible systems decision, because it avoids compounding communication and partitioning complexity that a dual-stream design would introduce.

The third piece is data and training signal design. The model is trained on a robot-augmented corpus that mixes internet-scale videos with robot manipulation, navigation, and egocentric data. Importantly, the paper frames this as a profiling, filtering, and rebalancing problem rather than naive concatenation. On top of that, the training objective includes a multi-dimensional reward system that extends beyond perceptual quality and text alignment to physical plausibility and task-level success.
