9:T51c,First, a unified perspective is derived: for each prompt, K sampled responses form a finite-dimensional response simplex. All mainstream group-based policy gradient methods implicitly construct a reward-weighted softmax target distribution on this simplex, with target sharpness determined by the advantage normalization scheme. Subsequent policy gradient updates are merely first-order approximations of reverse KL projection toward this implicit target.
Based on this insight, LPO makes the projection process explicit: 1) It constrains the proximal RL objective to the set of sampled responses to obtain a closed-form target distribution, with a temperature parameter to control target sharpness, eliminating the need for empirical advantage normalization tuning. 2) It projects the policy onto the target via divergence minimization on the response simplex, decoupling the two steps of target construction and projection, and natively supports different divergence choices (the paper implements both forward KL and reverse KL variants).
