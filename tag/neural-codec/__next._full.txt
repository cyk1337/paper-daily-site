18:Ta3b,GEPARD 是一个 decoder-only 自回归 transformer，输入文本，输出神经 codec 的离散音频 token，不做文本生成，只预测音频序列和结束标记。骨干基于 Qwen3.5 风格结构，但做了关键裁剪：保留 14 层、hidden size 1024、8 个 attention heads，约 500M 参数；加上音频接口和 voice cloning 后总参数约 555.7M。作者强调 backbone 必须保持“stock”——也就是标准 full-attention transformer，不在自回归循环中插入自定义算子，以保证兼容标准 vLLM 和 FlashAttention-2。

一个重要细节是，他们移除了原始 Qwen3.5 中的 LinearBlock，只保留 full attention。文本 embedding 和 tokenizer 继承自预训练 Qwen，其余 backbone 基本从头训练，因为结构变化后旧权重不再兼容。作者给出的理由主要是经验性的：在相同条件下，纯 full-attention 版本听感更好，同时更适合 vLLM。这个判断还不算严格验证，但符合系统目标。

音频侧使用 NVIDIA NanoCodec，22kHz、21.5 fps、1.89 kbps，把语音离散成 codec token 序列。模型的核心设计原则是：所有非标准模态变换都放到 prefill 或离线阶段。具体包括三点。

第一，zero-shot voice cloning 不在解码环中做动态 cross-attention，而是先用冻结的音频压缩器提取说话人表示，形成 K=8 个 speaker prefix tokens，作为输入前缀一次性送入 backbone。这样 speaker conditioning 成为普通 prefix conditioning，而不是逐步参与解码的额外模块。

第二，针对超短文本生成时常见的 runaway 或 skip 问题，作者提出“short register”分析，把它视为序列过短导致的分布异常和停止行为失衡。他们用文本重复等 augmentation 在 prefill 阶段补偿，而不是在推理时加入动态规则。文中还提到用 entropy 和 stop-probability probe 做诊断，这部分更像训练动态与失败模式分析。

第三，CFG 不在 serving 时做双路前向，而是先离线生成带/不带 CFG 的配对样本，再用 DPO 把 CFG 带来的偏好蒸馏到单路模型，且使用长度与质量归一化的 reward scaling。这个点对基座研究者最有意思，因为它体现的是“把昂贵推理策略蒸馏进参数”的思路，和 speculative decoding 之外的另一类 serving-aware training 很接近。

整体上，GEPARD 不是在 codec 结构或语音 tokenization 上做新算法，而是在标准 LM 骨干、codec 接口、prefix conditioning、offline preference distillation 之间做了一个尽量不破坏 serving 栈的组合设计。19:Ta95,GEPARD is a decoder-only autoregressive transformer that takes text as input and emits discrete neural-codec audio tokens. It does not generate text; it only predicts audio tokens and an end-of-speech signal. The backbone is Qwen3.5-inspired but simplified: 14 blocks, hidden size 1024, 8 attention heads, about 500M parameters; the full model including audio interface and voice cloning is about 555.7M parameters. A central design constraint is that the backbone must remain a stock full-attention transformer, with no custom operations inside the autoregressive loop, so that standard vLLM and FlashAttention-2 can be used directly.

A notable architectural choice is the removal of Qwen3.5’s LinearBlock layers, leaving only full attention. The text embeddings and tokenizer are inherited from pretrained Qwen, while the rest of the backbone is effectively retrained because the structural change makes the original weights incompatible. The justification is empirical and system-driven: the pure full-attention version sounded better in informal comparisons and is easier to serve with vLLM.

On the audio side, the model uses NVIDIA NanoCodec at 22 kHz, 21.5 frames per second, and 1.89 kbps, turning speech into discrete codec tokens. The overarching principle is to move all non-standard modal computation out of the decode loop.

First, zero-shot voice cloning is implemented as prefix conditioning. A frozen audio compressor extracts a speaker representation once during prefill, producing K=8 speaker tokens that are prepended to the input. This avoids dynamic cross-attention to speaker features during decoding.

Second, the paper studies a failure mode for very short utterances, called the “short register” problem, where AR TTS models may loop indefinitely or skip words. Instead of adding runtime control logic, the authors address it through prefill-side text augmentation, such as text repetition, and analyze it using entropy and stop-probability probes.

Third, classifier-free guidance is removed from serving. Rather than running conditional and unconditional passes at every generation step, the model uses offline paired generations and DPO to distill CFG behavior into a single-pass model, with reward scaling normalized by length and quality. This is arguably the most transferable idea for foundation-model researchers: expensive inference-time behavior is converted into a training-time preference signal and baked into the weights.

Overall, the paper is not proposing a new codec or a new speech tokenization method. It is a serving-aware integration of a standard LM backbone, discrete audio tokens, prefix-based speaker conditioning, and offline preference distillation.1a:T840,The evaluation is presented in two stages. In an early concept-validation setup on a rented RTX 5090, a single-stream vLLM run achieved an RTF of about 0.040 and a TTFA of about 0.032 seconds, roughly 25× faster than real time. This serves as a sanity check that the vLLM-native design is viable. In the more realistic production-style setup, using server-class GPUs and a full SSE streaming harness, the end-to-end system including backbone and neural codec reaches about 0.067 RTF and about 0.046 seconds TTFA/TTFB in single-thread mode. Aggregate throughput scales nearly linearly up to 256 concurrent streams, reaching around 204× real-time throughput. The reported practical operating range is 64–128 streams per GPU, where each stream still maintains interactive performance with RTF below 0.75.

The model size is about 500M for the backbone and 555.7M overall. The audio tokenizer is NanoCodec at 21.5 fps and 1.89 kbps. The authors explicitly state that the model is not aiming for top-tier speech-quality metrics; on Seed-TTS-eval it sits in the middle range. That is important for interpreting the paper correctly: the main result is not absolute MOS or intelligibility, but real-time and concurrent serving within a standard LLM stack.

The paper also analyzes two interesting phenomena. One is the short-utterance failure mode, where 1–2 word inputs can trigger runaway generation or skipped words; entropy and stop-probability probes are used for diagnosis. The other is representation leakage in voice cloning: speaker similarity on unseen voices is limited, and the authors attribute part of this to leakage in quantized representations rather than treating it as a generic quality issue. WavLM speaker embeddings are used for evaluation.
