c:T4f4,The study uses matched model pairs to separate base-model identity from reasoning-style training: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B versus Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B versus Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The Instruct models are evaluated in both direct-answer and CoT prompting modes, and DeepSeek-R1 671B is used as a scale anchor on MMLU. The datasets are MMLU with 1000 questions, ARC-Challenge with 496 questions, GPQA with 198 questions, plus a 200-question MMLU subset for the API-scale model. Each item is converted to a four-option MCQ, and each question is evaluated under four cyclic shifts of the answer options. The Position Bias Score measures how far the model’s empirical answer distribution over absolute positions deviates from the uniform distribution. The paper also defines CCP, the normalized prefix location where the model first states the final answer and keeps it thereafter. Two design choices are important: partial correlations are computed while controlling for accuracy, and a truncation intervention resumes generation from points around CCP at offsets -0.15, -0.05, +0.05, and +0.15. This allows the authors to test whether later portions of the reasoning trace causally increase shifts toward position-preferred answers.d:T505,The empirical pattern is consistent. In 12 of 13 reasoning-mode configurations, per-question trajectory length has a positive partial correlation with PBS after controlling for accuracy, with ρ ranging from 0.11 to 0.41 and p < 0.05. When questions are grouped into length quartiles, all 12 open-weight reasoning configurations show monotonically increasing PBS from the shortest to the longest quartile. The truncation probe strengthens the causal interpretation: for R1-Qwen-7B, later resumptions increasingly shift answers toward position-preferred options, rising from 16% to 32% across absolute-position buckets. The paper also separates direct-answer bias from length-driven reasoning bias. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in direct mode has very high PBS, 0.40 on MMLU, 0.26 on ARC, and 0.61 on GPQA, but this baseline bias is largely uncorrelated with trajectory length. CoT reduces that baseline bias for Llama while introducing a length-accumulated bias. Qwen starts with a milder direct-mode baseline, so CoT does not reduce much; the R1-style longer reasoning then increases the length-driven component. - Metric: Judge Sensitivity Score (JSS), the fraction of paraphrase pairs where the judge outputs exactly the same decision. Companion reporting includes flip rate (1−JSS), Cohen’s κ for chance correction, and bootstrap 95% confidence intervals.
- Benchmark: 494 paraphrase pairs across four judging dimensions (factuality, coherence, relevance, preference). Paraphrase equivalence is validated by an independent classifier; failed pairs are disclosed and removed.
