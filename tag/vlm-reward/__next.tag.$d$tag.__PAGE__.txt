9:T736,The method has three core pieces. First, a task-agnostic action-conditioned video world model. The implementation is built on WAN 2.1, but the important design choice is the training constraint: the world model is trained only on broad, target-task-independent interaction data such as play data or non-overlapping benchmark tasks. The goal is to learn transferable physical dynamics rather than task templates.

Second, zero-shot reward from a frozen VLM. Instead of training a reward model for each task, the method feeds imagined rollout videos and the language instruction into Qwen3-VL and asks for binary success judgments. This removes the need for task-specific reward supervision. The trade-off is clear: less reward shaping and less task specialization, in exchange for much better reuse across tasks.

Third, policy optimization inside the frozen world model. The policy backbone is OpenVLA-OFT. It is first lightly supervised with a few demonstrations to bind the policy to the new task semantics, then optimized with GRPO entirely in imagination. Because unseen-task rollouts from a generative world model can produce hallucinated successes, the paper adds a dual-noise verification mechanism: generate rollouts under different diffusion noise seeds from the same initial condition, and accept the reward only when the VLM judgment is consistent across them. This is a targeted defense against reward hacking induced by world-model hallucination. A success that appears only under one noise realization is treated as unreliable.

The novelty is therefore mostly in the system design and the constraints, not in inventing a new VLA, VLM, or video diffusion model. RAW-Dream turns existing components into a strictly task-agnostic post-training loop and adds the minimum extra machinery needed to make that loop stable on unseen tasks.a:T62a,The evaluation covers both simulation and real robots. In simulation, the world model is trained only on LIBERO-90 data, while four held-out suites—Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long—are used as downstream tasks that are completely unseen during world-model training. This is a stronger test than the usual low-data adaptation setting because the simulator has zero exposure to the target tasks. RAW-Dream improves over a 1-shot SFT baseline by +8.9% and outperforms online RL despite the latter using 50x more real-world data. That comparison is important: the method is not merely data-efficient at the cost of weak performance; it is competitive in a regime where real interaction is expensive.

On real robots, the world model is pretrained only on diverse, uncurated play data, again without target-task rollouts. Each new task starts from a 3-shot SFT policy and is then refined through imagined RL. The reported gain is +21.7% absolute success rate over the 3-shot SFT baseline. For robotic manipulation, that is a large enough margin to show that the imagined training signal is doing real work rather than producing simulator-only improvements.

The most important ablations appear to be: whether a task-agnostic world model can support unseen-task adaptation at all, whether zero-shot VLM reward is reliable enough to drive RL, and whether dual-noise verification reduces hallucinated success and false-positive reward hacking.
