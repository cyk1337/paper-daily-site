17:T7da,The technical route has three main pieces. First, the paper reformulates attention approximation in terms of softmax KDE. This is natural because attention weights are normalized exponential kernels between queries and keys. Once written this way, the problem can be attacked using tools from coresets, polynomial approximation, high-dimensional geometry, and communication complexity.

Second, the analysis is split by regime. The table of contents makes clear that the authors treat a high-temperature regime and a low-temperature regime separately. Intuitively, when temperature is high, softmax is smoother and more amenable to low-degree approximation; when temperature is low, the kernel becomes sharper, local geometry matters more, and compression becomes harder. The paper therefore avoids a single coarse statement and instead derives regime-specific upper and lower bounds.

Third, the upper and lower bounds use different toolchains. On the upper-bound side, the paper employs multivariate polynomial methods, Hermite polynomials, KDE coresets, and a merge-and-reduce style conversion from offline compression to streaming compression. In the low-temperature regime, the contents indicate a more delicate pipeline: partition the dataset into pseudo-random and low-radius components, recenter and rescale them, build coresets for each part, then turn the offline compressor into a streaming algorithm. This suggests that the main technical novelty lies in decomposing the geometry of the instance before compressing it.

On the lower-bound side, the paper uses information-theoretic and communication-complexity arguments to show that some memory requirements are inherent rather than artifacts of loose analysis. The final section transfers the softmax-KDE results back to attention, yielding formal bounds for streaming attention itself.

Relative to prior work by Haris et al. and Kochetkova et al., the contribution here is not a new framework but a tighter characterization of the same problem.18:T424,This is a theory paper, so there are no standard model-training experiments or benchmark tables. The main outputs are theorems: upper and lower bounds in different regimes, and the extent to which the gap between them is reduced. From the structure, Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.2 appear to correspond to the high-temperature and low-temperature settings, and Section 7 translates the softmax-KDE statements into attention guarantees.

If one reads the theorems as the equivalent of empirical results, the key quantities to inspect are the memory complexity as a function of approximation error epsilon, ambient dimension, and the temperature or radius parameter r, together with whether upper and lower bounds match up to logarithmic or lower-order factors. The provided excerpt contains the table of contents rather than the theorem statements, so it would be unsafe to invent exact formulas. What is clear is that the paper sharpens the theory by treating the two regimes separately and by using a finer geometric decomposition in the upper-bound construction.19:T427,Do not start from the proofs.
