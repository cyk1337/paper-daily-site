18:T628,The method is Distribution-aware Language Neuron selection (DLN). For each MLP neuron in an autoregressive Transformer with GLU feed-forward blocks, the authors collect continuous activation values for each language instead of reducing them to a binary active/inactive statistic. They then estimate per-language activation distributions and compute pairwise overlap coefficients between languages. Small overlap means the neuron separates those languages well. The resulting pairwise structure is clustered to recover language groupings.

This leads to two neuron types. Single-language neurons (SLNs) are neurons whose activation distribution for one language is separated from the rest. Multi-language neurons (MLNs) are neurons that support a cluster of languages that are similar to each other but separated from other languages. This is a broader and more faithful definition than entropy over positive activation rates, because it captures cases where all languages are mostly positive but occupy different regions, and cases where the informative regime is negative.

A useful detail is the intervention site. The paper evaluates both the usual gate activation site and the gate-up product site, the latter being closer to the effective contribution written back to the residual stream in GLU blocks. Baselines based on activation probability degrade substantially there, while DLN remains selective. The main innovation is therefore not a heavy model component, but a better neuron selection criterion built on continuous distributions and pairwise language relations.19:T4dc,Main experiments are conducted on Llama-3.1-8B and SmolLM3-3B-Base using two held-out corpora, with three additional models in the appendix. The key evaluation is causal ablation rather than correlation-style probing: remove selected neurons and measure target-language damage versus collateral damage on other languages.

For SLNs, DLN yields up to 4.9x higher on-target language damage per neuron while preserving off-target performance better than activation-probability baselines. This suggests the selected neurons are more causally specific rather than merely important in a generic sense. For MLNs, DLN uncovers shared structures such as a Chinese–Japanese coalition, producing 7.12x greater within-cluster damage while using 2.1x fewer neurons.

Two analyses stand out. First, DLN finds English SLNs in the negative activation regime that are invisible to positive-rate metrics but still act as functional triggers for language control. Second, DLN remains effective at the gate-up product site, where baseline methods lose much of their selectivity.
