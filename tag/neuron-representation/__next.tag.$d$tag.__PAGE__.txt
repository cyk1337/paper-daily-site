9:T4a9,The method starts by making neurons comparable across independently trained models. The authors focus on intermediate MLP activations in Transformer blocks and treat each layer-channel coordinate as a neuron. They run the same inputs through two models, collect token-wise activations, align activations by token positions for language models and by a common patch grid for vision models, and compute pairwise activation similarity between neurons. Reliable high-similarity pairs are retained as Rosetta Neurons. The second step is a scaling analysis: the size of the Rosetta population is modeled as a function of total neuron count, yielding a sublinear power law. The third step is a phenomenological explanation based on limited neuron capacity and superposition. As models scale, some stable and data-distribution-level features become cleanly represented by individual neurons and are therefore likely to recur across runs. But the broader pool of newly represented features grows faster and is often encoded in model-specific superposition patterns. This explains the observed polarization: Rosetta Neurons become more selective, while many non-Rosetta neurons remain more polysemantic.a:T45c,The experiments cover both language and vision models. The language-model sweep reaches 30B parameters, with the smallest models around 80M parameters; the vision-model sweep reaches 5B parameters. The central empirical finding is a sublinear power-law relationship between the number of Rosetta Neurons and total neuron count: the absolute count grows with scale, while the fraction decreases. Selectivity analyses show a widening separation between Rosetta and non-Rosetta populations as scale increases, with Rosetta Neurons moving toward cleaner, more selective responses. Domain analyses show a shift toward structured domains, especially code and mathematics. The functional test uses Rosetta activations to filter domain-specific code data from a mixed corpus; continued pretraining on the selected data matches the performance of training on ground-truth domain data closely enough to support the claim that these neurons carry usable data-selection signal.
