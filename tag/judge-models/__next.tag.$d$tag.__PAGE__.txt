9:T5d2,The paper performs a controlled empirical comparison of six evaluators: HarmBench, JailbreakBench, JailbreakRadar, StrongReject, JADES, and JailMeter. These represent several distinct evaluation paradigms: a dedicated classifier, generic LLM-as-a-judge methods, rubric-based structured judging, decomposition of harmful requests into weighted sub-questions, and evidence-extraction-based evaluation.

The most important methodological choice is variable control. For evaluators that rely on a general-purpose LLM judge, the authors use the same backbone model across methods, so differences can be attributed to evaluation design rather than judge capability. They then evaluate all six methods on two human-labeled datasets, JailbreakQR and JailMeter-Eva, using human annotations as the reference. The analysis covers overall agreement, error types, and consistency across attack families.

A key message from the full paper is that these evaluators are not merely noisy approximations of one common target. They operationalize different notions of jailbreak success. Some are closer to harmful-content detection, others to task fulfillment, and some are more sensitive to superficial compliance or prompt artifacts. JADES gains from decomposing the request before scoring, which reduces interference from irrelevant surface cues. StrongReject benefits from rubric-based structure. HarmBench remains competitive as a dedicated classifier, but its notion of success is more fixed by design.a:T414,The experiments use two human-labeled datasets and compare each evaluator directly against human judgments. The headline result is that JADES performs best overall, with HarmBench and StrongReject also showing strong and relatively stable performance. Other evaluators exhibit larger deviations or more dataset-dependent behavior.

The paper is especially useful because it goes beyond aggregate scores. Its error analysis shows that disagreements are systematic rather than random. Some evaluators over-credit responses that sound harmful but do not actually fulfill the request. Others miss responses that are phrased mildly yet provide actionable harmful assistance. The cross-attack-family analysis also shows that some evaluators are less stable when the jailbreak style changes.

The exact numeric margins are less important than the ranking and the behavioral differences.
