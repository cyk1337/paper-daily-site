18:T47d,The work combines controlled experiments, a minimal theory model, and mechanism checks. The empirical starting point is simple: train on compositional tasks where atomic skills are sampled either from a power-law distribution or a uniform distribution, then evaluate under a uniform test distribution. Power-law training still performs better, so the gain is not explained by matching the test distribution. The theoretical core is a k-multiplicative composition task, designed to isolate the difficulty of learning products or compositions of k latent skills. Under uniform sampling, the relevant learning signal remains highly symmetric and high-order; the paper proves that learning requires d^{Ω(k)} samples or runtime. Under power-law sampling, frequency asymmetry breaks this symmetry. Gradient descent can first fit high-frequency components, and this staged progress turns the remaining problem into a much easier one, with d^{O(1)} sample and runtime requirements. Power-law training consistently beats uniform training.
