9:T92e,方法结构很清楚：冻结 actor π_actor，训练 coach π_θ。先让 actor 在输入 x 上生成初始轨迹 y；coach 读取 (x, y)，输出一段自然语言经验 e；然后把 e 作为额外条件，再让同一个 actor 在目标样本 z 上作答，并用 verifier 判断 guided response 是否正确，把这个结果作为 coach 的奖励。关键点有三层。

第一层是奖励设计。same-instance reward 令 z=x，鼓励 coach 做错误诊断、保留有用中间结果、删掉误导性推理；cross-instance reward 则让 z 来自与 x 不同但同任务分布的样本集合 P，迫使 coach 提炼更抽象的任务规则，而不是只写针对单题的补丁。这个区分很重要，因为它把“经验知识”从 case-specific critique 和 transferable strategy 两种形态上拆开了。

第二层是优化方式。作者不是对单个 e 做 REINFORCE，而是对每个源轨迹采样多个候选经验片段，经过 actor rollout 得到组内相对优劣，再用 GRPO 训练 coach。这样做的原因很直接：经验文本质量很难做 token-level 监督，且 reward 只能通过 actor 的最终表现间接观测；组相对优化比单样本高方差策略梯度更稳。

第三层是多轮经验更新。初始 e^(0)=∅，第 k 轮 coach 根据 actor 最新轨迹和已有经验生成更新后的 e^(k)，再让 actor 继续解题。这里不是简单把历史越堆越长，而是“替换式更新”经验表示，让 coach 有机会删除前一轮错误结论、保留已验证信息、补充新发现。这个设计比 naive memory accumulation 更合理，因为原始轨迹里错误会累积，替换式摘要更接近 belief state 更新。

和 self-refinement 的区别在于：self-refinement 直接把原模型的长轨迹或自我批评再喂回原模型，提炼能力仍由 actor 自己承担；L2C 把这件事外包给专门训练过的 coach。和直接 RL 微调 actor 的区别在于：L2C 不改 actor 参数，因此不会轻易破坏 actor 的通用能力，也更适合服务多个冻结 actor 或部署后持续收集经验的场景。论文里还分析了 coach 的 actor-specific 性质：同一个 coach 对不同 actor 的帮助并不完全可互换，说明它学到的不是抽象“正确 critique”，而是“什么样的提示最能让这个 actor 改正”。a:T8d7,The setup is simple and well scoped. A frozen actor π_actor first produces a trajectory y on input x. A trainable coach π_θ reads (x, y) and outputs a natural-language experience snippet e. The same frozen actor is then prompted with e on a target instance z, and a deterministic verifier scores whether the guided response is correct. That score becomes the reward for the coach.

There are three important design choices.

First, reward variants. With same-instance reward, z=x, so the coach is encouraged to diagnose what went wrong on that specific example and preserve useful intermediate results. With cross-instance reward, z is sampled from a disjoint set of related instances, forcing the coach to extract reusable structure such as task rules or general strategies. This distinction is central: it separates local error correction from transferable experience.

Second, optimization. For each source trajectory, the coach samples multiple candidate experience snippets, each is evaluated through actor rollouts, and the coach is trained with GRPO using relative outcomes within the group. This makes sense because there is no token-level supervision for “good experience,” and the reward is only observable through the actor’s downstream behavior.

Third, iterative experience updating. Starting from empty experience, the coach repeatedly rewrites the current experience after each new actor attempt. The updated experience replaces the previous one rather than simply appending more history. That matters because raw trajectories accumulate errors and irrelevant details; replacement makes the experience representation closer to a belief-state update than to naive memory growth.

Compared with self-refinement, the key difference is division of labor. In self-refinement, the actor must also do the extraction and filtering of its own trajectory. In L2C, that filtering is delegated to a separately trained model. Compared with RL-tuning the actor, L2C preserves the actor’s parameters and broad capabilities, which is attractive for frozen-model deployment settings. The paper also argues that the learned coach is actor-specific: it learns not just what is correct, but what kind of guidance is most effective for that particular actor.b:T688,The experiments cover mathematical reasoning and interactive text games, which is a sensible pairing: math tests error diagnosis and reuse of intermediate results, while text games test whether the coach can extract latent environment rules from long, noisy interaction histories. Across tasks and model sizes, L2C outperforms the frozen actor, self-refinement, and an untrained coach.

Two findings stand out. First, gains are larger in text games. That is consistent with the method’s premise: when trajectories contain hidden rules and long-horizon evidence, a trained coach has more room to compress useful structure and discard noise. The cross-instance reward is especially helpful when different instances share underlying rules, because it pressures the coach to learn reusable guidance rather than case-specific patches.

Second, iterative coaching behaves like a form of test-time scaling. Performance keeps improving over more iterations with a trained coach, while an untrained coach quickly saturates. On math tasks, allocating extra compute to coach-guided iterations beats simply doubling the actor’s decoding budget. That is an important systems-level result: not all extra inference compute is equally useful, and structured experience extraction can dominate brute-force decoding.

The paper also includes analyses of reward variants, out-of-distribution transfer, and actor-specific transfer.
