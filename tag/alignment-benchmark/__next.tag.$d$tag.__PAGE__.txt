a:T454,The methodology has three notable layers. First, the attack space is factorized into near-atomic jailbreak primitives rather than a small set of famous prompts. Second, the paper defines an evaluation funnel: construct attacker goals across five high-risk domains, generate jailbreak variants, filter candidates through staged screening, and then label outcomes with evaluator validation against human annotation. Third, the paper reports not only counts of universal jailbreaks but also the estimated dollar cost of finding one. When no successful universal jailbreak is observed, it reports a statistical lower bound rather than treating the result as absolute zero. 1) Common notation: For each prompt x, a finite candidate set Y(x)={y1..yK}. The policy assigns candidate scores s_i (implemented as average token log-prob of the response) and forms a temperature-τ decision distribution p=softmax(s/τ). Pairwise data is the special case K=2.
2) DPO baseline: Uses the standard DPO loss for (y^+, y^-) relative to a reference policy π_ref with sharpness β, directly optimizing the chosen-over-rejected relation.
3) DDO-RM core update: Introduces reward-model scores r_i and uses centered rewards \tilde r_i = r_i - E_{j~p}[r_j], i.e., subtracting the policy-weighted baseline so only relative advantage remains.
4) One-step reward-guided redistribution (mirror-step view): Update scores additively s'_i = s_i + η \tilde r_i (the paper shows this closed form arises from the centered-reward gradient and cancels p_i/τ factors), define a target distribution q=softmax(s'/τ), then train the policy to match q via cross-entropy CE(q, p_θ).
5) Interpretation: DDO-RM learns from a soft target distribution (temperature-controlled, even for K=2) rather than a direct binary contrastive objective, effectively performing a reward-guided probability-mass transfer among candidates.

- DPO: optimize a pairwise preference objective.
