1a:T926,方法上，作者刻意把 extraction pipeline 设计得很“薄”，目的是减少工程技巧对结论的污染。整体流程分三步。第一步，固定 target model M，在某个 domain 的 train split 上执行任务，收集 experience pool，里面同时保留 task、trajectory 和 outcome，且包含成功与失败样本。第二步，固定 extractor E，对每条 trajectory 独立抽取 pattern set，允许从单条轨迹中提取多个 success / failure patterns；随后用树形归并的 reduction 方式，把局部 pattern 集合逐层合并成一个 domain-level skill。这里借用了 Trace2Skill 的高层两阶段结构，但去掉了子代理编排、冲突消解、skill deepening 等复杂机制，尽量把差异留给 extractor 本身。第三步，把得到的 skill 提供给原 target，在 held-out test split 上评估相对 no-skill baseline 的变化。

文章的关键设计点有几个。其一，skill schema 和 budget 是固定的，避免不同 extractor 通过输出长度或格式自由度取巧。其二，target 与 extractor 可以不同，从而能研究“谁更会总结经验”和“谁更会消费经验”是两件不同的事。其三，作者提出两个指标：EE 衡量固定 extractor 面对不同 target 时，能否稳定产出有帮助的 skill；TE 衡量固定 target 面对来自不同 extractor、但都基于自己经验提炼出的 skill 时，能否从中获益。这个拆分很像把数据蒸馏问题分成 teacher quality 与 student receptivity。其四，分析覆盖三阶段：经验生成阶段看轨迹质量、成功率、模式重复度与可抽象性；技能抽取阶段看 success/failure pattern 的组织方式、抽象层级、冗余与噪声；技能消费阶段看 target 是否真正读取、遵循并在新任务中调用 skill，而不是把 skill 当作无效上下文。

从引言和框架描述看，作者还做了一个 drop-in 改进：把经验阶段和抽取阶段得到的经验性发现，反过来用于改进 extraction 本身。虽然给出的全文片段没有完整展开 Section 6 的实现细节，但可以判断其思路不是换一个全新架构，而是把“哪些轨迹更值得抽、哪些模式更该保留、失败知识如何编码”这些分析结论，转成可直接插入原 extraction pipeline 的策略。1b:T4bf,Its relevance to pretraining is indirect but real. The paper examines an increasingly important alternative to weight updates: accumulating capability through externalized artifacts distilled from interaction data. That sits close to continual pretraining, trajectory distillation, memory-augmented systems, and test-time adaptation. The main contribution is analytical rather than architectural. First, it separates extractor-side quality from target-side receptivity through two metrics, Extraction Efficacy (EE) and Target Evolvability (TE). That is a useful decomposition, similar in spirit to separating teacher quality from student learnability. Second, it highlights that failed trajectories are not just noise; they can encode reusable negative knowledge, which matters for any pipeline that distills behavior traces into training signals. Third, it shows that skill utility is conditional: it depends on whether the experience pool contains recurring abstractions, whether the extractor can compress them into transferable procedures, and whether the target can actually consume those procedures at inference time. This is best read as a strong measurement-and-analysis paper, not as a new dominant method.1c:T879,The technical design is intentionally minimal. The authors want the extractor model, not pipeline scaffolding, to explain most of the variance. The lifecycle is instantiated as follows. A target model M executes tasks from a domain-specific training split and produces an experience pool containing task, trajectory, and outcome tuples, with both successful and failed runs retained. An extractor model E then processes each trajectory independently and produces a pattern set with multiple success and failure patterns per trajectory. These local pattern sets are merged through a tree-structured reduction procedure into a single domain-level skill under a fixed schema and budget. That skill is fed back to the same target model M, which is then evaluated on a held-out test split; the change relative to a no-skill baseline is used as the utility signal.

Several design choices matter. The schema and budget are fixed, so extractors cannot win by simply emitting longer or differently formatted artifacts. The extractor and target can differ, which allows the study to disentangle who is good at summarizing experience from who is good at using summarized experience. The two proposed metrics formalize this split: EE measures how reliably a fixed extractor produces helpful skills across targets, while TE measures how much a fixed target benefits from skills extracted by different extractors from its own experience. The analysis then probes all three stages: whether the experience pool has enough quality and recurring structure, whether extraction preserves the right abstractions and failure modes, and whether the target actually reads and operationalizes the skill rather than treating it as extra context.

The paper also includes a drop-in improvement to skill extraction derived from the empirical analysis. The provided text does not expose the full Section 6 details, but the framing makes clear that the improvement is not a wholesale new architecture. It is an intervention informed by lifecycle findings, likely around which trajectories to prioritize, how to encode failure knowledge, and how to preserve transferable abstractions during merging.1d:T6bc,The study spans five domains: embodied planning, productivity software, software engineering, web search, and tool calling. It systematically varies both the extractor and the target, which is much more informative than evaluating a single agent stack on one benchmark. Utility is measured as the performance delta on held-out tasks relative to a no-skill baseline.

The experimental program is organized around three questions. For RQ1, the paper tests whether model-generated domain-level skills provide reliable gains across domains, targets, and extractors, rather than assuming that skills are broadly useful. For RQ2, it analyzes the drivers of utility at each lifecycle stage: properties of the experience pool such as success/failure composition and pattern recurrence; extraction properties such as abstraction quality, redundancy, and the handling of success versus failure patterns; and consumption properties such as whether the target actually follows the skill on new tasks. For RQ3, it turns these findings into a drop-in extraction improvement and evaluates whether that improves robustness.

The full numerical tables are not available in the provided excerpt, so it would be unsafe to invent exact model sizes, data counts, or gain margins. What can be stated confidently is that the paper runs a controlled matrix over extractor-target-domain combinations under a unified low-scaffolding extraction framework, and that EE/TE are used to expose differences that average task scores would hide. For exact numbers, the main tables in Sections 4–6 and the appendix are necessary, especially the domain statistics, budget settings, and ablations over success/failure patterns and experience-pool composition.1e:T5a6,Start with the evaluation framework. Section 3 defines the objects that matter: target, extractor, experience pool, per-trajectory pattern extraction, tree reduction, and the EE/TE metrics. Without that setup, the rest can look like a standard benchmark comparison, which it is not.

Then read Section 4 for the main empirical picture. The key question is not which model is best, but whether skill utility is stable across domains and model pairings, how large the variance is, and how often negative transfer appears.

Section 5 is the most informative part. In 5.1, focus on what kinds of experience pools lead to useful skills and whether failed trajectories add distinct value. In 5.2, look at the extraction bottleneck: abstraction granularity, merging behavior, and the relative role of success and failure patterns. In 5.3, examine skill consumption: does the target genuinely use the skill, or does the skill become inert context? This section is especially relevant if you care about the interface between pretraining, post-training, and external memory artifacts.
