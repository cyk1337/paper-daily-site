Compression efficiency is measured by the ratio between the original text-token count and the visual-token count after rendering. Context preservation is approximated with complete-text OCR: the evaluator must recover the whole source text from the rendered image, rather than answer a downstream question. This choice is deliberate. Downstream QA, retrieval, or summarization may require only sparse evidence, while natural-text OCR lets the model use lexical, syntactic, and semantic priors to correct visually ambiguous characters. Complete-text recovery is much more sensitive to missing characters, ordering errors, and local visual degradation.

The text is synthesized to remove ordinary linguistic dependencies, making it difficult for an MLLM to fill in unreadable regions from context. The same source content can be rendered with different layouts, including Fox, Omni, and Glyph-style renderings, and across different compression ratios.
