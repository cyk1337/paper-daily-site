18:T546,The method has two stages. Stage one is REL-SFT. The authors build a 29.4K Reasoning-Evidence Localization chain-of-thought dataset, where each reasoning trace is aligned with page indices and normalized bounding boxes. Training is done across seven rendering resolutions, so the model learns to localize relevant evidence under compressed global views rather than simply reading everything at high fidelity. Stage two is GRPO. At inference, the model starts from low-DPI page images and iteratively generates a reasoning segment followed by an action: either answer directly or issue a region-enhancement call with a page index and bounding box. A tool then crops the aligned region from a higher-DPI rendering of the same page and returns it as an observation. The model incorporates that observation into subsequent reasoning until it answers or exhausts the call budget. A few design choices matter here: low- and high-resolution views share the same page coordinates, which avoids alignment drift; enhancement is local rather than full-page rerendering, which keeps added visual tokens under control; and policy learning must optimize not only correctness but also when to zoom, where to zoom, and when to stop. The paper also discusses font and point-size selection, highlighting that rendering choices materially affect downstream performance.19:T446,The strongest results are on compressed long-document reasoning. On RULER v1 at 72 DPI, FocusVTC reaches 87.4 with about 2.9x input compression, compared with 57.5 for Glyph at roughly 3.0x compression. On LongBench, it slightly surpasses its text-input backbone, 56.40 versus 55.86, suggesting that visual compression does not necessarily hurt long-context understanding and may even help through page-level structure plus selective reading. On MRCR, the macro average over six length bins and 2/4/8 needles improves from 31.65 to 45.56, a gain of 13.91, indicating clear benefits in multi-round retrieval and evidence revisiting. The latency evaluation reports a 2.79x online end-to-end speedup on MRCR. On VTCBench, it obtains a 51.19 macro average across Retrieval, Reasoning, and Memory. Compression efficiency is measured by the ratio between the original text-token count and the visual-token count after rendering. Context preservation is approximated with complete-text OCR: the evaluator must recover the whole source text from the rendered image, rather than answer a downstream question. This choice is deliberate. Downstream QA, retrieval, or summarization may require only sparse evidence, while natural-text OCR lets the model use lexical, syntactic, and semantic priors to correct visually ambiguous characters. Complete-text recovery is much more sensitive to missing characters, ordering errors, and local visual degradation.

The paper also introduces ZeroSense, a benchmark of long contexts with low semantic coherence and low predictability. The text is synthesized to remove ordinary linguistic dependencies, making it difficult for an MLLM to fill in unreadable regions from context. The same source content can be rendered with different layouts, including Fox, Omni, and Glyph-style renderings, and across different compression ratios.
