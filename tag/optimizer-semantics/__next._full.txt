18:T4e3,The work defines a use as one gradient computation reading a rounded tensor. A backward-state policy assigns a value to every such use. The reference is the gradient of the computation that actually produced the loss, with rounding handled by a straight-through convention. Under this reference, each use must read the value used by the corresponding forward operation, or a substitute that gives the same gradient in expectation conditional on the realized forward computation and the incoming gradient received by that use. This conditional requirement is the crucial step: an incoming gradient may depend on the realized rounding, so two values sampled from the same rounding distribution need not be interchangeable. The attention correction term D = rowsum(dO ∘ O) is used as the main example. If dO depends on the forward rounding of O, then reusing that rounded O and drawing a fresh rounding from the same distribution can produce different training behavior. The paper also shows that one tensor can require different values for different gradients, for example in normalization and in rounded attention probabilities. The proposed per-use requirements are then checked with an executable finite-case checker for concrete operator policies.19:T4b5,The experiments are built around 390M-parameter training runs and low-precision attention backward. In the FOG 390M setting, NVIDIA Transformer Engine FP8 attention fails in all three seeds tested. Replacing only the backward with BF16 while keeping the FP8 forward removes the failure, isolating the issue to the backward policy. In paired runs with an emulated FP8 attention backward, the forward randomly rounds the attention output O. Training fails when the D term reuses the forward-rounded O, but succeeds when D reads a fresh rounding from the same distribution; this is repeated in three paired 390M runs. The two choices have the same copy accuracy, so the difference comes from the dependency between the realized rounding and dO.
