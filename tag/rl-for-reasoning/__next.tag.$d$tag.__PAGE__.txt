9:T911,The method is called IOP, short for Internalizing Outcome Supervision into Process Supervision. Its core idea is to convert failed trajectories into usable local supervision. The training pipeline has two stages.

Stage 1 gives the model a basic repair capability. The authors build a small cold-start repair dataset with 500 examples and fine-tune the same model in a repair mode. The input contains the problem x, a failed reasoning trajectory y, a reference correct trajectory a, and a repair instruction. The output is a corrected trajectory. A notable design choice is parameter sharing: the policy mode and repair mode use the same model parameters. This avoids training a separate repair model and lets repair ability co-evolve with the policy.

Stage 2 performs joint RL. The model first generates reasoning trajectories in policy mode and receives binary outcome rewards. Instead of discarding failed samples as uniformly negative, IOP first selects repairable failed trajectories, then invokes the repair mode to produce corrected versions. A repair-alignment step compares the original failed trajectory with the repaired one and extracts localized difference regions. Three mechanisms are central here:
1. Audit gating: converts repair differences into token- or span-level gating signals, so updates focus on suspicious local regions rather than the whole chain.
2. Minimum-edit repair: encourages the repaired trajectory to stay close to the original and only modify critical errors. This is important because a full rewrite would make the difference signal noisy and less useful for credit assignment.
3. Verification-based adaptive truncation: uses verification outcomes to decide where to truncate or reweight supervision, reducing contamination from downstream reasoning that was built on an earlier mistake.

The gating mechanism is decoupled from the outer RL optimizer. The paper instantiates IOP with GSPO, yielding IOP-GSPO, but claims the same idea can be plugged into GRPO, GSPO, or RLOO-style group-comparison RL algorithms. Relative to exogenous process supervision, the method does not require a separately trained PRM or human step labels. Relative to pure outcome RL, it replaces uniform sequence-level updates with internally generated local process signals derived from repair differences.a:T60f,The main empirical result is straightforward: IOP-GSPO consistently improves over vanilla GSPO by about 4.9% to 6.9% on three reasoning benchmarks, and it also outperforms methods that rely on external process supervision. The paper further reports roughly 2.3x sample efficiency, suggesting that the method reaches similar quality with fewer samples or achieves better quality under the same sampling budget.

The more interesting findings are mechanistic. First, the paper reports a positive feedback loop between policy quality and repair quality. As the policy improves, failures become more localized rather than globally broken, which makes minimum-edit repair easier. Better repair then produces more precise gating signals, which in turn improves RL updates. Second, internally generated process supervision appears to be better matched to the current policy distribution than static external supervision. A PRM or fixed process labels may lag behind the evolving policy, while IOP derives supervision directly from current failed rollouts.

There are also clear boundaries. The cold-start repair stage uses only 500 examples, which is encouraging from a cost perspective but also suggests that initial repair quality may be a bottleneck. The evaluation is centered on reasoning benchmarks; broader validation on code, tool use, or long-horizon planning is not yet shown. The method is also presented mainly in a binary-reward setting, so its marginal benefit under denser rewards or stronger verifiers remains to be quantified more carefully.b:T4d2,For a skim read, focus on the framing first. The Introduction is worth reading because the key contribution is the reformulation: reasoning RL should be viewed as converting outcome supervision into process supervision. That framing is more important than the specific GSPO instantiation.

Then read the Method section with attention to the three steps: selecting repairable failures, generating repairs, and feeding back localized signals through gating and truncation. The two ideas to watch closely are minimum-edit repair and verification-based adaptive truncation. They are the main reasons the method can improve credit assignment instead of just adding another self-correction loop.

In the experiments, prioritize three things: the main comparison against GSPO, the sample-efficiency results, and the ablations that remove repair, gating, or truncation. A practical reading question is whether repair differences truly isolate error locations or merely introduce another layer of generation noise. Another useful question is whether the shared-parameter repair mode helps because of representation reuse or because it regularizes the policy toward self-consistency.
