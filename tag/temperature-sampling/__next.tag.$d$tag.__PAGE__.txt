9:T7a5,The model is a probabilistic context-free grammar defined on a fixed hierarchical tree. Sequences have fixed length over a discrete vocabulary, and the joint distribution is induced by hierarchical rules on the tree. Unlike experiments on neural LMs, the next-token conditional at each step is available exactly through belief propagation. This lets the authors cleanly separate the underlying language distribution from the decoding procedure: first compute the exact conditional, then apply a softmax temperature rescaling, and finally sample autoregressively.

The main observable is two-point mutual information. Because the hierarchical model is not translation invariant, the mutual information depends on absolute position as well as separation. The paper therefore focuses on the mutual information between the last token and the token located r positions earlier, which probes all hierarchical scales in a simple way. Across temperatures and grammar ensembles, the authors fit the decay of I(r) and test whether there is evidence for phenomena usually associated with criticality, such as a diverging correlation length, a transition from power-law behavior to a plateau, or a clear crossover to short-range exponential decay.

A notable part of the paper is the interpretation section. The authors give a tree-based statistical-mechanics view and an approximate mapping to an Ising model on a tree. This is used to explain why, in a fixed-tree hierarchical language, one should not expect finite-temperature correlation plateaus or the usual short-range exponential cutoff. Intuitively, the tree geometry itself induces multiscale dependence, while temperature mainly changes the strength of correlations and the fitted exponent rather than creating a sharp phase boundary. In that sense, the observed power-law behavior is better understood as a geometric consequence of hierarchical generation than as evidence of temperature-driven criticality.a:T67d,The experiments are numerical and deliberately clean. The focus is on correlation statistics, not task performance.

First, across the tested temperature range and multiple grammar ensembles, the two-point mutual information I(r) remains well described by a power-law envelope. Temperature changes the overall magnitude of correlations and also changes the fitted decay exponent, but there is no obvious special temperature at which the correlation structure changes qualitatively.

Second, this qualitative behavior is robust across different rule ensembles, although the detailed temperature response depends on the support structure and heterogeneity of the grammar rules. In other words, the exact exponent-versus-temperature curve varies with the grammar family, but the broader conclusion remains stable: power-law-like decay persists and no clear critical point appears.

Third, the Ising-on-tree analogy is used to interpret the numerical findings. In this finite-depth fixed-tree setting, the paper does not observe the standard signatures one would associate with true criticality, such as a diverging correlation length or a sharp phase transition. Therefore, changes in power-law exponents under temperature variation are not sufficient evidence for temperature-tuned critical behavior.
