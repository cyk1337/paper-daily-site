19:T429,FlashMorph follows three core steps. First, a morphable model is constructed: each pretrained full-attention layer is paired with a pre-converted linear-attention branch, and layer-wise learnable gates are added to enable continuous interpolation between the two attention types. Second, during the layer selection stage, all parameters of the pretrained backbone and linear-attention branches are frozen, and only the layer-wise gates are jointly optimized on synthetic retrieval data. This joint optimization estimates the relative necessity of retaining full attention for each layer under a global hybrid configuration, instead of relying on isolated per-layer scoring. Third, the optimized gates are discretized according to a predefined full-attention layer budget to instantiate the target hybrid architecture, which is further tuned via standard distillation and long-context finetuning. Compared to prior methods, FlashMorph explicitly models inter-layer complementarity and redundancy, and requires significantly fewer training tokens for layer selection.1a:T421,The model contains a D-layer Transformer block unit plus a dedicated correction FFN. During sequential generation, the current embedding e_t is combined with the cached block output from the previous position, z_{t-1}. The correction FFN produces a modified input for the block, so the token enters the block with information from the past already injected. Two design choices make the scheme trainable. First, the correction is non-cumulative: x^(k)=e+F_theta(x^(k-1), e), rather than a residual sum of K separate corrections. Iterations are used to approach a fixed point, not to accumulate depth. Second, the correction is past-only: position t depends on z_{t-1} and e_t, so at inference time the needed state is already cached. For training, the paper avoids T-step BPTT over the sequence. It unrolls the correction process K times over all positions in parallel, with K fixed and much smaller than the sequence length. On pointer chasing, a D=1 variant trained with BPTT solves all 10 composition levels, while standard Transformers show a depth-dependent staircase pattern.
