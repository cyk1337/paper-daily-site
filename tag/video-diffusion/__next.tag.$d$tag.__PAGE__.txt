9:T7e9,The method is built around three levels of unification.

First, representation unification. The paper introduces an Everything-as-RGB scheme: depth, embodiment segmentation, and first-frame-relative 2D flow are each mapped into a three-channel image-like sequence. These are then encoded by the same frozen video autoencoder used for RGB video. The motivation is straightforward: reuse the pretrained video latent space instead of introducing modality-specific encoders and decoders. The trade-off is that non-RGB modalities are forced through an RGB-like interface, so the burden is on experiments to show that important geometric structure is preserved.

Second, architectural unification. A shared Video DiT backbone handles all modalities, with lightweight task modulation providing task-specific conditioning. The transformer blocks and decoder remain shared, and the design explicitly avoids modality-specific output heads. Compared with standard multi-task systems, this is a stronger form of parameter sharing. The upside is tighter cross-task coupling and less architectural fragmentation. The downside is a higher risk of task interference, especially because generation and perception may prefer different internal representations.

Third, objective-space unification. All tasks are trained in a common velocity prediction space, following the native v-prediction formulation of diffusion models. Instead of attaching separate regression or classification heads for perception, the model learns all modalities through the same denoising-style prediction framework. At inference time, the model supports two modes: iterative future video generation with aligned dense outputs, and single-step dense perception from an observed video without full denoising rollout.

The important point is that the paper is not merely doing multi-task learning on top of a video generator.
