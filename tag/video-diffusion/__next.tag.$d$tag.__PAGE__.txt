9:T501,WorldWeaver (W²) is a streaming multi-agent video diffusion model centered on cross-agent world state registers: a set of learnable tokens that store shared world information and individual agent states, are dynamically updated after each generated chunk, and persist across all agents and rollout steps. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) Bidirectional training, which fine-tunes a multi-player teacher on synchronized multi-agent clips with full temporal context to learn cross-agent scene structure; (2) Causal training, which converts the teacher to a causal student, adds register supervision, and continues training with the flow matching objective; (3) Self-Forcing, which rolls out the student on its own predicted frames and updated registers to expose register drift. The architecture uses Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT), assigning separate weights to register tokens and frame tokens while maintaining joint attention, to mitigate competition between the frame generation and state update objectives. Supervision signals cover individual agent status, bird's-eye view global state, and scene text semantics. Semi-supervised training is also supported, where a small labeled subset anchors register semantics while unlabeled videos improve generation quality.a:T509,The system is built on a 15B video diffusion transformer and generates 24-fps video at 540p and 720p. Instead of producing a full long video in one pass, it generates short latent chunks autoregressively. Each chunk is conditioned on camera trajectories and switchable text prompts. The central mechanism is a bounded visual context composed of a global scene anchor, compressed recent history, geometry-aligned spatial memory, and recent-frame conditioning. Temporal memory maintains short-range dynamics and frame continuity. Spatial memory stores past observations and reprojects them into the current target view when the camera revisits a region. This keeps per-chunk compute approximately constant as rollout length grows. Training has three stages: adapt a general bidirectional video prior to world-modeling data; convert it into an autoregressive generator with camera control and spatiotemporal memory; then train with corrupted histories and replay prediction residuals collected from the model’s own rollouts. For inference, the paper uses discrete autoregressive distillation with distribution-matching distillation, self-forcing++, and consistency distillation, reducing sampling from roughly 30 steps to 4 steps per chunk while keeping the control and memory stack active.b:T4a5,The training corpus contains 222,147 clips from seven sources, including internally curated MUGEN and GameVerse data. Evaluation is performed on iWorld-Bench, covering generation quality, trajectory following, and memory ability. The paper reports stronger overall performance than compared systems. The most informative ablations concern spatial memory, corrupted-history training, replay residuals, and distillation. At the objective level, consistency models are treated as forward-divergence-like, trajectory-preserving, and mode-covering, whereas DMD is treated as reverse-divergence-like and directly aligned with the student-generated distribution. At the causal training level, teacher-forcing plays the role of stable offline supervision on teacher trajectories, while self-forcing plays the role of on-policy refinement under autoregressive rollouts.

Causal-rCM combines them as teacher-forcing CM plus self-forcing DMD. In the first part, the student learns few-step causal distillation on offline causal contexts and teacher trajectories, which gives a stable initialization under correct histories. In the second part, the student is optimized on its own rollouts so that the distilled few-step sampler better matches inference-time behavior. The paper argues that this complementarity can be realized either jointly or in a staged forward-to-reverse schedule.

A useful part of the paper is its systematic treatment of initialization for self-forcing DMD. It compares several existing choices, including ODE-pair regression, diffusion-forcing-style causal adaptation, and hybrid TF/DF initialization, against TF-CM initialization. The claimed takeaway is that reverse/on-policy optimization alone is too brittle when coverage is poor, while a forward/offline consistency stage provides a much better basin for subsequent DMD refinement.

The engineering layer is also nontrivial. The authors implement teacher-forcing continuous-time consistency models, including sCM and MeanFlow variants, in an autoregressive causal-mask setting. The custom-mask FlashAttention-2 JVP kernel is the key systems piece that makes this practical.
