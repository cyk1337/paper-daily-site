9:T44b,The attack is formulated as discrete optimization over a trigger token sequence whose sole purpose is to maximize the likelihood of producing a target harmful output. Unlike standard jailbreaks (suffix/prefix attacks) that keep a harmful instruction and rely on semantic scaffolding, this setting removes all semantics: the trigger sequence is the entire input.
Methodologically, the paper intentionally uses a weak black-box baseline: a greedy random-search procedure that proposes single-token substitutions and accepts a change if it increases the objective score (described as increasing the likelihood of generating the target). No gradients, no continuous relaxation, no auxiliary LM prompt rewriting, and no prompt templates.
A key diagnostic is distributional: the discovered triggers are evaluated via perplexity/likelihood under the same model to determine whether they lie in high-probability (training-like) regions or in the extreme tail.
