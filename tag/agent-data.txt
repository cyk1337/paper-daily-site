17:T490,Deep Research Pretraining (DRP) is an offline reverse-synthesis pipeline.
- Inputs: a document corpus plus an evidence graph (citation graph or Wikipedia hyperlink graph).
- Construction: pick a citation-bearing / link-bearing passage as the target report r; recover its evidence set Er (linked/cited documents that substantively support the passage); sample graph-neighborhood alternatives that are related but not selected.
- Proxy objective: infer a research objective u whose answer is expressed by r.
- Rendering: convert (u, evidence, alternatives) into standardized search–open–write trajectories.
- Training objective (predictive navigation): conditioned on the objective and the evidence observed so far, predict what to search, which documents to open, and how to synthesize/write grounded text.
- Key trade-off: no live retrieval environment and no executed policy rollouts; candidate sets and document contents are assembled offline, prioritizing scale and controllability over perfect interaction realism.
- Instantiations: DRP-Paper (citation graphs, Related Work passages) and DRP-Web (Wikipedia hyperlinks with filtering to keep substantive links).18:T446,Setup and reported findings from the provided excerpt:
- Starting point: Qwen3-14B-Base.
- Continual pretraining: 1B tokens on DRP-Paper or DRP-Web.
- Downstream: agent SFT on controlled fractions of 13K GPT-5-generated deep-research trajectories (DR Tulu), measuring performance vs number of expert trajectories.
- Results: consistent gains over no-DRP SFT at low-data budgets on DeepResearch Bench across multiple independently sampled subsets; with 1/4 trajectories, DRP-Web exceeds a fixed full-data Base checkpoint.
- Transfer: improvements carry to ResearchQA, WebWalkerQA, SimpleQA; the 1/16-SFT gain persists on Qwen3-30B-A3B.
- RL continuation: from matched 1/16-SFT checkpoints, DRP-Web stays ahead of Base through 80 steps of ARPO-style agentic RL.
- Controls: source-matched raw-document CPT and evidence-mismatch controls suggest the benefit comes from evidence-conditioned navigation rather than domain exposure or merely imitating agent formatting.

第一部分是 systematic breadth-first exploration。与 Explorer 这类从 homepage 提任务再向外扩展的方法不同，AutoSurfer 直接把网站探索建模成一个带队列的 BFS 过程。队列里维护已发现页面及到达这些页面的 action trace，因此每个新页面都带着“它是怎么被发现的”这一因果路径。这样做有两个好处：一是覆盖更全，能逐层深入网站结构，而不是停留在首页可见功能；二是后续任务合成时可以直接利用到达路径，而不是只看孤立页面截图或 DOM 片段。作者还强调 knowledge propagation：探索过程中从先前页面抽取的网站知识会传递给后续页面，用来理解导航结构、避免重复探索、识别哪些元素属于同一功能簇。

第二部分是对静态与动态元素做差异化处理。静态功能，如菜单、导航栏、dropdown、设置项，适合系统遍历，因为它们定义了网站的功能骨架；动态内容，如商品列表、推荐流、搜索结果、用户生成内容，如果也做穷举，成本会迅速爆炸且收益有限。因此 AutoSurfer 对动态区域采用代表性采样，而不是完全展开。这是一个很关键的设计取舍：作者不是追求状态空间穷举，而是追求“功能覆盖最大化 / 冗余最小化”。这比 random walk 更稳，因为 random walk 容易在动态内容里浪费大量步数，却漏掉真正重要的结构性入口。

第三部分是 task synthesis with exploration trajectory。现有方法常从最终页面或局部交互反推任务，容易产生 hallucinated、模糊或不符合用户意图的任务描述。AutoSurfer 利用 BFS 过程中保留下来的完整探索轨迹，把中间状态、页面语义和最终发现的能力一起输入任务合成器。这样生成的任务不只是“这个页面能做什么”，而是“用户如何通过一串合理步骤完成什么目标”。这一步把任务 grounding 从静态页面内容提升到了过程级别。

The reason is not a new model architecture, but a serious treatment of coverage in interactive data generation. In text pretraining, people already think in terms of data mixture, deduplication, and quality filtering. Web-agent data adds another axis: functional-space coverage. If the exploration stage misses deep menus, form branches, dynamic widgets, or cross-page dependencies, then later task synthesis is operating on an incomplete state space no matter how polished the prompting is. AutoSurfer’s main contribution is to elevate exploration from random walks or homepage-centered heuristics into an explicit, knowledge-carrying, website-structured process.

A second reason the paper is important is that it does not stop at generating demonstrations. It uses the generated data to train a website-specific LLM. That makes it relevant to the interface between data generation and targeted pretraining or continual training. The broader lesson is that in interactive environments, data quality is not just about whether a trajectory succeeds. It also depends on whether the task is properly grounded, whether exploration is systematic, and whether the collected transitions cover the right capability boundaries. The contribution is best viewed as data infrastructure for web agents, but one that directly touches how the training distribution is formed.1b:Tb94,The method has three main components: systematic exploration, trajectory-grounded task synthesis, and more reliable trajectory generation for downstream modeling.

The first component is systematic breadth-first exploration. Unlike homepage-based methods such as Explorer, AutoSurfer models website exploration as a queue-based BFS process. The queue stores discovered pages together with the action traces used to reach them, so each page is paired with its causal access path. This has two benefits. It improves coverage by progressively expanding into deeper site structure rather than staying near the homepage, and it preserves process information that can later be used for task synthesis instead of relying on isolated screenshots or DOM fragments. The paper also emphasizes knowledge propagation: information extracted from earlier pages is passed forward to help interpret later pages, recognize navigation structure, avoid redundant exploration, and group related functionality.

The second component is differentiated handling of static and dynamic elements. Static functionality—menus, navigation bars, dropdowns, settings—is traversed systematically because it defines the structural backbone of the site. Dynamic content—product lists, recommendation feeds, search results, user-generated content—is sampled selectively rather than exhaustively. This is a key design choice. The goal is not full state-space enumeration, but high functional coverage with low redundancy. That is a better fit than random walks, which can waste many steps in dynamic regions while still missing important structural entry points.

The third component is task synthesis with exploration trajectories. Prior methods often infer tasks from a final page or a local interaction snippet, which can lead to hallucinated, ambiguous, or weakly user-aligned tasks. AutoSurfer uses the full exploration trajectory produced by BFS, including intermediate states, page semantics, and the final discovered capability, to synthesize tasks. This moves task grounding from static page content to process-level evidence. The resulting task is not merely “what this page can do,” but “what a user can accomplish through a plausible sequence of actions.”

Finally, the paper connects data generation to modeling by using the resulting tasks and trajectories to train a website-specific LLM. The underlying hypothesis is that part of the failure of general-purpose multimodal LLMs on complex websites comes from weak familiarity with site-local semantics, navigation conventions, and capability composition. If systematic exploration can produce high-quality site-specific data, then a smaller model may gain a lot from targeted training.
