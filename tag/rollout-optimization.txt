1a:T8d9,方法叫 Recoverability-Aware Intervention Learning，简称 RAIL。核心概念是 recoverability。按作者的定义，recoverability 衡量的是：在某个任务或轨迹状态上施加 intervention 后，reward-distribution signal 能提升多少。这里的重点不是最终 reward 本身，而是干预是否让训练看到更有区分度、更有学习价值的回报分布。

围绕这个概念，RAIL 做了三层设计。

第一，它把 rollout intervention 建模成一个 online contextual bandit。context 是当前任务或轨迹状态，action 是一个结构化 intervention 选择，observed utility 是实际观测到的 recoverability gain。这样做的意义是，controller 学到的是“在当前 policy、当前状态分布下，什么干预最能带来额外学习信号”，而不是依赖固定规则。作者明确把这一步用来弥补 non-stationary gap：policy 在变，干预策略也应跟着学。

第二，action space 不是单一的 rollout 数量，而是 structured intervention space。虽然给出的全文片段没有展开全部细节，但从引言和方法定义看，至少包括在哪里 branch、采用哪种 intervention strategy 等耦合决策。作者把这一步用来弥补 non-scalar gap：很多有价值的干预不是“多采样几个”这么简单，而是要在正确的状态、以正确的方式扩展轨迹。

第三，部署方式采用 shadow-to-live procedure。先用一个短 shadow phase 收集 intervention traces 和 supervision，不直接让 learned controller 影响主训练；等 controller 对 utility 有一定把握后，再进入 utility-gated live phase，选择性地把它接入 policy optimization。这个设计比较务实，因为在线学 intervention policy 容易把训练搞偏，先 shadow 再 live 相当于给 controller 一个冷启动缓冲区，也降低了早期错误干预的代价。

从整体上看，RAIL 不是改 GRPO 的 policy gradient 公式，而是改 rollout generation process。它把 rollout 生成从固定程序变成可学习、可适应的训练环节。和基于 entropy、variance 的启发式 branching 方法相比，差别在于：RAIL 关心的是 intervention 的 realized utility，而不是某个代理指标是否高。1b:T7cf,RAIL stands for Recoverability-Aware Intervention Learning. Its central concept is recoverability, defined as how much an intervention improves the reward-distribution signal at a task or trajectory state. The emphasis is not just on final reward, but on whether the intervention exposes more informative reward contrast for learning.

The method has three main layers.

First, rollout intervention is formulated as an online contextual bandit. The context is the current task or trajectory state, the action is a structured intervention choice, and the observed utility is the realized recoverability gain. This directly addresses the non-stationary gap: as the policy evolves, the intervention policy should also adapt from observed outcomes rather than remain a fixed heuristic.

Second, the action space is structured rather than scalar. From the paper’s framing, it includes coupled decisions such as where to branch and which intervention strategy to apply, not merely how many additional rollouts to generate. This addresses the non-scalar gap: useful intervention often depends on acting at the right state with the right branching pattern, not just increasing sample count.

Third, deployment uses a shadow-to-live procedure. A short shadow phase collects intervention traces and supervision without directly affecting the main optimization loop. Once the controller has enough signal, a utility-gated live phase selectively applies it during policy optimization. This is a practical design choice because online intervention learning can destabilize training if deployed too early; the shadow phase acts as a cold-start buffer.

Overall, RAIL does not modify the GRPO gradient formula itself. It modifies the rollout-generation process, turning it from a fixed procedure into an adaptive, learned component of training. In diagnosis, the authors build an end-to-end FP4 RL pipeline using HiFloat4 (HiF4), and compare against MXFP4 to check whether the conclusions are format-specific. They systematically vary precision assignments across rollout and training: low-precision rollout with higher-precision training, higher-precision rollout with low-precision training, and fully FP4 on both sides. A striking result is that restoring the training policy to higher precision while keeping rollout in FP4 performs worse than the full-FP4 baseline. This shows that the main issue is not training-side numerical noise but mismatch between the rollout distribution and the training distribution induced by quantized rollout. Activation analysis then reveals the mechanism: a small number of outliers expand the scale so much that the bulk of activation values underflow to zero in FP4. Keeping rollout activations in higher precision recovers much of the lost accuracy, but sacrifices the throughput benefit of true FP4 GEMMs. In response, the paper proposes Rollout-ResQ, a residual quantization scheme for rollout activations. Instead of using static PTQ-style calibration, smoothing, clamping, or rotation methods, which assume stationary activation statistics, Rollout-ResQ separates outlier residuals from the main activation tensor and represents them as a second FP4 tensor. 具体流程分五步。1）先按当前策略跑出一条 backbone。2）在 backbone 上选 M 个分叉点。论文不是均匀选，而是用每步策略熵做 branch scheduler，优先把预算放在策略最不确定的位置。这样做的理由很直接：如果某一步策略已经很确定，再分叉的边际信息小；高熵位置更可能对应决策瓶颈。3）对每个 branch point，调用 sandbox snapshot/restore，从该状态 fork 出 K 个候选动作或 continuation。4）每个 fork 从该中间状态继续 rollout 到终止，得到 terminal reward。5）在更新时，对某个 sibling rollout 的 advantage，不再减去 prompt 下所有独立轨迹的均值，而是减去同一父节点下其他 siblings 的均值，形成 tree-structured sibling baseline，再配合 PPO-style clipped objective 做策略更新。

理论部分有两层。第一层是无偏性：只要 sibling baseline 不包含当前样本自身的回报，或按 leave-one-out 方式构造，优势估计对策略梯度仍无偏。第二层是方差上界：相较 GRPO 依赖 Var(R|s0)，BPO 的 sibling baseline 把方差压到与 E[Var(R|st)] 同量级，差值正是前缀解释掉的那部分 Var(E[R|st]|s0)。这正对应“共享前缀不该被当成独立样本噪声”。

Instead of sampling N independent full trajectories per prompt, BPO samples one backbone trajectory, chooses branch points along it, restores the sandbox at those states, expands multiple sibling continuations, and computes advantages using a sibling baseline within each branch family.

The pipeline has five steps. (1) Roll out one backbone trajectory under the current policy. (2) Select M branch points on the backbone. The paper uses an entropy-based scheduler rather than uniform sampling: branching budget is allocated to steps where the policy is most uncertain. This is sensible because branching at already-certain decisions adds little information, while high-entropy steps are more likely to be decision bottlenecks. (3) At each selected branch state, snapshot/restore is used to fork K alternative actions or continuations. (4) Each fork is rolled out to termination to obtain a final reward. (5) For policy updates, the advantage of one sibling rollout is computed against the mean return of its siblings under the same parent state, yielding a tree-structured sibling baseline, and then plugged into a PPO-style clipped objective.

The theory has two key parts. First, the estimator is unbiased as long as the sibling baseline is constructed without leaking the sample’s own return, e.g. via leave-one-out structure. Second, the variance bound improves over GRPO: instead of scaling with Var(R|s0), the relevant term becomes E[Var(R|st)] up to a K/(K-1) factor. The gap is exactly the prefix-explained component Var(E[R|st]|s0), which is the variance standard group baselines fail to remove.

It is also useful to compare BPO with VinePPO. VinePPO also branches from intermediate states, but it is more rooted in critic/value-estimation settings. BPO is tailored to the critic-free, baseline-only regime that has become common in LLM agent RL, and its main intervention is the rollout topology plus a state-conditional advantage estimator. These numbers support the central claim that the gain comes from better rollout structure rather than simply more sampling.

The paper also measures snapshot overhead explicitly and argues that, when checkpoint/restore is reasonably cheap, the variance-reduction benefit outweighs the systems cost.
