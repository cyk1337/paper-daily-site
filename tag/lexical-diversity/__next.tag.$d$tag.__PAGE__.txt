9:T5a7,The study uses paired comparisons between base and instruction-tuned models from three families: Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B-v0.3. Evaluation is done on three multiple-choice QA benchmarks: ARC-Easy, MMLU, and CommonsenseQA. Confidence is measured in two complementary ways. First, the authors compute likelihood-based answer uncertainty using the entropy of normalized answer probabilities over the candidate options. Second, they elicit verbalized confidence with a two-stage protocol: the model first selects an answer via likelihood, then in a separate pass reports a numerical probability that the chosen answer is correct.

For rationale diversity, the model generates K=5 chain-of-thought rationales per question. Diversity is measured using Unique-2, which captures lexical richness through distinct bigram proportion, and Self-BLEU, reported as 1-SelfBLEU so that larger values indicate greater variation across sampled rationales. A strong part of the design is the controlled comparison restricted to examples where base and instruction-tuned models choose the same answer, with rationale length matched across variants. This removes two major confounders: answer switching and generation-length effects.

The paper is careful not to collapse confidence, calibration, and rationale diversity into a single notion. Instead, it treats them as separate observables and asks whether they move together after instruction tuning.a:T5fe,The main empirical finding is consistent across all model families and benchmarks: instruction tuning increases confidence, but the increase is not matched by equally consistent gains in accuracy or calibration. Choice entropy drops substantially in every setting, for example from 0.430 to 0.131 for Qwen on MMLU and from 0.736 to 0.268 for Mistral on CSQA. Verbalized confidence also rises sharply, including from 49.2% to 90.4% for Llama on ARC-Easy and from 46.8% to 91.1% on CSQA. Yet accuracy does not move in parallel; Llama on ARC-Easy stays at 82.2% accuracy despite the large confidence increase.

Rationale diversity behaves less uniformly. Unique-2 changes are mixed across models and datasets, with some increases and some decreases. For instance, Mistral on CSQA increases from 0.719 to 0.750, while other settings show declines. By contrast, 1-SelfBLEU decreases across all models and benchmarks, meaning repeated rationale generations become more similar to one another after instruction tuning. The largest drop is reported for Mistral on ARC-Easy, from 0.813 to 0.626.

The controlled same-answer, length-matched analysis is especially informative. It shows that lower answer uncertainty or higher verbalized confidence does not consistently align with either lower or higher lexical diversity. So the story is not simply that instruction tuning makes models more confident because it makes their rationales uniformly more templated.
