Three necessary conditions for collapse are identified: blanket answering has negative expected score (the exact scenario the penalty rule targets), all prompts share a bounded readout head, and abstention is treated as an action-level choice. It then derives a finite-time collapse law: mean training reward converges to 0 at a 1/t rate (up to log factors) independent of anchor strength β. It further analyzes how advantage estimators modify the law: group normalization steepens decay to 1/t² below an answer rate knee of ~1/G (G = group size) and silently replaces the designed penalty λ with an effective λ_eff=1; dynamic resampling preserves collapse magnitude per rollout. 1) KAR: an online knowledge-boundary estimator per query, implemented via sampling-based within-group response statistics. By sampling multiple responses for the same question and checking whether correct answers appear in a group, the method estimates whether the query lies within the model’s current competence region. Rewards are then adjusted dynamically: if the estimator suggests the model can answer correctly, correct answering is strongly rewarded and unnecessary abstention is discouraged; if the estimator suggests the model is unlikely to answer correctly, guided abstention is rewarded to avoid confident fabrication.
2) Two-stage RL: decouples boundary exploration from abstention calibration.
- Stage 1 (boundary exploration) uses a mixed reward biased toward accuracy to avoid the early abstention surge induced by static ternary rewards.
- Stage 2 (abstention calibration) converts persistent errors into abstentions while trying to preserve the accuracy gains from Stage 1.
