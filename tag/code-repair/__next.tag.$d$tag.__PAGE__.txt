b:T53c,The core contribution is DDBench plus a two-condition evaluation protocol. The benchmark contains 60 historical bugs mined from 13 open-source distributed systems, partitioned into three difficulty tiers, with tier-1 being the hardest and containing 31 cases. Each case is independently reproduced, sanitized for leakage, and reviewed by distributed-systems researchers, so the emphasis is on fidelity and evaluability rather than raw scale. The evaluation runs each case under two conditions: symptom-only and context-augmented. In symptom-only, the agent receives the bug symptom and codebase, similar to standard SWE-style repair. In context-augmented, it also receives curated debugging context such as logs, traces, or diagnostic notes. This design cleanly separates model-internal reasoning from externally supplied evidence. The authors use mini-swe-agent as a unified harness and evaluate 10 proprietary and open-weight models, tracking pass rate, token consumption, and steps to completion, with bootstrap tests for significance. An important nuance is that the paper does not assume more context is always better. It explicitly studies cases where faithful but causally distant observations mislead strong models, which makes the benchmark relevant not only for evaluation but also for context construction and evidence selection.c:T555,The main empirical findings are sharp. Pairwise bootstrap tests on the 31 tier-1 cases distinguish 9 of 15 top-tier model pairs at p<0.05, suggesting the separation is statistically meaningful rather than noise.
