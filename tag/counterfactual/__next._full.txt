18:T7a8,CRAFT has three pillars. Pillar 1 is Counterfactual Token Importance (CTI). The key observation is that GRPO already samples G sibling rollouts for the same prompt, so the G−1 non-realized siblings can be reused as approximate counterfactuals. The method importance-weights these sibling rollouts using the teacher–student gap Δ_t to estimate how the group-level advantage would change if the policy had moved toward the teacher at token t. This yields a signed per-token credit c_t in (-1, 1), which can express both “move toward the teacher here” and “the teacher would be harmful here.” The paper states consistency and variance results for this estimator, with variance scaling as O(1/G), and the extra computational cost is essentially a group-wise softmax rather than new rollouts. Pillar 2 is a bivariate controller. Instead of tuning a single KL coefficient, CRAFT jointly adjusts the CTI weight λ(t) and the reference-KL weight μ(t), driving them in opposite directions according to an EMA over gate activity. Intuitively, when token-level counterfactual credit is active and trustworthy, the update can rely more on CTI; when it is weak or noisy, the KL term provides stabilization. The paper also analyzes the controller’s fixed point. Pillar 3 is a signed per-token KL penalty. The KL direction is no longer fixed as one-way imitation of the teacher. It is chosen according to sign(c_t): when c_t is positive, the update is teacher-seeking; when c_t is negative, the update goes in the opposite direction to avoid distilling harmful teacher preferences. The authors argue that this is structurally aligned with the credit signal and still forms a valid policy-gradient surrogate. An important engineering detail is bit-exact reproducibility: with all switches disabled, the loss and gradients are byte-identical to the baseline, which makes the empirical gains easier to attribute to the algorithm rather than implementation drift.19:T4e1,The evaluation spans three agentic environments, four model sizes, four baselines, out-of-distribution tests, and clean ablations. The provided text does not include the full result tables, so exact numbers should not be fabricated, but several conclusions are clear. First, CRAFT improves over the standard single-gate SDAR baseline across multiple environments, suggesting that signed counterfactual credit is more informative than a plain Δ_t gate. Second, the paper includes a stronger comparator, Adaptive-CRINGE, which shares Pillar 2 with CRAFT. This is a good control because it isolates whether gains come merely from adaptive coefficient tuning or from CTI plus signed KL specifically. Third, the ablations are factorized by the three pillars, allowing the marginal contribution of each component to be inspected cleanly. Fourth, the inclusion of OOD evaluation indicates that the method is not only exploiting in-distribution imitation effects; it is intended to improve actual decision credit assignment.
