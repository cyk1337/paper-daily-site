9:T87f,方法分三步：generation、routing、refinement。先从同一个生成模型独立采样 n 条完整轨迹，再用 reward model 对整条轨迹打分。与直接按绝对分数阈值决策不同，SRD 采用 rank-normalized score，把候选按 reward 排序后映射到 [0,1] 区间，再用两个阈值 θ_low 和 θ_high 做三路路由：高分样本 Keep，中间样本 Refine，低分样本 Discard。这样做的动机是减少不同题目、不同 reward 尺度下绝对分数不稳定的问题。

真正的创新在 refinement。对进入 Refine 的边界样本，算法不会整条重采，而是沿轨迹按固定步长 m 检查 prefix-level reward 变化，寻找最早出现“相对前缀退化”的位置 j，把 j 之后的 suffix 截掉并重新生成。重生成时提高采样温度，目的是增加局部探索，避免原轨迹在同一错误模式附近反复采样。新 suffix 接回原 prefix 后，再重新打分并与其他活跃候选一起排序，只保留最终进入 Keep 的样本。

It first samples n complete trajectories from the same generator, then scores them with a reward model. Instead of using an absolute reward threshold, it converts rewards into rank-normalized scores in [0,1], and applies two thresholds, theta_low and theta_high, to route candidates into Keep, Refine, or Discard. The motivation is practical: absolute reward scales are often unstable across tasks and prompts, while rank is more robust.

The main novelty is the refinement stage. For borderline candidates routed to Refine, SRD does not resample the whole trajectory. It scans the trajectory at fixed intervals m, evaluates prefix-level reward behavior, and finds the earliest point j where quality appears to degrade relative to the preceding prefix. It then truncates the suffix after j and regenerates only that suffix, using a higher sampling temperature to encourage local exploration and avoid repeating the same failure mode. The repaired trajectory is rescored and reranked jointly with the remaining candidates, and is kept only if it survives the final routing.

The paper also provides a theoretical analysis. Under mild assumptions, SRD achieves strictly higher expected best-trajectory quality than rejection sampling and improves sample efficiency by about 1.28–1.36x, with gains increasing as the candidate pool grows. The gain is characterized by a term of the form (1 + rho * p_M / p_H), where p_M and p_H capture the mass of repairable medium-quality samples and already-high-quality samples, and rho reflects refinement success. This matters because it clarifies when SRD should help: only when there is a meaningful population of salvageable trajectories and the reward signal can identify degradation reliably.

Relative to prior work, best-of-N only selects at the end and never recovers partial structure; speculative rejection can stop early but is irreversible once a prefix is rejected; Controlled Decoding uses a learned prefix-aware value function for token-level steering, whereas SRD needs only a generator and a reward model, with no larger teacher and no separately trained value function. The trade-off is that SRD still requires full-trajectory sampling up front and extra reward evaluations, and its boundary detection is fairly coarse, based on interval scanning rather than token-level attribution.b:T6b0,The experiments cover MATH500, GPQA, HotpotQA, and AlpacaEval, spanning mathematical reasoning, science QA, multi-hop QA, and instruction following. On the score side, the paper compares four intrinsic signals: perplexity, contrastive scoring, power-distribution likelihood, and self-verification. On the decoder side, it compares three families that consume those scores: optimization, sampling, and consensus. Optimization is instantiated as best-of-N. Sampling is implemented with SMC and MCMC over a reweighted target distribution p_final(y|x) ∝ p_LM(y|x) · s(y|x). Consensus selects the modal answer under that induced distribution, using either majority vote or score-weighted aggregation.

The central technical claim is that self-verification is structurally better suited than likelihood-derived scores for unsupervised elicitation. It conditions on the full candidate answer, is semantically aligned with the downstream objective, and is defined as a normalized verdict probability, s_sv = p(check)/(p(check)+p(cross)). That normalization reduces sensitivity to absolute logit scale and makes the score more comparable across candidates.

A second notable idea is the virtual-thinking prefix. Before producing the final verification verdict, the model is forced to consume a sequence of filler tokens that spend extra compute without exposing visible reasoning. The paper finds that structured fillers, such as countdown tokens, work better than uniform fillers like repeated dots. This is an interesting contrast with prior work showing that plug-and-play filler tokens are ineffective for generator-side latent reasoning without training. The authors’ explanation is plausible: verifier-side virtual thinking is an easier task because the model only needs to sharpen a binary judgment, not generate a coherent reasoning trace.

Another strong aspect of the paper is the evaluation design. It separates score quality, decoding effectiveness, and aggregation effectiveness. A score that ranks candidates well in a fixed pool does not necessarily improve best-of-N or sampling-based decoding. That restraint makes the empirical story more credible.

The evaluation has three layers. First, score quality: with a fixed candidate pool, can the score rank correct answers above incorrect ones, measured by per-question AUROC and top-k accuracy. Second, decoding effectiveness: once the score is plugged into best-of-N, SMC, or MCMC, does it improve single-answer accuracy over vanilla decoding. Third, aggregation effectiveness: when many samples are available, does score-weighted consensus beat majority vote or simple best-of-N selection.
