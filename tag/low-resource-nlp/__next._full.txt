17:T583,The study has three main components. First, it builds a broad evaluation setup over 22 translation directions: 10 non-English-centric and 12 English-centric pairs, spanning high-, medium-, and low-resource languages. Directions are treated separately rather than merged by language pair, which is the right choice because the target language directly affects token activation. Second, the authors tighten prompting to control output format. They found that a standard in-context translation prompt often caused some models to produce explanations or noisy text instead of clean translations, which would contaminate automatic evaluation. They therefore designed two additional prompts, used rule-based noise detection plus manual inspection, and selected Prompt 2 for the cleanest outputs. This is an important methodological detail because noisy outputs would distort both translation metrics and token-dynamics analysis. Third, they introduce Token Activation Rate (TAR), a metric intended to capture how effectively a model uses language-specific tokens of the target language during generation. TAR is then analyzed jointly with typological distance, COMET, BLEU, and the number of reasoning tokens. Relative to prior multilingual evaluation work, the novelty is not a new MT method but a token-dynamics-based failure analysis, with explicit attention to reasoning LLMs as a possible compensatory mechanism.18:T523,The empirical scale is respectable: 22 translation directions, 3,000 examples per direction, for 66,000 instances in total. The model set includes 15 LLMs, covering instruction-tuned Qwen models, Qwen Thinking variants, DeepSeek distillations, and chat/reasoner variants, so both standard instruction models and reasoning-oriented post-trained models are represented. The main findings are: (1) non-English-centric pairs consistently obtain lower COMET than English-centric pairs across many models; (2) TAR serves as a useful proxy for language coverage and is strongly associated with COMET and BLEU, with lower target-language TAR linked to worse translation quality; (3) typological distance also matters, but TAR offers a more model-internal explanation than language distance alone; (4) low target-language TAR is significantly correlated with more generated reasoning tokens, suggesting that reasoning models try to compensate when target-language representation is weak; and (5) this compensation is not uniformly effective, but for some models more reasoning tokens correlate with gains in COMET or BLEU.
