9:T451,The authors build AIMS, an intent-annotated safety dataset derived from WildGuardMix. They first train an ensemble classifier and select prompts whose predicted harmfulness lies in [0.35, 0.65], concentrating annotation on ambiguous, adversarial, and borderline examples. Annotators write a concise description of the user’s underlying intent and assign a four-level harm label, later collapsed into a binary label for training and evaluation. The same intent signal is then used across four regimes. In SFT, the model either predicts the label directly or generates intent followed by the label. In DPO, rejected completions are model-generated bad intents, including both label-flipping intents and label-preserving but semantically wrong intents. In reasoning distillation, teacher traces with intent supervision are compared against reasoning-only traces. In GRPO, a label-only reward is compared with a reward that verifies intent faithfulness. The contribution is not a new classifier architecture; it is a clean way to inject an intermediate semantic target into several post-training objectives.a:T4ea,The dataset statistics are informative. The selection process yields 1,724 candidate prompts, and after quality filtering the final dataset contains 1,275 unique prompts. Among selected prompts, 70.1% are marked adversarial in the original WildGuardMix metadata. After binarizing the four-level harm labels, annotator agreement reaches Cohen’s κ = 0.55. Human-written intents have a mean pairwise semantic similarity of 0.62, while Llama-3.1-8B generated intents reach only 0.50 similarity against human annotations. Human binary labels match the original WildGuardMix labels for 72% of prompts, with disagreements concentrated in adversarial cases. Models are evaluated on five external safety benchmarks. Intent-aware training gives the best average performance across the evaluated systems: intent SFT is already competitive, DPO over bad intents improves over SFT, intent-conditioned distillation beats reasoning-only distillation in most teacher-student settings, and the best average result comes from GRPO with an intent-faithfulness reward rather than from simply distilling a larger teacher.
