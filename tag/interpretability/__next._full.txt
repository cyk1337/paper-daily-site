18:T423,The method uses hRoPE, a hierarchical rotary positional encoding. Position is represented as three coordinates: p1 for paragraph index, p2 for sentence index inside a paragraph, and p3 for token index inside a sentence. Each level receives its own independent rotary channel group, so paragraph, sentence, and token displacements are not collapsed into a single scalar. The frequency base for each axis is calibrated from corpus statistics using the 99th-percentile coordinate range plus a safety margin, so low-frequency channels remain non-wrapping over typical ranges. The central experimental move is a counterfactual intervention: keep the tokens and lower-level coordinates fixed, modify only the paragraph coordinate p1, and measure cross-paragraph attention. A density-matched random-label channel with the same architecture is used as a control. This is important because an arbitrary extra positional axis can also compress attention; the diagnostic quantity is therefore not compression itself, but compression depth relative to the random control.19:T5b4,The paper uses classic Contrastive Activation Addition as a causal probe rather than proposing a new steering method. A task direction is built from contrastive activations, then injected into the residual stream at inference time.

The analysis is carefully split into two parts. First, representation displacement: the authors estimate an independent stance coordinate and measure how far the intervention moves hidden states along that coordinate, normalized by its standard deviation. Second, output response: they evaluate both generation-level steering success with an LLM judge and a judge-free token-level metric, the change in stance margin at the first generation step, computed from target-stance versus original-stance token sets. This separation is the core methodological strength of the paper.

To test the transition-gap hypothesis, they then ask three questions: whether task information remains decodable in continuous thoughts, whether the output distribution changes abruptly at the latent-to-language boundary, and whether task-related directions retain bidirectional control over generation.
