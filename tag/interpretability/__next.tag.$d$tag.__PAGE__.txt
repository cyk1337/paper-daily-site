5:Td9f,技术方案可以拆成四层：概念数据、可解释架构、训练目标、以及 scaling 分析。

第一层是概念数据 Atlas。作者认为 post-hoc 方法的问题，不只是解释器弱，而是训练时根本没有一个与预测计算耦合的语义接口。所以他们先构建大规模概念库和标注流水线：从数百万文档中抽取数亿 free-form tags，做 canonicalization 后得到 3.3 万多个概念，再训练 annotator 对任意文本做 chunk-level 概念标注。Atlas 最终覆盖 web text、code、math、academic prose，总标注规模超过 1T tokens。这里的关键不是“有标签”这么简单，而是把概念监督提升到预训练同量级，否则 concept bottleneck 很容易只停留在小数据实验。

第二层是架构。Steerling-8B 采用 diffusion language model，并使用 block-causal attention：块内保留 diffusion 式并行，块间保留类似 AR 的因果结构和 KV cache 能力。这一点很重要，因为它不是纯粹为了可解释性牺牲生成接口，而是在并行训练与可部署推理之间做折中。真正的解释接口插在 backbone 和 LM head 之间，是一个 additive concept bottleneck。作者强调它让 logit decomposition 变成 algebraically exact，也就是说输出可以精确分解为概念通道与 residual 通道的贡献，而不是事后近似投影。与此同时，masking objective 让模型学到“该位置无信息”的 in-distribution absence baseline，这使 feature removal / token attribution 不再依赖离分布的删词基线。论文把 faithful explanation 需要的几个条件——变量、干预、语义标签与预测计算耦合——都尽量写进了结构里。

第三层是训练目标与解释轴。模型对任意输出都沿三条轴给出归因：对输入 token 的影响、对人类可理解概念的贡献、以及对 residual 未解释部分的贡献。概念在模型中不是只供 probe 读取的标签，而是 forward pass 里真实参与计算的方向，因此可以直接做 intervention，比如放大或抑制某个概念方向，观察输出变化。这个设计和传统 probe / SAE 的差别在于：后者证明“信息可解码”，这里追求的是“概念变量就是计算接口的一部分”。

第四层是 scaling。作者做了四个 family 的 IsoFLOP 扫描：AR、causal diffusion、AR+concepts、causal diffusion+concepts。然后用 joint Chinchilla form 去拟合 loss 与 compute 的关系，并同时拟合多项 interpretability metrics。论文的核心判断来自这里：加入 concept module 后，compute-optimal scaling exponent 只发生小而固定的 per-backbone offset；解释约束没有随着规模放大而越来越贵。更有意思的是，多个解释性指标随 compute 一起改善，包括概念预测更准、known/unknown 表征分离更清楚、更多预测质量通过概念通道而非 residual 路由、概念 embedding 与人类标签更对齐。这不是“能力没掉太多”这么简单，而是“规模增长同时改善能力和可解释接口质量”。

和现有方法相比，区别主要有三点。第一，不是 post-hoc 解释，而是 training-time interpretability。第二，不是把 concept bottleneck 用在小模型分类任务，而是放进大规模 LM 预训练，并覆盖 diffusion LM。第三，不只给案例分析，而是给 scaling law 和大模型实例，试图说明这件事在现代训练规模下仍成立。6:T863,实验部分信息量很大，最关键的是 scaling 与 8B 实例化两块。

在 scaling 方面，作者做了跨三个数量级 compute 的 IsoFLOP sweep，比较四类模型：AR、causal diffusion、AR with concepts、causal diffusion with concepts。结论是，加入概念模块后，loss–compute 曲线出现的是小而固定的偏移，而不是随规模扩大的惩罚。Steerling-8B 的验证集 loss 可以被小模型拟合的 joint Chinchilla form 预测到 0.11 nats 以内，四个解释性指标里有三个也能被较紧地预测。这说明作者不是只在单一规模上讲故事，而是把“可解释性是否可扩展”当成 scaling 问题来处理。

在解释性指标上，论文报告了几类趋势：概念预测准确率随 compute 提升；known/unknown representation separation 变好；更多 logit 贡献通过 concept path 而不是 residual path；concept embedding 与人类可读标签的对齐增强。这里最值得注意的是“residual routing 比例下降”这一类结果，因为它直接对应一个核心担忧：概念瓶颈会不会只是挂名存在，真正计算仍绕过它。作者的结果表明，至少在他们的训练设定里，随着规模上升，模型更愿意把预测质量放到概念通道里。

在大模型实例化上，Steerling-8B 是一个带 causal attention mask 的 diffusion LM。训练数据是 1.2T token 预训练，再加 150B token 的 code/math augmented midtraining。概念模块在 8B 时只占 4% 参数；按同样参数化外推到更大规模，会降到 1% 以下。和同尺度开源 peer 相比，这个模型在 benchmark 平均表现上落在大约 10% 以内，而对手使用的训练计算量约为它的 2 到 16 倍。这个结果不能简单解读成“同算力最优”，但足以说明解释约束没有把模型能力拉到不可用的程度。

论文还强调它同时在 AR 和 diffusion backbone 上验证了趋势，这一点很重要。因为如果结论只在单一架构上成立，很难判断是 interpretability recipe 有效，还是某个 backbone 的偶然性。这里至少给出了更强的外推信号。7:Td83,The method has four layers: concept data, interpretable architecture, training objective, and scaling analysis.

The first layer is Atlas, a large-scale concept annotation pipeline. The paper argues that post-hoc interpretability fails not only because the explainer is weak, but because standard training never creates a semantic interface coupled to prediction. Atlas addresses that by extracting hundreds of millions of free-form tags from millions of documents, canonicalizing them into more than 33,000 concepts, and training an annotator that labels arbitrary text at the chunk level. The final annotation coverage exceeds 1 trillion tokens across web text, code, mathematics, and academic prose. The important point is scale: concept supervision is elevated to something comparable to pretraining scale, rather than remaining a small auxiliary dataset.

The second layer is architecture. Steerling-8B is a diffusion language model with block-causal attention. Within a block it preserves diffusion-style parallelism; across blocks it retains an autoregressive-style causal structure and KV-cache compatibility. This is a practical compromise between parallel training and deployable generation. The interpretability interface sits between the transformer backbone and the LM head as an additive concept bottleneck. The paper emphasizes that this makes the logit decomposition algebraically exact, so output contributions from concept channels and residual channels are not merely approximate post-hoc projections. In parallel, the masking objective teaches the model an in-distribution notion of “no information at this position,” which gives token-removal and feature-removal attributions a trained absence baseline rather than an out-of-distribution deletion heuristic.

The third layer is the objective and explanatory interface. For any output, the model attributes prediction along three axes: influential input tokens, human-understandable concepts, and residual unexplained contribution. Concepts are not passive labels decoded by a probe; they are directions used in the forward pass. That means interventions are native: amplifying or suppressing a concept direction directly changes computation. This is the main departure from probes or sparse autoencoders, which often establish decodability rather than computational use.

The fourth layer is scaling. The paper runs IsoFLOP sweeps over four model families: AR, causal diffusion, AR plus concepts, and causal diffusion plus concepts. It fits loss with a joint Chinchilla-style form and also tracks several interpretability metrics as functions of compute. The central empirical claim comes from this section: adding the concept module changes the compute-optimal scaling behavior by a small fixed per-backbone offset, not by a worsening penalty with scale. At the same time, interpretability metrics improve with compute: concept prediction gets better, known-versus-unknown representations separate more cleanly, more predictive mass is routed through concept channels rather than residual channels, and concept embeddings align better with human labels.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear. This is training-time interpretability rather than post-hoc explanation; it brings concept bottlenecks into large-scale language-model pretraining rather than small supervised tasks; and it supports the claim with scaling laws and an 8B model rather than isolated case studies.8:T915,The experimental section is dense, but two parts matter most: scaling and the 8B instantiation.

On scaling, the authors run IsoFLOP sweeps across three orders of magnitude of compute for four families: AR, causal diffusion, AR with concepts, and causal diffusion with concepts. Their main result is that adding the concept module produces a small fixed shift in the loss-compute tradeoff rather than a penalty that grows with scale. The validation loss of Steerling-8B is predicted within 0.11 nats by a joint Chinchilla-style fit from smaller runs, and three of four interpretability metrics are also predicted within tight bounds. This matters because the paper treats interpretability as a scaling question, not as a one-off model anecdote.

For interpretability, the paper reports several monotonic trends with compute: concept prediction accuracy improves; separation between known and unknown representations becomes cleaner; a larger fraction of the model’s predictive contribution flows through the concept path rather than the residual path; and concept embeddings align more closely with human-readable labels. The routing result is especially important because it addresses a central concern: a concept bottleneck could exist only nominally while the real computation bypasses it. Their evidence suggests that, under this recipe, larger models rely more rather than less on the concept channel.

For the large model, Steerling-8B is a diffusion LM with a causal attention mask. It is trained on 1.2T pretraining tokens followed by 150B midtraining tokens with a code-and-math-augmented mixture. The concept module accounts for 4% of parameters at 8B and would fall below 1% at larger scales under the same parameterization. Compared with open peers at similar parameter count, Steerling-8B lands within roughly 10% of their average benchmark performance, despite those peers using about 2x to 16x more training compute. This does not prove compute-optimality, but it does show that the interpretability constraints do not collapse model usefulness.

The fact that the paper validates the trend on both AR and diffusion backbones is also important. If the result held only for one architecture, it would be hard to tell whether the gain came from the interpretability recipe or from a backbone-specific artifact.9:T85e,This one deserves a close read. Five parts are worth real attention.

First, read the introduction and the formal discussion of why post-hoc interpretability is structurally limited. The key standard is not a generic complaint about probes; it is the requirement that variables, interventions, semantic labels, and prediction computation be coupled. Once that standard is clear, the rest of the recipe makes sense.

Second, read the data section carefully, especially Atlas. You want to understand how free-form tags are turned into a trainable concept system, how chunk-level annotation is produced, and how the pipeline reaches pretraining scale. Whether the whole approach is credible depends heavily on this layer.

Third, read the architecture in detail. Focus on why block-causal attention is a good fit for diffusion LM, how the additive concept bottleneck yields exact logit decomposition, and why the masking objective creates an in-distribution absence baseline. This is where much of the real novelty sits.

Fourth, spend time on the scaling-law section. Prioritize the IsoFLOP plots for the four families, the joint Chinchilla fits, and the scaling plots for interpretability metrics. The main question is not whether there is some loss penalty, but whether the offset is fixed, whether the slope changes, and whether the pattern is consistent across backbones.

Fifth, read the Steerling-8B training and evaluation sections as a full recipe instantiation rather than a model release. Pay attention to the 1.2T pretrain plus 150B midtrain mixture, the parameter share of the concept module, the compute comparison to peers, and the concept-versus-residual routing analysis.

If time is limited, at minimum read the concept-data pipeline figure, the architecture overview, the main scaling-law plots, the main interpretability-metric plots, and the 8B training table.
