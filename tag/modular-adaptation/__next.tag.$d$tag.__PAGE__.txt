9:T729,The method centers on a skill compiler, a hypernetwork G_phi that takes a skill document s and outputs LoRA updates for selected modules of a frozen backbone LLM. At inference time, the generated adapter is mounted onto the model, and the agent conditions only on the task history rather than re-inserting the skill text into the prompt.

Training has two stages. First, document-level pretraining initializes the compiler on a corpus of skill documents. Two self-supervised tasks are used. In reconstruction, the compiler reads the full skill document and the adapted backbone is trained to reproduce it under a reconstruction instruction. In completion, the compiler reads a truncated prefix and the adapted backbone is trained to complete the full document. The point is not document generation per se, but learning a stable mapping from procedural text to useful parameter updates.

Second, trajectory-supervised fine-tuning aligns the generated latent skills with actual downstream behavior in agent trajectories. The backbone remains frozen, while the compiler is updated so that the generated LoRA affects action generation in the intended way. This differs from ordinary SFT: the skill is constrained to live in adapter space, preserving modular loading, unloading, replacement, scaling, and potential composition.

The paper also studies three properties of the generated adapters. They are structured, in that skills from different domains cluster in weight space. They are controllable, since the LoRA scaling coefficient alpha smoothly modulates the skill effect. They are composable, but only when the original skills are decomposed into semantically aligned components before combining their adapters. That last point is important: composition is conditional on the granularity and alignment of the skill decomposition.a:T567,The main evaluations are on ALFWorld and Search-QA. Against the corresponding in-context skill baseline, LatentSkill improves ALFWorld success by 21.4 points on the seen split and 13.4 points on the unseen split, while reducing prefill tokens by 64.1%. The abstract truncates the Search-QA numbers, but the full paper reports the same qualitative pattern: better task performance with substantially less prompt overhead from skill text.

The analysis experiments are more informative than the headline numbers. Weight-space visualizations show domain-level clustering of generated skill adapters, suggesting that the compiler learns structured procedural representations rather than a trivial prompt compression. Alpha-scaling experiments show smooth control over skill strength. Composition experiments show that adapter combination works best when skills are decomposed into aligned subskills; coarse or entangled skills compose less reliably. This defines the method’s boundary: it is better viewed as compiling modular procedural knowledge than as a universal replacement for arbitrary natural-language instructions.

From a systems perspective, the gains should be largest in long-horizon agent settings where the same skill text would otherwise be reinserted many times. The advantage is likely smaller when interactions are short or the skill descriptions are already brief.b:T406,Start with the introduction and the opening of the method section.
