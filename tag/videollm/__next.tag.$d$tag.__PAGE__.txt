5:T6d9,The main contribution is DistractionBench. The benchmark is built around controlled temporal distractions: insert an unrelated short clip, often an advertisement, into a main video, or concatenate multiple action segments, then ask whether a subject from the main video participated in an event that only occurred in the distracting segment.

There are four subtasks. InjectedAds pairs a long video with an ad clip to mimic inserted commercials. Concat-Easy concatenates two UCF101 clips to control length and position. Concat-Hard concatenates five clips to increase event mixing difficulty. NaturalAds uses real YouTube videos with sponsored segments and manually written questions, providing a more realistic gold-standard setting.

Each video comes with three question types. BoE questions combine a subject from the main content with an object or event appearing only in the distracting segment, directly probing cross-segment misbinding. Yes-bias questions combine the same subject with an object absent from the entire video, controlling for a generic tendency to answer “Yes.” No-bias questions ask about facts that truly occur in the video, controlling for poor understanding or excessive denial. This design is important because it separates bag-of-events errors from generic hallucination and response bias.

Evaluation is based on whether the model output contains “Yes” or “No,” following prior hallucination benchmarks. The metric is simple and somewhat coarse, but it supports large-scale comparison. The paper also studies distraction position and frame sampling budget, which helps distinguish failures caused by missing frames from failures caused by weak temporal/entity binding even when the relevant content is observed.6:T583,The benchmark evaluates 11 VideoLLMs, including Aria 23.9B, LLaVA-Video 7B, LLaVA-OneVision 0.5B and 7B, Molmo2 4B and 8B, Qwen3VL 8B, Qwen3.5 9B, Gemma4 31B, Phi4-Multimodal, and InternVL3.5 8B. Models support between 8 and 256 frames. Uniform sampling is used by default, and for low-frame models the setup ensures that at least 25% of sampled frames come from the advertisement segment so the distraction is not accidentally missed.

Dataset sizes are moderate but meaningful: InjectedAds has 256 videos and 768 QA pairs; Concat-Easy and Concat-Hard each have 450 videos and 2,700 QA pairs; NaturalAds has 150 videos and 450 QA pairs. The paper argues that the benchmark is longer and larger than prior video hallucination QA sets.

The central empirical result is that BoE hallucination rates are consistently much higher than Yes-bias rates across all subtasks and model families. That matters because it shows the effect is not just a generic affirmative bias. The paper also studies two additional factors: where the distraction is placed and how many frames are sampled. The likely pattern, consistent with the paper’s framing, is that more frames help somewhat but do not eliminate the issue, while more complex or strategically placed distractions worsen cross-segment mixing. This supports the claim that the problem is not only coverage, but also weak temporal grounding and subject-event
