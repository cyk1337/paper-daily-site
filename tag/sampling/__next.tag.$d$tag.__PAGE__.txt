Two sampling algorithms are proposed. The Sequential Monte Carlo variant maintains a population of particles along an annealing path and uses importance weights plus resampling to avoid the degeneracy of one-shot importance sampling. The Replica Exchange variant runs chains at different temperatures or sharpness levels and swaps states across replicas, allowing high-temperature chains to explore while low-temperature chains focus on high-probability regions. It starts from the usual token-wise base score for each masked position, typically the confidence of the top predicted token. Instead of sorting positions independently by that score, ADAS builds the subset greedily. Once some positions have already been selected, each remaining candidate is discounted according to how strongly it attends to those selected positions and how uncertain those selected positions still are. Intuitively, if candidate i depends heavily on an already selected but still fragile position j, then the marginal value of committing i in the same step should be reduced.

The paper frames this as greedy construction under a non-additive subset utility: confidence acts as an individual utility proxy, while attention acts as a pairwise compatibility proxy. This is the key distinction from prior samplers. Top-k, Fast-dLLM, and EB-Sampler differ mostly in their budget constraints: fixed cardinality, confidence threshold, or entropy budget. They do not make the value of adding one position depend on which other positions are already in the subset. ADAS does exactly that, while remaining plug-compatible with all three.

A notable design choice is using attention as the dependency signal instead of explicit search, planning, or a verifier. The reason is practical: attention is already available from a single forward pass, so the method adds almost no modeling complexity. The paper also provides empirical evidence that masked-token self-attention separates dependent from non-dependent pairs in controlled settings. Its defining feature is that the corrector requires no additional training. Instead, it uses the structure of the already-trained concrete score function to construct the posterior quantities needed for Gibbs updates.

Operationally, GADD keeps a diffusion-style predictor that moves the sample from a high-noise state toward the target distribution. It then inserts a Gibbs-based corrector that updates discrete coordinates conditionally. The key observation is that the score estimator already contains enough local information to recover the relative posterior likelihoods needed for Gibbs sampling. This avoids the need for separately trained energy models, auxiliary correctors, or learned refinement networks.

A major part of the paper is the error-analysis framework. The total error is decomposed into initialization error, mixing error from the corrector, and score-estimation error. The argument is not simply that Gibbs mixes faster in isolation. Rather, the diffusion process provides a warm start, so the corrector does not need to mix from a poor initialization; at the same time, this warm start also reduces the impact of score-estimation error. Under this structure, the paper proves an overall sampling complexity of O(polylog(ε^{-1})). That is the central theoretical claim.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear. Compared with exact simulation, τ-leaping, Euler, or Tweedie-style discretizations, GADD is not just a better numerical integrator for the reverse process; it explicitly introduces an MCMC corrector. Compared with fast samplers for masked diffusion, it does not rely on absorbing-state structure. Compared with methods that train additional correctors, it is training-free beyond the standard score model, which is an important practical property for large models.11:T681,The experiments cover synthetic distributions, zero-shot unconditional text sampling, and zero-shot conditional music generation. The goal is to validate not only the theory, but also practical sample quality and wall-clock behavior.

Third, it shows favorable wall-clock efficiency in realistic discrete-generation settings, meaning the extra Gibbs updates do not erase the practical benefit.
