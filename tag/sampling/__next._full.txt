19:T558,The core reframing models repeated samples for a single prompt as cluster sampling rather than i.i.d. draws, building on Kish’s design effect and de Finetti’s exchangeability theorem. It disentangles three quantities confounded by nominal sample count: (1) The modal ceiling π_mode for answer selection: without a perfect verifier, plurality vote converges to the model’s most frequent answer for a given problem. If that modal answer is incorrect, additional samples only increase confidence in the wrong answer (anti-scaling), so selection accuracy is capped at the fraction of problems where the model’s modal answer is correct. (2) The correlation ceiling 1/ρ: within-problem response correlation ρ means n correlated samples have an effective sample size of n/(1+(n-1)ρ), which saturates at a hard upper bound of 1/ρ regardless of sampling budget, governing the accuracy of model performance estimates from sampling. (3) Between-problem difficulty heterogeneity ρ_b shapes the coverage curve; previously reported power-law coverage curves under i.i.d. assumptions are recovered as the special case where within-problem correlation ρ_w = 0. The framework strictly separates estimation (sampling to measure model accuracy) from selection (sampling to return a single answer), and provides a rule for optimal compute allocation across sampling budget.1a:T56d,The probe suite contains 686 prompts across six regimes. The ordering becomes more left-to-right as the analysis bin gets coarser; for example, in R1, tau_b rises from 0.59 at bin size 4 to 0.91 at bin size 64. On scorable regimes, accuracy is comparable to the matched AR sibling Gemma-4 26B-A4B, although the comparison is not powered as an equivalence test.1b:T782,The method, ADAS (Attention-Discounted Adaptive Sampler), keeps the original sampler’s stopping rule and changes only the ranking step used to build the reveal set. It starts from the usual token-wise base score for each masked position, typically the confidence of the top predicted token. Instead of sorting positions independently by that score, ADAS builds the subset greedily. Once some positions have already been selected, each remaining candidate is discounted according to how strongly it attends to those selected positions and how uncertain those selected positions still are. Intuitively, if candidate i depends heavily on an already selected but still fragile position j, then the marginal value of committing i in the same step should be reduced.

The paper frames this as greedy construction under a non-additive subset utility: confidence acts as an individual utility proxy, while attention acts as a pairwise compatibility proxy. This is the key distinction from prior samplers. Top-k, Fast-dLLM, and EB-Sampler differ mostly in their budget constraints: fixed cardinality, confidence threshold, or entropy budget. They do not make the value of adding one position depend on which other positions are already in the subset. ADAS does exactly that, while remaining plug-compatible with all three.

A notable design choice is using attention as the dependency signal instead of explicit search, planning, or a verifier. The reason is practical: attention is already available from a single forward pass, so the method adds almost no modeling complexity. The paper also provides empirical evidence that masked-token self-attention separates dependent from non-dependent pairs in controlled settings. Implementation-wise, ADAS only modifies the greedy subset construction inside each denoising step, which is why it can be inserted into existing samplers with about 3.1% runtime overhead per model forward.1c:T6fc,The empirical section addresses two questions: whether joint dependence is a real failure mode, and whether ADAS improves quality in the low-NFE regime where aggressive parallelism matters most.

In the controlled diagnostic, the authors force the model to predict coupled literals jointly. Accuracy drops from 71% to 30%, showing that good marginal predictions do not imply safe joint commitment. They also show that masked-token self-attention differs between dependent and non-dependent token pairs, which supports the use of attention as a discount signal.

The main evaluation uses two MDLMs, LLaDA-8B-Base and Dream-7B-Base, on four benchmarks covering mathematical reasoning and code generation. The comparison is done at matched denoiser-evaluation budgets rather than at a single operating point, which is the right way to evaluate speed-quality tradeoffs. Adding ADAS to Top-k, EB-Sampler, and Fast-dLLM consistently improves performance, especially in the highly parallel low-NFE regime. Averaged across stopping criteria, ADAS improves matched-NFE performance by 9.11 points on LLaDA-8B-Base and 10.46 points on Dream-7B-Base. It yields positive gains on 80 out of 90 matched operating points.

The pattern of gains is also informative. The benefit is strongest when decoding is aggressive and token-wise ranking is most brittle. As NFE increases and decoding becomes more conservative, the advantage shrinks, which is consistent with the paper’s claim that ADAS addresses joint commitment errors rather than the model’s underlying conditional prediction quality. An important experimental choice is that the authors isolate ranking from stopping: they do not jointly tune both, so the observed gains are easier to attribute to dependency-aware subset construction itself.1d:T4c7,This is a skim paper, but it is worth a careful read if you are tracking diffusion or masked language modeling beyond autoregressive decoding. Focus on three parts.

First, read the introduction closely. The key idea is not merely “another sampler,” but the separation between stopping rules and subset ranking, and the claim that joint dependence error is the real bottleneck in aggressive parallel decoding.

Second, inspect the controlled diagnostic and the attention analysis. Those sections determine whether the method has a real mechanism behind it. If attention were not predictive of dependency, the whole discounting rule would be much less convincing.

Third, look at the matched-NFE result tables and plots. The important question is whether gains hold across many operating points and multiple samplers, not whether one cherry-picked speed setting improves.

A useful reading lens is to ask whether attention is a sufficiently robust proxy for dependency, or whether this works mainly on structured tasks like reasoning and code. Another good question is whether ADAS could serve as a target or inductive bias for learned unmasking policies. Around 30-45 minutes should be enough to extract the main ideas.1e:T80c,方法核心是 Gibbs-Accelerated Discrete Diffusion，简称 GADD。它属于 predictor-corrector 采样框架，但与已有 corrector 最大的区别是：corrector 不需要额外训练，而是直接利用 uniform-rate 离散扩散里已有的 concrete score function 构造 Gibbs 更新所需的后验似然。

论文的理论部分给出一个比较完整的误差分解框架。总误差被拆成三部分：初始化误差、corrector 的 mixing 误差、以及 score estimation 误差。作者的论证重点不只是“Gibbs 会混得更快”，而是 diffusion predictor 提供了一个 warm start，使得 corrector 不需要从远离目标分布的位置开始混合；同时，这个 warm start 还能减轻 score 估计误差在后续步骤中的放大。基于这个结构，论文给出 GADD 的整体采样复杂度为 O(polylog(ε^{-1}))。这点是全文最核心的理论主张。

Operationally, GADD keeps a diffusion-style predictor that moves the sample from a high-noise state toward the target distribution. A major part of the paper is the error-analysis framework. The total error is decomposed into initialization error, mixing error from the corrector, and score-estimation error. Relative to prior work, the differences are clear.
