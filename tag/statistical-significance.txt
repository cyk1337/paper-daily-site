18:T563,The method has three parts. First, the authors build a controlled coordination substrate, ET-MCP, an ephemeral trace store exposed through MCP. It stores typed negative-knowledge events, especially failed_path events, and uses a simple TF-IDF ranker for retrieval. This keeps the memory mechanism intentionally simple so the comparison focuses on protocol effects rather than retrieval sophistication. Second, they implement two reader variants on top of the same store. In pull, the agent explicitly calls trace.query when peer information may help. In intercept, the framework automatically checks the store on every tool call and prepends a [PEER-WARNING] block when the current (name, arguments) matches a peer failure event. Because both variants share the same backend state, the comparison isolates who reads and when. Third, they define a paired measurement protocol around configuration equivalence rather than identity replay. They audit system prompt, tool list, sampling parameters, and message-array construction by code inspection plus a SHA-256 byte audit, but do not claim byte-identical replay. They also introduce pass_active^k, a pass@k metric restricted to tasks where the coordination store is non-empty at trial start.
