18:T5df,The paper starts from a tightly controlled comparison: two 32-layer ViTs trained from scratch under identical settings, differing only in the attention operator—full attention versus Qwen-style chunk-window attention. Inspecting per-head attention maps, the authors observe that full-attention heads tend to specialize into semantic roles, with some focusing more on foreground objects and others on background regions. Chunk-window attention shows much weaker specialization and more uniform grid-like patterns. This phenomenon is named Semantic Head Specialization (SHS).

To quantify it, the paper introduces SHS-Index, an AUROC-based metric that measures how well a head separates foreground from background tokens. The metric is then evaluated on 16 open-source visual encoders and VLMs, where it cleanly separates full-attention and chunk-window families. The authors also show that changing only the LLM backbone size in Qwen2.5-VL barely changes SHS-Index, suggesting that the signal is driven by ViT attention design rather than language model scale.

The paper then analyzes three structural factors that affect SHS: window interaction, token serialization, and local softmax allocation. Based on these findings, it proposes Ariadne Attention, which combines sliding windows, per-head sink bias, and row-column alternating serialization.
