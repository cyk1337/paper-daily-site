18:T8d9,The authors build Multi-SFT, a 502,818-example instruction-response collection spanning five languages: English, Italian, French, Spanish, and German. It aggregates 13 heterogeneous datasets. The construction has two main pipelines. The translation pipeline is used when an English source can be meaningfully adapted into target languages. For each language, a native or near-native expert designs the translation prompt, chooses the model and decoding settings, and iterates with a separate QA reviewer until the outputs meet quality expectations. The regeneration pipeline is used when translation is insufficient, such as for conversational data, constrained instruction following, and safety behavior. In those cases, the target-language examples are regenerated from scratch using permissively licensed open-weight models.

The strongest part of the paper is its treatment of provenance. The authors start from 59 candidate datasets and exclude 45 due to licensing or content-origin violations. If a dataset card, repository, or paper indicates that entries were generated by restricted proprietary models such as GPT, Claude, or Gemini, the affected content is not accepted at face value even if the dataset itself carries a permissive label. When only part of a dataset is contaminated, they apply selective regeneration rather than discarding the entire source. The open models used for translation and regeneration include Mistral-Small, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct, and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, chosen for both capability and license compatibility.

There are also several construction details worth noting. In multi-smol_summarize, the authors do not merely translate summaries; they translate source documents, regenerate summaries, and expand the instruction variants to include structured output formats such as bullet lists and Markdown tables. For safety data, they keep harmful prompts from AdvBench but regenerate all responses as safe refusals, and they partition prompts across languages to avoid cross-lingual overlap. Aya red-teaming is repurposed into instruction-following data with safe targets rather than kept in its original adversarial form. These choices show that the dataset is curated as a training asset, not just translated as a corpus.19:T578,The experimental validation fine-tunes nine models from three families—EuroLLM, OLMo 2, and Qwen3—covering scales from 1B to 32B, and evaluates them on 13 benchmarks. The headline findings are twofold. First, Multi-SFT alone is stronger on non-English languages than the original English-centric source datasets, even though those sources may contain more content overall. Second, in multilingual settings it often outperforms official instruct releases while remaining competitive on English benchmarks.

Three aspects of the experiments matter most. The first is human evaluation. The paper reports inter-annotator agreement of at least 0.87 across all datasets and languages, which suggests that translation and regeneration were not treated as a loose automated pipeline but as an expert-reviewed process. The second is the effect of provenance-clean data on model quality. Removing closed-model-generated content does not appear to cause a major regression; instead, the resulting multilingual data often improves target-language performance. That supports the idea that language-aligned, high-quality, legally usable data can substitute for larger but noisier and legally problematic SFT mixtures. The third is robustness across model families. The gains are not tied to a single backbone, which makes the result more about the dataset itself than about a narrow model-specific interaction.1a:T4c4,The study uses 751 fine-tuning runs across five Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 32B parameters and Mistral-24B. On Qwen3-32B, using definitions as a source raises multi-hop accuracy by 5.3 percentage points, while adding multi-hop as a source for definitions lowers definitions accuracy by 1.9 points. Methodological critiques reduce multi-hop accuracy by 9 to 12 points across all Qwen3 sizes and Mistral-24B. Directionality is common: among 28 ordered task pairs, the two directions differ in 14 cases for Qwen and 13 for Mistral; opposite signs with intervals excluding zero appear in 3 and 4 pairs respectively. The paper also shows that mixture-size effects cannot be separated from average source effects when sources have their own coefficients; measured mixture-size slopes track mean source effect with r=0.90 over 12 cells. On Qwen3-32B, a uniform mixture of all non-target tasks barely improves multi-hop from 20% to 22% and critiques from 7% to 8%, while a transfer-map-selected mixture raises multi-hop to 34% and critiques to 16%. In two held-out mixture prediction tests, the transfer map has less than half the error of a mixture-agnostic baseline and beats the similarity selector at the tested mixture sizes.1b:T71d,The method has two central pieces. The first is a local geometric view around the reference model. The reference model is treated as a common origin in behavioral space, and the allowed drift budget defines a local boundary. Within that neighborhood, drift magnitude determines distance from the origin, while direction determines how the limited behavioral change is allocated across capabilities. This gives a common coordinate system in which heterogeneous updates can be compared by directional efficiency: task improvement per unit of behavioral drift.

The second piece is a coarse but systematic layer-selective probe. Rather than introducing a complicated regularizer, the authors vary which layers are trainable while keeping the data, objective, and optimizer fixed as much as possible. This isolates accessible update directions as the main experimental variable. The result is that several layer subsets provide nearby directions that improve the QA-only target task while preserving reasoning and general capabilities. Full fine-tuning often fails because its large update space includes many easy loss-reducing directions that overwrite existing behaviors. LoRA also restricts directions, but its low-rank subspace does not consistently contain the efficient ones, so it helps in some settings and not in others.

Mechanistically, the paper argues that QA-only supervision specifies the answer but not the reasoning behavior. Optimization therefore tends to exploit whichever behavioral rewrite most easily reduces training loss. If the update space allows the model to bypass or compress its original reasoning style, standard SFT will often take that route. Layer selection changes the reachable subspace, nudging optimization toward adaptations that reuse rather than replace existing reasoning circuits.1c:T68e,The experiments use Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B, with scientific reasoning and multilingual translation as the main tasks. The key empirical pattern is consistent: direct QA-only full fine-tuning increases target-task performance while steadily degrading downstream reasoning as functional drift grows. LoRA sometimes softens this trade-off, but not reliably. The layer-selective probe identifies multiple trainable-layer configurations that, at similar drift levels, improve the target task and preserve both reasoning and broader capabilities.

The translation results are the strongest practical validation. The resulting model improves over the reference model across more than 100 language directions and is reported to match or outperform dedicated multilingual systems such as Seed-X-PPO-7B and Tower-Plus-9B. The paper also claims that this SFT initialization beats a Qwen3-8B translation model trained with reinforcement learning across all evaluated directions, and that applying the same RL pipeline on top of the new initialization yields larger gains and the best final performance. That is important because it suggests the benefit is not a one-off SFT artifact; it changes the quality of the starting point for later RL.
