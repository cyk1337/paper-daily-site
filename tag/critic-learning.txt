18:T595,The authors use Monte Carlo continuations as a diagnostic estimate of intermediate state values. For a given prefix, they sample multiple independent continuations from the same policy and average the terminal rewards. These MC values reveal local changes in expected success along a trajectory. When compared with the PPO critic, the MC values often show sharp transitions, while critic predictions remain smooth and sometimes move in the wrong direction. A controlled stochastic FrozenLake experiment isolates the phenomenon further: as the maze size grows, the critic becomes smoother and less accurate. The analysis identifies two causes. First, in terminal-only reward settings, the standard token-level critic MSE contains an implicit penalty on variation among values within the same response, which encourages a flatter profile. Second, neighboring LLM states share almost all prefix tokens, so their representations and critic gradients are similar; dense supervision aggregates many near-duplicate updates and further pulls nearby predictions together. The proposed method, SP3O, sparsifies critic supervision by training the value function only on a small number of well-separated states. This reduces both the scope of the implicit variance penalty and the amount of redundant temporally correlated updating. The actor side remains in the PPO family; the main change is where and how densely the critic is supervised.19:T40d,The method, HL-Gauss PPO, replaces the scalar MSE value head with a categorical head over a discretized value support. The critic is trained with cross-entropy against smoothed HL-Gauss targets. The actor side is unchanged: the categorical distribution is decoded into a scalar expectation, which is then used in standard GAE and PPO updates. This is not a distributional policy-improvement method; classification is used only as a surrogate objective for learning a scalar critic.

The paper includes controls for one-hot and two-hot targets, a Bernoulli two-bin baseline, support choices, and smoothing. These ablations separate several possible explanations: binary classification, extra output-head capacity, target smoothing, and the value-support parameterization. The main diagnostics are calibration and raw advantage dynamics.
