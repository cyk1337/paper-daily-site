19:T5d4,The method has two stages. Offline, the authors build a task-specific bank of draft trees. Starting from a small tree, they greedily expand frontier nodes and score each one-node augmentation via self-rollout, estimating mean accepted length. A subtle point is that candidate trees are first scored with a larger joint tree in a one-step what-if probe, which makes the estimate mildly optimistic; they therefore recalibrate candidates using each tree’s own proposal, attention mask, and KV-cache update before final ranking. They then evaluate the full acceptance frontier in the actual end-to-end decoding loop, including prefill, tree decoding, posterior evaluation, verification, and KV updates, and keep only throughput-Pareto-optimal trees. Online, these trees are stored in a precompiled TopologyBank containing masks, position offsets, and gather indices, so switching trees is an O(1) pointer swap rather than a kernel rebuild. The scheduler reads readily available signals such as base-model top-1 probability, EAGLE-2 path value, and recent acceptance history, and selects a tree per step. Three policies are studied: a threshold ladder, a static task-optimal rule, and a hysteretic binary policy with separate on/off thresholds. The hysteretic policy works best because it captures the main large-tree/small-tree trade-off while avoiding per-step oscillation. The core idea is simple: the optimal speculation depth should track local predictability, not benchmark-level averages.1a:T443,Experiments focus on the low-latency batch_size=1 regime and cover GSM8K, HumanEval, and ShareGPT. EntMTP-τ outperforms Hydra, Medusa, and the static EntMTP* rule on all three tasks. On GSM8K it reaches 112.0 tok/s versus 102.4 tok/s for Hydra, a 9.4% gain and 3.13× speedup over autoregressive decoding. On HumanEval it reaches 124.7 tok/s, a 14.0% gain over Hydra and 3.26× over AR. On ShareGPT it reaches 117.5 tok/s versus 109.0 tok/s, a 7.8% gain and 3.47× over AR. Response perplexity stays within 0.022 of Hydra across settings, indicating that the gains come from better scheduling rather than a change in the verification rule. The most informative analysis is the task dependence of acceptance signals: on ShareGPT, recent acceptance history predicts next-step accept length with EMA correlation 0.49, whereas on GSM8K and HumanEval the strongest entropy-based predictors are at least 2× weaker, with |r|≤0.22. The paper also shows that maximizing accepted depth is not the same as maximizing throughput, because larger trees increase verifier and posterior-evaluation cost.1b:T4a1,The paper first introduces a forward-looking analysis metric, then proposes an entropy-aware KV selection scheme (InfoKV).

1) Forward Influence (analysis): for a past token xi, remove it from the KV cache and measure the average KL divergence between the original future predictive distributions and the distributions after removal over a future chunk. This quantifies how much xi affects future generation, not just recent attention.

2) Entropy as informativeness: use the entropy of the next-token predictive distribution as a proxy for uncertainty/information content. High-entropy contexts are argued to carry richer semantic information and to have more persistent influence on distant future steps.

3) InfoKV (method): incorporate entropy into token-importance scoring for KV retention, instead of relying purely on attention scores. The method also mentions combining entropy with a layer-wise signal based on representation evolution from intermediate to final layers, aiming to capture importance that is orthogonal to the sequence dimension.

4) Scope: designed to work for both long prefilling and long decoding, optimizing reasoning quality under constrained KV budgets.1c:T584,1) Factor decomposition: acceptance degradation during RL could come from (a) higher policy entropy for exploration, making drafts harder to predict, and (b) distribution mismatch because the policy updates while the MTP module is frozen. The authors’ analysis and experiments conclude entropy is dominant, mismatch is negligible (Fig.3).
2) Entropy bound insight:
- Under common greedy target-only verification (draft proposes argmax tokens; verification uses only target probabilities without caching draft probs), acceptance is upper-bounded by max_y p(y). As entropy increases, max probability drops, producing a near-linear acceptance decline.
- With probabilistic rejection sampling, acceptance becomes the distributional overlap sum_v min(p(v), q(v)), which is much less sensitive to entropy shifts because it depends on global overlap rather than peak mass.
3) Training objective alignment: CE/KL-trained MTP drafts are not optimized for rejection-sampling acceptance since they only indirectly affect overlap. The paper proposes an end-to-end TV (total variation) loss that directly optimizes the multi-step overlap relevant to rejection sampling, improving acceptance and keeping it entropy-invariant.
4) Deployment recipe: do a lightweight pre-RL MTP training phase with e2e TV loss, then use rejection sampling during RL rollouts; no online MTP training during RL, reducing memory/latency overhead.1d:T56c,The analysis starts from GRPO. For each prompt, the policy samples a group of responses, receives response-level rewards, normalizes advantages within the group, and optimizes a clipped policy objective. At a decoding state s_t, the next-token distribution is represented by softmax logits. For a sampled token y_t with advantage A, the update from A log π(y_t|s_t) increases the sampled token logit and redistributes probability mass according to the current distribution. The paper derives a first-order approximation of the local entropy change ΔH_t after this logit perturbation. The decomposition contains a sampled-token term and a state-wise distributional correction. Entropy polarity is the resulting signed quantity that predicts whether the update expands or contracts entropy and by how much. The asymmetry is important: reinforcing an already high-probability continuation tends to make the distribution sharper, while entropy expansion usually requires a lower-probability sample with positive advantage or a strong correction from the surrounding distribution. Papo then uses polarity as an online control signal. It reweights token-level advantages differently for positive- and negative-polarity updates, increasing entropy-expanding pressure when collapse is likely and allowing entropy-contracting updates when the policy needs to consolidate reward-aligned behavior.1e:T4b4,The method has two layers. The first is High Entropy Phase (HEP), a variable-length segment of consecutive high-entropy tokens. Instead of averaging entropy over fixed windows, the paper detects bursts: a phase starts when token entropy crosses a high threshold and ends only after a specified number of consecutive tokens fall below a low threshold. This is meant to smooth token-level noise and better align the signal with a coherent reasoning unit under uncertainty. The second layer is the Entropy Centroid. Each HEP is assigned a mass equal to its token length and a position equal to its midpoint in the trajectory. The centroid is then the weighted average position of all HEPs in the trajectory. At selection time, among N sampled trajectories, the method simply chooses the one with the lowest centroid. The intuition is that good trajectories tend to explore early and settle later, whereas bad trajectories remain uncertain late into generation. Compared with prior intrinsic signals such as mean entropy, confidence, KL, or fixed-window aggregation, this method explicitly encodes temporal placement of uncertainty. Compared with reward-model-based selection, it adds no extra training cost.1f:T43c,The evaluation spans models from 14B to 480B parameters and covers mathematics, code generation, logical reasoning, and agentic tasks. The main empirical claim is consistency: Lowest Centroid outperforms existing intrinsic baselines across settings, and the gains remain stable as model size increases. The analysis section appears to be more important than any single benchmark number. The paper shows that high-entropy tokens indeed occur in bursts rather than as isolated spikes, which justifies moving from tokens to phases. It also shows a separation between correct and incorrect trajectories in centroid space: correct trajectories concentrate uncertainty earlier, while incorrect ones exhibit more late-stage confusion. Relative to token-level entropy, confidence, KL-based signals, and fixed-window averages, the centroid better distinguishes useful exploration from unresolved uncertainty.
