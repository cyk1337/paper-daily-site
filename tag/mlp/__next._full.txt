18:T5fb,The authors construct a closed-form fact-storing MLP and show that it is equivalent to a Hebbian memory with a sketched quadratic kernel. The central technical move is to analyze decoding margin under softmax decoding. Earlier work typically assumes that the query is the clean key itself, so it only studies noiseless separability. Here, the query is treated as the imperfect output of attention, which means the MLP must create enough logit margin on the correct value to survive query noise and still support factual recall inside a Transformer block.

The theory proceeds in three layers. First, under favorable embedding geometry, the construction achieves fact capacity F=Θ(W/log W), closing a large optimality gap relative to prior constructions; the paper states a log^11 F improvement over earlier bounds in the isotropic setting. Second, the margin and capacity analysis is generalized to arbitrary embedding geometries through four multiplicative geometric statistics that capture clustering and anisotropy in key and value embeddings. Third, the construction is inserted into Transformer blocks, where the authors prove and demonstrate factual recall under noisy queries.

The paper also includes a practical extension, MLP Swapping. If an MLP can be treated as a modular fact memory, then one can edit knowledge by replacing the MLP rather than by distributed fine-tuning. That is not the main theoretical contribution, but it supports the broader claim that MLPs can function as explicit, manipulable memory modules.19:T5ff,There are four main empirical results.

First, on capacity and parameter efficiency, the proposed construction is far more efficient than the NTK MLP baseline at matched fact count. The reported gap ranges from 10x to 10^4x in parameter count, with the strongest gains coming from the best data-dependent kernel construction. This suggests that the asymptotic improvement is not merely formal.

Second, for arbitrary embedding geometries, the generalized theory predicts empirical decoding margin scaling very well, with R^2≥0.95. This is important because real LLM embeddings are strongly anisotropic; a theory restricted to isotropic embeddings would have limited explanatory power.

Third, inside Transformer blocks, the construction supports factual recall under noisy queries while retaining optimal-capacity scaling. Compared with the NTK baseline, Transformer blocks using the proposed construction require about 15x to 63x fewer parameters at matched fact count. The key point is not just memory in isolation, but memory that remains usable inside the Transformer computation.

Fourth, the MLP Swapping experiment shows near-perfect fact editing while avoiding off-target changes. Prior methods reportedly degrade to around 30% score when editing 10% of the fact set. This gives a concrete downstream consequence of modular fact storage.

The headline numbers to remember are F=Θ(W/log W), R^2≥0.95 for geometry-aware margin prediction, and the 10–10^4x plus 15–63x parameter-efficiency gains over NTK-based baselines.1a:T4f5,For a skim, start with Section 2.1 on decoding margin, then move to Sections 4 and 5. Section 2.1 contains the real conceptual difference from earlier fact-storage theories: the paper is not only about whether an MLP can memorize facts, but whether those facts can still be decoded under the noisy queries produced inside a Transformer. In Section 4, focus on the capacity theorem and the four geometric statistics introduced for arbitrary embedding geometries. The useful question is what each statistic penalizes and how that maps to anisotropic LLM representations. In Section 5, look at the Transformer factual-recall experiments and the MLP Swapping demonstration to judge whether the construction is more than a clean theoretical object.

If time is short, prioritize Figures 2, 3, and 5. Figure 2 shows parameter efficiency, Figure 3 shows factual recall inside Transformers, and Figure 5 shows how well the geometry-aware theory explains margin scaling. A 25 to 35 minute read is enough to capture the main line. One caution: this is primarily a theory-and-mechanism paper, not a direct study of knowledge capacity in fully pretrained frontier LLMs. It is best read as an explanatory model with possible design implications, not as an immediate training recipe.1b:T432,The paper proceeds from a controlled ablation to a general design principle.
1) Directionality test: On a ~440M Transformer, split layers into three blocks and reallocate only FFN/MLP intermediate width (d_ff) across blocks while keeping total parameters fixed. Compare uniform vs wider-early vs wider-middle vs wider-late.
2) Tapered Language Models (TLM): replace blocky reallocations with a smooth, monotonic decay of a parameter-bearing dimension across depth under a fixed total budget. The main instantiation is tapering d_ff using linear/cosine/sigmoid schedules (cosine tends to work best). The authors argue the same principle could apply to other depth-wise capacity knobs (heads, KV dim, state size, memory slots, MoE experts), but the empirical focus is FFN tapering.
For explanation, they quantify how much each layer writes “novel” content into the residual stream: deeper-layer MLP outputs become increasingly aligned with the existing residual, consistent with refinement rather than feature creation. Tapering reduces capacity where it is less utilized.1c:T865,The setup is deliberately controlled. The authors build synthetic arithmetic datasets with prompts of the form “Please calculate a +/- b =”. They constrain operands and results to be at most 520 so that both GPT-2 XL and GPT-NeoX-20B represent each number as a single token. This design choice is important: if numbers were split into multiple tokens, tokenizer effects would interfere with any interpretation of layerwise behavior.

The analysis has three parts. First, they study when the task is recognized and when the answer is formed. After each attention and MLP update, they project the residual stream through the LM head and inspect the intermediate top predictions. The main pattern is that arithmetic-task awareness appears early, but the correct result token only becomes dominant in the final layers. This suggests early layers set up the task context, while later layers write the answer.

Second, they study how input information is propagated. By tracking intermediate predictions and their relation to input tokens, they argue that in stronger models attention blocks are more involved in carrying operand and operator information forward, whereas MLP blocks are more involved in combining that information into an answer representation. This aligns with prior observations that attention often acts as a routing mechanism and MLPs as transformation/aggregation modules, but here the claim is grounded in arithmetic behavior.

Third, they examine how operands are combined, including both two-operand and three-operand settings. The evidence suggests that the model is not merely recalling frequent operand-result pairs. Instead, one operand often behaves like an argument or conditioning signal, while the other is transformed depending on the operator and that argument. The authors interpret this as evidence for function-like processing beyond pure factual retrieval.

Compared with stronger causal tools such as activation patching or systematic ablations, the paper relies mostly on observational evidence from early decoding. That makes the method simple and interpretable, but also limits the strength of causal claims.1d:T6bc,The experiments compare two decoder-only models with clearly different arithmetic ability: GPT-NeoX-20B and GPT-2 XL. For both addition and subtraction, the authors build small and large datasets; the small setting keeps operands and results within 99, and each dataset contains 500 unique queries. Across all settings, numbers are capped at 520 to preserve single-token representations. The main paper focuses on addition and reports subtraction in the appendix, noting that the qualitative patterns are similar.

There are four main findings. First, task recognition happens early, but correct answer generation happens late: intermediate logits indicate arithmetic-related processing in early and middle layers, while the correct result token usually stabilizes only near the top of the network. Second, the difference between the stronger and weaker model is not just final accuracy; it is also a difference in internal organization. GPT-NeoX-20B shows a clearer attention/MLP division of labor, while GPT-2 XL does not. Third, information propagation and result aggregation appear partially separable at the module level: attention updates more often align with input-token propagation, and MLP updates more often move the representation toward the final result. Fourth, in multi-operand settings the model behavior looks more compositional than simple token-level memorization.

This is not a benchmark-heavy paper. The evidence is mostly in layerwise trends, visualizations, and module comparisons rather than large tables of task scores. The most important experimental controls are the zero-shot setup and the single-token number constraint, because they make the interpretation of intermediate states much cleaner.1e:T4bf,For a skim, read it as a mechanism paper, not as a math-reasoning benchmark. Start with the introduction to understand the scope: the paper is about internal processing of arithmetic, not about improving arithmetic performance. Then read the methodology section carefully enough to understand how early decoding is applied and why the tokenizer control matters.

After that, go straight to the figures corresponding to the three experimental questions: when the answer appears across layers, how attention and MLP blocks differ in propagating versus aggregating information, and how operand combination behaves in two- and three-operand settings. The useful reading habit here is to connect each visualization to a specific mechanistic claim rather than treating the plots as generic interpretability figures.
