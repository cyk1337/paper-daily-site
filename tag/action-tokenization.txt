18:T539,Neural Action Codec (NAC) treats an action chunk (continuous trajectory of shape Ha × Da) as a multi-channel 1D signal and directly adopts a multi-scale RVQGAN architecture borrowed from audio codecs: a convolutional encoder downsamples to latent representations, residual vector quantization (RVQ) produces discrete codes at multiple scales, and a vocoder-style convolutional decoder reconstructs the signal. Key design trade-offs: (1) mel-spectrogram losses, standard in audio, are removed or heavily regularized because they model a nonlinear perceptual frequency scale that does not match the linear, kinematic semantics of robot motion; (2) time-domain reconstruction loss combined with multi-scale discriminator adversarial loss is introduced — the first use of adversarial training in an action tokenizer; (3) the downstream autoregressive behavioral-cloning policy exploits the natural causal structure of NAC's multi-scale codebooks via offset-style next-token prediction, aligning generation order with quantization hierarchy. Compared with uniform per-dimension binning, NAC uses learned codebooks to compress both temporal and channel dimensions.
