18:T79f,There are three core technical ideas. First, the task is cast as a POMDP. The model runs an Observation-Thought-Action loop, where actions request raw media slices such as frames, audio segments, or short clips. Crucially, the environment only serves raw media extraction; perception, reasoning, and action selection all happen inside the same model. The most important design choice is the strict split between transient multimodal percepts and persistent textual memory. High-dimensional observations are consumed locally within a turn, distilled into text, and then discarded. Long-horizon state is therefore carried by the reasoning trace rather than by retained raw pixels or audio tokens.

Second, the model is bootstrapped with Agentic SFT built from best-of-N trajectory synthesis plus dual-stage quality control. This is more than a data-generation detail. In active perception, answer correctness alone is not enough: a trajectory may reach the right answer through poor observation choices, and a plausible trajectory may still produce weak memory compression. The dual filtering is intended to keep trajectories that are both correct and learnable, so the model acquires action syntax, observation timing, and memory-writing behavior before RL refinement.

Third, the paper introduces Agentic RL with TAURA. The authors argue that vanilla GRPO is poorly matched to multi-turn agentic reasoning because it assigns a uniform trajectory-level advantage, which blurs the distinction between pivotal discovery turns and trivial actions. They call this advantage homogenization. TAURA uses turn-level entropy as a steering signal to rescale advantages, giving more weight to uncertain and potentially decisive turns. Relative to standard GRPO, this is a more targeted credit-assignment mechanism for interleaved perception and reasoning. Relative to pure supervised trajectory imitation, it preserves room for policy improvement and exploration.19:T78d,The experimental story has four notable parts. First, the model improves over the direct Qwen2.5-Omni baseline on all ten reported benchmarks. Representative numbers in the paper include 50.5 on LVBench and 71.1 on MLVU. On omni-modal understanding, it reports gains of +4.7 on DailyOmni and +7.8 on OmniVideo. On LongVALE temporal grounding, the gain is an absolute +33.4. This suggests the benefit is not confined to one narrow task type; it extends across long-video QA, audio-visual understanding, and temporal localization.

Second, the gains are tied to efficiency rather than brute-force scale. A 7B OmniAgent reportedly outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-72B on LVBench, 50.5 versus 47.3, while using 73% fewer frames. That is the most practically meaningful result in the paper. It indicates that a learned active-perception policy can trade far fewer raw visual tokens for better task performance, which is more interesting for foundation-model design than another attention-side optimization alone.

Third, the framework shows positive test-time scaling. The paper reports a +6.2 improvement on VideoMME-Long when allowing more inference-time interaction steps. This is important because it extends the idea of spending extra compute at test time from text-only chain-of-thought into multimodal active perception.

Fourth, RL and TAURA appear to matter beyond cosmetic gains. From the full paper narrative, SFT alone teaches basic action execution, but policy quality degrades in longer multi-turn settings through redundant browsing and weak credit on key discovery turns. RL improves the policy, and TAURA further helps by distinguishing pivotal turns from routine ones better than direct GRPO.
