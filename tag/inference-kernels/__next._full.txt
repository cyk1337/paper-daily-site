1b:T6d1,The method starts from MoEfication. Intermediate FFN neurons are clustered with balanced k-means and permuted into E contiguous blocks, each treated as an expert. This preserves the original FFN computation while exposing a structured expert decomposition.

The main change is the routing mechanism. LTE uses sigmoid scores and activates every expert whose score exceeds a threshold, which is effectively a set of independent Bernoulli-style decisions. PrunePath instead computes a softmax-normalized distribution over experts, sorts experts by probability, and activates the smallest prefix whose cumulative mass reaches a threshold tau. This introduces three useful properties:
1. experts compete with each other under normalization;
2. sparsity becomes a token-level global probability budget rather than an indirect threshold effect;
3. the number of active experts can vary across tokens.

Mechanistically, this sits between fixed top-k and independent thresholding. Fixed top-k is too rigid because every token gets the same expert count. Independent thresholding lacks a global budget and can over-activate. Cumulative-mass routing keeps adaptivity while making the budget explicit.

A second contribution is single-checkpoint dynamic sparsity. The model is trained progressively so that one checkpoint supports a range of tau values at inference time, tracing a smooth efficiency-accuracy frontier without retraining separate sparse variants.

On the systems side, the authors implement Triton kernels for sparse FFN inference in autoregressive decoding. This is important because many pruning papers stop at sparsity statistics or FLOP estimates; here the goal is actual peak-memory and latency reduction under a realistic serving path.1c:T628,The evaluation covers NLU, NLG, and instruction-tuning settings, with LTE and related sparsification baselines as the main references. Two findings stand out.

First, PrunePath improves the sparsity-performance trade-off in practical regimes, especially when only a small number of experts can be kept active. The paper argues that LTE wastes budget through conservative over-activation, while cumulative-mass routing concentrates computation on the most useful experts. Figure 2 is central here: under small retained-expert budgets, PrunePath yields lower reconstruction error than LTE.

Second, the single-checkpoint claim is backed by experiments sweeping tau at inference time. One trained checkpoint produces a relatively smooth efficiency-accuracy curve, rather than requiring separate training runs for each sparsity target. That suggests the progressive training recipe gives the router some robustness across budgets.

The systems section reports Triton-accelerated sparse FFN inference with reductions in peak memory and improvements in decode-only latency during autoregressive generation. The abstract and the provided text fragment do not expose all numeric details, so it is better not to invent exact values here. Still, the paper clearly treats hardware-realizable gains as a primary result, not a side note.
