9:T513,The method constructs a two-segment training example:
- Segment A: keep the original short context intact (no chunking), with normal low position indices.
- Segment B: append a short terminal prompt, but assign its position indices near the target maximum length (e.g., near 64K).

This creates, within a short sequence, both (i) local relative distances inside A (preserving clean next-token supervision) and (ii) very long relative distances between A and B (forcing the model to experience long-range RoPE distances during training).

The “terminal anchoring” choice is deliberate: placing the anchor at the end avoids breaking semantic continuity (unlike chunk-based simulated long-context training) and provides a stable structural cue that is less about memorizing specific tokens and more about the positional configuration.

Their analysis leverages RoPE: attention scores can be viewed as sums of sinusoidal components over relative distances; position interpolation effectively reduces frequencies, inducing smoothness over unobserved distances.
