18:T62b,The method has three main components. First, a physical mechanism interpreter maps each action-outcome branch to a distribution over latent mechanisms rather than a point estimate. This matters because different interventions constrain the underlying physics to different degrees: a strong push may sharply identify friction, while a weak interaction may leave many mechanisms plausible. Second, the paper introduces a shared-world evidence score. Instead of forcing branch posteriors to be identical, it measures whether all branches retain common support for a shared mechanism relative to an initial-scene prior p(z|I0). The prior correction is important: it prevents uninformative branches from appearing falsely consistent simply because they remain broad and uncertain. Third, the learned compatibility signal is injected into both training and sampling. The interpreter is trained using same-world groups versus mismatched-world groups, then frozen. Its evidence score is used during flow training and also as sampling-time guidance, so the generated rollouts are nudged toward sets of futures that admit one common physical explanation. A key design choice is weak coupling: the model does not force videos or latent posteriors to collapse to the same representation, only to remain jointly explainable by one mechanism. For evaluation, the paper defines the shared-world gap: fit physical parameters separately for each branch, then fit one shared parameter set jointly across branches; the extra error under the shared fit quantifies inconsistency across interventions.19:T454,Experiments are conducted in three controlled physical environments: Push Cube, Push Rope, and Pour Water, covering rigid-body, deformable, and particle-based dynamics. The main baseline is ACWM-DiT under the ACWM-Phys interaction setup. Relative to ACWM-DiT, OneWorld reduces the mean shared-world gap by 84.3%, which is the central result, and also improves mean M-MSE by about 7.7×10^-4, suggesting that consistency gains do not come at the expense of ordinary rollout quality. The most convincing part of the empirical section is the separation between single-branch plausibility and multi-branch physical compatibility: some baseline predictions fit physics reasonably well branch by branch, but fail badly once a single shared physical configuration is required. The paper also highlights the role of distributional mechanism inference, same-world versus mismatched-world interpreter training, and the use of the compatibility signal at both training and sampling time. The contribution is less about scale and more about closing the loop between formulation, training signal, and evaluation protocol.1a:T6ac,(must_read: detailed technical analysis)
1) Physics Evaluator: a unified evaluator covering four aspects—Geometric Priors, Contact, Stability, and Deployability—further decomposed into nine measurable constraints. A key challenge is differentiability: Contact and Stability are (partly) differentiable, while Geometric Priors and Deployability are inherently non-differentiable.
2) PhyMix framework: augments diffusion-based scene generation backbones with evaluator-driven implicit and explicit optimization.
- Implicit optimization (training): Scene-GRPO (Scene-level Group Relative Policy Optimization). Inspired by preference alignment in LLMs, the model samples multiple candidate layouts per input, ranks them using evaluator scores, and optimizes the generator to assign higher probability to physically feasible layouts—thereby injecting especially non-differentiable constraints into the sampling distribution.
- Explicit optimization (inference): TTO (Test-Time Optimizer). At test time, it explicitly refines object poses using differentiable physical constraints (primarily contact/stability) to fix remaining violations.
3) Closed-loop design: the evaluator serves as both the benchmark and the guidance signal; implicit optimization handles non-differentiable constraints at the distribution level, while explicit optimization performs continuous refinement for differentiable constraints.
