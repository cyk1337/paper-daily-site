9:T6ff,The method has two stages: watermark embedding and watermark detection.

In the embedding stage, the signal is injected at the dataset level by modifying word-pair co-occurrence statistics rather than token-level probabilities. The authors first select moderately frequent words from the dataset, avoiding extremely common words that would look too natural and very rare words that would yield weak signal. Random pairs from this pool form the secret key. For each selected word, lexical variants are generated with ChatGPT, including synonyms and commonly associated terms. The dataset is then rephrased so that selected word pairs co-occur more often: examples containing variants of both words become insertion candidates, while examples containing one but not the other become deletion candidates. A rewriting model edits the text while trying to preserve semantics and utility. The resulting watermark is therefore a distributional correlation pattern rather than a fixed phrase.

In the detection stage, the auditor queries the target model through its API, collects generated text, and measures whether the secret word pairs co-occur more often than non-selected pairs. A statistical test is then used to reject the null hypothesis. The paper emphasizes that the false-positive guarantee is distribution-free and model-agnostic. This is the main difference from prior methods such as STAMP, which require open-model access to compare perplexities, or radioactive-style approaches that rely on persistent token-level watermark traces.

The design trade-off is deliberate: by moving to coarse but aggregatable output statistics, the method becomes usable in the closed-model setting and admits cleaner statistical guarantees, at the cost of relying on a weaker and more indirect signal.a:T4fc,The empirical evaluation is centered on fine-tuning rather than large-scale pretraining. Across multiple model architectures and benchmark datasets, the method achieves statistically significant detection, with the paper explicitly reporting p<0.01. One notable result is that detection remains effective even when the watermarked dataset contributes only about 1% of the total fine-tuning tokens in a mixed-data regime.

The robustness evaluation tests lightweight text modifications such as random deletion, synonym substitution, and emoji insertion. Detection degrades only modestly, and the method appears more robust than prior approaches that depend on more fragile surface forms. On the utility side, the paper reports that benchmark semantics and task usefulness are largely preserved after watermark embedding.

The limitations are also important: the evidence is strongest for fine-tuning, not trillion-token pretraining; detection power depends on query budget and the amount of generated text collected; and aggressive downstream filtering or training dynamics could wash out the induced correlations.
