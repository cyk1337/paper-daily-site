First, it replaces the learned waveform encoder with a causal log-mel frontend: 80-dim log-mel features with a 25 ms window and 10 ms hop, giving a 100 Hz feature stream. A temporal patching module groups 16 frames and linearly projects them, reducing the rate directly to 6.25 Hz. This is a deliberate trade-off: less end-to-end flexibility, but a simpler and more stable streaming frontend.

Second, it swaps focal modulation for a transformer backbone and introduces ErfFormer. The block is LLaMA-like—grouped-query attention plus gated FFN with SiLU—but with two notable changes: no normalization layers and no positional encodings. RMSNorm is replaced by DynamicErf, motivated by lower compute cost and simpler streaming behavior. Positional encodings are removed entirely. The argument is that continuous acoustic inputs already contain local temporal structure, causal attention preserves order, and removing explicit position dependence avoids extrapolation issues in long-running streaming inference.

Third, the quantizer uses scalar spherical quantization (SSQ). The compressor output is projected to a 64-dimensional latent, normalized to the unit sphere, and each dimension is independently quantized into 4 scalar levels. That yields 64 two-bit symbols per frame, i.e. 128 bits per frame; at 6.25 frames/s this gives 0.80 kbps. The important point is the factorized bottleneck: compared with large codebooks or hierarchical VQ, SSQ is easier to scale and better suited to extremely low frame-rate settings.

Fourth, the training target is not just waveform reconstruction. The model distills WavLM layer-6 representations at scale, using about 94k hours of English speech, and reproduces WavLM-style noise and overlap augmentation to better match the teacher distribution. Choosing an intermediate WavLM layer is meaningful: it preserves a balance between semantic content and acoustic detail.

Fifth, the decoder is explicitly latency-aware, keeping theoretical latency at 160 ms. That aligns with the 160 ms temporal span of each token, showing that streamability is built into the representation rather than patched on later.
