a:T650,The pipeline has two stages. First, the authors run an SFT warm-up to enforce a step-tagged reasoning format. Starting from Video-R1-CoT-165k, they use Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct as a teacher to rewrite traces into explicit, non-trivial, non-redundant steps. A second-pass teacher filter checks answer fidelity, consistency between steps and final answer, and step quality. They keep 19k examples for SFT and sample 1,250 prompts for RL. This stage is mainly about making the policy emit parseable intermediate steps, which is necessary for later step-wise rollout scoring.

Second, they run GRPO with a rollout-based process reward. The key notion is continuation solvability. For a given step prefix, they condition on the partial trace, sample multiple continuations from the current policy, and compute the empirical success rate under the same final-answer verifier used in RLVR. That success rate becomes the process reward for the step. So a step is considered useful if it moves the model into a state from which the correct answer is more reachable under the current policy.

This differs from rule-based step rewards, which mainly reward structure or key phrases, and from explicit PRMs, which require a separately trained step scorer. It is also distinct from VinePPO: VinePPO uses rollout estimates for PPO-style value assignment in text-only settings, while ProcessThinker injects rollout-derived step rewards directly into a GRPO framework for multimodal reasoning. The final reward is a weighted combination of sparse outcome reward, rollout-based process reward, and lightweight formatting incentives.b:T4cb,The experiments are built on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct and focus on four video reasoning benchmarks. The paper reports consistent gains over the base model, and the most important ablation is that increasing the weight of the process reward leads to larger improvements. That suggests the dense step-level signal is doing real work rather than acting as a minor regularizer.

A few experimental details matter more than the headline numbers. First, the training budget is relatively modest: 19k step-tagged SFT examples and 1,250 RL prompts. This makes the paper more about reward design than about scaling data or compute. Second, the comparisons are structured to separate the effects of formatting SFT from the effects of rollout-based process rewards: base Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, ProcessThinker-SFT, outcome-only GRPO, and the full method. Third, the method has a clear compute trade-off. It avoids training a PRM, but it pays for that with online continuation sampling at many step prefixes. So the engineering simplification is not the same as lower total cost.

The experimental takeaway is narrower than 'this solves multimodal reasoning.'
