18:T7dc,The setup is deliberately controlled. Each document is a sequence of assignment statements of the form [ent] [attr] [val] [SEP], followed by a query [QUERY] [ent] [attr] [ARROW]; the target is the value currently bound to that slot. Bindings are resampled per document from disjoint vocabularies, and the stream is infinite, so the task cannot be solved by memorizing weights. For the queried slot, an old value v_old is written and then repeated R_old times, later replaced by a new value v_new written once, and the query appears ΔD statements after the final update. Because the current value appears exactly once while the superseded value appears R_old times, “most recent” and “rarest” always select the same answer on training data.

To read out the mechanism, the authors introduce a minimal causal edit: they invert the multiplicities of the competing values while holding the ground truth, total token count, answer position, and all other tokens fixed. They then measure the signed change in answer log-odds, Δ. Positive Δ indicates sensitivity consistent with rarity; negative Δ indicates that repetition boosts the value that should be suppressed. This is stronger than a correlational probe because it directly tests causal sensitivity to one statistic while preserving task semantics.

A second technical contribution is the analysis of an early training shortcut. Before a retrieval circuit forms, models learn to copy a value from a fixed token offset near the end of the document. That strategy depends on layout regularities rather than slot matching plus temporal retrieval. Its accuracy ceiling is predicted by 1/|supp(ΔD)|, and the paper shows the models nearly saturate that ceiling when the support is narrow. Applying the intervention during this phase yields strongly negative Δ, which would look like a clean frequency-sensitive regime if one ignored training stage. The paper uses this to argue that mechanism readouts must be interpreted jointly with training dynamics.19:T613,The main experiments use 26M-parameter transformers, with 75 total runs across redundancy settings. Several findings stand out. First, every analyzed cell reaches held-in accuracy of at least 0.999, including strata where the trivial heuristic “the last update is the answer” fails. So standard evaluation cannot distinguish opposite internal dependencies on repetition count. Second, under the causal edit, the same nominal configuration can separate sharply across seeds: in 13 of 25 cells, the sign fraction differs by more than 0.3 across three seeds, with the largest difference reaching 0.879, while the document-level standard error is only 0.025. That gap is too large to attribute to sampling noise. Third, within a single run, post-formation checkpoints can still vary substantially, with within-run standard deviation up to 0.303; doubling the training schedule also moves cells that the data distribution does not distinguish in advance. By contrast, the timing of retrieval formation remains stable across these comparisons. Fourth, a control edit on a non-queried slot changes the same number of tokens but stays within 0.015 nats, supporting the specificity of the intervention. Fifth, when pooled across seeds, sign fraction increases monotonically with redundancy level through at least R_old = 12, showing that redundancy changes the tendency of the readout without uniquely fixing the mechanism. The paper also explains why R_old = 1 and 2 are excluded and why 16 is the structural upper bound under the document layout constraints.1a:T48d,Start with the setup and the exact equivalence construction. The key point is not that one rule beats another, but that the objective is made exactly indifferent between them. Then focus on the minimal causal edit and the interpretation of the signed log-odds change Δ; that is the central measurement device. After that, read the section on the pre-retrieval fixed-offset copying phase. It is the part that prevents an overly simple reading of the intervention results and gives the paper its strongest training-dynamics insight.

If reading selectively, prioritize: the task generator, the main figure contrasting held-in accuracy with intervention-based separation, the seed/checkpoint/schedule instability evidence, and the control edit. That is enough to get the main message in about 30–45 minutes. For deeper study, the appendices on other candidate rules and the discussion of why timing is stable while mechanism identity is not are worth attention. The paper is best read as a cautionary note for mechanistic claims: before attributing a circuit to a corpus statistic, check whether the training distribution ever made the alternatives distinguishable.1b:T55b,The method is a deterministic short-window probe. Starting from the last two tokens, the model predicts the next token by argmax; the window is rolled forward and the process is iterated, producing a discrete dynamical system. From 96 random two-token starts, the authors measure the fixed-point fraction φ, the proportion of trajectories that terminate in a fixed point. They also classify trajectory geometry into funnel, none, fragmented, and borderline, which gives a coarse structural summary of the attractor landscape. Prefixes are added as wrappers to every forward pass without changing the estimator, so the observed effect is attributed to the conditional distribution rather than to the readout definition. The paper compares raw, BOS, and ordinary text prefixes across six models and sweeps window size. The interesting part is that the paper is organized around failed factorizations. Prefix length is not a monotone driver. Two proposed content-side factors fail after widening the sample. Model-side hypotheses such as a universal direction, bidirectionality as a model property, and instruct-resistance also fail. Even the nearest mechanistic account, attention-sink strength, matches the sign of the fixed-point shift in only 2 of 5 models. What remains is pairwise and model-conditioned: the same prefix can push four models toward 0 and two toward 1.1c:T426,A minimal Turing-complete tape automaton F (Brainfuck family, five instructions) is fixed, and all ~1.5 billion programs of length L≤13 are exhaustively enumerated under a bounded universal (Levin–Solomonoff) prior. Because enumeration is exact, the map from program behavior to prior mass is a sufficient statistic, and the posterior predictive for any few-shot context is computed in closed form. To remove output-polarity bias, F is complement-symmetrized into sF; each task is paired with its bitwise complement twin, on which the optimum scores identically, so the original-twin gap isolates the model's own inductive bias. Ground-truth certification uses GPU-accelerated enumeration, an independent pure-Python reference, and exact cross-checks (byte-for-byte agreement of per-output program counts, witness re-execution, bit-identical posterior agreement). Models are scored with a bounded symmetric divergence against the optimal posterior, summarized by a difficulty-aware score and facet profile across induction, continuation, and abduction tasks.1d:T40a,The work has three parts. First, it compares 12 pre-trained LLMs against a set of classical and learning-based HMM predictors over 75 HMM configurations and a range of context lengths. This behavioral comparison rules out a single universal explanation and narrows the plausible mechanisms to three ansatz classes: Linear n-gram, Non-linear n-gram, and Spectral. Second, it shows that Transformers can implement these classes in context. The unifying view expresses next-token probabilities as linear functions of nonlinear features computed from past observations, which covers finite-window predictors, nonlinear feature predictors, and spectral-style methods. A small Transformer trained on a single HMM is used to validate that a finite-window-like predictor can emerge naturally. Third, the paper introduces Principal Activations Probe, a layer-wise probing and intervention pipeline that finds low-dimensional algorithmic signals in the residual stream and then tests whether changing those signals causally changes predictions.1e:T42b,The work uses declarative, zero-exemplar in-context specifications to impose conceptual structures (ordered cycles, hierarchical trees) that are either congruent or conflicting with pretrained priors. Tests cover entity types with varying prior strength: strong cyclic concepts (weekdays, months, clock hours), weak cyclic priors (zodiac signs), non-cyclic concepts (musical notes), and arbitrary meaningless tokens. Entity representations are read from the residual stream at the last prompt token — the integrated state consumed by next-token computation, rather than states induced by structural queries — and geometry is measured via pairwise dissimilarity and representational similarity analysis (RSA). Key innovations include: demonstrating topology-level control (cycle vs. tree) rather than just within-shape shifts; using conflicting specifications to disentangle context vs. pretrained prior effects; and using activation patching (entity substitution) to verify the context-set geometry is causally operative in generation, not just a probe correlate.1f:T435,RouteBench constructs episodes with a support set and a query. The solver bank contains four clean representatives of different inductive biases: ridge-like solvers for global shrinkage, lasso-like solvers for sparsity, Huber-like solvers for robustness to heavy-tailed noise, and kNN-like solvers for locality. Route labels are never given to the dense Transformers. At evaluation time, the model answer is compared with the outputs of these solver families, and the closest solver family defines a behavioral route proxy.
