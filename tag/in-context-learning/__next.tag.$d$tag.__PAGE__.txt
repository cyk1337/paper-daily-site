作者分析的对象是 Yang et al. 先前识别出的三阶段电路：
1. SA heads：把具体 token 抽象成变量角色，例如第 1 位、第 2 位是否相同；
2. SI heads：在变量层面做序列归纳，识别当前 prompt 服从 ABA 还是 ABB；
3. Retrieval heads：把归纳出的变量关系映射回具体 token，输出答案。

方法上主要用了三类机制分析工具。

第一类是 causal mediation analysis。作者在不同 shot 数下，对不同 head 在特定位置做 patching，测量其对正确答案 logit 或准确率的因果贡献。这里有两个对比条件：abstract condition 用来识别 SA 和 SI，token condition 用来识别 Retrieval。然后按 stage 汇总 head 的 CMA 分数，并比较不同 shot 下 top heads 的重合度。

第二类是 topology stability 分析。作者用 rank-biased overlap 比较各 shot 下每个 stage 的 head 排名，想回答“shot 增加后是不是换了一批头在工作”。结果显示不是。大部分 stage 的 top heads 从 1-shot 到 10-shot 基本稳定，变化主要是同一批头的贡献强度上升。

第三类是 activation patching 与 function vector 干预。作者把高 shot prompt 中相关 heads 的激活补丁打到低 shot，甚至 0-shot prompt 中，观察能否直接救回性能。进一步，他们从显著 heads 提取 function vectors，缩放后注入 residual stream，测试它是否能替代 SI 阶段的计算。结果表明，FV 在很大程度上可以替代 induction stage 的输出，但仍依赖下游 Retrieval heads 完整存在，说明它不是凭空生成答案，而是在已有读出通路上提供了中间表示。

Prompts contain several three-token examples following either an ABA rule or an ABB rule, and the model must complete the final token of a query example. Because the tokens are arbitrary strings with no semantic content, the setup minimizes contamination from memorized semantics or world knowledge.

The analysis builds on the three-stage circuit identified in earlier work:
1. Symbolic Abstraction (SA) heads map concrete tokens into variable-like roles.
2. Symbolic Induction (SI) heads infer the underlying rule over those abstract roles.
3. Retrieval heads map the inferred relation back to the concrete answer token.

The paper uses three main tools.

First, causal mediation analysis (CMA). At different shot counts, the authors patch activations for specific heads and positions and measure their causal contribution to the model’s output. They use two contrastive conditions: an abstract condition to identify SA and SI heads, and a token condition to identify Retrieval heads.

Second, topology-stability analysis. They rank heads by stage-specific CMA score and compare rankings across shot counts using rank-biased overlap. This tests whether more examples cause the model to recruit different heads or simply strengthen the same ones.

Third, intervention experiments with activation patching and function vectors. The authors transplant activations from high-shot prompts into low-shot or zero-shot prompts, and they also extract function vectors from CMA-significant heads and inject them into the residual stream. This lets them test whether the induction-stage computation can be externally supplied, and whether downstream retrieval circuitry is still required.

A few design choices are especially good. The arbitrary-token task makes the symbolic-circuit interpretation more credible. The study spans three model families—Gemma 2-2B, Llama 3.1-8B, and Qwen 3-4B—rather than telling a single-model story. And the zero-shot analysis is careful: SA cannot be directly tested there because the relevant C positions do not exist, so the paper focuses on whether SI and Retrieval can already be activated downstream.b:T8ac,The main empirical result is that the circuit appears early and then gets amplified.

Gemma 2-2B provides the clearest transition: accuracy is only 17% at 1-shot, rises to 93% by 4-shot, and reaches 99% by 10-shot. Despite poor 1-shot behavior, CMA already detects the full three-stage circuit. From 1-shot to 10-shot, the dominant change is quantitative growth in head contribution, not a qualitative reorganization of the circuit. In Gemma, some key heads increase their causal contribution by roughly 8x. For example, the main SI head L14H0 grows from 0.9 to 7.4 on ABB, and the top Retrieval head L18H6 grows from 1.3 to 7.0.

Topology is stable across shot counts. In Gemma, the top-5 heads for SI and Retrieval overlap strongly across adjacent shots, with RBO around 0.81–0.94 versus a random baseline of 0.12. SA is somewhat noisier but still shows a persistent core. This supports the interpretation that demonstrations amplify an existing circuit rather than switch the model to a different mechanism.

The intervention results are the strongest evidence. Patching 10-shot activations from SA, SI, and Retrieval into 1-shot prompts raises accuracy to 88%. At 0-shot, SA cannot be tested directly, but patching only SI and Retrieval activations still lifts accuracy from 1% to 56%. That suggests the downstream infrastructure already exists even before any demonstrations are present.

Function-vector experiments push the argument further. A function vector extracted from CMA-significant heads and injected at 0-shot rescues accuracy up to 86%, but performance collapses to 13% when downstream Retrieval heads are ablated. So the function vector does not bypass the whole computation and directly write the answer; it largely substitutes for the SI-stage output while relying on intact Retrieval heads to read it out. The paper also notes that this substitution is more complete for ABB than for ABA, which hints that the two rules are not implemented in exactly the same way.

The same qualitative pattern appears in Llama 3.1-8B and Qwen 3-4B, though the behavioral transition is less dramatic in Llama because its 1-shot performance is already stronger. 第二步是线性 probe。作者从不同层的 residual stream 提取激活，用线性回归去预测 belief state 向量，并报告 R2。这里的关键不是单纯 probe 成功，而是 belief state 的几何结构在多个模型、多个 HMM、多个层位上都能被线性读出，说明这种表示不是高度非线性、零散分布的偶然编码，而更像是残差空间中的一个相对规整子空间。

第三步是因果干预。论文做了两类 intervention：patching 和 steering。patching 可以理解为把某个样本在 belief 子空间上的表示替换成另一个表示，再看输出分布如何变化；steering 则是在该子空间上定向移动激活。作者比较了“替换成正确 belief 点”“替换成无关 belief 点”以及未干预模型的预测质量。若 belief 子空间只是相关但无功能，干预不应系统性改变输出；但实验显示，沿 probe 识别出的子空间做正确干预时，模型预测仍接近未干预水平，而替换成不相关 simplex 点会明显恶化。这说明该子空间对下游 next-token prediction 有因果作用。

理论背景上，作者借用了 computational mechanics。这个框架认为，对一个部分可观测随机过程做最优预测，需要维护能压缩过去并保留未来预测信息的充分统计量；在 HMM 场景下，这个量正是 belief state。论文的贡献不是提出新理论，而是把这个理论从 toy transformer 扩展到现成开源 LLM，并给出可证伪的表示预测、probe 和 intervention 证据链。f:Ta4a,The setup is clean and theory-driven. Instead of using natural-language tasks with ambiguous latent structure, the authors construct edge-emitting HMMs and feed their emitted token sequences to pretrained LLMs. For every time step, the ground-truth belief state can be computed exactly from the HMM dynamics, so the paper has a precise external target for what the model should represent if it is doing optimal latent-state tracking.

The first step is process selection. The authors evaluate six open-source LLMs on many HMMs and keep 40 with non-trivial belief structure on which the models achieve near-optimal next-token prediction. This is an important choice. They are not asking whether belief states are decodable for arbitrary HMMs; they are asking whether, when the model already behaves close to optimally, the corresponding latent posterior is present in the activations.

The second step is linear probing. Residual-stream activations from different layers are used to predict the belief-state vector with linear probes, and performance is measured with R2. The significance is not merely that a probe works. The belief geometry is linearly recoverable across multiple models, multiple HMMs, and at different depths, which suggests that the representation is organized in a relatively structured subspace rather than being diffusely encoded in a highly nonlinear way.

The third step is causal intervention. The paper uses patching and steering targeted at the probe-identified belief subspace. Roughly, patching replaces the belief-subspace component of one activation with another, while steering moves the activation along directions in that subspace. The authors compare interventions that preserve the correct belief geometry against interventions that substitute an unrelated point on the simplex. If the subspace were merely correlated with prediction, these manipulations should not systematically matter. Instead, interventions aligned with the identified belief state preserve prediction quality near the untampered model, while unrelated substitutions degrade it substantially. That makes the case functional rather than purely correlational.

The theoretical framing comes from computational mechanics. In that view, optimal prediction for a partially observed stochastic process requires tracking a sufficient statistic that compresses the past while preserving predictive information about the future. For HMMs, that statistic is exactly the belief state. The paper’s contribution is to carry this prediction from toy models into pretrained open-source LLMs and test it with both probes and interventions.10:T744,The study covers six open-source LLMs and 40 HMMs. The headline result is that belief states are linearly decodable from residual-stream activations, with peak probe R2 values ranging from 0.83 to 0.99 depending on the model and process. The best layer is not fixed: for some model–HMM pairs the strongest signal appears relatively early, for others later. That suggests belief-state formation is distributed and architecture-dependent rather than confined to the final layers.

A strong part of the experimental design is the behavioral filter. The authors first verify that the model is already near-optimal on next-token prediction for the selected HMMs, and only then analyze representations. This helps separate “the model never learned the process” from “the model learned it but represents it differently.”

The intervention results are the main functional evidence. When patching or steering within the identified belief subspace in a way that preserves the appropriate belief geometry, the KL divergence to the ground-truth next-token distribution remains on the same order as the untampered model. When the activation is replaced with an unrelated point on the belief simplex, prediction quality drops substantially. The abstract does not list all detailed numbers, but the qualitative pattern is consistent and important: correct manipulations of the belief subspace preserve behavior, incorrect ones damage it.
