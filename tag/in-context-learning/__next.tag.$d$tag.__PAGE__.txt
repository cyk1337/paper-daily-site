9:T959,方法设计很干净。作者不从自然语言任务入手，而是构造一组 edge-emitting HMM，让模型读取由这些 HMM 生成的 token 序列，并预测下一个 token。对于每个时间步，真实 belief state 都可以由 HMM 的转移与发射概率精确算出，因此有了一个外部可监督的、理论上正确的表示目标。

论文的第一步是任务筛选。作者在 40 个具有非平凡 belief 结构的 HMM 上评估六个开源 LLM，只保留那些模型已经能接近最优 next-token 预测的过程。这样做是为了避免 probe 结果被“模型根本没学会这个过程”污染。换句话说，他们不是在问任何 HMM 上都能不能读出 belief state，而是在问：当模型行为上已经接近最优时，内部是否真的形成了对应的状态表示。

第二步是线性 probe。作者从不同层的 residual stream 提取激活，用线性回归去预测 belief state 向量，并报告 R2。这里的关键不是单纯 probe 成功，而是 belief state 的几何结构在多个模型、多个 HMM、多个层位上都能被线性读出，说明这种表示不是高度非线性、零散分布的偶然编码，而更像是残差空间中的一个相对规整子空间。

第三步是因果干预。论文做了两类 intervention：patching 和 steering。patching 可以理解为把某个样本在 belief 子空间上的表示替换成另一个表示，再看输出分布如何变化；steering 则是在该子空间上定向移动激活。作者比较了“替换成正确 belief 点”“替换成无关 belief 点”以及未干预模型的预测质量。若 belief 子空间只是相关但无功能，干预不应系统性改变输出；但实验显示，沿 probe 识别出的子空间做正确干预时，模型预测仍接近未干预水平，而替换成不相关 simplex 点会明显恶化。这说明该子空间对下游 next-token prediction 有因果作用。

理论背景上，作者借用了 computational mechanics。这个框架认为，对一个部分可观测随机过程做最优预测，需要维护能压缩过去并保留未来预测信息的充分统计量；在 HMM 场景下，这个量正是 belief state。论文的贡献不是提出新理论，而是把这个理论从 toy transformer 扩展到现成开源 LLM，并给出可证伪的表示预测、probe 和 intervention 证据链。a:Ta4a,The setup is clean and theory-driven. Instead of using natural-language tasks with ambiguous latent structure, the authors construct edge-emitting HMMs and feed their emitted token sequences to pretrained LLMs. For every time step, the ground-truth belief state can be computed exactly from the HMM dynamics, so the paper has a precise external target for what the model should represent if it is doing optimal latent-state tracking.

The first step is process selection. The authors evaluate six open-source LLMs on many HMMs and keep 40 with non-trivial belief structure on which the models achieve near-optimal next-token prediction. This is an important choice. They are not asking whether belief states are decodable for arbitrary HMMs; they are asking whether, when the model already behaves close to optimally, the corresponding latent posterior is present in the activations.

The second step is linear probing. Residual-stream activations from different layers are used to predict the belief-state vector with linear probes, and performance is measured with R2. The significance is not merely that a probe works. The belief geometry is linearly recoverable across multiple models, multiple HMMs, and at different depths, which suggests that the representation is organized in a relatively structured subspace rather than being diffusely encoded in a highly nonlinear way.

The third step is causal intervention. The paper uses patching and steering targeted at the probe-identified belief subspace. Roughly, patching replaces the belief-subspace component of one activation with another, while steering moves the activation along directions in that subspace. The authors compare interventions that preserve the correct belief geometry against interventions that substitute an unrelated point on the simplex. If the subspace were merely correlated with prediction, these manipulations should not systematically matter. Instead, interventions aligned with the identified belief state preserve prediction quality near the untampered model, while unrelated substitutions degrade it substantially. That makes the case functional rather than purely correlational.

The theoretical framing comes from computational mechanics. In that view, optimal prediction for a partially observed stochastic process requires tracking a sufficient statistic that compresses the past while preserving predictive information about the future. For HMMs, that statistic is exactly the belief state. The paper’s contribution is to carry this prediction from toy models into pretrained open-source LLMs and test it with both probes and interventions.b:T744,The study covers six open-source LLMs and 40 HMMs. The headline result is that belief states are linearly decodable from residual-stream activations, with peak probe R2 values ranging from 0.83 to 0.99 depending on the model and process. The best layer is not fixed: for some model–HMM pairs the strongest signal appears relatively early, for others later. That suggests belief-state formation is distributed and architecture-dependent rather than confined to the final layers.

A strong part of the experimental design is the behavioral filter. The authors first verify that the model is already near-optimal on next-token prediction for the selected HMMs, and only then analyze representations. This helps separate “the model never learned the process” from “the model learned it but represents it differently.”

The intervention results are the main functional evidence. When patching or steering within the identified belief subspace in a way that preserves the appropriate belief geometry, the KL divergence to the ground-truth next-token distribution remains on the same order as the untampered model. When the activation is replaced with an unrelated point on the belief simplex, prediction quality drops substantially. The abstract does not list all detailed numbers, but the qualitative pattern is consistent and important: correct manipulations of the belief subspace preserve behavior, incorrect ones damage it.

Another good design choice is the use of HMMs with non-trivial belief structure, rather than processes where the next-token distribution already fully captures the hidden dynamics. The paper also includes a contrasting case, Mess3, where hidden-state dynamics are no richer than the observable predictive distribution. That helps rule out the simpler interpretation that the probe is only reading out shallow token statistics.c:T4b8,Read this one with a specific question in mind: is the paper showing a real mechanism or just a probe artifact? Start with the introduction and Figure 1 to understand the exact hypothesis: not generic latent-state encoding, but belief-state geometry predicted by computational mechanics. Then read the background section on HMMs and belief states carefully so the probe target is unambiguous.

The most important part of the main text is the experimental design: how the 40 HMMs are selected, why near-optimal behavioral performance is required before probing, where in the residual stream the probes are applied, and what the control conditions are. Without those details, the probe results are easy to overinterpret.

After that, focus on the intervention section. The patching and steering constructions, and the use of KL divergence to the ground-truth next-token distribution as the evaluation metric, are what elevate the paper from representational correlation to something closer to mechanistic evidence. The authors first sample an ICL episode in a modality-agnostic canonical form (hidden rule + demonstrations + query), then render it into two twins: a text twin (shape names as tokens) and an image twin (the same shapes rendered visually). Crucially, answers and targets remain textual in both conditions, so the comparison isolates the effect of presenting evidence visually.

The benchmark uses a shared vocabulary of 12 named shapes and defines 38 procedurally generated tasks grouped into four families: Selection, Relation, Aggregation, and Transformation. A subset is organized into position-controlled groups to probe position/window effects while holding other factors fixed.

Beyond measuring the modality gap across models and shot counts, the paper runs (i) recovery interventions targeting visual access, task framing, and reasoning support, and (ii) decomposition variants that separate induction (inferring the rule from demonstrations) from execution (carrying out a stated rule with multimodal inputs). The key methodological point is that the gap persists even when the task is stated directly, implying a more basic cross-modal execution difficulty; demonstrations then act as both evidence and additional context load, especially in multi-image settings.e:T410,Evaluations span 6 open-weight models, 38 tasks, and multiple shot settings. The main empirical finding is a consistent drop when the same ICL evidence is presented as images rather than text, across all tested model–shot combinations. The gap magnitude varies by task family, with larger gaps reported for Selection and Transformation. Another notable result is that strong text-only ICL does not reliably transfer to multimodal ICL.

On a diagnostic subset of high-gap tasks, individual interventions (visual access / framing / reasoning) show limited or inconsistent gains, while their combination can recover strong multimodal ICL performance (illustrated in the paper’s main figure). Decomposition experiments show the gap remains even without induction (rule given explicitly), supporting the interpretation that execution with visual evidence is a core bottleneck. Each document is a sequence of assignment statements of the form [ent] [attr] [val] [SEP], followed by a query [QUERY] [ent] [attr] [ARROW]; the target is the value currently bound to that slot. Bindings are resampled per document from disjoint vocabularies, and the stream is infinite, so the task cannot be solved by memorizing weights. For the queried slot, an old value v_old is written and then repeated R_old times, later replaced by a new value v_new written once, and the query appears ΔD statements after the final update. Because the current value appears exactly once while the superseded value appears R_old times, “most recent” and “rarest” always select the same answer on training data.

To read out the mechanism, the authors introduce a minimal causal edit: they invert the multiplicities of the competing values while holding the ground truth, total token count, answer position, and all other tokens fixed. They then measure the signed change in answer log-odds, Δ. Positive Δ indicates sensitivity consistent with rarity; negative Δ indicates that repetition boosts the value that should be suppressed. This is stronger than a correlational probe because it directly tests causal sensitivity to one statistic while preserving task semantics.

A second technical contribution is the analysis of an early training shortcut. Before a retrieval circuit forms, models learn to copy a value from a fixed token offset near the end of the document. That strategy depends on layout regularities rather than slot matching plus temporal retrieval. Its accuracy ceiling is predicted by 1/|supp(ΔD)|, and the paper shows the models nearly saturate that ceiling when the support is narrow. Applying the intervention during this phase yields strongly negative Δ, which would look like a clean frequency-sensitive regime if one ignored training stage. The paper uses this to argue that mechanism readouts must be interpreted jointly with training dynamics.10:T613,The main experiments use 26M-parameter transformers, with 75 total runs across redundancy settings. Several findings stand out. First, every analyzed cell reaches held-in accuracy of at least 0.999, including strata where the trivial heuristic “the last update is the answer” fails. So standard evaluation cannot distinguish opposite internal dependencies on repetition count. Second, under the causal edit, the same nominal configuration can separate sharply across seeds: in 13 of 25 cells, the sign fraction differs by more than 0.3 across three seeds, with the largest difference reaching 0.879, while the document-level standard error is only 0.025. That gap is too large to attribute to sampling noise. Third, within a single run, post-formation checkpoints can still vary substantially, with within-run standard deviation up to 0.303; doubling the training schedule also moves cells that the data distribution does not distinguish in advance. By contrast, the timing of retrieval formation remains stable across these comparisons. Fourth, a control edit on a non-queried slot changes the same number of tokens but stays within 0.015 nats, supporting the specificity of the intervention. Fifth, when pooled across seeds, sign fraction increases monotonically with redundancy level through at least R_old = 12, showing that redundancy changes the tendency of the readout without uniquely fixing the mechanism.
