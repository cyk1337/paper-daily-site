1a:T59e,Cognitive Pairwise Training (CPT) trains the policy model on pairwise comparisons of reasoning traces.

- Supervision format: input u = (problem x, reference answer a*, trace τA, trace τB). The model outputs a short comparative rationale plus a final label indicating which trace is more trustworthy.
- Key design choice: pairwise preference is used instead of pointwise scoring, motivated by higher label stability and reduced biases (length/style/template effects) that often contaminate single-trace grading.
- Data pipeline (as described at a high level): construct trace pairs from existing rollouts; use a strong teacher/judge to label which trace is more reliable; mid-train the target model on this comparison task.
- No external reward model: the “discriminator” is absorbed into the policy via supervised training, reducing RM–policy mismatch and system complexity.
- Placement before RL: the intent is to shape an internal reliability criterion first, then run downstream RL (e.g., RLVR) without immediately drifting toward overconfident policies driven by outcome-only rewards.

Relative to alternatives:
- Versus refusal SFT / abstention-aware RL: CPT targets internal reasoning-quality discrimination rather than explicit refusal behaviors.
- Versus reward-model training: CPT distills judgments directly into the policy, trading off flexibility for simplicity and potentially better generalization of the criterion.1b:T48d,Reported experiments span 3B/4B/8B/14B/32B across LLaMA, Qwen, and Olmo families, evaluated on seven math reasoning benchmarks plus AbstentionBench, with controlled compute/data budgets against strong baselines (SFT, standard SFT+RL, abstention-focused methods).

The headline claim is Pareto improvement: across 4B–32B, CPT variants more consistently sit on the frontier of (math reasoning score, abstention quality). A concrete number given in the introduction: at 14B, CPT+RL improves math reasoning by +2.2 and abstention by +5.6 over a math-optimized standard SFT+RL pipeline.

Additional evidence mentioned: better-quality reasoning traces (§6.2), generalization to RAG-style uncertainty handling (Table 4), and robustness/scalability analyses in appendices. The paper also includes judge-pipeline validation (Appendix E) and dataset audits (Appendix G), which are crucial given the reliance on LLM-as-a-judge labels.

Note: the provided full-text excerpt is truncated before the detailed experimental tables and ablations; exact per-benchmark numbers, data sizes, and training hyperparameters should be verified in the main results section and appendices.1c:T8f5,方法由三部分组成。第一部分是 Strategy Nudging。做法很直接：在同一个问题输入前后，附加一些轻量的、启发式的策略级文本上下文，让模型分别沿不同 reasoning mode 采样。例如对数学题，可以提示代数法、几何法、分步验证、边界检查之类。关键点不在提示词本身多精细，而在于它把原本混在一起的推理空间显式分桶，让 rollout 不再只是从一个单峰分布里反复抽样，而是被迫访问多个潜在子空间。作者强调这和 oracle-guided 方法不同：它不提供正确解，也不提供中间步骤，只提供“往哪个方向想”的弱引导，因此保留了发现新解法的空间。

第二部分是 Inter-Intra group advantage。标准 GRPO 假设同一 prompt 下的一组 rollout 来自同一采样条件，因此组内 reward 的均值和方差可以拿来做相对 advantage 归一化。NudgeRL 里不同 rollout 来自不同 strategy context，reward 差异既包含轨迹质量，也包含 context 本身带来的系统偏置。如果仍按原始 group-wise normalization 做，credit assignment 会失真：某些策略组可能整体更难或更易，导致组内相对分数不能横向比较。作者因此把 advantage 估计拆成 intra-group 和 inter-group 两层，既保留同一策略组内部的相对排序，又引入跨策略组的校正，让更新信号能区分“这个 rollout 在本策略下好不好”和“这个策略整体值不值得学”。从问题设定看，这一步是方法成立的关键，不然 strategy nudging 只会把采样分布打散，却未必能稳定训练。

第三部分是 distillation-augmented objective。因为训练时 rollout 是在带 strategy context 的条件下采样，而测试时通常不会给这些 context，所以存在明显的 train-test mismatch。作者的处理是把在 context-forced exploration 中发现的有效行为蒸馏回 base policy，让基础模型在无额外提示时也能复现这些更优轨迹。这个设计很像“探索策略”和“部署策略”解耦：前者负责发现，后者负责吸收。相比直接把带 context 的 policy 当最终模型，这样更符合 RLVR 的实际使用方式，也减少了推理时额外 prompt 工程的依赖。1d:T840,The method has three main components. First, Strategy Nudging. For each prompt, the model is asked to generate rollouts under lightweight strategy-level textual contexts, such as hints that encourage different reasoning modes. The key idea is not that the prompts are highly informative, but that they partition the reasoning space into multiple semantically meaningful subspaces. Instead of repeatedly sampling from one dominant mode, the policy is nudged to visit alternative trajectories it would otherwise ignore. Unlike oracle-guided methods, these nudges do not reveal the answer or intermediate steps; they only bias the search direction, leaving room for novel successful trajectories to emerge.

Second, Inter-Intra group advantage. Standard GRPO assumes that rollouts for a prompt are sampled under the same condition, so within-group reward normalization is meaningful. Here, rollouts come from different strategy contexts, and reward variation mixes two factors: trajectory quality and context-specific bias. A naive group-relative normalization would therefore distort credit assignment. The proposed estimator separates intra-context comparison from inter-context calibration, so the update can capture both whether a rollout is strong within its own strategy group and whether that strategy is globally useful. This is likely the central technical piece: without it, strategy-conditioned exploration may diversify samples but produce unstable or misleading policy updates.

Third, a distillation-augmented objective. Training-time exploration is performed under explicit strategy contexts, but inference typically uses the base prompt only. That creates a train-test mismatch. The paper addresses this by distilling useful behaviors discovered under context-forced exploration back into the base policy. Conceptually, this decouples discovery from deployment: the conditioned policy explores, the unconditioned policy absorbs. This is a practical design choice, because it avoids relying on extra prompting at inference time and makes the learned gains usable in standard RLVR deployment settings.1e:T599,The headline result stated in the paper is that NudgeRL outperforms standard GRPO even when GRPO is given an 8x larger rollout budget, and it also beats oracle-guided baselines. The paper also grounds its motivation in prior analysis of one-step RLVR improvement, where unsampled correct token mass acts as a dominant negative term; this gives a clear mechanistic expectation for the experiments: a successful method should discover low-probability but correct reasoning paths more often under the same rollout budget.

The training component, CASPO (Confidence-Aware Step-wise Preference Optimization), aims to align token- or step-level confidence with logical correctness. The motivation is that raw token probabilities mostly reflect fluency and frequency, not deductive validity. To fix this, the method constructs step-wise preference pairs that contrast logically correct but under-confident steps against confidently wrong steps. These pairs are optimized with iterative DPO, pushing probability mass away from invalid but high-confidence continuations and toward valid reasoning steps. The intended outcome is not only better step selection, but better calibration: correct steps should correspond to lower-entropy, more concentrated predictive distributions.

The inference component, Confidence-aware Thought (CaT), uses this calibrated confidence to guide reasoning-tree exploration. High-confidence branches are expanded, while uncertain branches are pruned, with claimed overhead on the order of O(V). The key point is that this is not just beam search over raw token probabilities. CaT depends on CASPO having made confidence more semantically meaningful; otherwise token probabilities would still favor fluent but logically weak steps.

Relative to prior step-wise preference or process-reward methods, CASPO avoids training a separate reward model and instead performs iterative calibration directly through DPO-style optimization.
