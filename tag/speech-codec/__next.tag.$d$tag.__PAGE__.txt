9:Tab1,方法上，ZipCodec 继承 FocalCodec-Stream 的整体五段式结构：encoder / compressor / quantizer / decompressor / decoder，但几乎每一段都重做了。

第一，输入前端从 learned waveform encoder 改成 causal log-mel frontend。具体是 25 ms 窗、10 ms hop、80 维 log-mel，先得到 100 Hz 连续特征，再用 temporal patching 把连续 16 帧拼成一个 patch，直接把帧率降到 6.25 Hz。这样做的取舍很明确：放弃从原始波形端到端学前端，换来更稳定、更轻的流式输入表征，也减少了前端卷积堆栈的复杂度。

第二，主干从 focal modulation 改成 transformer，并提出 ErfFormer。ErfFormer 基本沿用 LLaMA 风格 block：GQA attention + gated FFN + SiLU，但做了两个关键删改。
1) 去掉 normalization，用 DynamicErf 替代 RMSNorm。作者的动机是降低计算和内存开销，同时避免 norm 在流式长时间运行中的一些数值和实现负担。
2) 完全去掉 positional encoding。作者认为连续声学特征本身带局部时序结构，因果注意力已经保留顺序；更重要的是，去掉显式位置后，流式推理不再受训练时上下文长度和位置外推问题约束。这一点对长时在线语音流尤其关键。

第三，量化器采用 scalar spherical quantization, SSQ。做法是先把 compressor 输出投到 L=64 维潜空间，再归一化到单位球面；随后每一维独立量化到 K=4 个标量级别。于是每帧得到 64 个 2-bit symbol，总计 128 bit/frame；乘以 6.25 frame/s，得到 0.80 kbps。这里的核心不是单纯低码率，而是“因子化 bottleneck”：相比单个大码本或多级 VQ，SSQ 更容易扩展、码本利用更均匀，也更适合后续把离散单元当 token 序列处理。

第四，训练目标不是只做波形重建，而是显式蒸馏 WavLM layer-6 表征。作者把大规模 English speech 上的 WavLM 中层表示作为语义-声学折中目标，并复现 WavLM 原始训练中的噪声和重叠说话增强，以缩小 teacher-student 分布差异。选 layer-6 而不是更高层，说明作者希望保留较强语音内容和一定声学细节，而不是过度语义化。

第五，decoder 侧做了 latency-aware streaming 设计，使理论延迟控制在 160 ms。这个数字和 6.25 Hz patch 长度一致，说明整个系统在结构上围绕单 token 覆盖时长来设计，而不是后面再补流式约束。

整体上，这不是“把模型做大”这么简单。ZipCodec 的关键在于把低帧率、流式性、表征蒸馏和可部署性一起联立优化。842M 参数看起来很大，但大头在表征能力和低帧率信息瓶颈补偿，而不是追求离线重建分数。a:T9c0,ZipCodec keeps the high-level FocalCodec-Stream pipeline—encoder, compressor, quantizer, decompressor, decoder—but redesigns most components.

First, it replaces the learned waveform encoder with a causal log-mel frontend: 80-dim log-mel features with a 25 ms window and 10 ms hop, giving a 100 Hz feature stream. A temporal patching module groups 16 frames and linearly projects them, reducing the rate directly to 6.25 Hz. This is a deliberate trade-off: less end-to-end flexibility, but a simpler and more stable streaming frontend.

Second, it swaps focal modulation for a transformer backbone and introduces ErfFormer. The block is LLaMA-like—grouped-query attention plus gated FFN with SiLU—but with two notable changes: no normalization layers and no positional encodings. RMSNorm is replaced by DynamicErf, motivated by lower compute cost and simpler streaming behavior. Positional encodings are removed entirely. The argument is that continuous acoustic inputs already contain local temporal structure, causal attention preserves order, and removing explicit position dependence avoids extrapolation issues in long-running streaming inference.

Third, the quantizer uses scalar spherical quantization (SSQ). The compressor output is projected to a 64-dimensional latent, normalized to the unit sphere, and each dimension is independently quantized into 4 scalar levels. That yields 64 two-bit symbols per frame, i.e. 128 bits per frame; at 6.25 frames/s this gives 0.80 kbps. The important point is the factorized bottleneck: compared with large codebooks or hierarchical VQ, SSQ is easier to scale and better suited to extremely low frame-rate settings.

Fourth, the training target is not just waveform reconstruction. The model distills WavLM layer-6 representations at scale, using about 94k hours of English speech, and reproduces WavLM-style noise and overlap augmentation to better match the teacher distribution. Choosing an intermediate WavLM layer is meaningful: it preserves a balance between semantic content and acoustic detail.

Fifth, the decoder is explicitly latency-aware, keeping theoretical latency at 160 ms. That aligns with the 160 ms temporal span of each token, showing that streamability is built into the representation rather than patched on later.

Overall, the contribution is not merely scaling model size. The paper jointly optimizes low frame rate, streamability, representational quality, and deployability under a severe information bottleneck.b:T62a,The key setup details visible from the full text are: 842M parameters, about 94,000 hours of English speech for WavLM distillation, 6.25 Hz frame rate, 0.80 kbps bitrate, and 160 ms theoretical latency. The model is also claimed to run in real time for a single stream on a consumer CPU.

The main empirical claim is that ZipCodec substantially outperforms prior streaming codecs at matched bitrate on both reconstruction and downstream representation tasks, despite operating at a much lower frame rate. The comparisons include streaming baselines such as Mimi, and lower-frame-rate but non-streaming or side-information-based methods such as U-Codec, TaDiCodec, FlexiCodec, and DyCAST. The important result is the Pareto position: ZipCodec improves quality while also reducing frame rate, rather than trading one for the other.

The most relevant ablation takeaways are likely the following:
1) large-scale WavLM distillation and matching augmentation are important for preserving semantic quality at such a low frame rate;
2) the transformer/ErfFormer backbone improves scalability and hardware efficiency over focal modulation;
3) removing positional encodings helps long-running streaming inference rather than merely simplifying the model;
4) SSQ is better suited than conventional VQ under this extreme compression regime.
