18:T641,The iterate average optimization problem is formalized as a continuous-time optimal control problem: noisy gradient descent near a quadratic minimum is approximated by the standard Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, with the control objective of minimizing squared error of the returned iterate average subject to a quadratic penalty on control magnitude, forming a solvable linear-quadratic stochastic control problem. The optimal controller applies two corrective forces to the live iterate: pulling it toward the estimated optimum, and pulling it toward consistency with the accumulated trajectory average, to prevent the live weights from drifting too far from the average. Since the exact optimal controller requires unavailable quantities during training (true optimum location, loss Hessian), practical heuristics are applied: the EMA of iterates is used as a proxy for the optimum, reducing the control term to a simple pull of live weights toward their EMA. The resulting PACE algorithm requires only one extra copy of model weights, identical to standard EMA overhead, with no additional complex state. Formal analysis establishes two key results: (1) for general convex losses with unbiased gradients, PACE achieves the standard SGD convergence rate up to a constant factor dependent on EMA strength, meaning it is never substantially worse than SGD; (2) in the quadratic setting used for derivation, PACE strictly reduces the limiting mean squared error of the averaged estimator relative to uncontrolled SGD with EMA, with arbitrarily large improvements possible on specific quadratic instances.19:T5ee,Start with the problem framing to understand the core misalignment between standard optimization targets and the deployed averaged model, the most important conceptual contribution of the work.
