a:T411,They build a three-stream comparison setup: Human continuation (H), base-model continuation (B), and instruction-/preference-trained continuation (I), all generated from the same prompts. Prompts come from splitting PubMed abstracts into two halves at a sentence boundary near the midpoint: first half is the prompt, second half is the human gold continuation. For each prompt, they generate B and I with deterministic greedy decoding (T=0, no sampling/top-k/top-p) and standardized preprocessing. The Triangulated Preference Shift (TPS) score is then computed from lexical frequency distributions across H/B/I, designed to flag tokens/words that become overrepresented in I in a way consistent with a preference-stage-induced shift, rather than being inherited from B or simply reflecting human frequency. A key design choice is triangulation: avoiding the common pitfall of only comparing I vs H (which confounds base-model bias with preference-stage effects).
