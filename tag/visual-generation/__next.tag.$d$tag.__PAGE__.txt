a:T753,The paper introduces VGAU-Diag, a fine-grained evaluation framework for vision generation-assisted understanding. The framework has three main components. First, it performs difficulty-aware evaluation by stratifying samples rather than collapsing everything into one aggregate score. This is meant to reveal whether visual generation helps consistently or only on easier cases. Second, it supports controlled comparison across three representative paradigms under the same setting: Vision-CoT, where textual and visual reasoning are interleaved; Generate-then-Answer, where the model first creates auxiliary visual content and then reasons in text; and Vision-Reasoning, where the visual output itself serves as the reasoning product. Third, it introduces Oracle-Assisted Reference Protocols that decouple generation quality from understanding ability by supplying faithful or controlled visual aids.

The task domain is visual planning. Models must infer task-relevant spatial states from images and produce executable action sequences. This is a useful choice because it is verifiable and exposes a clearer causal chain between visual state estimation and final decision quality than standard multiple-choice benchmarks. The paper also revisits prior Vision-CoT evaluations and argues that some gains were inflated by optimal-step prior leakage, meaning the model could benefit from structural priors rather than from the actual generated visual content. After correcting for that issue, targeted Generate-then-Answer becomes more reliable than generic stepwise visual reasoning.

This is fundamentally an evaluation paper. Its value lies in decomposing the broad claim that generation helps understanding into measurable sub-questions: Is the generated visual aid relevant? Is it faithful? Can the model use it? Does the benefit persist as reasoning difficulty increases?b:T643,The empirical findings are organized around diagnosis rather than leaderboard performance. First, the benefit of visual generation is strongly difficulty-dependent. It can help on easier instances, but the gains become unstable as reasoning complexity increases. Second, oracle-assisted analysis suggests that visual understanding is often the dominant bottleneck. Even when faithful visual aids are provided, current unified multimodal models may still fail to exploit them effectively. In contrast, stronger proprietary VLMs benefit more from oracle aids, which indicates that the limitation is not only generation quality.

Third, the paper identifies a three-stage transition in generation-assisted understanding. Task-irrelevant visuals mostly act as interference. Visually plausible but incorrect aids can actively mislead the model. Only high-fidelity, task-aligned visual aids provide stable assistance. Fourth, after removing prior leakage in earlier Vision-CoT-style evaluations, a more targeted Generate-then-Answer strategy is more reliable than generic visual step-by-step reasoning. The implication is that useful visual generation should focus on decision-critical states rather than simply adding more intermediate visual outputs.

The provided text does not include the full numerical tables, but the structure of the experiments is clear. The key evidence comes from stratified performance by difficulty, oracle versus non-oracle comparisons, and controlled variation in the quality and relevance of generated visual aids. Those analyses matter more here than any single average score.c:T504,The paper introduces RationalRewards, a reasoning-based reward model that outputs structured, multi-dimensional critiques—covering text faithfulness, physical/visual quality, text rendering, and (for editing) source-image faithfulness—before producing scores/preferences.
To train such a model without labeled rationales, it proposes PARROT (Preference-Anchored Rationalization). PARROT treats the rationale z as a latent variable and learns a student evaluator Pθ(z,y|x) from pairwise preference tuples x=(IA,IB,c) with label y. A variational ELBO yields a teacher–student training recipe (Fig.4): (1) a teacher VLM generates candidate rationales anchored to the known preference y (posterior / hindsight qϕ(z|x,y)); (2) a consistency filter removes hallucinated or non-predictive rationales, keeping those that are sufficient to predict y; (3) a student is distilled to generate rationales (without seeing y) and predict preference/score.
RationalRewards is then used in two complementary optimization spaces: (a) parameter space—structured critiques yield interpretable, fine-grained RL rewards; (b) prompt space—at test time, a Generate–Critique–Refine loop turns critiques into targeted prompt revisions, trading compute for quality without any parameter updates.d:T69e,must_read (deep technical + how to read)
