18:T970,方法上，论文的核心不是提出新的 latent reasoning 模型，而是提出一套统一诊断工具，并在 21 个 model-task 配置上系统跑通。实验网格覆盖两类 backbone：GPT-2 和 Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct；三类任务：ProsQA、PrOntoQA、GSM8k；三种训练 recipe：COCONUT、CODI、SIM-COT-COCONUT。这里最有信息量的是后两者与 COCONUT 的对比。COCONUT 是把 hidden state 直接回灌成下一步输入，不直接监督每个 latent slot 的语义；CODI 在答案位置对齐 latent 与 written reasoning；SIM-COT-COCONUT 则在保持 latent inference 机制和 slot budget 不变的前提下，加上 per-step supervision。作者还做了 continued-training control，用来区分“多训练了一点”与“确实加了逐步监督”这两件事。

围绕 RQ1，作者设计了 deletion + replacement 的组合干预。删除 latent slots 只能回答“有没有依赖链”；替换则进一步区分依赖的是位置还是内容。替换有三种：噪声、数据集均值向量、另一道题的 latent vectors。这个设计很讲究，因为如果删除有害但各种替换都几乎不影响答案，就更像是 placeholder/position dependence；如果换成别题的 slots 比直接删除还更伤，才更像模型真的在读链内容。

围绕 RQ2，作者不是只 probe 最终答案，而是对每个 slot、每一层同时 probe 最终答案、候选答案、以及每一步 reasoning step，并用 permuted-label null 做对照。这样能区分“答案早就可读”“路径信息分散存在”“步骤是否按顺序布局”“是否存在 candidate superposition”等不同现象。论文的一个关键发现正来自把 probe 结果和 RQ1 的因果结果并排看：可读不等于可用。

围绕 RQ3，作者分析 latent trajectory 的 step size、最后显著变化位置 L、是否早早 settle，并做 mid-chain editing：在链条中途把前缀替换成另一个样本的 twin prefix，观察最终答案是跟着被编辑内容走，还是保持原答案。这个测试比单纯看轨迹几何更强，因为它直接问“已经收敛的状态还能不能被后续 latent step 改写”。整套方法最后汇总成一个 taxonomy：placeholder chain、collapsed/superposed chain、step-dependent causal chain 等都不是互斥说法，而是对应不同任务、训练方式和测量维度下的不同链类型。19:Ta5b,论文最重要的结果集中在逻辑任务与算术任务的分化，以及不同 recipe 的差异。

1. 逻辑任务上，COCONUT 经常在链开始前就已经把答案编码出来。作者发现，在 ProsQA 和 PrOntoQA 这类逻辑任务里，最终答案在 question 的最后 hidden state 上就已经可被读出；latent slots 的状态变化也很快收敛，后续 slot 只做很小更新。更关键的是，删除整条链通常几乎不改变预测，论文摘要里给出的量级是：在逻辑任务上，删除无监督 recipe 的链，最多只改变 3 个答案。这说明这些 latent steps 在很多配置里并没有承担形成答案的因果角色。

2. 逻辑任务上，per-step supervision 会改变“链的形状”，但不必然改变“链的内容因果性”。SIM-COT-COCONUT 能把 reasoning steps 更整齐地一一映射到不同 slot，形成 ordered layout；删除链会明显伤性能，说明模型开始依赖链。但作者进一步发现，在某些配置里，替换链内容并不会改变答案。也就是说，模型依赖的可能是 slot 的存在、顺序、长度或结构，而不是其中承载的具体语义内容。这是全文最值得记住的结论之一。

3. 算术任务上，三种 recipe 都表现出更明显的 content dependence。也就是删除链会伤性能，替换内容也会改变答案，说明 latent chain 在 GSM8k 这类任务里更像真实计算轨迹，而不是占位结构。论文没有在给定片段里展开所有具体表格数字，但从摘要和方法描述可以确认：作者把 arithmetic cell 作为与 logical cell 对照的核心证据，说明 latent reasoning 的有效性不是统一成立，而是任务相关的。

4. probe 结果方面，逻辑任务中的无监督链往往“答案早可读、路径分散存储、候选不共存于单 slot”。这与一些先前工作提出的 candidate superposition 或 collapse 现象并不矛盾，而是说明不同论文测到的是不同维度。per-step supervision 则能让 step k 更集中地出现在 slot k，但这种可解释布局本身仍不能证明模型在用它。

5. state dynamics 方面，作者报告逻辑任务中的 latent states 往往早早 settle；而算术任务中状态沿链持续移动，mid-chain edit 更容易改变最终输出。这进一步支持“逻辑任务里链常是冗余或占位，算术任务里链更像真实中间计算”的判断。

从实验设计角度看，最有价值的不是某个单点提升，而是作者把 21 个配置放进同一套干预—probe—轨迹分析框架里，得到了一组可比较、可复现的判据。1a:T44a,For pretraining and alternative language modeling research, the value is methodological and diagnostic. First, it provides a causal analysis protocol for latent reasoning, rather than relying on surface indicators such as accuracy, probe readability, or trajectory geometry. Second, it shows that latent-chain behavior depends strongly on task type and training recipe. On logical tasks, COCONUT often already contains the answer before the latent chain starts, so the chain can be redundant or placeholder-like. On arithmetic tasks, chain content is much more likely to be genuinely used. Third, it clarifies what per-step supervision does and does not buy: it can impose an ordered step-by-slot layout and induce dependence on the chain, but that dependence may still be on structure or position rather than semantic content. This matters beyond latent CoT. The same caution applies to iterative LMs, any-order generation, diffusion-style language modeling, and test-time compute methods: extra computation steps are not evidence of useful intermediate reasoning unless causal use is established.1b:T96e,The paper does not introduce a new latent reasoning model. Its main contribution is a unified diagnostic framework evaluated over 21 model-task configurations. The grid spans two backbones, GPT-2 and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct; three tasks, ProsQA, PrOntoQA, and GSM8k; and three training recipes, COCONUT, CODI, and SIM-COT-COCONUT. The comparison between COCONUT and SIM-COT-COCONUT is especially informative because the latent inference mechanism and slot budget are held fixed, while per-step supervision is added. Continued-training controls are used to separate the effect of extra training from the effect of explicit step supervision.

For RQ1, the authors combine deletion with multiple replacement interventions. Deletion only tests whether the model depends on the chain at all. Replacement distinguishes dependence on position from dependence on content. They use three replacements: noise, dataset-mean vectors, and latent vectors from another problem. This is a careful design. If deletion hurts but all replacements leave the answer unchanged, the chain behaves more like a placeholder. If replacing with another problem’s slots hurts more than deletion, that is stronger evidence that the model actually reads chain content.

For RQ2, the probing setup is broader than prior work. Instead of probing only the final answer, the paper probes every slot and every layer for the final answer, candidate answers, and each reasoning step, with permuted-label null baselines. This allows the authors to distinguish early answer readability, distributed path information, ordered step layouts, and candidate superposition. The key point is not probing alone, but probing interpreted jointly with the causal verdict from RQ1.

For RQ3, the paper studies trajectory evolution through step size, the last substantial change position L, and mid-chain editing. In the editing test, the chain prefix is replaced partway through with a twin prefix from another example, and the final answer is checked for whether it follows the edited content or remains anchored to the original computation. This goes beyond geometric description and directly tests whether a seemingly settled latent state can still be revised. The final taxonomy organizes behaviors such as placeholder chains, collapsed or superposed chains, and step-dependent causal chains into a single framework rather than treating them as competing claims.1c:Ta82,The central empirical result is the split between logical and arithmetic tasks, together with the effect of training recipe.

1. On logical tasks, COCONUT often has the answer available before the latent chain begins. In ProsQA and PrOntoQA, the final answer is frequently readable from the last hidden state of the question prefix itself. Latent states also settle early, with only small subsequent updates. More importantly, deleting the entire chain usually leaves predictions almost unchanged. The abstract gives the key scale: for the unsupervised recipe on logical tasks, deletion changes at most three answers. This is strong evidence that many latent steps in these settings are not causally responsible for answer formation.

2. On logical tasks, per-step supervision changes the organization of the chain, but not necessarily its content-level causal role. SIM-COT-COCONUT lays out reasoning steps in a cleaner one-step-per-slot manner, and deletion now hurts performance, indicating chain dependence. But in some configurations, replacing the chain content still leaves answers unchanged. That means the model may depend on the existence, order, or structural pattern of slots rather than the semantic information stored in them. This is one of the paper’s most important findings.

3. On arithmetic tasks, all tested recipes show stronger content dependence. Deleting the chain hurts, and replacing content also changes answers, which makes the latent chain look more like a genuine intermediate computation trace on GSM8k-style problems. The provided text does not include every table entry, but the paper clearly uses arithmetic cells as the main contrast against the logical-task results.

4. The probing results show that, on logical tasks with unsupervised chains, the answer is often readable early, path information is distributed across slots rather than neatly ordered, and no single slot consistently holds both candidates. This helps reconcile prior reports of candidate superposition, collapse, and unused chains: different papers were measuring different properties.

5. In the dynamics analysis, logical-task chains tend to settle early, whereas arithmetic-task chains continue to move across slots and are more sensitive to mid-chain edits. This supports the broader interpretation that logical-task chains are often redundant or placeholder-like, while arithmetic-task chains more often participate in actual computation.

The most valuable part of the experiments is not a single performance gain. It is the fact that 21 configurations are analyzed under one intervention-probe-dynamics framework, producing comparable criteria rather than isolated anecdotes.1d:T6af,Read it in the order of problem setup, causal tests, probing, dynamics, then taxonomy.

Start with Figure 1 and the first part of the introduction. That figure is the backbone of the paper. It defines the three research questions and the five tests, and the rest of the paper is easiest to follow once that structure is clear.

Next, focus on the RQ1 section with deletion and the three replacement interventions. This is the strongest evidence in the paper because it separates content use from placeholder use. Pay attention to why deletion alone is insufficient, and why replacing with another example’s latent slots is a stronger test of semantic dependence.

Then read the RQ2 probing results. Do not interpret probe accuracy in isolation. The paper’s main lesson is that readable latent states are not evidence of reasoning unless the same states are also shown to matter causally.

After that, read the RQ3 trajectory and mid-chain editing analysis. This part clarifies whether the chain is still computing across slots or whether it settles early and merely carries a stable representation forward. Watch for step size, the last-change statistic L, and whether edited prefixes redirect the final answer.

Finish with the taxonomy section. It is not just terminology. It is a practical framework for classifying latent-chain behavior and for designing future experiments. If time is limited, Figure 1, the main RQ1 results, the logical-versus-arithmetic comparisons in RQ2, and the taxonomy summary are enough to capture the paper’s core argument in about 30 to 40 minutes.
