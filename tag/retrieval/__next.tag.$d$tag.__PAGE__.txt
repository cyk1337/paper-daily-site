9:T520,The method has three parts. First, the authors adapt causal tracing to masked DLMs. The clean input masks the object span, matching the model’s training objective. The corrupted input masks both subject and object, removing the factual cue. Internal states are restored from the clean run into the corrupted run, and the indirect effect measures how much the correct masked-object prediction recovers. The corruption uses [MASK] rather than Gaussian noise, because [MASK] is the native perturbation seen during DLM training and decoding. Second, they identify how the factual access state is built. Windowed restoration finds early and late peaks on entity positions, but single-layer interventions show that the dominant individual effect comes from layer-0 attention. Attention surgery then attributes about 84% of that layer’s causal effect to relation-to-visible-entity attention edges. MLP layers 1–8 use the resulting fused representation for factual lookup. Third, the paper uses the mechanism for writing and reading. Multi-View Edit constructs masked forward, reverse, relation, and contextual views of the same fact, and fits a shared value per direction in early MLPs. For retrieval, each entity position is used as a relation-specific vector, rather than pooling the whole query representation.a:T41c,The mechanism experiments are centered on LLaDA-8B and replicated on Dream-7B, including swapped subject/object visibility. Single-layer intervention gives a large effect for layer-0 attention, around IE ≈ 0.23. Relation-to-visible-entity attention edges account for roughly 84% of the layer-0 causal effect. Early MLPs, especially layers 1–8, perform the lookup on the fused state. On editing, Multi-View Edit keeps source-prompt reliability at least 97.6% on CounterFact and BAKE. On BAKE reverse QA, LLaDA-8B improves from 22.4% to 63.2%, and Dream-7B from 20.3% to 83.6%; published AR editors gain less than three points in the same setting. On QAEdit, 32.5% and 44.6% of previously wrong free-form answers become at least partially correct. Unrelated-query activation remains unchanged, suggesting the edit is not achieved by broad activation drift. In retrieval, per-entity vectors recover both pieces of evidence in two-entity queries without training or question decomposition. Removing the layer-0 relation messages removes this advantage.b:T6c7,The paper has two parts: diagnosing the phenomenon and then intervening on training. For diagnosis, the authors build a synthetic task called Spaced Retrieval, designed to isolate retrieval ability from the messiness of natural language. The task uses controllable source-target gaps, with training and evaluation using t=5 and gaps of 64 and 8192 to represent short- and long-range retrieval. They then introduce a layer-wise contribution attribution method to quantify how much each layer contributes to retrieval over the course of training. The purpose is to track who learns retrieval first and who gets suppressed later. On top of that, they run freezing and unfreezing interventions. When recurrent layers are allowed to train normally, they acquire retrieval behavior earlier. When their retrieval learning is manually blocked, attention layers begin learning retrieval almost immediately. This is strong evidence that attention is not inherently incapable; it is being outcompeted. The paper further shows that the winner depends on data distribution: natural language contains many more short-range than long-range patterns, and recurrent layers learn those short-range patterns faster, which gives them an early optimization advantage that spills over into retrieval. To address this, the authors propose Better Layer Division (BLD), a training pipeline that adds a preliminary stage before standard pretraining. During this stage, selected components are frozen so that full-attention layers get an early window to learn retrieval first. Standard pretraining then resumes, but from a more favorable specialization trajectory. The key idea is not extra capacity, but changing the order in which modules are allowed to learn.c:T54a,The experiments include both mechanism studies and end-to-end pretraining results. On the synthetic Spaced Retrieval task, the paper provides three kinds of evidence. First, layer-attribution trajectories show that recurrent/KDA layers take over retrieval earlier than attention. Second, freezing and later unfreezing gives a more causal result: once recurrent learning is stopped, attention’s retrieval contribution rises quickly. Third, changing the ratio of short- and long-distance patterns changes which mixer wins the competition, showing that the outcome is jointly determined by data distribution and learning speed. For realistic language modeling, the authors apply BLD to Kimi Delta Attention based hybrid LLMs at multiple scales and report gains over vanilla pretraining on training loss and several benchmarks, including commonsense reasoning, recall-intensive tasks, RULER, and LongPPL. The abstract does not expose all numbers, but the full paper argues that the gains are consistent across scales and not limited to one recurrent architecture. An important nuance is that the paper is not arguing recurrent layers should never do retrieval.
