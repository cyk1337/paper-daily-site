5:T648,The work has two main parts. First, it establishes and explains the mismatch. Under fixed top-k input settings, the authors evaluate three multimodal DLMs on five visual QA benchmarks using the same VisRAG-ranked candidate pools, prompts, decoding budgets, and answer scorer. They show that Recall@k increases with k, while macro-average answer accuracy drops. They then isolate the cause and find that semantic conflict, not merely the presence of multiple images, is the main driver of degradation. The theoretical analysis focuses on the first unresolved denoising state. When a correct page and a conflicting page each support a different coherent answer, the correct latent structure requires multiple answer positions to share one source choice. A position-factorized proposal breaks this dependence and allocates probability mass to cross-source answers; the unsupported mass grows as the conflict spans more answer positions. Second, the paper proposes ECF, a training-free evidence admission framework. It combines multi-granularity evidence construction, where both full pages and layout-derived regions are indexed, with generator-side candidate filtering. For each additional candidate ci, ECF compares the target DLM’s first-step answer-block entropy under {c1, ci} versus {c1, blank(ci)}. This yields a conditional confidence gain that controls for the structural effect of adding another visual slot and measures whether the candidate content itself reduces competing-source risk. A rank-prior selection rule then decides whether to expand beyond the top-1 page and which candidate to admit.6:T4d4,The evaluation spans three multimodal DLMs and five visual QA benchmarks. The main empirical findings come in three layers. First, the phenomenon itself: increasing top-k from 1 to 2 or 3 consistently raises answer-page recall, but lowers macro-average answer accuracy across 15 model-dataset combinations. Second, the mechanism: conflicting pages produce the largest accuracy drop among the tested evidence conditions. The same interference is already visible in the first-step answer-block distribution. On naturally retrieved ChartQA cases, the conflicting-page condition causes the largest drop in first-step answer-block accuracy and the largest Jensen-Shannon divergence from the correct-only distribution, and this aligns with the largest final-answer degradation. Third, the method result: across five benchmarks and three DLMs, ECF improves over the strongest fixed top-k input by 2.62 percentage points on average. With LLaDA2.0-Uni, it also beats the strongest training-free baseline by 2.37 points on average. Each new bounded chunk is reconciled with a small set of semantically related prior chunks before being written back to memory. This is meant to keep maintenance cost bounded while recovering evidence that would otherwise be fragmented across independently summarized segments.
