18:T466,The method, DISCORD, is a distribution-corrected offline distillation objective. The pipeline starts with a strong teacher prompted by a reasoning-eliciting instruction to produce high-quality math traces. Instead of standard behavior cloning that maximizes log-likelihood on all teacher tokens equally, DISCORD reweights supervision using an advantage-style signal so that teacher actions more compatible with the student’s reachable support receive larger weight. The intuition is simple: do not imitate every teacher continuation uniformly; emphasize teacher behavior that the student is likely to encounter under its own decoding dynamics and that is associated with better outcomes. This is conceptually close to offline RL corrections based on support and advantage, but the method remains fully offline and does not require online rollouts. The architectural footprint is minimal; the main change is the training objective. Compared with bidirectional divergence variants, the distinction is that those still fit a teacher-centered distribution, whereas DISCORD explicitly tries to reduce the prefix-distribution gap.19:Ta39,技术路线可以拆成三部分。第一部分是问题重述。标准 OPD 的做法是：student 在当前策略下采样 rollout，teacher 对 rollout 上每个 token 给出 log-prob，再结合 dense per-token advantage 做蒸馏更新。难点在于 rollout 分布随 student 变化，所以看起来 teacher 必须在线。作者先做经验观察，发现 OPD 训练期间 student 相对 SFT reference 的分布漂移并不大，这给了“固定 rollout 分布”的空间。

第二部分是 teacher consistency 的理论分析。作者指出，OPD 实际上有两个 teacher：一个用于生成 SFT 训练轨迹，决定了 reference policy π_ref 所在的数据支持；另一个用于 OPD 阶段提供目标分布。如果这两个 teacher 不一致，那么 student 被要求在一个由 teacher A 诱导的数据分布上，去拟合 teacher B 的 token 分布，目标函数会出现不可消除的偏差。作者把这种偏差写成优化目标中的系统性 mismatch，并分析其固定点性质：即使在线采样、即使训练足够久，也不会收敛到标准 OPD 想要的最优解。离线场景下，这个问题更严重，因为 rollout 分布也被冻结在错误 teacher 诱导的参考策略附近。

第三部分是 Lightning OPD 的构造。做法并不复杂，但关键在于约束关系被理顺了：先选定一个 teacher π_T；用这个同一个 teacher 生成 SFT 轨迹，把 base model 训成 reference policy π_ref；然后从 π_ref 采样固定 rollouts，对这些 responses 预计算同一个 teacher 的 token log-prob；最后在训练阶段只更新 student，不再需要在线 teacher。理论上，作者证明在 teacher consistency 成立时，Lightning OPD 与 standard OPD 共享相同最优点；两者的梯度差异在训练过程中有界，不会无限累积。更有意思的是，离线目标由于固定在 π_ref 支撑集上，天然带来一种抑制 policy drift 的隐式正则效果，因此不需要额外显式 KL penalty 也能保持稳定。这一点很像把 on-policy distillation 的有效区域锚定在 reference neighborhood 内，用数据支持而不是额外正则去限制漂移。

A natural workaround is to precompute teacher log-probabilities on SFT rollouts and reuse them offline. In practice, that naive offline variant often underperforms. The central claim of the paper is that the main issue is not offline approximation itself, but a missing condition in prior OPD pipelines: the teacher used to generate SFT trajectories and the teacher used to provide OPD token distributions must be the same model. The paper calls this teacher consistency. Once this condition is violated, both offline and online OPD optimize a biased objective and converge to a suboptimal fixed point. Lightning OPD is built on this observation: enforce teacher consistency, precompute teacher scores once on reference-policy rollouts, and remove the live teacher server without changing the target optimum.1b:T967,The method has three layers.

First, the paper reframes the online requirement in OPD. Standard OPD samples rollouts from the current student policy, queries the teacher for token log-probabilities on those rollouts, and updates the student with dense token-level advantage weighting. Because the rollout distribution changes during training, the teacher appears to be inherently online. The authors start from an empirical observation: during OPD, the student policy does not drift very far from the SFT reference policy. That opens the door to using a fixed rollout distribution.

Second, the paper introduces and analyzes teacher consistency. OPD implicitly involves two teachers: one teacher generates the trajectories used in SFT and therefore shapes the support of the reference policy, while another teacher may be used during OPD to provide target token distributions. If these are different, the student is asked to fit teacher B on data induced by teacher A. The paper argues that this creates an irreducible mismatch in the objective. The analysis goes beyond offline caching: even standard online OPD remains biased under this mismatch and converges to the wrong fixed point. Offline training makes the issue worse because the rollout distribution is also frozen around the wrong reference policy.

Third, Lightning OPD is the consistent offline construction. Choose one teacher π_T. Use that same teacher to generate SFT trajectories and train the base model into a reference policy π_ref. Then sample fixed rollouts from π_ref, precompute token log-probabilities from the same teacher on those responses, and train the student without any live teacher server. The theoretical result is the key part: under teacher consistency, Lightning OPD shares the same optimum as standard OPD, and the gradient discrepancy between the offline and online objectives stays bounded during training. The paper also argues that the offline objective introduces an implicit regularization effect: because training remains anchored to the support of π_ref, policy drift is naturally limited even without an explicit KL penalty.

Relative to prior OPD work, the novelty is not just caching teacher outputs. The paper explains why some offline attempts fail, isolates the failure mode as teacher mismatch, and turns offline OPD from a heuristic approximation into a conditionally equivalent training procedure.1c:T777,The experiments cover mathematical reasoning and code generation, with student–teacher pairs including Qwen3-4B distilled from Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-8B distilled from Qwen3-32B. The headline result stated in the paper is that Lightning OPD matches or exceeds standard OPD across all reported benchmarks while delivering about a 4.0× training speedup by removing parallel teacher serving.

The most important empirical evidence appears to come from four types of comparisons. First, a naive offline baseline: simply caching teacher log-probabilities on existing SFT rollouts does not reliably match standard OPD when the SFT teacher and the OPD teacher differ. Second, teacher-consistency ablations: once the same teacher is used for both SFT trajectory generation and OPD supervision, offline performance recovers and becomes competitive with online OPD. Third, online mismatch controls: the paper shows that teacher inconsistency also hurts standard online OPD, which is crucial because it demonstrates that the issue is objective bias rather than offline approximation error. Fourth, training-dynamics analysis: the student stays relatively close to the reference policy, and the offline variant shows stable optimization behavior, supporting the implicit-regularization argument.
