Standard nested dropout randomly truncates the latent sequence during training so that prefixes can reconstruct the image, but this often pushes most information into the earliest tokens and leaves tail tokens underused. NesTok adds cross-length training: reconstruction is jointly optimized across multiple token lengths, and shorter-sequence representations are explicitly aligned in latent space to the full-length representation. Short prefixes are encouraged not only to reconstruct reasonably well but also to stay semantically close to the full representation, while later tokens are left to refine details progressively. Importantly, the framework stays within a discrete 1D tokenizer plus ViT decoder plus standard next-token AR generation pipeline. The gains therefore come from better tokenizer learning rather than from switching to a more complex diffusion or flow decoder.
