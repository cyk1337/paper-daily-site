9:T649,Talker-T2AV is an autoregressive diffusion framework with two levels. A shared autoregressive backbone handles high-level cross-modal modeling, while two independent diffusion transformer heads render audio and video separately. Speech and video are first encoded into temporally aligned latent sequences at the same frame rate. At each time step, the two modality embeddings are fused by element-wise summation and fed into the backbone as a joint representation. The backbone autoregressively predicts the next joint patch hidden state conditioned on the text prefix and previous context. From that hidden state, the audio head and the video head each run their own diffusion decoding process to produce frame-level latents.

This differs sharply from dual-DiT systems. Dual-DiT uses two parallel towers with bidirectional cross-attention at every block and every denoising step. Here, cross-modal interaction is concentrated in the shared backbone, while low-level rendering is explicitly decoupled. The underlying claim is that speech content, timing, and facial motion need joint planning, but acoustic realization and visual texture synthesis do not benefit from being entangled all the way down.

The element-wise summation interface also gives the model a unified conditioning mechanism. If one modality is available, its embedding can be injected directly into the backbone while the other modality is autoregressively predicted.
