9:T4a9,The authors learn binary gates over attention heads while keeping all model weights frozen. Each head output is multiplied by a hard binary gate in the forward pass; gradients are passed through a sigmoid relaxation with a straight-through estimator. The objective penalizes configurations that exceed a target perplexity ratio while encouraging sparsity. The reported setup initializes all gate logits to 5.0, uses r=1.00, λ=1e-4, AdamW with learning rate 1e-2, batch size 8, and 1,000 optimization steps. The selected pruning mask is the one with the best observed batch-level perplexity ratio during training, not necessarily the final step. Memorization is measured with k-extractability: given the first k tokens, the model is counted as memorizing the sequence if greedy decoding reproduces the next k tokens exactly. The paper evaluates k=16, 32, and 50. For mechanism, it examines attention entropy, softmax saturation, agreement across heads, and the contribution of pruned heads to the residual stream. The key observation is that memorized sequences induce sharper attention and stronger cross-head agreement; pruning task-redundant heads weakens this coordinated retrieval signal.a:T8db,The setup fine-tunes the base LM to explain itself, rather than training a separate probe. Given an input x and a natural-language query qc that specifies a target locale c inside the model, the system must report the corresponding internal signal. The locale can identify a layer, token position, neuron index, attention head, or attention source-target pair. The authors first cache the true activation a_c(x) from the original forward pass, then train a LoRA-adapted version of the same model to output a discretized or verbalized prediction of that signal conditioned on (x, qc). Because the target is the model's own low-level state, supervision is exact and can be generated at scale.

The central methodological move is to separate endogenous and intervention settings. In the endogenous case, the target signal arises naturally from the input. In the intervention case, the target internal variable is explicitly modified during the forward pass. The paper argues that many prior introspection setups can be solved by detecting that something unusual was injected, rather than by reading the intended internal state. To address this, the authors compare ordinary SFT against interventional training, where intervention examples are included during training so that correlated shortcuts become less reliable and the model is pushed toward the actual target signal.

The mechanism analysis goes beyond behavior. The authors trace the attention heads involved in moving information relevant to introspection. They find that endogenous and intervention reporting rely on overlapping but distinct circuits. Endogenous reporting often uses a larger set of heads to transport naturally occurring internal information, while intervention-related heads behave more like gates that selectively attend to the introspection token when an intervention is present. This distinction is important: being able to report an injected anomaly is not the same capability as reading naturally generated internal states.

Relative to external explainers or hidden-state probes, the paper's distinguishing feature is that the same model is trained to report its own low-level signals in context, under a setup where causal interventions make bypass diagnosable rather than invisible.b:T640,The study trains hundreds of introspectors across models, signal types, and target locales. Four findings stand out. First, standard SFT can achieve high empirical reporting accuracy, but intervention-based tests and circuit analysis reveal substantial causal bypass. In other words, good answers do not guarantee that the model is using the intended internal variable. Second, adding interventions during training improves reporting of endogenous signals, with especially clear gains on out-of-distribution data. That suggests the method is not just fitting templates; it is making the model rely on more robust internal evidence. Third, generalization is asymmetric: transfer across token positions is relatively good, but transfer to unseen neurons or unseen layers is weak. The paper argues that this looks more like a coverage issue than a hard capability limit, since adding data for more neurons and layers improves performance. Fourth, the circuit analysis shows partially overlapping but functionally distinct attention heads for endogenous versus intervention reporting; endogenous circuits often involve more heads, while intervention circuits look more selective and gate-like.

Still, the experimental structure is already informative: the authors do not rely on a single average accuracy metric, but separate endogenous vs intervention, in-distribution vs OOD, and seen vs unseen positions/layers/neurons. That makes the conclusions much stronger than a standard finetuning benchmark.c:T402,Start with the introduction. The main point is not that neuron activations are intrinsically the most important introspection target, but that they are precise, intervenable, and falsifiable, which makes them ideal for studying causal bypass. Then read the method section carefully to understand how locales are defined and how endogenous and intervention examples differ.

After that, go straight to the main behavioral results comparing standard SFT and interventional training, especially on endogenous and OOD evaluations. Then read the generalization experiments over token positions, neurons, and layers. Those results tell you what the model is actually learning. Finally, read the mechanism-analysis figures on attention heads, since they provide the strongest evidence that intervention reporting and endogenous reporting are related but not identical circuits.

A useful skim should take around 25-35 minutes. If time is limited, focus on the task setup, the main comparison tables, and the circuit-analysis section.d:T62e,The task is deliberately simple. There are four labeled boxes and four letters A–D. Starting from an initial arrangement, the model must apply k swaps, with k in {2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24}, writing the full state after each swap and then the final answer. Because every intermediate state is deterministic, step correctness can be scored exactly. The key intervention is the Edit condition: after the model has produced a partial chain up to step m, the state at step m is replaced with a different but valid arrangement, and the model is asked to continue. If the continuation and final answer evolve consistently from the corrupted state, the run is labeled follows-edit; if the model returns to the true trajectory, it is labeled repair; other categories capture additional failure modes.

This design has several strengths. The intervention is applied to the model’s own chain rather than to an externally written explanation, so it directly tests whether the model uses previously written text as computational input. The corrupted state is valid, which prevents trivial rejection based on malformed formatting. The paper also separates compliance, prefix accuracy, CoT accuracy, and direct accuracy, so reliance is not conflated with formatting failures, low capability, or answer bias.

The evaluation spans 25 open-weight models: Qwen3 size series with both base and post-trained variants, Gemma 3, Llama 3, and multiple OLMo 2 pretraining checkpoints. The protocol runs on a single 16GB GPU, which makes it a practical diagnostic rather than a one-off large-scale benchmark.e:T6b3,There are five headline findings. First, reliance rises monotonically with size and then saturates. In Qwen3, the follows-edit rate is well fit by a logistic curve in log parameter count, with a midpoint near 1B, reaching 0.97–1.00 by 14B. Gemma 3 and Llama 3 show the same qualitative trend. Second, capability is the main explanatory variable. Across all 25 models, reliance and step accuracy correlate at about r = 0.99; at matched capability, models from different families and sizes show similar reliance, such as 1.7B Qwen3, 4B Gemma 3, and 8B Llama 3. Third, size still matters beyond raw correctness: even when restricting to items the model originally solved correctly before the edit, reliance continues to increase with size among capable models. Fourth, post-training is not the main driver. For Qwen3, base and post-trained checkpoints differ by at most about ±0.03 in reliance from 4B upward; the only large gap appears at 1.7B and coincides with a large capability gap. Fifth, for k >= 4, direct answering without a chain stays at chance for all capable models, with mean accuracy 0.041 versus the theoretical 1/24 ≈ 0.042. That strongly suggests there is no hidden internal shortcut on this task at these scales: if the model solves it, it does so by using the explicit step-by-step trace.

The OLMo 2 checkpoint results are also informative as a limitation. The authors intended to probe the training-token axis, but those checkpoints never cross the capability threshold needed for the task, so the token-scaling analysis remains inconclusive.
