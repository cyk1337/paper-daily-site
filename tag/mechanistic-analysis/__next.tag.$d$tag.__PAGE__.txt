9:T7bf,The method is deliberately simple but well targeted. The authors define two axes. Direct rate (DR) measures how often the model repeats its top answer over 30 samples of the original prompt at temperature 0.6. This captures concentration under one wording. Framed rate (FR) then asks whether that same direct top answer survives 30 matched scenario reframings of the underlying question. Importantly, FR is not the concentration of answers under reframing; it is the reappearance rate of the original direct top answer. That design choice is the core of the paper, because it measures persistence of a specific preference rather than generic consistency. The final bias depth score is π = DR × FR, which is high only when the model is both concentrated under direct prompting and robust to reframing.

The dataset construction is also central. They build 4,442 opinion prompt families, each grounded in a real Olmo-3-7B-SFT training example, and create matched everyday-scenario reframings for each family. Because the task is open-ended generation rather than multiple choice, answer normalization is necessary to merge semantically equivalent outputs and avoid contaminating FR with superficial phrasing variation. The contribution is therefore less about a new model and more about turning a causally meaningful question—does a preference survive paraphrastic and situational variation?—into a scalable evaluation protocol.

The paper then runs this protocol across the full Olmo-3-7B release pipeline: Pretrained → SFT → DPO → RLVR. That allows stage-wise tracking of bias depth. This is meant to show that the degradation is not just a generic consequence of continued training, but is tied to multimodal adaptation.

Second, it proposes a mechanism: attention-sink corruption. Many modern LLMs place a large fraction of attention mass on a few early positions. Those positions are dominated by a small set of high-magnitude feature dimensions and act as sinks. The paper argues that these sinks help stabilize attention allocation in ways that matter for tasks graded on exact output form. During VL fine-tuning, perturbations to the query and key projections, Wq and Wk, induce logit shifts that scale with input magnitude. Because sink positions have unusually large magnitude, the perturbation is amplified there, causing per-head sink collapse. The architecture analysis then distinguishes per-head QK-RMSNorm from layerwise normalization: per-head normalization removes explicit raw-magnitude dependence, while layerwise normalization can still compress weak heads because each head’s scale is tied to the layer’s total energy.

The result is that shrinkage LDA closes about 73% of the gap between the 1D mean-shift classifier and the full-dimensional classifier, supporting the view that most of the remaining gain is about covariance estimation under noise, not richer geometry.

LayerMix is then introduced as a practical consequence of the analysis rather than a major algorithmic invention. Since the signal is not confined to a single oracle layer but spread across a contiguous band of layers, LayerMix uses lightweight cross-validated aggregation across layers to identify and combine that informative band. A practical L2-regularized logistic regression reaches 0.952 AUROC, showing that there is still useful structure beyond raw δ, but that structure remains largely linear and covariance-related.
