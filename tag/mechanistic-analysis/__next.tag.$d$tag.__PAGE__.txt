In pretraining, the token budget, data, model configuration, and optimization setup are fixed, while the training context window is varied. Coherent document span length serves as a proxy for the amount of available relevant information. This design separates longer-context learning from simply seeing more tokens. In supervised fine-tuning, the context length is fixed and the amount of task-relevant context is directly controlled, isolating relevance from length. The theoretical account treats language modeling as compression with two predictive channels: information stored in parameters and information supplied in context. When context offers a lower-complexity solution, less task information needs to be encoded in the weights to reach the same risk threshold. The mechanistic analyses then connect this to lower-complexity contextual solutions, a shift in update pressure from feed-forward networks toward attention modules, and stronger reliance on context tokens at inference time.b:T712,The proposed audit, J-Access, measures how accessible target knowledge remains along the model’s output pathway. Instead of relying on a standard logit lens, which directly decodes intermediate residual states with the unembedding matrix, the method uses a Jacobian lens. The motivation is specific: after unlearning, the geometry of the residual stream may shift, so the shared-basis assumption behind the logit lens becomes less reliable. The Jacobian lens locally linearizes the downstream computation from an intermediate state to vocabulary space, making it a better proxy for whether hidden knowledge is still close to being verbalized.

Operationally, the audit uses probe queries that implicate a forgotten entity without explicitly naming it. For a band of mid-to-late layers, the residual representation is projected into vocabulary space through the Jacobian lens, and the method checks whether any token from a target concept set appears among the top-ranked decoded tokens. The concept set is defined at two granularities: an identity-level set containing full-name and name-piece tokens, and a broader knowledge-level set containing answer content words filtered by document frequency and inferability constraints. The resulting access rate is normalized against both the original pre-unlearning model and a retain-only gold model trained without the forget set.

The paper is structured around two evaluation regimes rather than a new unlearning algorithm. In the predictive regime, pre-attack J-Access is measured and then compared against recovery under cross-entity relearning attacks. In the optimization regime, J-Access suppression is added directly to the unlearning objective to test whether the signal is robust or merely gameable. That design is the paper’s strongest contribution.c:T5df,The study evaluates two unlearning benchmarks, TOFU and OpenUnlearning, covering 8 unlearning methods and 398 public unlearned models. Three findings drive the paper.

First, many models that pass standard forgetting and utility checks still show J-Access substantially above the retain-only gold reference. So behavioral forgetting does not imply internal erasure. The result appears across methods and datasets, suggesting this is not an artifact of one benchmark.

Second, pre-attack J-Access predicts model-level recovery speed under cross-entity relearning attacks. Models with higher residual access tend to recover forgotten knowledge faster under a small amount of continued training. However, the signal is much weaker for predicting which specific items will recover. That indicates J-Access behaves more like a global accessibility or connectivity measure than an instance-level fate predictor.

Third, directly minimizing J-Access during unlearning lowers the audit score but increases post-attack recovery. The model learns to evade the readout rather than delete the underlying knowledge. This is the most consequential result in the paper. It shows that a useful audit can become a misleading optimization target.

The paper also includes analyses across identity-level versus knowledge-level token sets, different layer bands, and comparisons with standard behavioral metrics. The emphasis is on trends, correlations, and attack outcomes rather than a single headline scalar improvement.d:T421,This is a skim paper, but the setup is worth reading carefully.
