They then construct controlled variants along five axes. Instead of relying on a standard logit lens, which directly decodes intermediate residual states with the unembedding matrix, the method uses a Jacobian lens. The motivation is specific: after unlearning, the geometry of the residual stream may shift, so the shared-basis assumption behind the logit lens becomes less reliable. The Jacobian lens locally linearizes the downstream computation from an intermediate state to vocabulary space, making it a better proxy for whether hidden knowledge is still close to being verbalized.

Operationally, the audit uses probe queries that implicate a forgotten entity without explicitly naming it. For a band of mid-to-late layers, the residual representation is projected into vocabulary space through the Jacobian lens, and the method checks whether any token from a target concept set appears among the top-ranked decoded tokens. The concept set is defined at two granularities: an identity-level set containing full-name and name-piece tokens, and a broader knowledge-level set containing answer content words filtered by document frequency and inferability constraints. The resulting access rate is normalized against both the original pre-unlearning model and a retain-only gold model trained without the forget set.

The paper is structured around two evaluation regimes rather than a new unlearning algorithm. In the predictive regime, pre-attack J-Access is measured and then compared against recovery under cross-entity relearning attacks. In the optimization regime, J-Access suppression is added directly to the unlearning objective to test whether the signal is robust or merely gameable.
