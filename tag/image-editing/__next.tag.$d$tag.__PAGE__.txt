9:T6fd,ReasonUni is built on a unified multimodal model with a shared discrete token space, instantiated in the paper with an X-Omni-style pure AR architecture. Images are quantized into visual tokens by a visual tokenizer, text remains language tokens, and visual inputs and outputs share the same semantic codebook. This gives one backbone two conditional policies: understanding p(theta)(T|V) and generation p(theta)(V|T). The paper’s critique of prior reconstruction methods is that they mostly supervise the model to describe what is already present. That encourages copying and preservation, but does not force explicit reasoning about intended edits, structural changes, or invariances. ReasonUni changes the training problem into reasoning-driven editing. Given a source image, an edit instruction, and a target image, the model first generates a textual description of the desired target state, then generates the target image conditioned on that description. The intermediate text is therefore not just a caption; it is a reasoning-bearing representation of the post-edit world state. To jointly optimize both stages, the paper introduces TreeGRPO. For the same input, the model samples multiple reasoning paths, each treated as a branch. Under each branch, it samples multiple generated images as leaves. Rewards are then used at two levels: leaf rewards are aggregated to produce a more reliable branch-level signal for the understanding module, and within-branch normalization guides the generation module under a fixed target-state description. The key idea is structured credit assignment. A reasoning path is judged by the distribution of images it induces, which reduces the noise of single-sample feedback and lets image-level rewards shape the intermediate reasoning process.a:T4dd,From the provided full-text sections, the paper reports consistent gains on both understanding and generation benchmarks relative to UAE, X-Omni, and related baselines. That is an important result because it suggests the benefit is not merely better visual-detail reconstruction. The input is an instruction q and two candidate images I_A and I_B; image editing additionally includes a source image. Instead of directly predicting which image is better, the model generates a structured rubric and dimension-level assessments for both candidates in one pass. The rubric includes task intent, atomic evaluation dimensions d_i, weights w_i over those dimensions, and scoring criteria L_i for each dimension. The model then assigns per-dimension scores s_i^k to each image, aggregates them with the learned weights, and derives the final preference from the weighted totals.

The key design choice is not merely to produce free-form rationales, but to turn explanation into a structured scoring protocol. That makes the output more controllable and allows training signals to operate at the dimension level rather than only at the final pairwise label.

Training has two stages. In the SFT stage, the model learns rubric trajectories synthesized by a teacher: how to decompose the instruction into intent, dimensions, weights, criteria, and then score the candidates. In the RL stage, the paper uses dimension-level GRPO. The objective aligns predicted score differences with reference score differences on each rubric dimension, and penalizes preference-direction reversals more strongly. This indicates that the authors care not only about final pairwise accuracy but also about the internal correctness of the dimension-wise judgment process.

Data construction is a major part of the contribution. The paper argues that dynamic rubric learning requires broad instruction coverage; otherwise the model will learn biased rubric templates and generalize poorly. To address this, the authors aggregate multiple open-source preference datasets, analyze instruction types for text-to-image generation and image editing, define classification criteria, and use DeepSeek-V3.2 for automatic taxonomy annotation. They then perform balanced sampling, identify underrepresented categories, synthesize additional prompts for those categories, generate candidate images, collect expert preference annotations, and finally synthesize rubric trajectories for each preference sample. The resulting training sets contain 31.8K text-to-image samples and 30.7K image-editing samples.d:T54f,The experiments train dedicated models for text-to-image generation and image editing on Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B backbones.
