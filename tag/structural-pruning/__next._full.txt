Two structured pruning paradigms are compared. The first is layerwise pruning, which removes entire transformer blocks. This follows prior LLM work that treats some layers as redundant. It is simple and hardware-friendly, but it is also coarse and may disrupt the pathways through which multimodal information is integrated at deeper layers. After pruning, the authors evaluate several recovery strategies. The base recovery method is supervised finetuning. On top of that, they add knowledge distillation at two levels: output logits and hidden states, with hidden-state distillation implemented via an L2 objective. That makes sense for LVLMs, where pruning perturbs not only the final token distribution but also the intermediate representation space that supports vision-language alignment.
