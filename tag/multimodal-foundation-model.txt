Instead of encoding uniformly sampled frames, the video is split into anchor frames, or I-frames, and predicted frames, or P-frames. I-frames are encoded densely. P-frames keep only 16×16 patches selected by motion vectors and residual energy, so static or low-information regions are not repeatedly passed to the language model. The model is trained with a five-stage curriculum. Stage 1 warms up image and short-video understanding with dense captions, using roughly four times the captioning data of LLaVA-OneVision2. Stage 2 adds image instructions, temporal grounding, spatial supervision, and more video captions. Stage 3 uses long-video captions to teach event ordering and longer-range dependencies. Stage 4 switches to codec video streams, where the predictive patch mechanism uses about one eighth or fewer visual tokens than dense frame sampling, allowing training on sequences about eight times longer than in Stage 3. Stage 5 converts previous video captions into a streaming format and trains a proactive gate on top of Mage-ViT. The gate decides when the model should respond; the full model is invoked only after a trigger.
