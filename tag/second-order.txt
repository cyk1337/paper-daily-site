19:T73e,The core construction is group averaging. If the loss is invariant under a parameter transformation group G, then the gradient transforms equivariantly under the same group action. Starting from a single gradient at one parameter point, the method analytically averages gradient-derived quantities over the group orbit and uses that average as a proxy for local curvature. The resulting Hessian approximation is guaranteed to lie in the commutant algebra of the group representation. This is the key structural result: instead of storing a dense curvature matrix, one only needs coefficients over a small basis of symmetry-compatible operators.

The paper then instantiates this idea for MLPs and Transformers. For each architecture, the complexity of the approximation is tied to the dimension of the commutant algebra induced by the selected symmetry group. Large groups impose stronger constraints, giving cheaper and more compact approximations, but their orbits are less local and may wash out true local curvature. Smaller groups preserve more local information but increase computational burden. This is presented as an explicit design knob rather than a side effect.

A practical contribution is a just-in-time compiler that takes symbolic symmetry specifications and emits efficient PyTorch routines for computing the low-dimensional factors. So the framework is not only an abstract characterization; it is meant to be operational.

A major theoretical bridge is the derivation showing that particular permutation subgroup choices recover Shampoo/Muon-like curvature structures. This is important because it turns those optimizers from isolated engineering designs into points inside a broader symmetry-aware family.
