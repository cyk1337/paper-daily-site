Prior work showed the model’s behavior follows observational association on held-out confounded worlds despite causal structure being linearly decodable from the residual stream.

Pipeline components (all preregistered):
- An OOD detector u that decides whether to trigger intervention.
- A contrast forward pass to construct signals for direction estimation.
- A direction generator gθ producing either a calibration-fitted global direction or per-instance directions.
- A residual-stream injector that adds the direction at a localized site with a tunable dose.

Key design choice: prove localization sufficiency first via activation transplantation (a strong, non-linear “oracle-ish” intervention). Only then evaluate the full gated pipeline; if it fails, remove the gate and test unconditional injection. This sequencing enables a credible failure decomposition: if transplant works but linear injection saturates, the gap is not “we didn’t find the right place,” but “linear release at this resolution is bounded.” Preregistration (hashed thresholds, datasets, claim templates, decision branches) is treated as part of the technical contribution.c:T45c,OmniVAE is a jointly trained audio-video VAE framework explicitly designed for cross-modal latent space alignment, retaining separate audio and video encoder-decoder branches (preserving per-modality independent reconstruction capability, with no extra inference cost). First, the authors train per-layer MLP probes on residual-stream hidden states to regress the current denoising progress τ during inference. They do this on two representative masked DLMs, LLaDA-1.5 and Dream, and vary which token positions are used: masked tokens only, non-masked tokens only, or all tokens. This is an important design choice because it tests whether the signal is localized to visibly masked positions or distributed more globally across the sequence representation.

Second, they move from readout to intervention. From the learned time-related representation, they identify a low-dimensional subspace associated with inferred denoising progress and steer activations along that subspace. The key point is that they do not alter the external unmasking schedule or the observed mask ratio; they only perturb internal states. If generation behavior shifts in a systematic way, that is stronger evidence that the model is using an internal notion of progress rather than merely exposing a correlational artifact.

Third, they analyze the geometry of the recovered signal across layers. The paper argues that denoising progress is encoded in a relatively low-dimensional subspace rather than as a diffuse weak statistic spread across the full hidden state. That matters because a compact control-like representation is easier to interpret as an internal state variable. The layerwise analysis also helps distinguish whether the signal is strongest in early, middle, or late computation and whether similar structure appears across different DLM families.

Relative to prior DLM interpretability work, this paper is narrower and more central: it does not focus on attention sinks, harmful mask manipulations, or generic SAE features, but on the internal representation of denoising progress itself. The first is an annotation-free 4D data synthesis pipeline. Starting from raw videos in SpatialVID and using only automatically estimated geometric annotations, the pipeline samples frames, identifies salient landmarks or objects with rules plus a high-level model, extracts masks, and constructs motion-centric QA pairs along two axes: camera motion and object motion. The resulting supervision is not plain text CoT. Instead, the chain of thought is interleaved with dynamic mental imagery, meaning some latent positions are trained to represent how highlighted landmarks evolve over time.

The second component is DIFT, Dynamic-Imagery Fine-Tuning. This is a joint objective: standard cross-entropy on text tokens, plus a cosine-similarity loss on selected latent positions. Those latent states are encouraged to match target representations derived from highlighted frames. The key idea is to supervise what the model should internally imagine, without forcing explicit 3D decoding or reducing everything to language. Compared with earlier latent reasoning work on static scenes, this extends the idea to temporal dynamics and removes the need for manually annotated reference images or heavy teacher distillation.

The third component is 4DRL, a reinforcement-learning stage built on top of DIFT for harder compound-motion reasoning. The important implementation detail is that policy gradients are applied only to text tokens, not to latent positions. The paper argues that latent hidden-state propagation is not aligned with discrete action log-probabilities, so directly optimizing latent positions with GRPO-style updates would be ill-posed and unstable.
