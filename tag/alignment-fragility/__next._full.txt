17:T58a,The paper does not claim to directly observe a global 'reversion manifold'. Instead, it introduces an operational proxy based on local witnesses. Starting from the same base model, the authors construct several short helpful-only fine-tuned checkpoints and treat them as local witnesses of an earlier behavioral region. They then define the displacement from the current safety-aligned model to such a witness as v_rev, the local reversion direction. The technical argument has three parts. First, geometric measurement: during benign post-alignment fine-tuning, they track how strongly the update trajectory aligns with v_rev, and compare it against isotropic random-direction baselines. Second, predictive analysis: they test whether early alignment with v_rev predicts later behavioral degradation. Third, causal intervention: they explicitly block motion along v_rev during fine-tuning and measure whether harmfulness is reduced with limited task cost. A key detail is that 'manifold' is used behaviorally and operationally, not as a directly recovered object in weight space. So the method is a combination of proxy directions, local linearization, and behavioral validation. Relative to prior work showing that safety alignment is shallow and low-rank, or that safety holds only within a narrow basin, this paper goes one step further by proposing a concrete reversion direction and testing it with intervention.18:T45c,The main evidence is reported in an 8B setting. During early benign fine-tuning, alignment with v_rev rises from 0.429±0.052 after the first update to 0.647±0.021 by step 20, and this same window coincides with substantial unsafe drift.
