First, it extends the OneVision-Encoder line with native dynamic-resolution processing and codec patchification, so the model is not restricted to uniformly decoded frames. Second, the key contribution is codec-stream tokenization. Instead of treating a video as a fixed-GOP frame sequence, the model views the compressed stream as a continuous bit-cost signal. Temporal group boundaries are chosen adaptively from bit-cost dynamics, under the intuition that bitrate changes often correlate with scene transitions, motion changes, or semantic novelty. Within each temporal group, motion-residual cues are used to select salient spatial evidence and condense it into compact visual canvases aligned with the merge structure. This makes token density track information change in the stream rather than wall-clock time. Third, the architecture uses spatial windowed attention to keep native-resolution computation manageable, together with group-visible masks to preserve the intended visibility pattern. A shared 3D RoPE places codec canvases, sampled video frames, and still images into one spatiotemporal coordinate system, which is important because the model is trained on mixed input pathways. Fourth, the training recipe is progressive. Stage 1 uses about 85M image-text pairs and 4.2M 30-second video captions. Stage 2 adds roughly 22M LLaVA-OneVision-1.5 instruction samples, 24M FineVision samples, plus 2.7M 30–60s and 700K 60–180s video captions. Stage 3 extends to long-form video with video-instruction corpora and 350K captions for 10–15 minute videos at 384 frames. Stage 4 re-encodes long videos with a variable-length-GOP codec pipeline at 384/768 frames and adds a 4M-sample 2D/3D spatial supervision corpus. An important design choice is that codec-patchified videos, uniformly sampled videos, and image/tiled inputs are interleaved throughout training rather than replacing one another.1b:T585,Three result groups matter most. More importantly, under matched visual-token budgets, codec-stream inputs outperform frame-sampling inputs by 9.7 points on the same benchmark. That is the clearest evidence that the token-allocation mechanism itself is doing useful work. Second, at the model level, LLaVA-OV-2-8B reports average gains of +4.3 on video benchmarks, +5.3 on spatial benchmarks, and +15.6 J&F on tracking benchmarks over Qwen3-VL-8B. Third, the paper is careful about where the method helps and where it does not. Codec-stream inputs are better for tasks dominated by coarse temporal structure—temporal grounding, event understanding, event ordering, salient retrieval—because they concentrate tokens around high-bit-cost intervals and high-residual regions. Frame sampling remains preferable for detail-sensitive queries where the decisive evidence is static, tiny, trajectory-specific, or boundary-level, because dense frame observations preserve local texture and frame-to-frame continuity better.
