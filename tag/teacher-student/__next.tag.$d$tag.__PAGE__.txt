Instead of letting the student generate a full trajectory and querying the teacher afterward, or letting the teacher dominate the whole rollout, IPD uses a bidirectional propose-and-verify state machine. Student and teacher alternate between proposer and verifier roles. The current proposer emits tokens, and the verifier checks whether the trajectory remains within an acceptable region. If the student starts to drift, the teacher takes over and provides a corrective continuation. Once the trajectory returns to a region the student can plausibly continue from, control is handed back to the student. The resulting training sample is therefore a mixed-source trajectory, with some tokens proposed by the student and others by the teacher.

The loss is the most interesting part. Rather than applying one KL objective to the entire mixed trajectory, the paper splits supervision by token source. On student-generated positions, it uses reverse KL, which is more aligned with on-policy distillation: the student is trained on states it actually visits and is encouraged to concentrate on realizable modes instead of covering every teacher mode. On teacher-generated positions, it uses forward KL, effectively imitating the teacher’s corrective continuation. This source-split objective is the main methodological contribution because it combines on-policy and off-policy distillation within the same trajectory at token granularity, not just by mixing datasets.

The paper also includes a fused inference engine that co-hosts teacher and student in one serving instance, keeps separate KV caches, and schedules requests according to the current proposer/verifier roles. This is not the conceptual core, but it matters in practice because frequent teacher intervention would otherwise make rollout expensive.

Relative to prior OPD work, the differences are clear. Standard OPD assumes teacher supervision remains meaningful on student rollouts; IPD explicitly handles the case where that assumption breaks. Prior methods often mix on-policy and off-policy data at the sample level; IPD distinguishes them at the token level. And the teacher is not just a static labeler, but an interactive corrector. The overall flavor is reminiscent of DAgger-style correction, but adapted to token-level KL training for language models.c:T683,The experiments focus on mathematical reasoning distillation across four teacher-student pairs. The main metrics are benchmark-averaged mean@8 and best@8 accuracy. The headline result is that IPD achieves the best average performance and leads on most individual benchmarks. The authors probe fixed incorrect trajectories and show that a frozen gold-conditioned teacher often prefers EOS at incorrect endpoints rather than tokens that initiate reflection or correction. Dynamic Co-Evolution addresses this by updating the privileged teacher across rounds. After each training update, the resulting checkpoint initializes both the next student and a detached gold-conditioned teacher.
