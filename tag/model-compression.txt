18:T7d5,The method has three key components. First, the architecture. HydraLLM uses an MQA backbone and treats the number of active query heads k as the single runtime width-control variable. Embedding width, attention projections, FFN width, and output-head width all scale linearly with k/Hq, and each subnetwork always takes a prefix of the corresponding dimensions. This creates a strictly nested family of subnetworks inside one parameter set. MQA is a deliberate choice: shared KV heads make head-aligned slicing cleaner than in standard multi-head attention.

Second, offline importance-based reordering. A pretrained static checkpoint does not satisfy the monotonicity needed by prefix slicing. Important units may be scattered across channels. The authors therefore collect activation statistics on a calibration corpus, score unit importance, and permute sliceable dimensions so that more important embedding channels, attention units, FFN neurons, and output channels move toward the front. They compare activation magnitude, VxC, and CETT. Their conclusion is that CETT is better aligned with prefix slicing because it measures normalized contribution to component outputs rather than raw activation strength. The permutation must be propagated consistently across linked tensors so that the model function is preserved.

Third, stochastic subnetwork training. After reordering, they adapt the checkpoint by sampling one width k per step and updating only the active subnetwork. Earlier prefix units are active under more sampled widths, so they naturally receive more gradient updates. This reinforces the importance ordering and helps shared parameters support all budgets. The training objective remains standard language modeling; the novelty is in the conversion and training schedule rather than in a new loss. For LVLMs, they add an elastic projection module that maps visual features into text embedding spaces of varying widths, so the same slicing principle extends to multimodal generation.19:T672,The experiments support three main claims. First, medium-width subnetworks remain competitive. On TriviaQA, the 75%-head HydraLLM subnetwork improves accuracy by 2.2 points over a baseline configuration that still has 17.25% more parameters. That suggests the converted subnetwork is not just a degraded pruned model.

Second, reordering is critical at small widths. At 25% active heads, importance-based reordering improves post-training perplexity by 16.2%. This is one of the most informative numbers in the paper, because it shows that the bottleneck is not only adaptation training; the checkpoint must first be reorganized for prefix slicing to make sense.

Third, the runtime gains are real. The 25% configuration increases token-generation throughput by 75.29% relative to the full model, so the method yields actual inference savings rather than only parameter-count reductions.

For multimodal experiments, the paper extends the same idea to LVLMs and reports a CLIPScore of 66.9 at 50% active heads, above the 65.3 score of Gemma 3 4B, which is described as roughly 7x larger. This indicates that the elastic projection does not destroy visual-text alignment. From a foundation-model perspective, though, CLIPScore alone is not enough; broader VLM evaluation would be needed to judge robustness.

The most useful ablations are the comparisons among importance metrics, the effect of reordering versus no reordering, and the behavior across 25%/50%/75% widths. The paper’s main empirical message is that small-subnetwork quality is driven mostly by the quality of reordering and by whether adaptation training covers the width range well.1a:T419,The experiments cover Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 14B, with detailed results on Qwen3-8B-Base and post-trained Qwen3-8B. On the post-trained Qwen3-8B, 1.99 bpw surpasses CAT-Q on all three reported measures while using 0.28 more bpw. The scale-placement ablations are also informative: in a rotated basis, RoleQ matches AdaDim-like reconstruction quality with about half the solver work and no stored axis choices; per-vector INT4 matches MXFP4 with about 8% fewer bits; ternary perplexity improves by up to roughly 2x over the best uniform placement. At 14B, RoleQ lifts MMLU from 25.4 with per-output-channel scales to 38.5. Fisher selection for the second plane beats random by 6.2 MMLU points and Hessian × weight by 9.0 points on the post-trained 8B setting.1b:T474,The paper formulates DiffusionOPD as pathwise velocity matching on states visited by the student. At each denoising step, student and teacher define Gaussian transition kernels with sampler-fixed covariance, so the per-step reverse KL reduces to a squared error between transition means. Since the transition mean is affine in the velocity field, the objective becomes matching the teacher's guided velocity along student rollouts. The standard objective matches the CFG-composed velocity, v_g = v_u + w(v_c - v_u). In cross-scale distillation, the student has limited capacity to match both the teacher's conditional and unconditional branches, and the guidance scale can amplify the residual errors. To measure this cleanly, the authors introduce Fixed-State KL, computed on fixed states from normal teacher trajectories and noise-corrupted real-image trajectories. The proposed Guidance Folding Distillation folds the effect of guidance into the distillation target, reducing the need to separately match branches and then recombine them at inference time. This reduces trajectory drift at the cost of less explicit branch decomposition.1c:T41b,The method defines the original routed MoE output for a token as a consensus, meaning the weighted mixture to be preserved, not an assumption that experts agree. Each expert is represented through its residual relative to this consensus. For a fixed layer input, RAZOR derives an exact single-deletion identity for the local output change. The identity accounts for survivor weight renormalization and, when applicable, router-selected refill that promotes an unselected expert into the active set. Without refill, the deletion shift depends on the deviation between the removed expert output and the consensus, scaled by its routing weight; this already shows why gate mass and output norm are insufficient.
