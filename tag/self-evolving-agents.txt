19:T541,The core contribution is AgentStream, a unified streaming evaluation framework. It organizes tasks from multiple agent benchmarks into a sequential task stream. After each task, the agent updates an evolution state using its trajectory and self-generated feedback. That state can represent different self-evolution mechanisms: prompt/context refinement, structured memory, reusable skill libraries, or a more integrated harness.

The most important design choice is the three streaming scenarios. In Isolated, each benchmark forms its own stream, so experience is accumulated only within domain. In Sequential, benchmarks are concatenated in a fixed order, so experience transfers from one benchmark to the next. In Interleaved, tasks from all benchmarks are shuffled into a single mixed stream. This is not just a data-order variation. It explicitly controls two variables: the scope of experience transfer and the degree of domain mixing.

The evaluation spans five representative self-evolving methods, ACE, A-Mem, ReasoningBank, AutoSkill, and Harness; three frontier foundation models, GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Opus 4.7; and six agent benchmarks, AppWorld, BFCL, BrowseComp-Plus, HLE, SWE-bench-Verified, and Tau2. The paper is therefore an interaction study across scenario, model, and method, rather than a new algorithm paper.1a:T6aa,There are three main findings.

First, self-evolution is not reliably beneficial, and the streaming scenario matters a lot. Isolated is usually the most reliable because the experience distribution is narrower and less contaminated. Sequential is often worse than Interleaved, which is somewhat counterintuitive. The paper’s interpretation is that fixed-order transfer can inject strong domain-specific bias from earlier benchmarks into later ones, causing negative transfer, whereas interleaving keeps the agent exposed to multiple domains and can work better for retrieval-based experience mechanisms.

Second, gains are gated by model capability. The weakest model can show negative evolution gain, meaning self-evolution makes it worse. A mid-capability model can benefit more than a stronger one, so the effect is not monotonic in base-model strength. A reasonable reading is that the model must be strong enough to extract signal from noisy self-generated experience, but once the base model is already very capable, the marginal value of external evolution mechanisms may shrink.

Third, no single self-evolution method dominates across all models and scenarios. Context-integrated methods tend to work better in Isolated streams, while retrieval-based methods are stronger in Interleaved streams. Method choice also depends on the underlying model, so one should not assume that a method that works on one model will transfer cleanly to another.

The paper’s value lies less in any single score and more in the combinatorial structure of the evaluation.
