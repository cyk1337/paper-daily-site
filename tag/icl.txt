18:T5d8,There are two main components. First, FakeContext-bench. Each instance contains demonstrations consistent with a contextual rule P, a query, and two reference answers: y_R under the real rule and y_P under the misleading contextual rule. Outputs are categorized into Reality, Context, or Other using a deterministic rule-based judge, with human validation of the scoring protocol. The benchmark spans seven domains: physics, mathematics, chemistry, biology, statistics, history, and computer science. The point is not domain difficulty, but controlled construction of cases where the context is coherent yet lacks real authority.

Second, Jurisdiction In-Context Learning (J-ICL). The method keeps the standard few-shot interface and uses only query-only LM loss. In J+ episodes, the demonstrated rule genuinely applies to the query, so the correct answer should follow the context. In J- episodes, the demonstrations still support a coherent rule, but that rule is private, conflicting, or undeployed, while the query requires the reality-grounded or formally valid answer. No explicit authority labels, auxiliary classifier, or rationale supervision is added. The model is expected to infer applicability from natural-language cues and episode structure. This is the key distinction from methods such as MetaICL or Symbol Tuning, which mainly improve rule extraction from demonstrations. J-ICL tries to train both rule induction and rule applicability judgment within the same prompting format.19:T63d,The empirical story has three important parts. First, baseline diagnosis: both proprietary and open-weight models are vulnerable on FakeContext-bench. Larger scale and stronger pretraining do not reliably yield context-authority discrimination. The failure is often not that the model misses the demonstrated pattern, but that it successfully infers the false rule and applies it where it should not.

Second, existing ICL-tuning methods can worsen the problem. The paper reports that prevalent ICL fine-tuning approaches improve conventional few-shot performance but can reduce reality-grounded accuracy by as much as 14.95 percentage points relative to the base model under misleading context. This is the central result, because it shows a systematic objective-level trade-off rather than a mere capability gap.

Third, J-ICL mitigates that trade-off. Training with mixed J+ and J- episodes improves robustness to misleading context while preserving, and in some settings improving, standard ICL ability. The abstract does not expose the full table of numbers, but the full paper appears to emphasize: (1) higher Reality rates and lower Context rates on FakeContext-bench, (2) no major collapse on conventional few-shot tasks, and (3) ablations showing that the gain does not come from simply adding negative examples, but from exposing the model to both apply-context and reject-context episodes in a shared format. 后半部分把这个模板推广到 KL-regularized RL、OPD 和 test-time search。虽然给出的全文片段主要覆盖 Part I，但从摘要与行文结构可以看出，作者采用的是统一的 Gibbs posterior 形式：q*(y|x) ∝ π_ref(y|x) exp(U(x,y)/β)，其中 U 可以是 log-likelihood、reward、advantage 或 search-induced utility。SFT 对应 U=log q_data 或等价的 teacher log-density；KL-regularized RL 对应 reward-weighted posterior；OPD 则是在 student on-policy 轨迹上，用 teacher 分布去近似某个参考后验；search / CoT 则可被看成 test-time 对 latent reasoning path 的 posterior reweighting。这个统一视角的关键区别在于，它不把这些方法按“训练范式”分开，而是按“先验 + 效用 + 近似器”来分解。这样能更清楚地解释：为什么 reverse-KL 风格的 policy matching 往往 mode-seeking，为什么 SFT 的 forward-KL 更像覆盖 teacher 支持集，以及为什么 few-shot demonstrations 有时像给 posterior 提供额外观测，有时却会和 RL 后的 policy 发生冲突。1c:T46f,This note is not proposing a new training recipe. It is trying to give a single probabilistic lens for several procedures that are usually discussed separately: few-shot ICL, SFT, KL-regularized RL, on-policy distillation, and test-time search / chain-of-thought. The central claim is that they can all be written in the same two-step pattern: first construct a generalized Bayes or Gibbs posterior from a prior/reference policy plus a utility signal; then fit a parametric model or policy to approximate that posterior or its posterior predictive.
