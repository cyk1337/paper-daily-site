18:T518,The central thesis is “substrate inversion” with two structural commitments:
- A single agent-native reasoning surface: the agent reads and reasons over one shared substrate expressed in a form the LLM can consume in one pass (Markdown is presented as a pragmatic instantiation, not a final primitive).
- Schema translation isolated at the action boundary: a Sync Agent (or boundary layer) compiles legacy typed data into the substrate and translates decisions back into executable actions, preventing repeated schema crossings during reasoning.
Two mechanisms justify why these commitments matter:
1) Context-bandwidth asymmetry: LLMs can ingest large, relationally rich prose in one forward pass, while field-by-field typed access strips relations at each schema boundary, forcing the model to reconstruct relational context in working memory.
2) Cross-loop coupling: action, skill, and policy improvement loops compound only if they share the same substrate; otherwise improvements remain siloed across incompatible representations.
