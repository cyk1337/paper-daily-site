9:T407,The paper is not merely about faster video generation. It targets a stricter setting: single-GPU, real-time, native streaming, joint audio-video autoregressive generation over long horizons. The motivating domain is social video, where speech rhythm, lip motion, facial expression, ambient sound, and conversational pacing must stay synchronized while the model emits content continuously. Standard bidirectional video diffusion models are a poor fit for this setting for two structural reasons: they are expensive because they denoise large spatiotemporal token grids over many steps, and they are not naturally streamable because an entire clip is generated jointly before frames can be finalized. Prior streaming or autoregressive video generators improve latency, but many are vision-centric, treat audio only as conditioning, drift over long rollouts, or fail to achieve true real-time generation on a single GPU. MaineCoon is positioned as a first prototype for a social world model that addresses these constraints together.a:T5c0,From the visible full text, the central move is to build social video generation as native streaming autoregressive audio-visual generation rather than patching a standard diffusion pipeline. The design appears to have several components. First, generation is causal: the model emits future frames or chunks conditioned on historical context and reuses past states, making it closer to language-model-style rollout than full-clip bidirectional denoising. Second, audio is not just an external condition; it is part of the generated target, so the model must learn a shared temporal representation linking speech content, prosody, lip motion, facial dynamics, and scene evolution. Third, the emphasis on long-horizon continuation and single-GPU real-time inference suggests system-level choices such as caching, chunked context, local attention, or very low-step generation to avoid the scaling bottleneck of full spatiotemporal attention. Fourth, the paper introduces the broader notion of a social world model, where the generator is only the generative core and may later be paired with observation, memory, user-state simulation, and planning modules. Relative to standard video diffusion, the novelty is less about visual quality tuning and more about changing both the training target and inference paradigm toward online interaction.
