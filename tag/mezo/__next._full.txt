17:T663,AdaMeZO keeps the SPSA/MeZO gradient estimator as the base primitive. At each step, it perturbs parameters along a random direction z_t, computes a finite-difference loss projection from forward passes, and reconstructs the update direction using the same pseudorandom seed. The challenge is that Adam requires m_t and v_t, two full-size state vectors.

The key idea is to replace exact persistent moments with truncated, reconstructable approximations. First, AdaMeZO uses truncated first- and second-moment estimates rather than full EMAs over the entire optimization history. Older gradients are discarded, motivated by the fact that EMA already downweights them exponentially and their contribution is small relative to recent noisy zeroth-order estimates.

Second, the method exploits finer-grained PRNG replay. Instead of materializing full random directions globally, it regenerates them block by block. This allows the optimizer to reconstruct local pieces of the recent moment history on demand from stored scalar projections and PRNG seeds, rather than storing full moment tensors. In effect, it trades lightweight recomputation for memory.

This is the main distinction from prior adaptive ZO methods. HiZOO explicitly approximates diagonal Hessian information. Helene is closer to a direct Adam-style ZO optimizer with explicit state. AdaMeZO instead focuses on eliminating optimizer-state residency while preserving most of the benefit of preconditioning. The paper also provides a non-convex convergence result showing that the rate recovers that of preconditioned MeZO up to approximation terms introduced by truncation.18:T444,The experiments are designed to show both optimization behavior and practical LLM tuning gains. On 2D toy landscapes, the optimization trajectories illustrate the intended effect clearly: AdaMeZO follows more stable paths toward minima, while MeZO is more prone to oscillation or poor convergence under the same step budget.

The main empirical section fine-tunes RoBERTa, OPT, and LLaMA on the same task suite used by MeZO, which makes the comparison clean. The headline result is efficiency rather than just end accuracy: AdaMeZO almost always reaches the same stopping criterion as MeZO with up to 70% fewer forward passes, while also delivering better final task performance. That matters because forward evaluations are the dominant cost in zeroth-order tuning.

The most informative ablations are the ones around truncation length, block-wise reconstruction granularity, and the stability of gains across model families. The paper’s claim is not merely that adaptive scaling helps, but that most of the benefit can be retained without paying the usual memory cost of Adam-like
