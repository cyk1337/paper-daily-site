9:T7fe,The paper combines a behavioral analysis with a mechanistic one. In the behavioral part, the authors track fact acquisition across training checkpoints of OLMo-3-7B IT. They define an emergence step E(f, t) for each fact-task pair and test whether facts co-emerge across tasks. Under a task-invariant storage hypothesis, once the model has the competence to perform multiple tasks, learning a fact on one task should coincide with learning it on the others. The evaluation uses five relational datasets, 230 facts in total, and six task formats: next-token completion, fill-in-the-blank, open-ended QA, multiple-choice QA, negative multiple-choice QA, and binary verification. Each dataset-task pair is probed with 10 paraphrases, and discrimination tasks are further expanded by answer-position rotation or true/false pairing to reduce prompt artifacts.

The mechanistic part adapts a parameter localization framework from prior work. For each (fact, task) pair, the authors search for a sparse parameter subset whose removal selectively degrades performance on that pair while minimally affecting other facts on the same task or the same fact on other tasks. If factual knowledge were task-invariant, such selective localization should be difficult or impossible. Instead, the paper finds that these sparse, pair-specific parameter subsets can be identified consistently across three models and five relational datasets.

The analysis is then extended in two ways. First, the authors define separability metrics to quantify how isolated a given (fact, task) encoding is from others. They find that discrimination tasks such as MCQA are more entangled, while generation tasks such as fill-in-the-blank are more separable. Second, they study chain-of-thought. After ablating localized encodings, CoT can recover much of the loss caused by removing a task's own encoding, but it suffers more than direct answering when encodings from other tasks are removed. The mechanistic analysis spans three models and five relational datasets.
