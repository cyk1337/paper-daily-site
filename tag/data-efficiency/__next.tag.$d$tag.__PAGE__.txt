At each search state, given the evidence accumulated so far, an evaluator generates a state-conditioned answer. That answer is then scored with the same verifiable metric used for the final answer, such as exact match or F1. The score improvement between consecutive states becomes the process reward. This makes the reward signal answer-oriented rather than proxy-oriented: the question is whether a search step makes the final answer more supportable, not whether it merely looks relevant.

The second key design is search–evaluation co-training. Instead of using a separate frozen evaluator, external judge, or fixed likelihood model, OASES uses a shared-parameter model as both the search policy and the state evaluator, optimized with PPO. The motivation is to reduce evaluator–policy mismatch. A frozen evaluator may become stale as the policy changes its search behavior, while likelihood-based evaluators can be inconsistent with the policy’s own generation dynamics under partial prefixes. Jointly training search and evaluation is meant to keep the process reward adaptive to the current policy distribution.
