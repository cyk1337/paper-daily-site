a:T60b,Built on a pretrained text-to-audio diffusion transformer trained with flow matching over VAE latent tokens. The target audio is encoded to z0 and noised to zt; K reference clips are encoded with the same VAE to rk and kept clean. The transformer input is a single concatenated token sequence [zt; r1; ...; rK] with full self-attention across target and references. References are distinguished by lightweight slot identity embeddings (one learned vector per reference slot, added to all tokens of that reference) rather than speaker encoders/adapters. A single holistic natural-language prompt describes the whole scene and refers to voices as “reference 1/2/...”.

Core technical contribution: identifying the “reference shortcut” in reference-conditioned flow matching. Under the commonly used logit-normal timestep distribution, training mass concentrates on low-to-moderate noise where zt still preserves speaker cues; the model can pick the correct reference by acoustic similarity, achieving low loss while ignoring text-based role binding. Since generation starts from pure noise, this shortcut is unavailable at test time. The authors localize the shortcut with a probe trained on frozen audio features, showing robust reference–target matching across the low/moderate noise range, and then run noise-schedule ablations showing binding metrics improve monotonically as training shifts to higher noise.
