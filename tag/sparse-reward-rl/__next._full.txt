On this pool, the authors train 11 arms under identical data, budget, seed, and hardware conditions. For text priors, they prepend part of a reference solution so exploration starts from a more promising state. An important decomposition is that the gain comes from improved exploration itself, while the auxiliary UFT-style likelihood term adds little. For distribution priors, they compare several on-policy distillation variants and show that delivery matters: if the signal is capped by teacher ability, gated only to failures, or otherwise poorly routed, it does not help much. Annealed global distillation works; failure-gated persistent distillation does not. For value priors, they train a critic to provide token-level credit assignment even when sequence-level outcomes are uniform. The key finding is not merely that a critic helps, but that critic parameterization matters a lot: replacing clipped MSE scalar regression with an HL-Gauss categorical loss substantially improves signal delivery.
