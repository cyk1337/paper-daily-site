18:T4ab,The method is called Declarative Attention (DA). The model is prompted to emit parseable control tags inside its chain-of-thought, declaring the attention scope it needs at each phase of generation. An inference-side state machine reads these tags online and constructs the attention mask dynamically. DA defines three modes. <global> allows access to the full context and is used when the model needs to survey or search broadly. <focus> commits the model to a specific region of the context, restricting attention to that region. <local> limits attention to recent generated tokens, avoiding long-context reads altogether. This is the key departure from prior sparse-attention methods: the selection signal comes from the model’s own generated reasoning trace, not from hidden-state proxy scorers, recency heuristics, historical attention statistics, or an auxiliary scan over the cache. The paper also includes a practical implementation in vLLM using block-aligned, in-place KV-cache masking compatible with FlashAttention. Another notable point is that the protocol works zero-shot on off-the-shelf models, without task-specific fine-tuning or hand-designed partitions for each benchmark.19:T4cc,The evaluation covers 15 long-context tasks, with main results reported on Gemma-4-31B and Qwen-3.6-27B. The central finding is a favorable cost-accuracy trade-off. DA reduces average decoding attention cost by 52.0% on Gemma-4-31B with only a 1.27 percentage-point accuracy drop, and by 31.1% on Qwen-3.6-27B with a 2.75-point drop. The paper also shows positive scaling behavior: as model size increases from 4B to 31B, the accuracy gap between DA and full attention narrows, suggesting that self-declared attention depends on model capability. Absolute savings grow with context length, reaching up to 21 million attended tokens saved per response. The ablations are important. Relative to a maskless ablation, the DA-induced dynamic mask cuts attended tokens by as much as 71.1%, indicating that the savings are driven by the masking mechanism itself rather than by prompt formatting alone. On the systems side, a roofline-style analysis estimates decode wall-clock cost reductions to 0.71× of vanilla for Gemma-4-31B and 0.77× for Qwen-3.6-27B on a well-optimized serving stack. For each token, the model computes spectral entropy from the DCT power spectrum of its representation and routes the token to one of three operators: DCT spectral mixing, RBF kernel mixing, or full self-attention. Low-entropy tokens are treated as smooth and sent to DCT mixing. High-entropy tokens are treated as complex and sent to attention. Mid-range tokens are initially assigned to an RBF kernel branch implemented with random Fourier features.

The paper provides a theoretical motivation for this partition: low-entropy tokens admit bounded DCT truncation error, medium-entropy tokens can be handled by kernel approximation, and high-entropy tokens benefit from dynamic cross-token projection. Relative to FNet, the key difference is selective rather than universal spectral mixing. Relative to Mixture-of-Depths or LayerSkip, the model does not skip computation but changes the operator type while preserving depth. The most important technical outcome, however, is that learned routing collapses: the router repeatedly rejects the RBF branch.
