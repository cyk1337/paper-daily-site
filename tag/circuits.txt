18:T4be,The approach adapts neuroscience-style functional localizers and network recruitment analyses to LLM circuits:
- Task suite: 46 tasks spanning four domains, used to elicit domain-consistent activation patterns rather than to optimize a single benchmark.
- Module identification: contrastive analyses over internal representations (e.g., residual stream components, MLP/attention outputs, or discovered directions) to find units/subnetworks selectively engaged by a domain.
- Segregation tests: quantify overlap vs separation between domain-selective sets, checking whether domain modules are approximately distinct and consistent across tasks/prompts.
- Causal tests (if included in the full paper): ablation/patching/activation interventions to show domain-specific performance dependence, moving from correlation to causation.
- Brain analogy: map discovered LLM modules onto coarse human networks (language, Multiple Demand, Theory of Mind, intuitive physics) to argue convergent functional decomposition.
Key difference from prior ‘feature neuron’ work is the attempt to characterize domain-level functional organization with a unified task-contrast methodology and explicit overlap/segregation evaluation.19:T532,The work follows a mechanistic interpretability/circuit-discovery approach. The central decomposition is: a shared, abstract Latent Evaluator subgraph (primarily in mid-to-late MLP blocks) computes a stable judgment, while format-specific terminal pathways (Task Formatters) map that judgment into the requested output tokens.

Pipeline:
1) Construct contrastive minimal pairs (clean vs. corrupted prompts with matched token structure) for multiple judging tasks.
2) Use PEAP (Position-aware Edge Attribution Patching) to attribute and causally test cross-token edges efficiently—important because judge prompts often require integrating separated spans (premise vs. hypothesis, instruction vs. candidate answer).
3) Identify sparse top-k edge subgraphs (k ≤ 200) that explain judgment behavior, and test cross-task overlap to isolate a generalized evaluator circuit.
4) Triangulate with three independent causal probes to reduce non-identifiability: cumulative edge patching, subspace steering, and cross-format activation transfer (testing whether internal evaluator activations can be transplanted across output formats).
5) Validate modularity via zero-ablation of the Latent Evaluator and measure whether judgment collapses while world knowledge remains intact; compare this separability across model families and scales.1a:T40d,Tasks/datasets: CoLA, MultiNLI, STS-B (structured understanding), RewardBench (open-ended preference judging), Yelp (ordinal sentiment). Models: Gemma-3-12B-it/27B-it, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct/14B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (via TransformerLens). Minimal-pair subsets are ~100–200 per dataset (MNLI capped at 500) with split-half checks for circuit IoU stability.

Main empirical takeaways are structural/causal rather than leaderboard-style numbers:
- A highly sparse shared circuit (recoverable with top-k ≤ 200 edges) supports judgment across tasks, concentrated in mid-to-late MLPs.
- Modularity is architecture/scale dependent: Qwen shows evaluator/knowledge separability already at 7B; Gemma-3-12B exhibits stronger entanglement (ablations harm both judgment and knowledge), while Gemma-3-27B is more modular.
