19:T4be,They introduce CAPE (Capability Coupling Analysis of Phase Emergence). The key metric is *local coupling* within a family: γ12(N)=ΔTruthfulQA/ΔHellaSwag between adjacent sizes, i.e., a derivative of one capability with respect to another rather than with respect to N. They distinguish this from *population coupling* r(B1,B2), a Pearson correlation across a panel of models.
Empirically, γ12 is negative at small scale and crosses zero at a recipe-dependent critical size Nc, then grows roughly linearly with log10(N/Nc): γ12(N,D)=γ0(D)·log10(N/Nc(D)), with both slope and Nc depending on the training recipe D.
They argue Nc is independently shiftable by (i) data curation (Qwen generations), (ii) architecture/distillation/post-training (Gemma-4 at 4B showing “large-model-like” coupling), and (iii) width at fixed parameter count (width-normalized coupling being more consistently positive).
For mechanism, they report little evidence of “competing attention heads” in most models and suggest the bottleneck is closer to the output projection/readout than internal attention competition. They also fit a sparse-regression ODE to coupling dynamics and report cross-prediction on held-out Llama-2.1a:T40e,The study aggregates public benchmark results over 63 base models from 16 families. Reported headline numbers include:
- Pythia (70M–12B, 8 models): reasoning vs truthfulness population correlation r=-0.989 (p<1e-5).
- Larger models across families (e.g., Llama/Falcon/OPT above ~7B): correlation becomes strongly positive (stated as r>+0.78), and a “frontier” panel of 34 models from 10 labs yields r=+0.72.
- Estimated critical scale Nc ≈ 3.5B parameters with bootstrap 95% CI [2.9B, 13.4B], emphasizing strong family/recipe dependence.
- Recipe shift examples: Qwen coupling at matched scale improves from 0.025 to 0.830 across generations; Gemma-4 at 4B reports coupling 0.871, described as typical of standard-trained 13B+; OLMo sits near γ12=0.000 at the predicted transition scale.
- Sparse ODE cross-predicts held-out Llama-2 with 5.6% error (as defined by the authors).
