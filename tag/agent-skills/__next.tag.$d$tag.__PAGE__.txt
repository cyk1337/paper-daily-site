5:T729,The method centers on a skill compiler, a hypernetwork G_phi that takes a skill document s and outputs LoRA updates for selected modules of a frozen backbone LLM. At inference time, the generated adapter is mounted onto the model, and the agent conditions only on the task history rather than re-inserting the skill text into the prompt.

Training has two stages. First, document-level pretraining initializes the compiler on a corpus of skill documents. Two self-supervised tasks are used. In reconstruction, the compiler reads the full skill document and the adapted backbone is trained to reproduce it under a reconstruction instruction. In completion, the compiler reads a truncated prefix and the adapted backbone is trained to complete the full document. The point is not document generation per se, but learning a stable mapping from procedural text to useful parameter updates.

Second, trajectory-supervised fine-tuning aligns the generated latent skills with actual downstream behavior in agent trajectories. The backbone remains frozen, while the compiler is updated so that the generated LoRA affects action generation in the intended way. This differs from ordinary SFT: the skill is constrained to live in adapter space, preserving modular loading, unloading, replacement, scaling, and potential composition.

The paper also studies three properties of the generated adapters. They are structured, in that skills from different domains cluster in weight space. They are controllable, since the LoRA scaling coefficient alpha smoothly modulates the skill effect. They are composable, but only when the original skills are decomposed into semantically aligned components before combining their adapters. That last point is important: composition is conditional on the granularity and alignment of the skill decomposition.6:T567,The main evaluations are on ALFWorld and Search-QA. Against the corresponding in-context skill baseline, LatentSkill improves ALFWorld success by 21.4 points on the seen split and 13.4 points on the unseen split, while reducing prefill tokens by 64.1%. The abstract truncates the Search-QA numbers, but the full paper reports the same qualitative pattern: better task performance with substantially less prompt overhead from skill text.

The analysis experiments are more informative than the headline numbers. Weight-space visualizations show domain-level clustering of generated skill adapters, suggesting that the compiler learns structured procedural representations rather than a trivial prompt compression. Alpha-scaling experiments show smooth control over skill strength. Composition experiments show that adapter combination works best when skills are decomposed into aligned subskills; coarse or entangled skills compose less reliably. This defines the method’s boundary: it is better viewed as compiling modular procedural knowledge than as a universal replacement for arbitrary natural-language instructions.

From a systems perspective, the gains should be largest in long-horizon agent settings where the same skill text would otherwise be reinserted many times. The paper examines an increasingly important alternative to weight updates: accumulating capability through externalized artifacts distilled from interaction data. That sits close to continual pretraining, trajectory distillation, memory-augmented systems, and test-time adaptation. The main contribution is analytical rather than architectural. First, it separates extractor-side quality from target-side receptivity through two metrics, Extraction Efficacy (EE) and Target Evolvability (TE). That is a useful decomposition, similar in spirit to separating teacher quality from student learnability. Second, it highlights that failed trajectories are not just noise; they can encode reusable negative knowledge, which matters for any pipeline that distills behavior traces into training signals. Third, it shows that skill utility is conditional: it depends on whether the experience pool contains recurring abstractions, whether the extractor can compress them into transferable procedures, and whether the target can actually consume those procedures at inference time. This is best read as a strong measurement-and-analysis paper, not as a new dominant method.c:T879,The technical design is intentionally minimal. The authors want the extractor model, not pipeline scaffolding, to explain most of the variance. The lifecycle is instantiated as follows. A target model M executes tasks from a domain-specific training split and produces an experience pool containing task, trajectory, and outcome tuples, with both successful and failed runs retained. An extractor model E then processes each trajectory independently and produces a pattern set with multiple success and failure patterns per trajectory. These local pattern sets are merged through a tree-structured reduction procedure into a single domain-level skill under a fixed schema and budget. That skill is fed back to the same target model M, which is then evaluated on a held-out test split; the change relative to a no-skill baseline is used as the utility signal.

Several design choices matter. The schema and budget are fixed, so extractors cannot win by simply emitting longer or differently formatted artifacts. The extractor and target can differ, which allows the study to disentangle who is good at summarizing experience from who is good at using summarized experience. The two proposed metrics formalize this split: EE measures how reliably a fixed extractor produces helpful skills across targets, while TE measures how much a fixed target benefits from skills extracted by different extractors from its own experience. The analysis then probes all three stages: whether the experience pool has enough quality and recurring structure, whether extraction preserves the right abstractions and failure modes, and whether the target actually reads and operationalizes the skill rather than treating it as extra context.

The paper also includes a drop-in improvement to skill extraction derived from the empirical analysis. The provided text does not expose the full Section 6 details, but the framing makes clear that the improvement is not a wholesale new architecture. It is an intervention informed by lifecycle findings, likely around which trajectories to prioritize, how to encode failure knowledge, and how to preserve transferable abstractions during merging.d:T6bc,The study spans five domains: embodied planning, productivity software, software engineering, web search, and tool calling. It systematically varies both the extractor and the target, which is much more informative than evaluating a single agent stack on one benchmark. Utility is measured as the performance delta on held-out tasks relative to a no-skill baseline.

The experimental program is organized around three questions. For RQ1, the paper tests whether model-generated domain-level skills provide reliable gains across domains, targets, and extractors, rather than assuming that skills are broadly useful. For RQ2, it analyzes the drivers of utility at each lifecycle stage: properties of the experience pool such as success/failure composition and pattern recurrence; extraction properties such as abstraction quality, redundancy, and the handling of success versus failure patterns; and consumption properties such as whether the target actually follows the skill on new tasks. For RQ3, it turns these findings into a drop-in extraction improvement and evaluates whether that improves robustness.
