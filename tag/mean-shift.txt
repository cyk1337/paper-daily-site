18:T74a,The analysis starts from the simplest binary geometry. For hidden states labeled factual vs hallucinated, define class means μ0 and μ1, and let δ = μ1 − μ0 be the mean-shift direction. The authors first test necessity: remove the component along δ and see whether classification survives. It does not; performance collapses to chance. They then test sufficiency: project onto δ alone and classify in 1D. That already recovers a large fraction of the signal, but still trails regularized logistic regression.

This motivates the second layer of analysis. In the binary case, Fisher LDA uses ΣW^-1δ rather than raw δ, so if within-class covariance is far from spherical, covariance-aware linear methods can outperform the pure mean-shift direction without invoking nonlinear structure. Since the hidden dimension is around 4,000, covariance estimation is nontrivial. The paper therefore compares raw mean-shift projection, shrinkage LDA, L2-regularized logistic regression, and a set of controlled alternatives designed to isolate mean-targeting, variance-targeting, and nonlinear effects. The result is that shrinkage LDA closes about 73% of the gap between the 1D mean-shift classifier and the full-dimensional classifier, supporting the view that most of the remaining gain is about covariance estimation under noise, not richer geometry.

LayerMix is then introduced as a practical consequence of the analysis rather than a major algorithmic invention. Since the signal is not confined to a single oracle layer but spread across a contiguous band of layers, LayerMix uses lightweight cross-validated aggregation across layers to identify and combine that informative band. The framing is intentionally modest: it is a strong, cheap baseline derived from the geometry, not a claim that multi-layer aggregation is itself the main scientific contribution.19:T5fb,The experiments cover three 7B-scale models and three datasets under a paired-example protocol. The headline result is that removing the mean-shift direction δ drops AUROC to 0.499, essentially chance, which makes δ necessary for detection. Using only the 1D projection onto δ yields 0.834 AUROC, recovering about 78% of the above-chance performance. A practical L2-regularized logistic regression reaches 0.952 AUROC, showing that there is still useful structure beyond raw δ, but that structure remains largely linear and covariance-related.

The paper also reports Cohen’s d of roughly 1.2–1.6 along δ, even though that direction explains less than 3% of total variance. This is an important geometric point: discriminative signal can live in a very low-variance direction. In controlled comparisons against 12 alternative probe architectures, L2-LR matches or outperforms them, which supports the claim that many complex probes are mostly dealing with estimation noise rather than exploiting meaningful nonlinear structure. LayerMix reaches 0.954 AUROC without oracle-layer access and adds only about 35 seconds of overhead, suggesting that the main benefit comes from exploiting a distributed informative layer band rather than from more expressive modeling.

A good aspect of the paper is that it states its boundary conditions clearly.
