9:T4ee,WPMIA replaces token-level likelihood (unavailable) with an estimable word-level likelihood derived from Monte Carlo sampling.

1) Word-level probability estimation: represent the target text as a sequence of words. For each position, query the model multiple times conditioned on the preceding words, collect sampled continuations, and estimate P(next_word = w_t | prefix) by frequency. Local kernel smoothing is applied to reduce variance and avoid brittle zero counts under finite sampling.

2) Sequence-level likelihood estimator: aggregate the estimated word probabilities via a chain-rule style log-likelihood (typically mean log-likelihood) to obtain a membership score that mirrors classic likelihood-based MIAs.

3) Prefix-conditioned contrast: build two conditioning contexts (member-like vs non-member-like prefixes) and estimate the sequence likelihood under each, then contrast them to amplify the membership gap. A notable design choice is to contrast at the sequence level (after aggregation) instead of taking unstable word-level probability ratios.
