9:T5dc,The paper defines the premium of a language as the total number of tokens for its translated corpus divided by the total number of tokens for the English source. Texts are NFC-normalized and counted without special tokens. The main benchmark is NTREX-128: 1,997 English news sentences from 123 documents with professional translations. After excluding English variants and one duplicate Spanish reference, the analysis uses 124 non-English references. For regional languages missing from NTREX, it uses the Universal Declaration of Human Rights; Universal Dependencies corpora are used for additional checks.

The measured tokenizers include OpenAI o200k, Meta Llama 3.1, Alibaba Qwen3, DeepSeek V3/V4, Google Gemma 3, Mistral Tekken, and the Claude generation-5 tokenizer via Anthropic’s counting API. Public tokenizers are cross-checked against official implementations or official Hugging Face files. Claude’s tokenizer is not public, so counts are obtained sentence by sentence through 7,991 counting requests, subtracting a measured 7-token message overhead. Confidence intervals are obtained by bootstrapping the 123 NTREX documents, and paired comparisons use document-level resampling. The paper also decomposes the French premium, runs a controlled experiment on the French share in tokenizer training data, and introduces Baracoda FR v1.2. The prototype is compared with Mistral Tekken and CroissantLLM, with an explicit caveat that it does not always win at comparable vocabulary size.a:T4ae,On NTREX, French requires 1.31 to 1.58 times as many tokens as English across the seven mainstream tokenizers. Mistral Tekken is around 1.31, Llama 3 around 1.58, and Claude generation-5 around 1.45 with a 95% confidence interval of 1.44–1.47. Document-level 95% intervals for French and Chinese are within ±2.3 percentage points. Chinese ranges from 0.95 to 1.40 and is cheaper than French on six of the seven tokenizers. Among the 124 non-English references, French ranks only between the 9th and 24th cheapest, while the median language requires 1.69 to 2.15 times the English token count.

The paper also reports measurements for 18 regional, overseas, and Francophone languages, where tokenization costs are generally higher. Claude generation-5 reduces the French premium compared with Haiku 4.5, which was about 1.57 on concatenated text, but Claude 5 segments all texts more finely. The Baracoda FR v1.2 experiments show that French-oriented tokenizer optimization can reduce parts of the French premium, but controlling for vocabulary size weakens the advantage. This points to a real tradeoff between language-specific compression, vocabulary capacity, and broad multilingual coverage.b:T57b,Experiments use Claude Code with Claude Sonnet and Codex with GPT-5 on real repositories such as textual, jinja2, werkzeug, rich, httpx, and tox. To isolate tool filtering, they toggle it while holding the content path fixed, using strict MCP configuration to reduce tool-block jitter. The local Ollama backend and GPU server produce identical compression outputs; the reported segment ratio is about 0.44 for line-numbered reads, with some content compressed to roughly 26% of its original size. The main empirical result is that tool filtering is the dominant and reliably positive lever, saving about 21K tokens per turn. Content compression is often only a roughly 2% marginal effect in real sessions and is easily drowned by turn-count variance. End-to-end savings are about 25% on a single turn and about 39% by turn five in a long session. The measured scaling is linear in N for tool filtering and approximately quadratic for content compression, with a fitted content saving near 3,350 N^2 tokens and a crossover around N≈6, subject to context-window limits.
