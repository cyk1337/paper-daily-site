18:T47a,The authors build a controlled pretraining setup using a Fictional Knowledge Dataset with 2,048 synthetic facts about invented entities and attributes. Each fact is rendered into diverse documents across languages for injection during pretraining, with held-out multiple-choice questions for evaluation. For a fact, the authors know exactly how many times it appeared in language A and language B. They then regress target-language accuracy on native and foreign exposure counts. The ratio between the foreign-exposure coefficient and the native-exposure coefficient defines CL_eq. A score of 100% means that one exposure in the foreign language is as useful as one exposure in the target language; 0% means no effective transfer. To remove natural-language confounds, the paper also evaluates cloned language copies that are structurally identical but occupy different token spaces. This isolates vocabulary separation from syntax or semantics. The main intervention, word-wise translation, is motivated by this mechanism: it tries to unify token spaces for otherwise vocabulary-disjoint languages rather than relying on post-training alignment.19:T426,The experiments pretrain bilingual models from scratch while injecting fictional facts at controlled rates. Two scales are used: a 360M-parameter SmolLM2-style model and a 7B-parameter Llama-3 adaptation, both with a 65,536-token BPE vocabulary. The dataset validation shows chance-level MCQ accuracy with zero injection and a roughly log-shaped improvement as injected paraphrases increase, confirming that the facts are learnable and exposure-controlled. The main measurements show that compartmentalization already appears during pretraining. Disjoint token spaces strongly reduce cross-lingual transfer, and this effect persists across model scales. The authors also replicate key checks with a fictive-persona dataset built around high-frequency human names, reducing the chance that the result is only about rare synthetic entity tokens.
