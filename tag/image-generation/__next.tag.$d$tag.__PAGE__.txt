第一是 T2I data engine，目标是建立广覆盖的 text-image grounding。全文提到它会做概念覆盖扩展、长尾重平衡、多粒度 caption 对齐，以及 dense caption 构造。这里的关键不是单纯提高 caption 质量，而是让 T2I 数据承担“概念底座”的角色，为后续结构化生成、文本渲染和编辑提供可迁移的视觉词汇与语义对齐。

第二是 image-editing data engine。作者把编辑监督视为多种操作类型的组合，因此不是只收集 source-target pairs，而是为不同编辑类型设计专门构造流程，同时从自然相关图像中挖掘真实关联，并用专家生成样本补足稀缺编辑类型。更重要的是，编辑指令在词汇和描述结构上与 T2I caption 对齐，这样编辑任务可以复用 T2I 已学到的概念表示，而不必在编辑数据里重复覆盖全部视觉概念。

第三是 knowledge-grounded data engine。它通过知识图谱引导的数据获取，把视觉模式与命名实体、结构化知识关联起来。这个设计针对的是一般图像生成模型常见的知识薄弱问题：模型能画出“像某物”的图，但未必能稳定绑定实体、属性和关系。

On the data side, the authors build three specialized but interoperable engines instead of throwing all samples into one global pool.

The first is a T2I data engine for broad text-image grounding. It expands concept coverage, rebalances long-tail distributions, aligns captions at multiple granularities, and uses dense captions where needed. The important design choice is that T2I data is treated as the conceptual base layer. It is not only for standalone text-to-image quality; it is meant to provide reusable visual vocabulary and semantic alignment for later capabilities such as structured generation, text rendering, and editing.

The second is an image-editing data engine. Rather than collecting generic source-target pairs only, the paper describes specialized pipelines for different edit types, mining realistic associations from naturally related images, and using expert-generated examples for sparse edit categories. A key interoperability choice is that editing instructions are aligned with T2I captions in vocabulary and descriptive structure. This allows editing supervision to reuse concepts already learned from T2I data instead of duplicating full concept coverage inside editing datasets.

The third is a knowledge-grounded data engine. It links visual patterns to named entities and structured knowledge through knowledge-graph-guided acquisition. This targets a common weakness in image generators: they may produce plausible imagery without reliably binding entities, attributes, and relations.

On the curriculum side, the paper proposes a five-stage schedule that follows the dependency order of capability acquisition. Training starts with large-scale T2I data to establish broad visual-semantic alignment and a basic generative prior. It then adds structurally complex, knowledge-grounded, and text-rich examples to develop composition, knowledge grounding, and text rendering. Once the T2I prior is stable, editing data is introduced to learn reference preservation and controlled transformation. Later continual training and supervised fine-tuning shift the mixture toward smaller, more balanced, and more refined subsets, while image resolution increases with content complexity. The schedule evolves jointly along four axes: task composition, visual concept distribution, data quality, and image resolution.

A notable systems idea is the active refinement loop. The schedule is not fixed once and for all. Capability-aware evaluation maps observed failures back to the relevant data engine, which then performs targeted retrieval, expert construction, or resampling. The refined data is fed back into later training mixtures. This makes the proposal a closed-loop data system rather than a static recipe.

The implementation uses the Haar wavelet throughout, mainly because it is orthogonal, invertible, simple, and effectively acts like a lossless 2×2 average-pooling-plus-residual decomposition. Unlike a learned latent space, this transform introduces no reconstruction loss and requires no extra encoder training.

The core change is not architectural. The model keeps the JiT pixel-space transformer and modifies the noise schedule. The LF band is trained over the full time interval t in [0,1], while the HF bands are only active over [t*,1]. During inference, the model first denoises the coarse band alone. At time t*, HF noise is injected, the bands are recombined through the inverse wavelet transform, and the model continues with joint refinement. This preserves cross-band interaction in the later stage, but avoids spending early trajectory steps on HF content that is effectively pure noise.

Conceptually, this is a frequency-aware flow-matching schedule. Compared with standard pixel-space FM, it explicitly respects the natural frequency hierarchy of images. Compared with latent diffusion, it obtains a coarse-to-fine decomposition without a lossy VAE bottleneck. Compared with progressive multi-scale architectures, it is much simpler and cheaper to integrate into an existing transformer pipeline.

Training is split into two stages.

Stage 1 is unified vision tokenizer training. The vision encoder from a pretrained VLM is frozen. On top of its continuous features, the authors train a quantizer to produce discrete visual tokens and an image decoder to reconstruct images. Because the encoder is frozen and already aligned with language through the original VLM, this stage can focus on image reconstruction rather than multimodal alignment. Stage 2 is unified multimodal training. Instead of initializing from a plain LLM, the model is initialized from a pretrained VLM so that the language model and multimodal interface inherit existing vision-language priors. Understanding tasks consume continuous visual tokens. Generation tasks consume discrete visual tokens produced by the learned quantizer, and the same autoregressive decoder models them jointly with text.

Relative to prior work, the distinctions are clear. Compared with models that use discrete tokens for both understanding and generation, Argus-Unified preserves continuous semantic features for understanding. Compared with dual-encoder designs such as Janus-style systems, it keeps a single encoder and prioritizes compactness. Compared with unified models trained from an LLM backbone and aligned from scratch, it starts from a pretrained VLM and shifts the burden from alignment learning to generation interface learning.
