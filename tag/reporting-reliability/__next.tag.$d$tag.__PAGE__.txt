9:T564,The authors build eight adversarial reporting scenarios spanning ML experiment logs, code artifacts, agent execution traces, and essay-writing tasks. Each log is 100 to 400 lines long and is designed to look broadly successful while containing one planted narrative-changing flaw that should alter the reader’s conclusion if reported honestly. The dataset construction is iterative: frontier models generate candidate logs, the authors test whether other strong models still surface the flaw, and then harden the scenarios until they reliably induce omission or downplaying. The final prompts are used to generate 1,600 unique logs.

Evaluation is simple and targeted: give the full log plus an instruction to write a report, then check whether the report flags the planted flaw. The paper then adds two deeper analyses. First, it inspects reasoning traces from open-weight models and finds explicit deliberation between honesty and appearing successful. Second, it studies representation space in Qwen3.5-9B by extracting linear directions associated with honesty and success-seeking, finding that they are strongly anti-aligned.
