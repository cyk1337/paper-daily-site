a:T78c,AnE consists of three tightly coupled stages.

First, Failing-Frontier Discovery. Instead of expanding data uniformly, the model is rolled out multiple times on a seed set to expose unstable or consistently failing regions. A teacher model then analyzes these failures and extracts failure-relevant keywords, hints, and diagnostic signals. This makes the next round of training target the model’s current capability boundary rather than a generic task distribution.

Second, Truth Anchor Expansion. This is the central idea. Rather than trusting synthetic QA pairs generated inside a self-evolution loop, the method retrieves verified samples from an external real-world database as truth anchors. These anchors are used as high-fidelity references for constructing new training examples, enriching missing knowledge, and correcting factual or logical nodes in reasoning chains. The key distinction from standard self-evolution is that data growth is constrained by externally grounded evidence, not only by teacher generations. That directly addresses cognitive drift and hallucinated trajectories.

Third, Scaffold-Stripping Mechanism. Teacher hints are first injected explicitly into training examples, producing scaffold-augmented supervision for SFT. This gives the student a guided path toward correct reasoning. RL is then used to remove the scaffolds, so the model learns to reproduce the behavior without depending on explicit templates or hints. The division of labor is deliberate: SFT introduces the reasoning pattern and missing knowledge; RL turns externally visible guidance into an internalized policy.

Conceptually, AnE is not just retrieval augmentation and not just distillation. It is an iterative reasoning-training loop where failure diagnosis decides what to learn, truth anchors decide what can be trusted, and scaffold stripping decides whether the learned behavior survives after the support is removed.b:T5fd,The experiments use Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the base model and evaluate on eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks. The headline result reported in the paper is an average improvement of 10.3% over the base model after multiple AnE iterations. The paper also highlights strong performance on difficult benchmarks such as MathVision and EMMA.

The more interesting result is the shape of improvement over iterations. Static-data post-training reaches a plateau relatively early, while AnE continues to improve across rounds, which supports the claim that failure-frontier targeting keeps exposing useful new supervision. Another important comparison is the role of truth anchors. Removing the anchor mechanism weakens gains and makes the evolution loop more prone to reasoning drift, suggesting that verified external grounding is not optional but central to stability.

The scaffold mechanism is also tested through ablations. Scaffolded SFT without the RL stripping stage tends to leave the model dependent on prompt-like support. RL without prior scaffold supervision struggles to learn stable reasoning from sparse answer rewards alone. The combination works best, matching the paper’s broader argument about the complementary roles of SFT and RL.
