18:T56b,The paper first characterizes the failure of OPRD-style methods, then proposes LastOPD. In the baseline recipe, the student rolls out its own responses, the teacher scores them, and latent supervision is added by pairing student and teacher layers at matched relative depth, with a projector bridging hidden-size differences. The authors argue that this setup has two structural problems. First, depth-matched layers need not play the same functional role. Their J-Lens analysis shows that the teacher’s final answer becomes readable mainly in the last two layers, whereas the student’s answer emerges more gradually across layers. Second, common alignment metrics can be dominated by a few large dimensions, so improving similarity scores may not reflect useful functional matching. LastOPD changes the recipe in three ways. It aligns only the final hidden states immediately before the LM heads, avoiding intermediate layer pairing altogether. It uses a small trainable MLP to bridge width differences at that final interface. And it treats latent supervision as a short-lived scaffold: over the first 10 steps, the latent loss is linearly decayed while a token-level reverse top-k loss is linearly increased, after which training continues with token-only OPD. The key idea is that latent supervision is useful as an early directional signal but harmful when kept active too long.19:T595,The main setup distills Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B teachers into Qwen3-1.7B-Base using DAPO-Math-17k, with MATH-500 as the main validation benchmark. The collapse is striking. In the 4B-to-1.7B setting, latent-only OPRD raises MATH-500 from 25.4 to 46.2 within 10 steps, outperforming vanilla OPD by 28 points at that stage. But continued optimization drives the score down to 11.3, below the student’s initial level, with no recovery. The 8B teacher shows the same pattern. At the same time, the optimized alignment metric keeps improving: projected cosine similarity rises from 0.97 to 0.98 with the 4B teacher and from 0.95 to 0.98 with the 8B teacher, even as behavior deteriorates by roughly 35 points. The paper also reports that the untrained student and the 4B teacher already have mid-layer self-CKA around 0.99 despite a large capability gap on MATH-500, which further weakens the case for taking high similarity at face value. Diagnostics show that the main representational changes are concentrated in later student layers, that teacher and student have different layerwise answer-readout patterns under J-Lens, and that neither masking dominant dimensions nor remapping layers by CKA prevents collapse.
