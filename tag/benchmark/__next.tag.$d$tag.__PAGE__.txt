First, the authors build PB-CSTE, a deterministic stateful tool-use environment designed for causal isolation. Tasks operate over typed entities through allowlisted create, update, delete, restore, and goal-revision actions. Every successful operation logs timestamps, actor class, state diffs, and snapshots. The environment intentionally excludes networking, concurrency, permissions, stochastic failures, and open-ended browsing. That simplification is deliberate: the goal is to isolate evaluator failure modes, not to mimic the full messiness of real-world agents.

Second, they define dual-vector certification. For each trusted terminal state, a private certifier constructs a state vector indicating which goal predicates hold at termination, and an attribution vector indicating which satisfied predicates receive standardized agent credit under an event-log rule. A pair is eligible only if the honest trajectory H and adversarial trajectory A match component-wise on both vectors. This controls both final retained progress and credited progress. The attribution rule is not presented as general causal attribution; it is a benchmark-internal standardization that makes the comparison executable and auditable.

Third, they introduce two stress protocols. Equal-progress pairs test invariance: if an evaluator gives A a higher score than H despite identical certified progress, the extra score must come from historical peaks, churn, recreated entities, or attribution ambiguity. Strict rollback tests sensitivity: starting from H, apply a legal inverse transition that lowers certified current-state progress while keeping the active goal fixed. If the evaluator fails to penalize rollback, it is not tracking actual progress loss.
