Key methodological components:
1) Four preference settings capturing different relationships between objectives: distributional conflict, multi-attribute interaction, strong safety signal, and compatible response-quality objectives, each tested under both objective orderings.
2) Metrics beyond aggregates: in addition to objective-level relative preference accuracy and DPO-style relative reward margins, they track length-normalized policy margins for individual chosen–rejected pairs across checkpoints to localize which pairs degrade or improve.
3) Mechanistic probe: they measure gradient cosine similarity during sequential training (and analyze LoRA adapter movement) to test whether observed preference changes are explained by direct gradient conflict. First, it spectrally orthogonalizes the momentum matrix: for matrix-valued parameters, it approximates the SVD of the momentum B_t and uses the polar factor U_t V_t^T as the update, flattening the singular spectrum so that a few dominant modes do not monopolize optimization. This inherits from Muon's matrix orthogonalization, but FOGO ties it to a unified forgetting interpretation. Second, it maintains a directional memory via random projection: high-dimensional gradients are projected with a Rademacher matrix into a low-dimensional space where pairwise distances are approximately preserved by the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma; representative past directions are stored as centroids in a compact codebook, and new updates are corrected by lightweight orthogonalization in projected space before a proximal step maps them back to parameter space. Compared with OGD-style methods that store past directions in parameter space, FOGO has much lower storage and per-step cost, making it scalable to large models.d:T5dc,The method is ATWU, Alternating Token-Weighted Unlearning. The paper first formalizes a notion of forget-specificity: a forget token is important to unlearn if applying the forget objective to that token is compatible with staying near retain optimality; if forgetting that token strongly conflicts with the retain objective, it is more likely to be structural rather than target-bearing. This turns token weights into latent variables in a joint optimization over model parameters and token importance. Under a stated separation condition, the authors argue that the objective can recover the oracle support of forget-specific tokens. In practice, ATWU uses a lightweight scorer to assign token weights from hidden states, parameterized as a linear direction in the LM representation space rather than a separate large auxiliary model. Training alternates between updating the scorer and updating the model. The intuition is that direct simultaneous optimization can be unstable because the model and the weights co-adapt too quickly; alternating updates make the procedure closer to an EM-like loop where token responsibility is estimated and then used to drive parameter updates. The method is compared against sample-level unlearning, probability-based token heuristics, and auxiliary-model-based weighting. 1) Empirical identification under matched SFT loss: the authors compare finetuning runs that reach the same (or comparable) SFT loss but use different learning rates. Despite similar SFT fit, OOD behavior differs substantially, implying LR changes the *type* of solution reached, not merely convergence quality.

2) Implicit regularization view: small-step (low-LR) SFT is biased toward staying near the pretrained initialization, preserving pretrained features/capabilities (stability). Larger steps more readily traverse high-curvature directions and/or jump basins, producing structural feature drift and forgetting (plasticity).

3) Sharpness/landscape mechanism: forgetting is linked to sharpness (top Hessian eigenvalue or related notions). The claim is that sharper pretrained checkpoints are more susceptible to being rewritten by SFT dynamics, especially under schedules that eventually take small steps in a sharp region. The authors ground this in the progressive sharpening literature: during training, sharpness increases until it approaches ~2/η (edge-of-stability threshold), so pretraining LR decay raises the attainable sharpness of later checkpoints.
