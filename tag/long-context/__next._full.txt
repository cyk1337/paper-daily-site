19:T9b2,方法分成理论拆解和算法设计两部分。

理论部分，作者先区分 mass coverage、output fidelity、task utility 三层目标。对 mass coverage，他们指出阈值计数 k(epsilon) 只是在问“有多少权重大于某阈值”，但压缩预算真正关心的是“要保留多少 token 才能覆盖 90% 之类的总概率质量”。决定后者的不是局部亮点，而是全局 score-gap profile，也就是排序后 logit 的衰减几何。论文给出一组 all-rank gap 条件：如果 gap envelope 在 log-rank 意义下斜率大于 1，tail 可求和，固定质量覆盖所需 budget 可以与长度无关；如果斜率低于 1，budget 会随长度增长。作者还用 Gaussian logits 说明一个反直觉点：即便看起来有“校准后的少量大权重”，要覆盖 90% mass 仍可能需要接近 0.61n 的 token，压缩空间并不大。

对 output fidelity，作者指出遗漏 mass 本身不是误差证书，关键在于被丢弃 value 的加权和是否能被恢复或近似。对一个固定 attention row，遗漏部分的 weighted sum 是精确的缺失统计量。进一步，如果希望一个线性 sketch 同时恢复完整输出和任意保留子预算下的输出，那么除了保留的真实 token 外，再加一个 tail aggregate 就达到匹配的维度下界。这给后面的算法设计提供了结构性依据：不是只保留 top-k token，而是要显式存一个 tail summary slot。

对 task utility，作者构造了 retrieval–aggregation 的 planted-token 模型，说明 exact attention 并不总是最适合任务目标。对 retrieval-like 任务，renormalized top-k 有时会因为压掉 diffuse noisy bulk 而优于 exact attention；但对 aggregation 任务，保留完整 softmax 更合理。也就是说，“更接近原 attention”不自动等于“更接近任务最优”。

基于这些分析，作者提出 CertKV。它是 training-free 的 KV compressor。设计上每个 head 预留一个 tail-summary slot，其余预算从 observation window 中选真实 token；分配策略不只看 attention score，还看 value dispersion，也就是尾部 value 是否容易被一个聚合向量概括。被省略的尾部用加权 key-value summary 存起来，且 synthetic slot 与真实 token 占用同样的物理预算。这个设计和很多只做 token selection 的方法不同，核心差异在于它承认“尾部不是纯噪声”，而是用一个显式统计量去补偿。1a:T995,The paper has two layers: a theoretical decomposition and a practical compressor.

On mass coverage, the authors argue that threshold counts are the wrong primitive. Counting weights above epsilon does not answer the cache-budget question, which is how many tokens are needed to retain, say, 90% of the softmax mass. That quantity is governed by the global score-gap profile after sorting logits, not by a few bright entries. They derive complementary all-rank gap conditions: if the log-rank gap envelope has slope above one, the tail is summable and a fixed-mass budget can remain length-independent; if the slope is below one, the required budget grows with sequence length. A Gaussian-logit example makes the point concrete: one can observe a seemingly sparse calibrated count while still needing roughly 61% of tokens to retain 90% of the mass.

On output fidelity, the paper shows that omitted mass alone is not an error certificate. What matters is the weighted sum of omitted values. For a fixed realized attention row, that omitted weighted value sum is the exact missing statistic. Under a stronger linear-sketch requirement—recovering both the full output and every retained sub-budget—the authors show that keeping the selected values plus one tail aggregate achieves the matching dimension bound. This is the structural motivation for the algorithmic design: a compressor should not only keep top tokens, but also maintain an explicit tail summary.

On task utility, the paper introduces a planted retrieval–aggregation model. It shows that exact attention is not always the right target. For retrieval-like tasks, renormalized top-k can outperform exact attention by suppressing diffuse noisy mass, while for aggregation tasks exact attention remains preferable. So preserving the original attention output is not automatically the same as preserving task performance.

These analyses motivate CertKV, a training-free KV compressor. CertKV reserves one tail-summary slot per head, selects real tokens from an observation window, and allocates more token budget to heads whose omitted values are harder to summarize, measured through value dispersion. The omitted tail is stored as a weighted key-value summary, and the synthetic slot counts against the same physical budget as real tokens. This differs from pure token-selection methods by explicitly modeling the tail as compressed state rather than treating it as disposable noise.1b:T4d5,The evaluation covers all 503 LongBench-v2 examples, three backbones, three compression ratios, multi-length RULER, one-factor studies, and a packed-cache prototype. The headline result is that under the pre-specified four-baseline comparison grid, CertKV is top-two in 7 of 9 LongBench-v2 settings. At 128K context on RULER it stays in the leading tier, and on Llama with 64K context and 10x compression it achieves the intended physical cache reduction.

The more useful part is how the experiments map onto the theory. One set of studies examines score-gap profiles versus mass coverage, showing that sparsity observed at shorter lengths does not reliably extrapolate to longer contexts. Another set tests the benefit of the tail summary for output fidelity, validating that the extra aggregate is not an ad hoc engineering trick but the exact statistic suggested by the analysis. A third controlled retrieval–aggregation setup recovers the predicted transition where renormalized top-k can beat exact attention for retrieval-like targets but not for aggregation. So the paper is not just reporting benchmark wins; it is checking whether the three separations—coverage, fidelity, and utility—actually explain empirical behavior.1c:T520,WnW has two coordinated components: offline head triage and online chunk recall. In the offline stage, each KV head is scored along two axes. Voice score measures how strongly the head is grounded in audio rather than other modalities or generic sink behavior. Head sensitivity estimates how much quality is lost when that head’s KV cache is compressed, using a gradient-based sensitivity signal. Based on these two quantities, heads are partitioned into three roles. Anchor heads keep the full audio KV cache on GPU and act as decode-time observers of audio importance. Tidal heads keep only part of their audio KV on GPU while storing the complement on CPU, making those positions recallable. Fixed heads use a static prefill-time subset with no recall path. During decoding, attention from anchor heads is aggregated into chunk-level importance scores over the audio timeline. The highest-scoring chunks are recalled from CPU into the tidal heads’ GPU cache, while chunks that lose relevance are swapped out. The on-GPU cache therefore waxes and wanes with the generation process instead of shrinking monotonically. A key distinction from many text-side methods is that recall is driven by a small set of heads that preserve full temporal access to the audio, rather than by generic similarity heuristics.1d:T421,The empirical story has two parts. First, the paper directly measures the mismatch between prefill and decode attention on LibriSpeech-Long with Voxtral. Prefill attention from the first generated token is concentrated near the start of the audio, whereas attention accumulated over the full decode is much more distributed. The top-K overlap between the two rankings is weak, which undermines the premise of prefill-only retention. Second, WnW delivers strong results under tight memory budgets. On two 3B backbones, Voxtral-mini-3b and Qwen2.5-Omni-3B, WnW stays within about 1.6 WER points of full-cache decoding even when only 20% of audio tokens remain on GPU.
