19:T853,The main idea is to introduce Binomial flows. The motivation is precise: Gaussian noise is special in continuous diffusion not merely because it is easy to sample, but because it yields Tweedie’s formula, which connects the denoiser to the score and thereby links training to sampling. The discrete setting lacks this bridge. For data living on a non-negative ordered space, the authors propose Binomial noising as the discrete analogue of Gaussian perturbation.

They first define a family of conditional distributions p_{t|T} that maps a clean sample X_T to an intermediate noisy sample X_t, with the usual three properties: easy perturbation, identity at t=T, and complete forgetting at t=0. On top of this flow they train a denoiser m(t,x)=E[X_T|X_t=x] using a time-weighted Bregman-divergence objective. This mirrors the standard denoiser-training view in continuous diffusion.

The technical contribution then comes in three linked steps:
1. under Binomial noising, the denoiser satisfies a discrete Tweedie formula, allowing recovery of a score-like object from the denoiser;
2. this relation leads to a sampling procedure via the Poisson-Föllmer process, which reaches the target distribution exactly in finite time rather than through a purely heuristic reverse chain;
3. the authors derive a likelihood identity involving the Binomial-flow denoiser, enabling exact likelihood estimation and corresponding training.

This differs from many discrete diffusion models that directly learn p(x_0|x_t), a discrete score, or a jump-rate matrix and then sample using approximate reverse dynamics. Here the claim is stronger: with the right noising family, the denoiser alone can support training, sampling, and likelihood in a unified recipe, much closer in spirit to continuous diffusion.

An important limitation is that the framework relies on non-negative ordinal structure. It does not directly apply to unordered categorical spaces, so there is still a gap to standard text tokens, which are nominal rather than ordinal. Extending the idea to language would require additional structure that this paper does not provide.1a:T4a7,The experiments appear to validate three points.
1. On synthetic data, the theoretical chain from denoiser to sampling and likelihood is numerically verified, showing that the proposed construction is internally consistent.
2. On real datasets, the method achieves competitive generative performance; the abstract explicitly mentions competitive FID on imaging datasets.
3. Unlike many discrete diffusion papers that only report sample quality, this one also demonstrates exact likelihood estimation.
