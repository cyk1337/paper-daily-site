19:T888,The core module is a causal resonant field mixer. Each head is parameterized as a damped resonator, with a kernel defined over lag t using exponential decay and oscillatory response. The main parameters are alpha for damping, omega for angular frequency, and phi for phase. In implementation, unconstrained parameters are mapped into valid ranges with softplus/sigmoid transforms, and heads are initialized with log-spaced half-lives and frequencies so different channels start with different timescales.

The input is projected through grouped linear layers into a drive tensor and a gate tensor. The drive is processed by the resonant mixer, while the gate is applied after mixing to provide content-dependent modulation without constructing query-key similarity matrices. During training and prefill, each head performs causal convolution with its resonant kernel, implemented with FFT for O(T log T) complexity. During autoregressive decoding, the model does not maintain a growing KV cache. Instead, each head keeps a fixed-size complex recurrent state, stored as paired real tensors for numerical stability. Each new token updates that state according to the same damped resonant dynamics, and the output is read from the updated state.

Two auxiliary components are important. A local lexical path preserves short-range token detail, which the resonant field alone may blur. An optional cross-head coupling matrix allows information exchange across resonant channels, reducing the expressivity loss from fully independent heads. The block is then combined with a standard feed-forward layer, so the overall architecture still resembles a modern LM block.

Relative to SSM-style models such as S4 or Mamba, the paper emphasizes explicit resonant kernels and frequency-selective dynamics rather than a more general state-space formulation. Relative to linear attention, it removes token-token similarity entirely and relies on learned causal kernels plus gating. That gives efficiency, but it also raises the usual concern: content-based addressing is weaker than explicit matching, so long-range retrieval may depend heavily on how much the gate and local branch can compensate.1a:T55e,The empirical message is modest but clear: at very small scale, ResonatorLM shows strong long-context decoding efficiency and slightly better language-modeling quality than a matched Transformer baseline. The main end-to-end result is on a 6M-parameter character-level WikiText-2 setup. At 32K tokens, the paper reports a 6.47x decoding speedup over the baseline Transformer, while also improving perplexity and accuracy. A separate kernel-reference benchmark isolates the mixer implementation rather than the full LM stack; there the reported speedups are 440.29x at 8K and 575.86x at 32K. Those numbers mainly validate the constant-state recurrent execution path.

From a pretraining perspective, the limitations are substantial. The model scale is only in the millions, not enough to establish training dynamics or scaling trends. The task is character-level WikiText-2, far from realistic modern pretraining. The paper also appears stronger on mechanism validation than on systematic ablation. The most useful missing analyses would be the contribution of the local lexical path, the effect of cross-head coupling, sensitivity to frequency/half-life initialization, and numerical consistency between FFT prefill and recurrent decode.
