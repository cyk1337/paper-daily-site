18:T788,The main method is VB-EGE, short for Vector-Borda Empirical-Gap Elimination. The central difficulty is that the target is defined on latent score vectors theta_i, while the learner only observes binary pairwise outcomes on one objective at a time. The paper resolves this by defining, for each objective, a Borda coordinate: the average win probability of a model against all opponents on that objective. Under the Bradley–Terry model, higher latent score implies higher pairwise win probability against every opponent, so the Borda transform preserves the per-objective ordering of latent scores. As a result, strict dominance and the strict Pareto frontier are unchanged under this mapping. The original problem is thus reduced to identifying the Pareto set of K vectors of d Bernoulli means.

Algorithmically, VB-EGE extends empirical-gap elimination from the scalar setting to the vector setting. It maintains an active set and keeps sampling only models whose Pareto status is still uncertain. Once there is enough statistical evidence that one model is strictly dominated by another across all objectives, the dominated model is eliminated. The sampling policy is adaptive: budget is concentrated on the hardest-to-certify dominance relations rather than spread uniformly across all pairs and objectives. A substantial part of the paper is devoted to confidence construction that is valid simultaneously over models, objectives, and rounds, and to translating those confidence intervals into safe elimination rules. The paper also proves delta-correctness and gives an instance-dependent sample complexity upper bound, together with lower-bound discussion. One design choice worth noting is the use of strict Pareto dominance rather than the usual weak Pareto notion. This is motivated both by decision semantics and by the technical need to avoid coordinate-wise ties that would break finite-sample stopping guarantees.19:T511,The experiments are designed to validate three points. First, on real arena data, the method can recover the frontier with fewer comparisons. Using Arena-Human-Preference-140K and treating code, math, and instruction-following as three objectives, the method identifies three models on the strict Pareto frontier. Second, compared with non-adaptive or simpler allocation strategies, VB-EGE reduces the number of required comparisons at the same confidence level. The savings come from quickly eliminating clearly dominated models and focusing effort on near-frontier cases. Third, synthetic experiments show the expected gap dependence: sample complexity rises when classification gaps shrink, especially for models that are close on some objectives and hard to certify as dominated or non-dominated.

The main value of the experimental section is not one headline number but the structure of the evidence. The paper includes both replay on real preference data and controlled simulations, and it evaluates both correctness and stopping time or comparison count.
