18:T49e,The framework is called Harness-Policy Optimization (HPO). A coding agent is modeled as a pair of policy P and harness H, with trajectories scored by an executable verifier. Each round alternates two updates. In the H-step, the current policy is frozen and used to analyze its own failed trajectories, propose harness revisions, and select the best candidate on a search set. The harness can modify prompts, context organization, tool usage flow, feedback format, and stopping rules. In the P-step, the selected harness is frozen and the policy is updated with reinforcement learning under that harness. The updated policy then becomes the proposer for the next H-step. Crucially, the harness search set is also updated using hard tasks identified during the preceding policy-learning phase, so harness search stays aligned with the policy’s current weaknesses. The paper instantiates two versions: Self-HPO, where the evolving policy proposes its own harness candidates, and an external-model variant using Opus 4.8 as a fixed proposer. The main design insight is not merely “optimize both,” but to couple proposer identity and search-set construction to the evolving policy.19:T410,The base policy is Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and the seed harness is mini-SWE-agent. Experiments are run on three coding benchmarks. The headline aggregate result is an average resolve-rate improvement from 57.8% to 64.8%. On SWE-Bench Verified, the base agent improves from 61.0% to 69.5% under Self-HPO, and to 71.6% when the same HPO loop uses Opus 4.8 as an external harness proposer. The gains transfer to out-of-distribution settings, including SWE-Bench Multilingual and SWE-Bench Pro. One of the most important ablations concerns the search set: dynamically updating it with hard tasks discovered during policy learning works better than reusing a fixed set. Another notable result is that Self-HPO improves both pass@1 and pass@k, which suggests it is not only making existing successful behaviors more reliable, but also expanding the set of successful trajectories the agent can reach. The provided text does not expose all RL details or per-round tables, but the paper states that average resolve rate improves at every optimization step.1a:T409,For a skim, start with the decomposition itself: policy defines the capability boundary, harness determines how that capability is elicited. The introduction and Figures 1–2 are enough to understand why harness-only and policy-only optimization are both incomplete. In the method section, focus on the interface between H-step and P-step: how failures are summarized into harness proposals, how candidate harnesses are selected, how RL is run under a fixed harness, and how the hard-task search set is refreshed. In experiments, prioritize three pieces: Self-HPO versus policy-only and harness-only baselines, cross-combinations of optimized policy and optimized harness, and the pass@k plus OOD results. Those are the sections that tell you whether the method is merely overfitting a scaffold or actually improving the agent loop. A quick read takes 20–30 minutes. A deeper read is only necessary if you want to port the idea beyond coding agents, in which case the definition of the harness search space becomes the key detail.1b:T99b,方法分两步看更清楚。HeirCommit 解决的是“如何决定是否继承这次更新”。在每一轮，系统从当前 root state St 出发，原生 host 会产生若干 native branches。传统做法通常直接从这些 branch endpoint 中选一个继承。HeirCommit 不这样做。它先冻结候选 successor，然后在与训练、筛选都严格分离的新题池上，把 successor 和当前 root 做对比；只有当 successor 在 fresh probes 上确实优于 root，才 commit，否则保留 root。这里的关键不是又加了一层验证，而是验证对象从 task 变成了 realized transition。作者还强调，若直接复用筛选证据去接受某个 branch，会为多分支搜索支付 union bound，容易把偶然高分当成真实改进；fresh confirmation 则把 proposal bias 和 acceptance evidence 分开。

EvoHeir 则进一步处理 whole-state acceptance 的耦合问题。传统继承把一个分支的 gains 和 losses 打包接受：如果这个分支学到了一些新能力，但也损伤了已有能力，你很难只拿前者不要后者。EvoHeir 把流程拆成 screening、recovery、confirmation 三段。screening 先在 branch endpoint 和当前 root 之间选 recipient，决定下一轮 learner 的参数底座来自谁；这个 recipient 可以是某个 branch，也可以仍是 root。然后做 Experience Recovery：用当前轮和历史 archive 中积累的 reference solutions 去修复 recipient 的失败，同时尽量保留其成功。这样，经验吸收和参数继承被解耦。最后再用 HeirCommit 风格的 fresh confirmation 决定是否接受 recovery 后的 proposal。一个很有意思的设计是：只要经验本身合格，就进入 archive，不受这轮参数是否被继承的影响。于是“参数继承停止”不等于“经验作废”。

Systems such as R-Zero, Agent0, and Skill Self-Play generate tasks, filter those that appear learnable, train on them, and then inherit the resulting model state into the next round. The authors show that this pipeline conflates pre-update task-side evidence with post-update utility. A task can look frontier-level or verifiable before training, yet the optimizer step induced by that task may fail to increase the likelihood of correct answers on fresh questions, or may even damage existing capabilities. They call this mismatch the selection-to-update gap. The audits make the point concrete rather than philosophical: 42–52% of native admissions in Agent0 and R-Zero leave the frontier under independent responses, and 60 out of 96 Skill-SP updates have non-positive correct-target NLL gain. It is a reframing of self-evolution as a state-selection problem. That matters for pretraining and self-training because once training is closed-loop, a bad inheritance decision contaminates both parameters and future curriculum. Errors compound over rounds. The paper contributes at three levels. First, it sharpens the problem formulation: task-side signals and update value are different objects, and pre-update behavior alone cannot identify whether an optimizer step is beneficial. Second, it proposes a practical correction: HeirCommit accepts an update only after comparing the frozen successor against the current root on fresh probes, while EvoHeir further decouples parameter inheritance from experience inheritance. Third, it provides an empirical lesson with broader relevance: useful experience can outlive the model state that produced it. Even references from rejected rounds can help later learners. That is directly relevant to continual pretraining, self-training, RL-generated data reuse, and any setting where one currently binds data value and parameter value to the same acceptance decision.20:T96a,HeirCommit addresses the acceptance rule. In each round, the current root state St produces native branches through the host self-evolution loop. Standard systems typically inherit one branch endpoint. HeirCommit instead freezes a candidate successor and evaluates it against the current root on a fresh probe set that is disjoint from training and screening. The system commits the successor only if it beats the root on this new evidence; otherwise it retains the root. The key idea is that acceptance should be based on the realized transition, not on the task that motivated the update. The paper also gives a statistical argument: if one reuses screening evidence to accept among multiple branches, one pays a union-bound penalty over branches and can easily mistake selection noise for genuine improvement. Fresh confirmation removes that coupling.

EvoHeir tackles a deeper issue: whole-state acceptance bundles gains and losses together. A branch may acquire new skills while also degrading old ones, but standard inheritance forces an all-or-nothing choice. EvoHeir splits the round into screening, recovery, and confirmation. Screening chooses the recipient that will supply the next learner’s parameters, from either a branch endpoint or the current root. Recovery then repairs that recipient’s failures using reference solutions drawn from both the current round and an archive of past qualified experience, while trying to preserve existing successes. Confirmation finally applies a HeirCommit-style fresh comparison between the post-recovery proposal and the root. A notable design choice is that qualified experience enters the archive regardless of whether the donor model is inherited. Parameter inheritance can stop without discarding potentially useful experience.

The measurement choices are also important. The paper does not rely only on accuracy. Update audits use correct-target negative log-likelihood gain, which can detect whether the model actually shifts probability mass toward the correct answer even when accuracy stays unchanged. Value is further decomposed into acquired successes and lost successes, making harmful updates observable as specific trade-offs rather than only as end-to-end score drops. This gives the paper a stronger handle on why native self-evolution loops can look productive at the task level while still producing weak or negative state transitions.21:T86b,There are two main experimental blocks. The first is an audit of native updates from existing self-evolution hosts. The authors analyze R-Zero, Agent0, and Skill-SP, first establishing the selection-to-update gap and then comparing inheritance rules. At the same acceptance rate, HeirCommit reduces the harmful share of accepted updates by 68–83% relative to random inheritance, and this holds on every host. More importantly, the method is not merely becoming conservative by accepting fewer updates. On two of the three hosts, it turns negative pool gain into positive gain, indicating that the issue is indeed poor acceptance of realized updates rather than a lack of learnable material.

The second block is a full ten-round self-evolution study. The paper runs three training seeds per method on four backbones: Qwen3-4B-Base, Qwen3-8B-Base, OLMo-3-7B, and the instruction-tuned Gemma-4-E4B-it. Agent i faces its own task distribution D_i and is rewarded only on its own tasks. Each round has two decisions. First, the agent chooses a learning action: search privately, observe a peer, or abstain. Second, it chooses a task action by executing a skill from its current library. A skill can be a simple option in a controlled environment or a full skill file in the LLM setting.

A central design choice is token accounting. Search, observation, and acting all draw from the same budget, and the cost of discovering a copied skill is counted as part of the total social-learning cost. This avoids the common mistake of treating copied knowledge as free.

The experiments proceed in two stages. In the controlled stage, options have known value structure, allowing the authors to test whether established social-learning algorithms can in principle yield an advantage. In the LLM stage, agents write, revise, and exchange skill files for instruction-following tasks. These skill files are inference-time textual procedures rather than weight updates. Policies are implemented by fixed-weight LLMs learning in context: the model decides whether to revise its own skill, copy a peer’s skill, or act.

The paper also makes an important distinction between two notions of success. Efficient social learning means better performance per token. Effective social learning means higher final performance than independent learners at the same total cost. This distinction is one of the strongest parts of the paper because many multi-agent results show speed or cost gains without showing superiority under matched compute.

Across tested LLMs, populations with peer observation are 30% to 52% less efficient than solo populations of the same model. In other words, useful social information exists, but the models do not manage it well enough; they reduce exploration too aggressively.

The first changes rollout collection. Instead of K independent parallel attempts, the agent solves the same task sequentially. The next attempt is conditioned on the original task plus the accumulated insights. This converts some tasks from a zero-success sampling regime into a regime where at least one successful trajectory can be found.

The second component changes optimization. Sequential exploration alone does not solve test-time performance, because evaluation requires a first-attempt solution without any accumulated insights in context.
