EvoHeir 则进一步处理 whole-state acceptance 的耦合问题。传统继承把一个分支的 gains 和 losses 打包接受：如果这个分支学到了一些新能力，但也损伤了已有能力，你很难只拿前者不要后者。EvoHeir 把流程拆成 screening、recovery、confirmation 三段。screening 先在 branch endpoint 和当前 root 之间选 recipient，决定下一轮 learner 的参数底座来自谁；这个 recipient 可以是某个 branch，也可以仍是 root。然后做 Experience Recovery：用当前轮和历史 archive 中积累的 reference solutions 去修复 recipient 的失败，同时尽量保留其成功。这样，经验吸收和参数继承被解耦。最后再用 HeirCommit 风格的 fresh confirmation 决定是否接受 recovery 后的 proposal。一个很有意思的设计是：只要经验本身合格，就进入 archive，不受这轮参数是否被继承的影响。于是“参数继承停止”不等于“经验作废”。

从度量上，作者没有只看 accuracy，而是引入更细的状态价值定义。更新审计主要看 correct-target NLL 的变化，因为 accuracy 只能看到答对/答错翻转，看不到模型对正确目标概率质量的真实变化。文中还把 value 分解为 acquired successes 和 lost successes，用来区分“学到了什么”和“丢了什么”。这使得 harmful update 不再只是最终分数下降，而是可以被更细地定位为：某次更新虽然在 admission task 上看似成功，但对独立问题集的正确目标概率没有提升，甚至伤害了已有正确行为。13:T7c8,实验分成两块。第一块是对现有自演化 host 的原生更新做审计和继承规则评估。作者在 R-Zero、Agent0、Skill-SP 三个 host 上检查 native updates，先证明 selection-to-update gap 的存在，再比较不同继承策略。结果是，HeirCommit 在相同 acceptance rate 下，相比随机继承，把被接受更新中的 harmful share 降低了 68–83%，而且在三个 host 上都成立。更重要的是，它不是只减少接受次数来换稳定性；在两类 host 上，原本 native update pool 的负增益被翻成了正增益。这说明问题确实出在“接受了错误的更新”，而不是“根本没有可学内容”。

第二块是完整的十轮 self-evolution。作者在四个 backbone 上跑了十轮、每个方法三种训练 seed：Qwen3-4B-Base、Qwen3-8B-Base、OLMo-3-7B，以及 instruction-tuned 的 Gemma-4-E4B-it。评测覆盖 7 个数学 benchmark 和 5 个通用推理 benchmark。EvoHeir 在四个 backbone 上都拿到最高的数学与通用推理平均分，并且在 48 个 benchmark-backbone 组合里，45 个优于 Agent0。按文中汇总，EvoHeir 让 base model 的数学平均分提升 7.9% 到 30.1%，是对比方法里提升幅度最大的。

几个消融和分析值得注意。其一，Recovery 本身不是简单再微调。用同样 64 条 references，Recovery 比 direct fine-tuning 平均高 1.93 个百分点，说明“修复失败且保留成功”的吸收方式比把参考解直接当监督样本更有效。其二，来自被拒绝轮次模型的经验仍然有用：把这些 rejected-round history 加进来，平均还能再带来 0.66 个百分点。这直接支持了“experience outlives the model that produced it”。其三，作者还指出十个 donor 里有八个 donor 本身弱于当前模型，但它们提供的 references 依然能提升 recipient，说明经验供体不必是更强模型；关键是它在某些局部问题上提供了可吸收的正确信号。14:T784,建议先看 Introduction 和 Figure 1，把 selection-to-update gap、whole-state acceptance、parameter inheritance vs experience inheritance 这三个概念先立住。没有这一步，后面的设计会看成普通的 update gating。

然后重点看 Self-Evolution Environment 那一节和 Figure 2。这里定义了 root state、native branches、archive、screening、recovery、confirmation 这些对象，也说明了训练池、筛选池、确认池、最终评测池是如何严格分离的。这个实验设计很关键，因为这篇工作的说服力很大程度来自“确认证据是 fresh 的”。

方法部分里，优先看 HeirCommit 的 acceptance logic，再看 EvoHeir 的 Recovery。前者回答“为什么必须用 realized transition 做判断”；后者回答“为什么参数和经验要拆开”。如果时间有限，至少把 transition regret、absorption gain、correct-target NLL gain 这几个指标定义读清楚，它们决定了作者如何把 harmful update 从概念变成可审计对象。

实验部分建议先看三类 host 的 update audit 图表，再看四个 backbone 十轮演化的主结果表。审计结果是整篇文章的地基；如果不先确认 native admission 确实经常接纳无益更新，就很难判断后面方法收益来自哪里。最后看 Recovery 和 archive 的消融，尤其是 rejected rounds 经验仍有效的分析，这部分对做 self-training、continual pretrain、RL data replay 的人最有延展性。

如果要深挖，有两个点值得继续想。第一，fresh confirmation 的样本效率和统计功效如何随 probe 数量、分支数、任务噪声变化，这关系到它能否扩展到更大规模在线训练。第二，Experience Recovery 目前依赖 reference solutions 和 archive 质量，未来可以和 verifier、process reward、uncertainty estimation 结合，形成更细粒度的经验吸收机制。15:T42b,The paper isolates a specific failure mode in self-evolving language models: task admission is not the same as learning. Systems such as R-Zero, Agent0, and Skill Self-Play generate tasks, filter those that appear learnable, train on them, and then inherit the resulting model state into the next round. The authors show that this pipeline conflates pre-update task-side evidence with post-update utility. A task can look frontier-level or verifiable before training, yet the optimizer step induced by that task may fail to increase the likelihood of correct answers on fresh questions, or may even damage existing capabilities. They call this mismatch the selection-to-update gap. The audits make the point concrete rather than philosophical: 42–52% of native admissions in Agent0 and R-Zero leave the frontier under independent responses, and 60 out of 96 Skill-SP updates have non-positive correct-target NLL gain. The real decision variable in self-evolution is therefore not which tasks to admit, but which realized successor states are worth carrying forward.16:T49e,The main contribution is not another self-play curriculum mechanism. It is a reframing of self-evolution as a state-selection problem. That matters for pretraining and self-training because once training is closed-loop, a bad inheritance decision contaminates both parameters and future curriculum. Errors compound over rounds. The paper contributes at three levels. First, it sharpens the problem formulation: task-side signals and update value are different objects, and pre-update behavior alone cannot identify whether an optimizer step is beneficial. Second, it proposes a practical correction: HeirCommit accepts an update only after comparing the frozen successor against the current root on fresh probes, while EvoHeir further decouples parameter inheritance from experience inheritance. Third, it provides an empirical lesson with broader relevance: useful experience can outlive the model state that produced it. Even references from rejected rounds can help later learners. That is directly relevant to continual pretraining, self-training, RL-generated data reuse, and any setting where one currently binds data value and parameter value to the same acceptance decision.17:T96a,HeirCommit addresses the acceptance rule. In each round, the current root state St produces native branches through the host self-evolution loop. Standard systems typically inherit one branch endpoint. HeirCommit instead freezes a candidate successor and evaluates it against the current root on a fresh probe set that is disjoint from training and screening. The system commits the successor only if it beats the root on this new evidence; otherwise it retains the root. The key idea is that acceptance should be based on the realized transition, not on the task that motivated the update. The paper also gives a statistical argument: if one reuses screening evidence to accept among multiple branches, one pays a union-bound penalty over branches and can easily mistake selection noise for genuine improvement. Fresh confirmation removes that coupling.

EvoHeir tackles a deeper issue: whole-state acceptance bundles gains and losses together. A branch may acquire new skills while also degrading old ones, but standard inheritance forces an all-or-nothing choice. EvoHeir splits the round into screening, recovery, and confirmation. Screening chooses the recipient that will supply the next learner’s parameters, from either a branch endpoint or the current root. Recovery then repairs that recipient’s failures using reference solutions drawn from both the current round and an archive of past qualified experience, while trying to preserve existing successes. Confirmation finally applies a HeirCommit-style fresh comparison between the post-recovery proposal and the root. A notable design choice is that qualified experience enters the archive regardless of whether the donor model is inherited. Parameter inheritance can stop without discarding potentially useful experience.

The measurement choices are also important. The paper does not rely only on accuracy. Update audits use correct-target negative log-likelihood gain, which can detect whether the model actually shifts probability mass toward the correct answer even when accuracy stays unchanged. Value is further decomposed into acquired successes and lost successes, making harmful updates observable as specific trade-offs rather than only as end-to-end score drops. This gives the paper a stronger handle on why native self-evolution loops can look productive at the task level while still producing weak or negative state transitions.18:T86b,There are two main experimental blocks. The first is an audit of native updates from existing self-evolution hosts. The authors analyze R-Zero, Agent0, and Skill-SP, first establishing the selection-to-update gap and then comparing inheritance rules. At the same acceptance rate, HeirCommit reduces the harmful share of accepted updates by 68–83% relative to random inheritance, and this holds on every host. More importantly, the method is not merely becoming conservative by accepting fewer updates. On two of the three hosts, it turns negative pool gain into positive gain, indicating that the issue is indeed poor acceptance of realized updates rather than a lack of learnable material.

The second block is a full ten-round self-evolution study. The paper runs three training seeds per method on four backbones: Qwen3-4B-Base, Qwen3-8B-Base, OLMo-3-7B, and the instruction-tuned Gemma-4-E4B-it. Evaluation covers seven mathematics benchmarks and five general-reasoning benchmarks. EvoHeir achieves the best math and general-reasoning averages on all four backbones, and beats Agent0 on 45 of 48 benchmark–backbone pairs. The reported gain on the base model’s math average ranges from 7.9% to 30.1%, the largest among compared methods.

Several analyses are especially useful. Recovery is not equivalent to simply fine-tuning on the same references: with the same 64 reference solutions, Recovery outperforms direct fine-tuning by 1.93 percentage points on average. That suggests the repair-style absorption procedure is doing something more structured than ordinary supervised replay. The archive analysis is also strong: adding experience from rounds whose models were rejected still yields an additional 0.66 percentage point gain beyond committed-round history. This directly supports the claim that experience can remain valuable even when the producing model state should not be inherited. Finally, eight of ten donors that are weaker than the current model still improve the recipient through their references, showing that an experience donor does not need to be globally stronger; it only needs to contribute locally useful supervision.19:T823,Start with the introduction and Figure 1. The three ideas to lock in early are the selection-to-update gap, the limitations of whole-state acceptance, and the distinction between parameter inheritance and experience inheritance. Without that framing, the method can look like a generic update-gating trick.

Then read the self-evolution environment section together with Figure 2. This is where the paper defines root state, native branches, archive, screening, recovery, and confirmation, and where it explains the strict separation among training pools, screening pools, confirmation pools, and final evaluation pools. That separation is central to the paper’s credibility because the whole argument depends on fresh evidence.

In the method section, prioritize the HeirCommit acceptance logic first, then EvoHeir’s recovery mechanism. The former explains why realized transitions must be judged directly; the latter explains why parameter choice and experience absorption should be decoupled. If time is limited, at least read the definitions of transition regret, absorption gain, and correct-target NLL gain carefully. Those metrics are what turn “harmful update” from an intuition into an auditable object.

For experiments, read the update-audit figures for the three hosts before the ten-round benchmark tables. The audits are the foundation of the paper. If native admission were already well aligned with positive updates, the rest of the method would matter much less. After that, read the ablations on recovery and archive usage, especially the analysis showing that rejected-round experience still helps. That part has the most direct implications for self-training, continual pretraining, and RL data replay.

Two deeper questions are worth carrying forward. One is how the sample efficiency and statistical power of fresh confirmation scale with probe budget, branch count, and task noise.
