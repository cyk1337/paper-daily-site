18:T4d4,The authors first introduce Layer-wise Compositional Divergence (LCD), a metric that measures per-layer representation separation between negated and affirmative captions. Combining LCD measurements with per-layer visual alignment scores, they identify a "compositional peak layer" where syntactic information is maximally preserved, and a stable "anchor layer" located before the sharp rise in visual alignment, confirming the representational collapse in final layers. Based on this observation, they propose PeakPatch, a lightweight correction system that keeps CLIP fully frozen and adds two small modules: an Embedding Correction Network (ECN) that uses cross-attention to extract negation signals from the compositional peak layer, anchors these signals to stable representations from the anchor layer, and predicts a deviation vector to inject lost syntax into final-layer embeddings; a Score Correction Network (SCN) that compares peak-layer representations, corrected embeddings, and image embeddings to output bounded scalar corrections for cosine similarity scores. The two modules are trained end-to-end, adding 5.2M parameters (3.5% of the CLIP backbone) while preserving the standard cosine similarity inference interface.19:T53f,The method is a training-free loop: stochastic concept mining → attribution-based ranking → circuit intervention.
1) Stochastic concept mining: under the linear representation hypothesis, hidden states are treated as linear mixtures of concept directions. Instead of learning a dictionary/SAE, the authors sample pseudo-concept vectors. A key design choice is to sample/search in the per-head low-dimensional subspaces induced by MHSA projections (d_head), which empirically increases “concept hit” probability and reduces entanglement.
2) Vulnerability explanation via attribution: they propose a gradient-based attribution score that combines (a) whether an attention head focuses on text patches (focus) and (b) whether the sampled concept direction aligns with lexical semantics (direction). This yields a normalized score to identify TA-vulnerable concepts and the hosting heads/modules.
3) Mechanistic intervention: once harmful circuits are identified, they apply attention reweighting or ablation of selected heads/paths at test time to suppress lexical-dominated routes and recover visual-semantic behavior.
Compared to residual-stream-only analyses or SDL/SAE-based disentanglement, the emphasis here is head-subspace concept sampling plus an attribution metric that directly maps to actionable attention-level interventions.1a:T441,AdvFLYP (Adversarially Finetune Like You Pretrain) proposes to adversarially finetune CLIP by following CLIP’s pretraining recipe in both data and objective:
1) Data: use web-scale image–text pairs rather than proxy classification datasets with class labels.
2) Objective: use CLIP’s contrastive loss (image–text matching within a batch) instead of cross-entropy classification.
3) Adversarial training loop: alternately generate adversarial images and update the model to align adversarial images with their paired texts under the contrastive objective.
4) Regularization:
- Logit-level regularization guided by the original CLIP to improve robustness/generalization.
- Feature-level regularization (a key addition): penalize deviation between adversarial and clean image features in embedding space to prevent representation drift caused by noisy web pairs, helping preserve the vision encoder and zero-shot performance.
