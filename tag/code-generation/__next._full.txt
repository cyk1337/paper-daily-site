18:T415,The method extends ELF. A frozen Qwen3-0.6B-Base encoder provides clean text states. Response states are corrupted in continuous space, and a bidirectional Transformer denoiser predicts clean states through velocity regression. The model also learns a token decoder, so the final response tokens are emitted in parallel. ELF-REG adds REPA+REG. REPA aligns intermediate denoiser states with hidden states from a frozen Qwen3-1.7B-Base AR teacher, using a learned projector and cosine loss at matching token positions. REG adds a global teacher-derived target: the last content-token state from the teacher is standardized, corrupted, and denoised as an extra token. This token exchanges information with text through self-attention and receives velocity-regression and REPA supervision. At inference time, the teacher and projector are removed; text states and the REG token start from noise, and only text is decoded. The paper also studies early stopping by decoding intermediate clean predictions before the full denoising trajectory finishes.19:T83a,The framework is organized around five roles. The God of Concepts provides high-level intent in text or images. The Creator, implemented with an LLM, recursively expands that intent into a world blueprint with themes, shared rules, hidden states, and system constraints. Executors are coding agents that translate the blueprint into executable entities, behaviors, and subsystems. Artists are video generation models that render observations from visual conditions derived from the executable world. Travelers act inside the world and receive observations from their own viewpoints.

Two design ideas carry most of the technical weight. The first is Logical Spaces. A world is decomposed into heterogeneous subsystems such as transportation, construction, resources, and social relations. Each subsystem has local mechanisms, but all must obey shared global constraints. Logical Spaces provides a modular structure for collaborative world construction, so different agents can implement different subsystems while remaining consistent through shared interfaces and rules. This is more controllable than asking one model to generate an entire world monolithically.

The second is Observation as World Registration. Generated observations are not treated as disposable outputs. They are registered back into a persistent world representation, so information obtained from one viewpoint or one interaction can be retained and reused later. This directly targets a weakness of standard video world models, where past frames are consumed as context but are not written back into a structured memory.

The division of labor is deliberate. CoDeR does not ask the diffusion video model to infer rules, track hidden variables, or maintain long-term state, because those capabilities are hard to learn reliably from pixel supervision alone. Instead, the LLM-plus-code layer handles explicit world logic, and the video model focuses on conditional rendering. Compared with end-to-end visual world models, this sacrifices some architectural purity but gains interpretability, editability, and long-horizon consistency.1a:T5ad,The experimental story is organized around four capabilities: persistent memory, open-ended interaction, autonomous world evolution, and multi-agent collaboration. The paper claims clear gains over prior video-based world models on these dimensions, especially for long-term consistency, off-screen state continuation, cross-view information reuse, and sustained interaction among multiple entities. From the visible content, the evaluation appears more system- and capability-oriented than a standard pretraining paper with large-scale scaling curves or tightly standardized benchmarks.

The most informative comparisons are likely to be: direct text-to-video or video continuation baselines, variants without explicit world registration, variants without Logical Spaces, and long-horizon multi-agent scenarios where hidden state matters. For this kind of system, the key question is not a single visual quality metric. It is whether explicit hidden state, modular executable structure, and observation write-back actually change the capability boundary.

For a pretraining-oriented reader, the most useful evidence would be ablations showing separate contributions from explicit hidden variables, modular logical decomposition, and observation registration.
