17:T516,The paper formalizes a harness h as a persistent collection of tools, prompts, and skills. Given a task t, solve(h, t) produces a trajectory τ containing observations, reasoning traces, tool calls, and the final answer. The true utility U(t, τ) is latent in deployment because there is no representative future validation set. RHO replaces this unavailable utility with a self-preference estimator: the agent compares multiple trajectories for the same task, produces a ranking, and explains the preference. The pipeline has three stages. First, coreset selection chooses diverse and challenging tasks from past trajectories, so the optimization budget is spent on informative cases rather than easy or redundant ones. Second, group rollout re-solves each selected task multiple times in parallel, producing alternative trajectories under the current harness. Third, best-of-N harness proposal uses diagnostic signals to generate candidate harness updates and selects among them with pairwise self-preference. The two key diagnostics are self-validation within a trajectory and self-consistency across parallel trajectories. The method differs from simple experience accumulation: it does not merely append past traces to context, but turns them into targeted changes to skills, tools, and workflows.18:T402,The evaluation covers three agent domains: software engineering, technical work, and knowledge work. Diagnostic analysis shows that RHO tends to design skills and tools aimed at typical failure modes found in past trajectories. These updates change the agent’s action patterns and help maintain higher accuracy in long-horizon sessions.
