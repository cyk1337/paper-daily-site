First, the authors reproduce a GPT-2-class collapse in a PyTorch implementation of FlashAttention-2's tiled online-softmax recurrence. This implementation exposes the running output O and the softmax statistics carry, which allows controlled precision edits. The decisive intervention is fp32 accumulation in the streaming accumulator: it prevents the reproduced collapse while preserving the tiled computation. This rules out several simpler explanations, including bf16 operands, tiling itself, the FlashAttention family as a whole, or a single-scale artifact.

Second, the authors introduce an identification assay based on controlled fault-source moves and always-on module-routed gradient correction. The source mechanism remains enabled, while clean fp32 gradients are routed through selected modules. This tests channel blocking rather than source repair. The result is the central source-channel dissociation: faults placed inside attention and faults placed outside attention both drive the same dominant QK channel; QK-route correction stabilizes training, while MLP-route correction does not.

Third, the paper explains how errors enter the QK channel. The distinguishing property is temporal sign-coherence: the error sign for a head persists across optimizer steps. This is separated from per-tile scalar mean and total error magnitude. The authors also test causality with equal-energy off-target controls and by removing each run's own dominant QK weight directions, showing that the QK channel drives the early runaway rather than merely tracking it.

Finally, the paper proposes QK-Guard, a dormant containment controller. It monitors attention-logit saturation and, once a runaway is developing, applies parameter-free per-head QK normalization at the QK locus. It does not repair the underlying fault or rewind the run. At the same trigger point, a softmax-local intervention and a learning-rate cut fail, indicating that the rescue depends on where the action is applied.1b:T5ef,The empirical base is a bf16 GPT-2 small training setup on OpenWebText. The baseline collapses in every seed within the observed window. In contrast, fp32-accumulation variants do not collapse in the reported runs: two seeds for the accumulator-to-fp32 arm, two seeds for the fp32/fp32 carry condition, and one long run that was manually stopped while still healthy at 103400 logged steps. The paper treats these as sufficiency counterexamples, not as collapse-rate estimates.

The accumulator analysis separates the backward paths of the streaming carries. The running output O re-enters backward through the D-term, perturbing the score gradient and therefore dQ and dK, but not dV. The statistics carry affects recomputed probabilities through the saved log normalizer and can affect dQ, dK, and dV. This explains why a carry-precision fault can show up as a QK-driven instability rather than as uniform attention-parameter noise.

The channel-identification experiments move the fault outside attention and still observe the same QK spectral runaway. With module-routed clean gradients, QK correction blocks the collapse while MLP correction does not. The mechanism experiments manipulate error-sign sequences and show that temporal sign-coherence, rather than scalar mean or aggregate energy, predicts entry into the QK channel. For a format with m mantissa bits, the spacing at coordinate wi is ULP_m(wi)=2^{floor(log2|wi|)-m}. Under round-to-nearest, a gradient step leaves wi unchanged whenever |Δwi| < 1/2 ULP_m(wi). This gives a per-coordinate freeze condition and leads to a measurable frozen fraction f, defined as the proportion of coordinates that remain bitwise unchanged after a step. The freeze time τ* is the first step where f reaches 1/2.

The key theoretical move is to rewrite the condition in dimensionless form as ρ_i < 2^{-φ_i}, where φ_i is the fractional part of log2|wi| and indicates where the weight lies within its binade. If φ_i is approximately equidistributed, the half-ULP boundary occupies a one-bit-wide band, whose median yields a parameter-free threshold ρ*=1/sqrt(2). This explains why the onset is an O(1) crossover rather than a sharp size-dependent phase transition.

The predictor is deliberately simple. Run one fp64 trajectory {w_t, g_t}. For any target mantissa length m, compute the predicted frozen fraction at each step as \hat f_t(m)=<1[η|g_{t,i}| < 1/2 ULP_m(w_{t,i})]>. The predicted τ* is where \hat f_t crosses 1/2. Since the predictor uses no low-precision data, successful prediction would imply that the phenomenon is governed primarily by arithmetic discretization rather than by altered low-precision dynamics.

The experimental design separates arithmetic effects cleanly. Real bf16 hardware establishes that the freeze is physically present. A mantissa-truncation emulator performs all arithmetic in fp64 but rounds weights to an m-bit grid after each step, allowing controlled sweeps over mantissa precision without confounding exponent range or denormals. The paper also emphasizes an important practical nuance: in multilayer networks, a single pooled L2-based ρ can become misleading because layers live at different scales and tiny bias vectors may never freeze.
