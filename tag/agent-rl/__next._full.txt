18:T4d1,Terminal-RSI alternates actor-only Recursive Self-Rewrite with critic-inheriting Recursive Policy-Refine. The stages are Base, RSR-1, RPR-1, RSR-2, and RPR-2. RSR updates only the actor: successful trajectories are converted into runbooks, filtered through leakage and compatibility checks, and replayed in a fresh public execution environment before being used for supervised actor training. RPR then continues RL from the rewritten actor while inheriting the critic. Each refinement phase performs local value calibration so that the inherited critic is adapted to the new actor distribution, reward definition, and execution horizon. The paper introduces Turn-Boundary Skip-Observation GAE, which applies discounting and trace propagation across decision turns rather than repeatedly through long observation token spans, while keeping token-level baselines for language-model optimization. On the reward side, Family Dense Reward calibrates progress within task families, and Success-Prioritized Reward separates strict success from bounded partial credit. The second cycle reduces the actor learning rate by five times and uses critic warm-up and phase-local calibration to reduce instability from recursive distribution shifts.19:T430,SLCA-GRPO introduces Segment-Locked Credit Assignment. The rollout is first segmented with masks: environment tokens are excluded from policy gradients, and learnable policy tokens are split into a tool segment and a summary segment. The final contiguous learnable block is treated as the summary segment; all earlier learnable blocks, including reasoning traces and structured tool calls, form the tool segment. The method then computes separate group-relative advantages for tool-side and summary-side rewards. During optimization, tool advantages are routed only to tool tokens, and summary advantages only to summary tokens. This blocks cross-segment advantage contamination before backpropagation, without extra rollouts and without separating the agent into planner and summarizer models. Hierarchical Rewards provide segment-level feedback, and a Schema-Guided LLM Simulator supplies scalable tool-interaction rollouts. Unlike VinePPO, SPO, or GiGPO, which mainly operate along the temporal axis, SLCA operates along a structural execution-versus-articulation axis.1a:T4bf,ArenaFlow starts with tournament-based relative ranking. For each query, the policy samples a group of trajectories. A seeded single-elimination tournament compares candidates, with a greedy decoding trajectory used as an anchor for the initial ranking prior. The judge does more than choose the better trajectory. It also marks pivotal success steps, extracts strategy-like skills, and attributes whether retrieved historical skills contributed to decisive reasoning. The framework then propagates credit at two levels. For step-level optimization, tournament survival depth is used as a confidence signal: pivotal steps that support wins in later rounds receive higher weight because they have survived comparison against stronger candidates. Depth-aware weighting and intra-trajectory normalization convert trajectory-level advantages into more targeted step-level training signals. For skill-level propagation, a global memory stores candidate skills. Their utility is estimated from group-level usage attribution; skills repeatedly used in pivotal success steps are reinforced, while stale or ineffective ones are pruned. During later rollouts, high-utility skills are retrieved as priors to guide exploration.1b:T782,方法由两部分组成。第一部分是 dynamic per-trajectory rubric。不是为整个任务分布预先写一套固定 rubric，而是先根据任务指令生成初始 criteria，再结合每个 rollout 暴露出来的子目标、失败模式和执行路径，对 rubric 做扩展，最后把同一 group 内多个 rollout 提出的 criteria 合并成共享 rubric 集合。这样做的原因很直接：固定 rubric 往往覆盖不到长轨迹里真实发生的分叉和失败细节，而按轨迹动态生成可以让 judge 评价的是“这条轨迹实际试图完成了什么、卡在了哪里”，不是抽象模板。

第二部分是 distribution of rubric-conditioned advantage。标准 GRPO 对同一 trajectory 的所有 token 使用同一个 advantage；DRACO 改成先让 judge 对 rubric 中每个 criterion 给 verdict，再把 criterion 与轨迹中负责该 criterion 的步骤对齐，然后用一个闭式规则把 criterion-level judgment 分摊成 per-step advantage。论文强调这个分配规则满足若干形式性质，包括总量守恒、符号保持、长度无关等，目的是避免 credit 在长轨迹里被无控制地稀释，或者因为轨迹更长而系统性偏置。这里的关键不是“做 step reward”本身，而是 step reward 不依赖终局 verifier，也不依赖 learned reward model，而是直接从 rubric verdict 推出。

训练上仍然套在 GRPO 框架里：对每个任务采样一组轨迹，judge 对每条轨迹按动态 rubric 打分，再把 rubric verdict 重新分配到步骤，形成替代统一 trajectory advantage 的 step-level advantage。论文还比较了 frontier judge 与 self-judge，说明 judge 质量会直接影响 credit 质量。与已有 process reward 或 step-level RL 方法的差别在于，很多先前方法仍然锚定 gold answer、unit test 或 terminal reward；DRACO 刻意去掉了这些训练时真值依赖。1c:T6ef,DRACO has two main components. The first is dynamic per-trajectory rubric generation. Instead of writing one fixed rubric for the whole task distribution, the judge first proposes criteria from the task instruction, then extends those criteria using what each sampled rollout actually attempted, where it failed, and which subgoals emerged during execution. The criteria proposed from all trajectories in the same GRPO group are merged into a shared rubric set. The motivation is straightforward: fixed rubrics under-specify long trajectories, while rollout-conditioned rubrics can evaluate what the policy concretely did rather than what a generic template expected.

The second component is rubric-conditioned advantage redistribution. Standard GRPO assigns one scalar advantage to every token in a trajectory. DRACO asks the judge to score each rubric criterion, aligns criteria to the steps responsible for them, and then applies a closed-form redistribution rule to produce per-step advantages. The paper emphasizes several formal properties of this rule, including conservation of total push, sign preservation, and length independence. These properties are meant to prevent the usual failure mode where long trajectories dilute credit or where trajectory length itself introduces systematic bias.

Training remains within the GRPO framework: sample a group of trajectories, score them with a frozen judge under the dynamic rubric, and replace uniform trajectory-level advantage with redistributed step-level advantage. A notable design choice is that the method does not rely on a learned reward model and does not anchor step rewards to terminal verifiers or gold answers.
