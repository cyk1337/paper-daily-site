It formalizes intra-group cancellation: at a timestep where all trajectories share the same (context, token) and that token carries no distinguishing credit, the aggregated group gradient for that token should be zero (or approximately so). If exchangeability is broken and the aggregated gradient is non-zero, the update induces a strictly positive KL drift in the conditional token distribution at that context.
The key proof uses a second-order Taylor expansion of KL around the update and identifies the Hessian with the conditional Fisher information; if g_{t*}≠0 and Fisher is non-degenerate along g_{t*}, then KL increases for sufficiently small step size—i.e., reward-irrelevant drift is guaranteed.
The paper then argues that two common design patterns structurally break exchangeability: (1) sequence-coupled trajectory aggregation with stop-gradient on sequence-level coefficients, and (2) asymmetric segment pruning/selection across trajectories. Finally, it proposes minimal intra-group transformations intended to restore or approximate the cancellation structure without abandoning intra-group comparison
