9:T4d6,There are three main design choices. First, the benchmark builds three diagnostic settings that expose different failure modes. Parallel evidence requires preserving two parallel reasoning chains for ambiguous references. Conditional evidence requires keeping both the supporting statement and its qualifier, so the compressor cannot turn a conditional claim into an unconditional one. Imbalanced evidence tests whether minority-supported perspectives survive when most evidence points elsewhere. Second, for each query, the required evidence is extracted from the source context and decomposed into minimal self-contained propositions. Evaluation is then done proposition by proposition, based on whether each one is explicitly stated or unambiguously inferable from the compressed context alone. Third, the paper introduces All Proposition Recall (All-Rec), which only counts a case as correct if every required proposition is preserved. This is intentionally stricter than average proposition recall, because many reasoning failures are caused by a single missing piece. They also define Evidence Preservation Return (EPR), the ratio of overall All-Rec to average inference TFLOPs, to compare preservation quality against compute cost.a:T546,KEEP-Bench contains 4,102 instances across the three evidence structures. The authors evaluate 17 compressor configurations, including dedicated compression models and general-purpose LLMs. The headline result is that current systems are far from reliable: all dedicated compressors stay below 10% overall All-Rec, and general LLMs under 10B parameters remain below 30%. Stronger models improve, but not enough. Gemma-4-31B-it reaches 66.8% overall All-Rec, and Claude-Haiku-4.5 reaches 61.6%. This happens even though the average source context is only 3.1K tokens, far below the tested models’ 32K–400K context windows, so the issue is not raw context capacity. Performance also varies sharply by evidence structure. Claude-Haiku-4.5 leads in the parallel setting, roughly doubling GPT-5.4-mini there, but trails it on imbalanced science cases. That means a single benchmark style can give a misleading picture of compressor quality. Additional analyses show that larger output budgets mostly help already strong models, widening capability gaps; evidence pieces that are farther apart are less likely to be jointly preserved; and earlier evidence in the context is more likely to survive compression. Together these results suggest that many compressors behave more like heuristic salience filters than faithful evidence-preserving mechanisms.b:Taaa,核心做法是把 faithfulness 操作化为“对 CoT 的依赖程度（reliance）”：对同一个问题、同一个原始 CoT，做干预得到 c'，观察模型对原答案 a 的置信度 z=P(a|x;c) 如何变化到 z'=P(a|x;c')。如果改动 CoT 会显著降低模型对原答案的概率，说明模型确实在用这段推理链。

关键设计点：
- 数据：使用 MMMLU（OpenAI 2024）的专业人工翻译平行题，保证不同语言是同一批题，从而把“语言差异”尽量归因到模型语言能力/资源差异，而不是题目分布差异。英文用原版 MMLU。
- 语言资源水平：用 CulturaX/Common Crawl token 量作为 proxy，跨度接近 8 个数量级；选 8 种语言覆盖不同语系与脚本。
- 任务分解：从 MMMLU 选 8 个子任务，按知识密集/推理密集各半。作者的核心结论依赖这个分组：资源水平的效应主要出现在知识密集任务。
- 干预（interventions）：沿用/改造 Lanham et al. (2023) 的思路，对 CoT 做四类干预：
  1) Early Answering：只保留前 1/3 推理链（相当于截断推理）。
  2) Filler Tokens：把推理链字符替换成点（相当于移除推理信息但保留长度/位置）。
  3) Adding Mistakes：把推理链中一个词替换成反义词（引入局部错误）。
  4) Paraphrasing：重写前 3/4 推理链并让模型续写。作者发现该操作经常改变语义、比 Adding Mistakes 更容易改答案，因此把它也视为“破坏性干预”。
  其中 3/4 需要一个“扰动器”模型来编辑 CoT，作者用 Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct。
- 指标：主指标用 Δlog p（对原答案概率的相对对数下降），并对比 Δp、以及一个基于选项分布变化的指标 Φ。选择 Δlog p 的理由是：低资源语言原始 z 更低，Δp 会被上限压缩；Δlog p 更可比。
- 统计建模：对模型 m、子任务 s、语言 ℓ 的平均 faithfulness 做带 fixed effects 的线性回归，检验资源水平与任务类型的交互效应，并做稳健性检查。
- 机制检验：提出 Reliance Hypothesis，并用“模型置信度”做中介分析：低资源语言里模型更不自信，部分解释了更高 reliance；但置信度不足以解释为什么效应主要在知识密集任务上。
- 进一步刻画：作者报告一个类似 Simpson’s paradox 的现象：跨语言看，低置信度语言更依赖 CoT；但在同一语言内部，低置信度样本并不更依赖 CoT。并把“依赖更重”拆成“依赖更频繁 vs 每次依赖更强”，结论是低资源语言主要是更频繁地依赖（例如英文约 4% vs Yoruba 约 35% 的回答出现明显 reliance），而不是每次依赖幅度更大。c:T7b1,Methodologically, the paper defines faithfulness via interventions on the CoT and measures how the model’s confidence in its original answer changes.

Key components:
- Parallel evaluation set: MMMLU (professionally translated) so each language sees the same multiple-choice questions; English uses MMLU. This controls for content differences across languages.
- Resource level proxy: token counts from CulturaX/Common Crawl, spanning ~8 orders of magnitude across 8 selected languages.
- Task-type control: 8 subtasks split into knowledge-intensive vs reasoning-intensive, enabling a within-model, within-language comparison of how the resource-level effect depends on task type.
- Four CoT interventions (inspired by Lanham et al., 2023): (1) truncate to the first third (Early Answering), (2) replace the chain with filler dots (Filler Tokens), (3) inject a local mistake by antonym substitution (Adding Mistakes), (4) paraphrase/rewrite most of the chain and let the model complete it (Paraphrasing). Importantly, they empirically find their paraphrases often change meaning and flip answers more frequently than Adding Mistakes, so they treat Paraphrasing as a corrupting intervention rather than meaning-preserving.
- Faithfulness metrics: primarily Δlog p on the original answer to avoid ceiling effects when baseline probabilities are lower in low-resource languages; they also report Δp and a distribution-shift metric Φ.
- Modeling/analysis: regress mean faithfulness on resource level with fixed effects, and test an explicit mechanism (the Reliance Hypothesis) via mediation analysis using model confidence. They further analyze a Simpson’s-paradox-style pattern: across languages, lower confidence correlates with higher reliance, but within a language, low-confidence items are not more reliant.
- Reliance decomposition: show low-resource languages rely more often (higher frequency of reliance events) rather than relying more strongly per event.d:T4d5,Setup: 4 open-weight instruction-tuned multilingual LLMs (Gemma-3-4B-it, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Aya-Expanse-8B). CoT perturbations for mistakes/paraphrases are generated by Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct. Evaluation uses up to 250 questions per subtask, 8 languages, 8 subtasks.

Main findings (the provided full-text excerpt does not include all table values):
- On knowledge-intensive subtasks, CoT faithfulness (reliance) increases as language resource level decreases; on reasoning-intensive subtasks, this trend is weak or absent.
- The direction of the knowledge-vs-reasoning gap is consistent across models and multiple faithfulness metrics and survives robustness checks.
- Confidence partially mediates the resource-level effect, but does not explain why the effect is much stronger for knowledge-heavy tasks.
- Reliance is more frequent in low-resource languages (example reported: ~4% of answers in English vs ~35% in Yoruba show reliance), rather than being uniformly stronger per instance.
