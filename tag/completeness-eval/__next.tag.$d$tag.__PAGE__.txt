9:T49a,The core idea is a two-level rubric system. The upper level is a structured meta-rubric that captures how a complete answer should be organized. It includes typed components such as simple knowledge, strict lists, flexible lists, and processes, along with importance tiers like answer-critical, valuable, and context, all grounded in cited evidence. The lower level is a mechanically compiled flat checklist of binary items used for grading.

This split is the main contribution. Rich structure is needed to express completeness faithfully, but judges are more reliable on flat pass/fail checks. GAMUT keeps the expressive representation for rubric authoring and verification, while using the compiled binary form for low-variance automatic scoring.

The benchmark targets what the paper calls everyday deep research: realistic, image-grounded, knowledge-seeking questions that require multi-step web research and synthesis. Construction uses frontier search-capable LLMs together with expert human annotators over multiple rounds, with human-verified web evidence.
