9:T588,The architecture has three parts: a frozen backbone LLM, a safety-specific LoRA adapter, and a learned gate that outputs a continuous routing score g in [0,1] from hidden-state representations. The adapter is not simply turned on or off. Its strength is scaled continuously inside the model, with the effective transformation written as h' = (W + gΔW)h. This makes the method closer to interpolation between the base model and a safety-adapted model, rather than hard switching or post-hoc filtering.

A key design choice is that the gate is trained jointly with the safety adapter. It is not an external moderation classifier. The gate directly modulates internal computation during generation. The paper adds two training ingredients to make routing discriminative. First, subtype-aware gate weighting gives different emphasis to different harmful and benign prompt categories, aiming to reduce false positives on safety-adjacent benign inputs. Second, a hard pairwise margin objective explicitly separates harmful and benign prompts in gating space. This is important because plain generation loss may not force a clean routing boundary. Compared with standard safety LoRA, the novelty is not the adapter itself but the input-conditioned continuous activation. On Llama-3-8B-Instruct, CLEAR reportedly drives HarmBench attack success rate close to zero. This supports the idea that smaller models suffer more from global safety tuning and therefore benefit more from conditional routing.

XSTest is used to probe routing quality rather than end-task generation alone. The hidden-state gate, with fewer than 1M parameters, is claimed to achieve a favorable harmful-versus-benign routing trade-off. That matters because it distinguishes CLEAR from simply adding another classifier in front of the model.
