18:T692,The method has two layers. The first is the verifier interface itself. Seva produces a structured verification record: evidence-alignment spans, step-by-step reasoning, a final support decision, calibrated confidence, a six-way error diagnosis, and actionable fixes. This is not added verbosity for its own sake; it creates separable subproblems that can be scored independently. The second layer is RL training. The model is first initialized with SFT, then optimized with GRPO. The key change is the reward. Instead of a binary correct/incorrect signal, the paper defines a process reward R(v, y) over the structured output. The reward decomposes quality into five components plus a calibration term. The reported weights are 0.10 for format, 0.30 for evidence alignment, 0.30 for reasoning chain, 0.15 for label correctness, and 0.15 for diagnosis, with an asymmetric calibration bonus/penalty. Correct high-confidence predictions are rewarded more than incorrect high-confidence predictions are penalized. Two design choices matter. First, each component is computed from a different region of the output, so the components are only weakly correlated; this preserves reward variance within a GRPO group. Second, the reward is intentionally process-heavy, roughly 70/30 toward process over final outcome. A response with good grounding and reasoning but a wrong final label can still receive a middling score instead of collapsing to zero together with malformed outputs. The paper also adds a self-evolution loop—Verify, Reflect, Probe, Refine—where the model diagnoses its weak error categories and generates targeted adversarial data for those categories over four rounds.19:T582,There are three main empirical takeaways. First, the paper shows that process reward is what makes RL trainable in this setting. With binary reward, GRPO makes essentially no progress over 350 steps; with group size G=8, most responses score zero and the advantage spread shrinks to about ±0.05. With the decomposed reward, alignment quality reaches 0.997, format compliance reaches 100%, and final F1 rises to 69.0, which is +4.1 over SFT and close to GPT-4o-mini at 69.8. Second, the training dynamics reveal an implicit curriculum. The model first learns verification behavior and output structure within roughly the first 150 steps, then spends the remaining ~200 steps improving final verification outcomes. That supports the claim that the reward decomposition separates behavioral competence from end-task correctness. Third, the self-evolution study on the 7B model is more interesting than a standard iterative-improvement story. After four rounds, with Round 4 reaching 7,787 training samples, the model does not become a uniformly better generalist. Instead, each round produces a benchmark specialist: for example, about +15 points on HaluEval can coincide with a 10–14 point drop on TruthfulQA. The authors argue that iterative refinement tracks the induced data distribution rather than yielding monotonic general improvement.
