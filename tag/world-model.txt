17:T569,The method is State Transition Pretraining, or STP. Training data consists of step-level tuples (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}), which can be extracted from existing trajectories or collected automatically from interactive environments. STP jointly optimizes two objectives. Forward dynamics predicts the next GUI state from the current state and action, effectively an image-text-to-image task. This is meant to build an internal model of how GUI states change under actions such as click, type, or scroll. Inverse dynamics predicts the action from consecutive screenshots, an image-image-to-text task. This forces the model to identify what changed, where the interaction occurred, and which visual details are action-relevant. A notable design choice is the use of a unified multimodal model rather than separate modules for perception, action classification, and state prediction. That allows both objectives to share a common tokenized interface and makes the pretrained model easier to transfer into downstream trajectory fine-tuning. The paper positions STP as initialization rather than replacement: it removes some of the burden from trajectory SFT by learning local structure first. The ablations suggest that forward and inverse dynamics provide complementary signals, and that the gains are not sensitive to whether transitions are sampled as full trajectories or as independent steps.18:T40f,The main results cover both desktop and mobile GUI benchmarks. On AgentNetBench, under matched trajectory fine-tuning settings, STP improves average success rate by 2.3% to 6.2% and coordinate success rate by 1.6% to 6.8%, depending on the fine-tuning split. On AndroidControl, it improves step success rate by 0.9% and coordinate prediction accuracy by 1.3%. On GUIOdyssey, it improves step success rate by 0.6% and coordinate prediction accuracy by 1.6%. The paper also provides optimization evidence: the left panel of Figure 1 shows better cross-entropy behavior during downstream trajectory fine-tuning when initialized with STP. The scalability results are important. With fine-tuning data fixed, increasing transition-pretraining data steadily improves downstream performance. With STP fixed, scaling trajectory fine-tuning data from 2K to 18K still leaves STP ahead of the fine-tune-only baseline. That suggests the gains are not just due to extra data exposure, but to the structure of the forward and inverse dynamics objectives.19:T509,The system is built on a 15B video diffusion transformer and generates 24-fps video at 540p and 720p. Instead of producing a full long video in one pass, it generates short latent chunks autoregressively. Each chunk is conditioned on camera trajectories and switchable text prompts. The central mechanism is a bounded visual context composed of a global scene anchor, compressed recent history, geometry-aligned spatial memory, and recent-frame conditioning. Temporal memory maintains short-range dynamics and frame continuity. Spatial memory stores past observations and reprojects them into the current target view when the camera revisits a region. This keeps per-chunk compute approximately constant as rollout length grows. Training has three stages: adapt a general bidirectional video prior to world-modeling data; convert it into an autoregressive generator with camera control and spatiotemporal memory; then train with corrupted histories and replay prediction residuals collected from the model’s own rollouts. For inference, the paper uses discrete autoregressive distillation with distribution-matching distillation, self-forcing++, and consistency distillation, reducing sampling from roughly 30 steps to 4 steps per chunk while keeping the control and memory stack active.1a:T4a5,The training corpus contains 222,147 clips from seven sources, including internally curated MUGEN and GameVerse data. It mixes real-world videos, gameplay recordings, synthetic renderings, and generated event videos. Each record is normalized into video frames, camera intrinsics and poses when available or recoverable, and timeline-aligned hierarchical captions. Evaluation is performed on iWorld-Bench, covering generation quality, trajectory following, and memory ability. The paper reports stronger overall performance than compared systems. The most informative ablations concern spatial memory, corrupted-history training, replay residuals, and distillation. The reported pattern is consistent: recent-frame conditioning alone is insufficient for revisitation; geometry-aligned memory improves preservation of previously seen regions; training on model-induced imperfect histories reduces long-horizon blur, illumination drift, and geometric drift. The paper also states a clear limitation: the model still represents the world mainly through visible observations, estimated geometry, and visual memory, so object state, physical causality, and long-term task structure remain weak.1b:T4f2,The main contribution is the Causal-Generative Dual-Judge (CGDJ), a benchmark and evaluation framework with two complementary modules. The first is Explicit Causal Perception, which tests whether a model can identify and articulate causal structure from visual evidence. Since most video generators do not support interleaved video-text prompting, the authors introduce Flatten Temporal Video: multiple frames plus rasterized queries are packed into a unified image grid, turning the task into a directly judgeable visual QA setup. This forces the model to expose what causal relation it recognizes instead of hiding behind free-form continuation.

The second module is Implicit Generative Prediction. Given a causal antecedent, the model must generate the consequent future video. This tests whether the model can enact its putative reasoning in the pixel domain over time. The key idea is to separate “knowing” from “rendering.” A model may answer causal questions correctly yet fail to generate a causally valid rollout, or it may generate something visually plausible without robust explicit causal understanding. That decomposition is more informative for world-model claims than standard fidelity metrics such as FVD or static multimodal QA
