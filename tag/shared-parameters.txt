18:Tae8,技术路线分成三部分：稳定性建模、稳定架构设计、以及面向随机深度训练的算法修正。

先看建模。作者把 middle-looped 架构写成三段：prelude 单元把输入 token 映射成条件向量 e，recurrent unit 在循环步 t 上更新 hidden state h_t，coda 单元把最终状态映射到 logits。关键是把 recurrent update 视为一个关于 residual stream 的非线性时变系统，其中一部分参数控制“保留多少旧状态、注入多少新状态”。在对系统做线性化近似后，这个更新退化成经典 LTI 系统，稳定性可以用状态转移矩阵的谱性质来分析。作者发现，已有 looped 架构中的 injection 参数会学到过大的谱范数，从而导致 residual explosion。这不是经验上“有时会炸”，而是能被动力系统视角解释的结构性风险。

基于这个分析，Parcae 的核心设计是对 A 做连续到离散的稳定参数化，并把连续形式的 A 约束为 negative diagonal matrix。这样离散化后的状态更新天然带有收缩性质，谱范数被控制住，残差不会在循环中指数放大。这个设计很像把 recurrent depth 的自由度从“任意线性耦合”收缩到“逐维稳定衰减/保留”，牺牲了一部分表达灵活性，换来训练稳定性和可预测性。作者没有选择更宽松的全矩阵稳定化，而是用对角负矩阵，原因很实际：它足够稳定、参数少、实现简单，而且在大模型训练里更容易和现有 Transformer block 兼容。

除了结构约束，作者还处理了两个训练层面的尖点来源。第一，对输入条件 e 做归一化，缓解训练后期的 loss spikes。这里可以理解为 recurrent unit 在不同 loop depth 下反复接收同一个条件信号，如果其尺度漂移，容易在深循环时放大不稳定模式。第二，训练目标需要对随机深度的期望损失做优化。已有做法常在 batch 级别采样统一深度，导致梯度方差和深度切换带来的不连续更明显。Parcae 改成 intra-batch per-sequence depth sampling，让同一 batch 内不同样本走不同 loop 次数，从而平滑优化过程，也更接近“对深度分布求期望”的目标。

论文还有一个重要点是把训练时 scaling 和测试时 scaling 统一起来。训练时，他们在 isoFLOP 设定下研究给定总训练 FLOPs 时，应该把预算分给更多数据还是更多 looping；测试时，他们研究增加 loop 次数带来的 loss 改善曲线，并拟合出带 irreducible loss 的指数衰减形式。最终得到一个统一的 looping scaling 视角：训练阶段学到的 recurrent refinement 能在推理阶段继续通过更多循环释放出来，但收益遵循可预测的递减规律。19:T93b,实验分成稳定性、端到端质量、训练 FLOP scaling、测试时 scaling 四块。

稳定性方面，作者先证明旧配方确实容易出现 residual explosion 和 loss spikes，并把问题和 injection 参数的谱范数联系起来。Table 2 是关键证据之一：不稳定模型会学到更危险的 A 参数化，而 Parcae 的约束能显著抑制这种现象。文中还强调，过去一些 looped 模型往往依赖敏感超参和 Post-Norm 才能勉强收敛，Parcae 则把稳定性更多前移到参数化本身。

端到端质量方面，Parcae 与参数量、数据量匹配的 RDMs 和标准 Transformer 比较。结果上，相比 RDM，验证集 perplexity 降低 6.3%。当规模扩展到 1.3B 参数、100B tokens 时，Parcae 在 Core 和 Core-Extended benchmark 上分别比参数匹配 Transformer 高最多 2.99 和 1.18 分，并能匹配约两倍参数量的 Transformer。这是全文最重要的结果之一，因为它说明 looping 不是只在 toy setting 下省参数，而是在较实在的预训练规模上也能换到质量。

训练 FLOP scaling 方面，作者做了 parameter-matched isoFLOP 实验：总训练 FLOPs 固定时，比较把预算用于更多数据，还是用于更多 loop depth。结论是 looping 构成了与参数、数据并列的独立 scaling 轴，最优配方会同时增加数据和 looping，并满足经验幂律。Figure 1 右图是这里的核心图。这个结果的含义不是“looping 总比数据好”，而是“在固定参数下，compute-optimal 解不只是一味加数据”。

测试时 scaling 方面，作者观察到增加循环次数会带来可预测的 loss 下降，形式接近指数衰减并存在不可约下界。也就是说，模型确实学会了可继续 refinement 的动态，但额外 loop 的边际收益会越来越小。论文进一步把训练期和测试期的 scaling law 合在一起，形成统一拟合。这一点对部署很关键，因为它支持按场景做 per-token 或 per-request 的计算分配。

从全文叙述看，消融实验还覆盖了 A 的参数化方式、e normalization、以及 per-sequence depth sampling 对 loss spikes 的影响。值得注意的是，作者不是只报告最终分数，而是把训练曲线稳定性本身当成一等结果来分析，这对做大规模预训练的人更有参考价值。1a:T8f4,The technical story has three parts: stability analysis, stable parameterization, and training-algorithm fixes for stochastic depth.

The model is written as a middle-looped architecture with a prelude module, a recurrent middle block, and a coda module. The recurrent update over the residual stream is cast as a nonlinear time-varying dynamical system. After linearization, the update reduces to a linear time-invariant system, which makes classical stability analysis available. The key empirical observation is that prior looped architectures can learn unstable injection operators with large spectral norms, leading to residual explosion.

Parcae addresses this by parameterizing the continuous-time state matrix A and discretizing it in a controlled way, while constraining A to be a negative diagonal matrix. This gives the recurrent update an inherently contractive character and prevents the hidden state from blowing up across loop iterations. The design is deliberately conservative: a diagonal stable parameterization gives up some flexibility relative to a full matrix, but it is simple, cheap, and robust enough for large-scale Transformer training.

The paper also identifies two practical sources of loss spikes. First, the conditioning signal e is repeatedly injected across loop steps, so scale drift in e can destabilize deep recurrence. Parcae adds normalization on e to mitigate this. Second, the training objective optimizes expected loss over variable depths. Prior recipes often sample one depth per batch, which creates higher variance and sharper optimization discontinuities. Parcae instead uses intra-batch per-sequence depth sampling, so different sequences in the same batch use different loop counts. This better matches the expectation-over-depth objective and empirically smooths training.

A further strength of the paper is that it connects training-time and inference-time scaling. Under isoFLOP training, it studies how compute should be split between more data and more looping. At test time, it studies how additional loop iterations reduce loss, finding an exponential-decay form with an irreducible floor. The result is a unified view in which recurrence learned during training can be exploited at inference, but with predictable diminishing returns.1b:T98d,The experiments cover stability, end-to-end quality, training-FLOP scaling, and test-time scaling.

On stability, the paper shows that earlier looped recipes indeed suffer from residual explosion and loss spikes, and links this behavior to the learned spectral properties of the injection operator. Table 2 is central here: unstable models learn more dangerous parameterizations, while Parcae’s constrained form suppresses that failure mode. An important practical point is that prior looped models often relied on sensitive hyperparameters and normalization choices such as Post-Norm just to converge; Parcae moves much of the stability burden into the parameterization itself.

On end-to-end quality, Parcae is compared against parameter- and data-matched RDMs and standard Transformers. Relative to RDMs, it reduces validation perplexity by 6.3%. At 1.3B parameters trained on 100B tokens, it outperforms parameter-matched Transformers by up to 2.99 points on Core and 1.18 points on Core-Extended, while matching Transformers roughly twice as large. That is the headline result: looping is not only a toy parameter-saving trick, but can buy real quality at meaningful pretraining scale.

For training-FLOP scaling, the paper runs parameter-matched isoFLOP studies and asks whether extra compute should go into more data or more loop depth. The answer is that looping behaves like an additional scaling axis. FLOP-optimal training increases both data and looping according to empirical power laws. The implication is not that looping dominates data, but that under a fixed parameter budget, the compute-optimal solution is not exhausted by “just add more tokens.”

For test-time scaling, increasing the number of loop iterations yields predictable loss improvements that follow an exponential decay toward an irreducible floor. This suggests that the model learns a genuine iterative refinement process, but one with diminishing returns. The paper then combines training-time and test-time fits into a unified scaling-law view. That is especially relevant for adaptive inference and per-request compute allocation.

The ablations also examine the parameterization of A, normalization of e, and the effect of per-sequence depth sampling on loss spikes. Importantly, the paper treats training-curve stability itself as a first-class result, which makes it more useful for practitioners than papers that only report final benchmark scores.1c:T5e4,This one is worth a close read. The most important part is not the benchmark table but the way the paper reformulates looped Transformers as dynamical systems and then turns that analysis into a trainable architecture.

A good reading order is: Introduction, then the formal definition of the middle-looped architecture in the background section, then the stability analysis. Pay special attention to the step from the nonlinear time-varying system to the linearized LTI view, and to why the spectral norm governs residual explosion. That is the conceptual core of the paper. After that, read the Parcae parameterization carefully: continuous A, discretization, and the negative-diagonal constraint. Then read the training fixes, especially e normalization and intra-batch per-sequence depth sampling. Finally, study three experimental anchors: the stability evidence in Table 2, the scaling-law plots in Figure 1, and the main 1.3B / 100B-token quality results.

Several follow-up questions are worth keeping in mind. The negative-diagonal parameterization is stable, but perhaps restrictive; future work could explore low-rank or block-diagonal stable forms. The exponential test-time scaling curve suggests a finite but usable reserve of iterative computation, which connects naturally to latent reasoning, adaptive compute, and early exit. Also, the choice of training-time depth distribution likely shapes the quality/compute Pareto frontier at inference.
