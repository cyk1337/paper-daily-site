It also keeps the learned continuous-time reverse process nonexplosive, with moments of all orders, and arbitrarily close to the exact reverse process in path-space total variation. The failure appears after explicit Euler–Maruyama discretization. Rare numerical trajectories can enter the remote perturbed region, and the explicit updates then repeatedly amplify their magnitude. The probability of such events is tiny, but the amplification dominates the probability cost, so all positive moments of the endpoint diverge. Weak convergence, and even convergence in probability, can coexist with divergence in every Wasserstein-Wp distance. The paper also shows that the same issue can occur in arbitrary dimension, for probability-flow ODEs, and even within a fixed finite GELU architecture.
