Each action selects one attention head and zero-ablates its output at hook_z. Episodes last up to 50 steps, and repeated selection of the same head within an episode is masked out. Training uses two tasks only, induction and IOI. At every reset, the environment samples one of these tasks and regenerates a fresh batch, so the policy cannot simply memorize a fixed ranking of heads. The reward is the key design choice. For the target task, the paper measures the drop in a task-specific logit-difference metric after ablation. For a control batch of natural English text from WikiText, it measures the increase in cross-entropy. The final reward is the difference between these two effects. Heads that are broadly useful to the model should hurt both the target task and ordinary language modeling, yielding a smaller net reward; heads that are disproportionately important for the target task should receive larger positive reward. The observation includes a one-hot task indicator, a binary mask of previously tried heads, and a vector of normalized rewards for heads already tested in the current episode. This lets the policy adapt its search online within an episode. The policy itself is a small feedforward actor-critic trained with PPO over 8 parallel environments for 2e5 environment steps. At inference time, the authors also add a best-of-K wrapper: the policy samples K candidate actions, the environment scores them in parallel, and the highest-reward action is executed. The most informative experiments are likely the reward ablations, comparisons against oracle and random baselines, training stability with vectorized PPO versus the collapsed single-environment variant, and transfer to the held-out docstring task.
