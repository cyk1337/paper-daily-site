- Literature-level discrepancy: for LLaDA-8B-Instruct on GSM8K, reported accuracy can differ by up to ~9 percentage points across studies even when the decoding method and generation configuration are claimed to be the same, indicating evaluation details dominate.

(Note: the provided full-text excerpt does not include all tables/numbers; the ~9% GSM8K discrepancy is explicitly stated, while other results are summarized at the conclusion level.)1a:T45d,Proposes ASRD (Anchor Supervised Revocable Decoding), a fully embedding-space framework compatible with FlashAttention, requiring no model retraining and working as a drop-in module for existing dLLMs. The core component is the dynamic Anchor Tokens Cache (ATC): tokens that maintain temporal consistency (stable predictions across multiple decoding steps) are promoted to trusted anchors and stored in the cache to form a reliable context skeleton. Two complementary embedding-level strategies are built on the ATC: (1) Anchor-Guided Generation injects entropy-weighted anchor signals into [MASK] token embeddings to reduce interference from erroneous tentative tokens, mitigating Error Propagation; (2) Anchor-Perturbed Verification probes tentative tokens with an orthogonal anchor-derived perturbation, only revoking tokens that rely on local error clusters rather than anchor-supported global consistency, addressing Local Error Reinforcement. 结论很清楚。第一，多 token 对 diffusion backbone 是稳定增益，对 AR backbone 则不是。作者报告 multi-token DiffRetriever 在所有测试的 diffusion backbone 上都显著优于 single-token，而 AR 版本的 multi-token 没有稳定超过 single-token。也就是说，先前 PromptReps 多 token 不稳定，不足以否定“多代表位点”这个方向，更可能是被顺序生成的代价和噪声掩盖了。

第二，延迟收益来自并行解码，而不是 FLOPs 数量级变化。文中明确说明，从渐近 FLOPs 看，带 KV cache 的 AR 和 diffusion 都可随 K 线性增长；真正的差别在 wall-clock latency：AR 必须串行跑多次前向，diffusion 一次并行完成。Figure 1 是理解整篇文章最关键的图之一，因为它把“效果提升”和“延迟近似不变”放在同一个坐标系里。

第三，DiffRetriever 的优势在 zero-shot 和 fine-tuned 两种场景都能保留。作者指出，zero-shot 时基于 LLaDA 的 DiffRetriever 是对比系统里最强的；在 fine-tuning 后，基于 Dream 的 DiffRetriever 成为 BEIR-7 上整体最强的 retriever，超过 fine-tuned PromptReps、DiffEmbed 和 RepLLaMA。摘要里没有给出完整表格数字，但从正文结论看，这不是个别数据集上的偶然提升，而是跨 in-domain / out-of-domain 的一致趋势。

第四，预算选择是一个被低估的问题。作者做了一个“完美 per-query budget predictor”的 oracle 分析，发现如果能在冻结基座模型上按 query 自适应选择预算，在相同固定平均预算下，oracle 甚至能超过 contrastive fine-tuning 的固定预算方案，而且这一点在所有 backbone-benchmark 组合上都成立。这说明当前固定 Kq/Kp 的部署方式留下了明显性能空间，也提示未来可以把预算预测当成 retrieval-time policy 问题来做。1c:T6b8,The overall recipe follows PromptReps. A query or passage is prompted in natural language, and the model is asked to represent it with a few words. From the model response, hidden states are used as dense representations and token logits as sparse representations; retrieval scoring is then performed against indexed passage representations.

The key change is how those representative tokens are produced. In the AR setting, representative tokens are generated left-to-right after the assistant prefix, so producing K tokens requires K sequential decoding steps. In the diffusion setting, the prompt is extended with K [MASK] positions plus closing tokens, and the model predicts all masked positions jointly under bidirectional attention. The hidden states and logits at those K positions are read out in a single forward pass.

Two design choices matter. First, the authors explicitly frame this as training-aligned usage of a diffusion LM. This differs from encoder-style baselines such as DiffEmbed and pplx-embed, which use the diffusion backbone more like BERT and do not exploit the masked prediction interface at inference time. Second, diffusion requires fixed sequence length, so query and passage budgets (Kq and Kp) must be chosen in advance, unlike variable-length AR decoding. The paper later shows that this fixed-budget choice leaves measurable headroom, motivating adaptive per-query budget selection.

The paper also adds a contrastive fine-tuning stage on top of the zero-shot prompted retriever. This is useful because it keeps the retrieval pipeline close to PromptReps while allowing a comparison between prompted retrieval and trained retrieval under the same representation-generation principle.1d:T814,The study compares two AR backbones, LLaMA3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B, against two diffusion backbones, LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B. Evaluation covers MS MARCO, TREC DL 2019/2020, and BEIR-7, in both zero-shot and supervised fine-tuning settings.

The central empirical result is consistent: multi-token retrieval helps diffusion backbones and does not reliably help AR backbones. Multi-token DiffRetriever improves over the single-token variant on every tested diffusion model, while AR multi-token decoding shows no stable gain over the single-token baseline. This directly supports the paper’s thesis that the bottleneck is sequential generation rather than the multi-token retrieval idea itself.

The latency story is also important. The authors note that diffusion does not have an asymptotic FLOPs advantage over AR with KV caching; both scale linearly with K. The practical advantage is wall-clock latency: AR requires multiple sequential forward passes, whereas diffusion finishes in one parallel pass. Figure 1 is therefore central because it ties quality gains to nearly constant latency as K grows.

The gains transfer across settings. In zero-shot evaluation, DiffRetriever on LLaDA is reported as the strongest system among the compared methods, ahead of PromptReps on AR backbones and ahead of the encoder-style DiffEmbed baseline. After supervised fine-tuning, DiffRetriever on Dream becomes the strongest overall retriever on BEIR-7, outperforming fine-tuned PromptReps, DiffEmbed, and RepLLaMA. The paper text emphasizes that this is not a one-dataset anomaly but a repeated pattern across in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks.

A particularly useful analysis concerns budget selection. With a perfect per-query budget predictor on the frozen base model, the oracle adaptive-budget setup exceeds contrastive fine-tuning under the same fixed average budget on every backbone-benchmark pair tested. The appendix on prompt wording and AR token caps is useful for checking fairness but not essential on a first pass.1f:T6f0,The central idea is Multi-Path Discrete Consistency (MPDC). In continuous space, consistency training works because any noisy state lies on a unique probability-flow trajectory toward the data manifold. In discrete space, there is no such deterministic path, so pointwise trajectory consistency is ill-defined.

The paper replaces that missing object with a stochastic one: the exact posterior bridge q(x_s | x_t, x_0) between two noise levels s < t. For broad corruption families, including masked and uniform diffusion, this bridge is available in closed form. Given a noisier state x_t and the clean sample x_0, one can sample a cleaner intermediate state x_s exactly from the bridge. This yields a family of valid stochastic paths from x_t toward x_0, ranging from direct long jumps to gradual refinements.

CDLM trains a time-conditional predictor f_theta(x_t, t) to be consistent with f_theta(x_s, s) under this bridge coupling. Intuitively, predicting from a noisy state should agree with first hopping to a cleaner state via the true posterior bridge and then solving the easier denoising problem there. Few-step generation then emerges from path equivalence rather than from post hoc distillation.

This is the main departure from prior work. Standard masked diffusion mostly learns local denoising transitions and therefore degrades quickly when the number of sampling steps is reduced. Distillation-based accelerators such as SDTT or DUO+DCD compress a many-step teacher into a short-step student, often in multiple stages. CDLM is single-stage and teacher-free: it directly trains on both short and long bridges from scratch. The framework is also presented as general beyond masked diffusion, as long as the corruption process admits a tractable posterior bridge.20:T576,The experiments focus on the quality-speed-diversity trade-off under limited sampling budgets. CDLM is compared against strong base DLMs trained from scratch, including MDLM and DUO, as well as accelerated or distilled systems such as SDTT and DUO+DCD. The main empirical claim is that CDLM sets the strongest results among single-stage models across a range of sampling budgets, often matching or surpassing multi-stage distilled methods while preserving better sample diversity.

The clearest quantitative statement in the paper is that a distilled CDLM variant achieves up to 32x speedup over autoregressive baselines. Importantly, the paper distinguishes between the base CDLM trained in a single stage from scratch and a further distilled variant used to push speed even further. That separation matters: the contribution is not merely another distillation recipe, but a training principle that already improves few-step generation before any teacher-student compression.
