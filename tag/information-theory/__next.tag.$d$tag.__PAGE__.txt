Conditional entropy, and more generally data-model cross-entropy, can be expressed as the integral of its derivative with respect to the noise parameter. The nontrivial step is to turn the derivative of a joint quantity into something local and usable. The paper imports the EXIT/GEXIT machinery from coding theory and proves that for memoryless channels, the derivative decomposes into a sum of coordinate-wise local derivatives.

The main result is a mismatched conservation law for cross-entropy. Instead of assuming the denoiser equals the true posterior, the analysis allows a learned posterior family and shows that the global data-model CE is exactly the integral of local information derivatives along the noise axis. This yields several concrete consequences. One, the framework is channel-agnostic at the level of admissible memoryless corruption and recovers familiar special cases such as the Gaussian I-MMSE relation. Two, there is a locality property: the relevant derivatives depend only on marginal posteriors P(X_i | Y(τ)), not on the full joint posterior P(X | Y(τ)). This is a major simplification for high-dimensional sequence modeling. Three, the paper derives an implementation-oriented implication: it is sufficient to model an extrinsic posterior of the form P(X_i | Y_{-i}(τ)) and combine it with the known channel likelihood through a tilt. The first is a decision-theoretic formalization. Multi-agent multiple-choice QA is cast as a Bayesian decision problem where the latent state is the correct answer, each agent observes a private signal d_m, and forms a posterior p(s|d_m). In Blackwell’s framework, an information structure is defined by the conditional distribution from states to observations. If a protocol exposes only a coarser statistic than the original private signals, it is a garbling of the underlying information structure. Under this view, majority voting keeps only each agent’s discrete choice and therefore discards posterior mass information. Debate allows communication, but the final public transcript is still a protocol-constrained summary of the agents’ private evidence, not the evidence itself. Hence pooled posterior maximization serves as an information-theoretic upper reference.

The second layer is the practical method, MA-PoP. For each candidate answer, the method multiplies the per-agent posterior probabilities and renormalizes to obtain a pooled posterior. Under conditional independence of private signals given the true state, this is exactly the joint posterior. Under correlation, it becomes an evidence-accumulation approximation. The implementation is tailored to multiple-choice QA: each agent must expose answer probabilities, either directly or via logprob-based estimation and calibration. Compared with majority voting, MA-PoP uses confidence information. Compared with debate, it avoids long interaction traces, social drift, and error propagation. The paper also studies both homogeneous and heterogeneous agent pools, with stronger gains when agents provide complementary evidence.18:T59f,The empirical study covers six multiple-choice QA benchmarks, with both homogeneous and heterogeneous multi-agent settings, and compares MA-PoP against voting-based baselines and recent debate methods. The main result is consistent rather than flashy: direct posterior aggregation usually outperforms majority voting, weighted voting, and several debate variants, especially in heterogeneous settings where agents have complementary strengths.

The ablations are the more informative part. First, when the conditional-independence assumption is violated, product-of-posteriors still works reasonably well, but gains shrink as inter-agent correlation increases. That is exactly what one would expect from an overconfident approximate Bayesian combiner. Second, adding more debate rounds does not reliably help and can even hurt, matching the theoretical message that communication protocols do not create new information and may instead compress evidence into a worse shared narrative. Third, MA-PoP depends on posterior quality: if individual agents are poorly calibrated, the aggregation quality degrades. So the bottleneck is often uncertainty estimation, not debate skill.
