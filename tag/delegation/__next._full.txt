17:T899,方法上，WebRider的关键设计是把“长期委托控制”和“当前页面动作实现”硬性拆开，而不是让一个单体 LLM 在同一上下文里同时负责计划、证据判断、动作生成和终止决策。顶层 controller 维护一个显式状态，里面包含 goal、hard constraints、soft preferences、blocking conditions、evidence ledger、progress 和 next intention。它只负责高层决策：继续浏览、发起澄清、停止、标记外部阻塞、还是给出答案；如果继续浏览，它会发出一个紧凑的 intention，例如“核验该商品的退货政策”或“为兼容性再找一个来源”。

中层不允许判断“证据是否足够”或“是否可以最终回答”，它只根据当前 observation、顶层 intention、persona state 和近期历史，生成一个且仅一个可执行的 JSON Action AST，动作空间包括 Click、Type、Scroll、Search、Maps、Back、Wait 以及安全回退。这里的重点不是让模型自由生成文本动作，而是通过确定性的 compiler 和 guard 把动作约束成可执行、可校验的局部目标：语法错误、非法 target、重复状态、阻塞状态都会被拦截，并附带恢复动作。训练时监督的不是原始模型文本，而是 guard 之后最终真正执行的 action。这样做的好处是，中层 policy 的学习目标被压缩成一个局部、可验证、可替换的 action realization 问题，而 CAPTCHA、访问策略、证据充分性、最终回答等复杂控制仍由 guard 或顶层负责。

另一条主线是 persona-conditioned intent contract。作者强调 persona 不是人口统计属性，也不是长期记忆，而是任务局部的控制策略，决定证据收集、排序偏好、不确定性处理和 stopping 规则。contract 由 goal、硬约束、软偏好、阻塞条件、证据义务、答案 schema 等组成，并且除非用户显式澄清，否则在执行期间保持稳定。这个设计让同一个 request 在固定 hard constraints 下可以配不同 persona，形成 counterfactual setting，用来测“同一个任务，不同委托 policy 下 agent 是否真的走了不同且正确的路径”。18:T8c4,The main design choice is a hard separation between long-horizon delegation control and page-local action realization. Instead of letting one monolithic LLM jointly decide plans, evidence sufficiency, browser actions, and stopping, WebRider uses a hierarchy.

The top layer maintains an explicit state containing the goal, hard constraints, soft preferences, blocking conditions, evidence ledger, progress, and next intention. It decides whether to continue browsing, ask for clarification, stop, flag an external blocker, or answer. If browsing continues, it emits a compact intention such as “verify the return policy for this candidate” or “find a second source for compatibility.”

The middle layer is deliberately narrower. Given the current observation, the top-layer intention, persona state, and recent history, it emits exactly one JSON Action AST from a constrained action space: Click, Type, Scroll, Search, Maps, Back, Wait, or a safe fallback. Crucially, it is not allowed to decide whether evidence is sufficient or whether the task is ready for final answer. A deterministic compiler plus guards enforce executability: malformed targets, invalid terminals, repeated states, and blocked states are intercepted, with recovery actions attached when needed. Training supervision is defined on the final post-guard executable action, not on raw model text. That turns the middle policy into a local, verifiable action-realization problem, while harder issues such as CAPTCHA, access policy, evidence sufficiency, and final answering remain with the guards or the top layer.

The second core contribution is the persona-conditioned intent contract. Persona here is not demographic identity or long-term memory; it is a task-local control policy governing evidence gathering, uncertainty handling, ranking, interaction, and stopping. The contract includes the goal, hard constraints, soft preferences, blockers, evidence obligations, and answer schema, and remains stable unless the user explicitly clarifies. This lets the benchmark create counterfactual variants of the same request under different personas, so the evaluation can test whether the agent truly follows different delegated policies under fixed hard constraints.19:T6ab,The most informative part of the empirical section is the evaluation design and the failure decomposition, not model scaling. The paper introduces RiderBench, expanding 768 audited requests into 4,096 persona-conditioned contracts across 42 sites. The setup supports paired comparisons: the same request can be evaluated under different personas while keeping hard constraints and evidence obligations fixed.

The key result is the gap between completion and fidelity. The strong baseline controller, Full-Pro, terminates on 99.2% of contracts, but only 38.8% pass all contract gates. In other words, finishing the task and honoring the delegated policy are very different things. The paired human study reinforces this point: final-trace success and stepwise acceptability diverge significantly. A trajectory may look acceptable from the final answer alone, yet fail under stepwise audit because it skipped required evidence checks, failed to ask when uncertainty should have triggered clarification, or violated the persona’s risk policy.

The mechanism studies focus on persistent intention. The paper compares settings such as persona-only prompting, intention text alone, and explicit persistent intention state, and finds that maintaining intention as a state variable matters more than merely including persona text in the prompt. Another useful result concerns trainability of the middle layer: because each step yields a guarded executable action as a local target, the middle policy can be trained and swapped while holding the top controller, guards, and executor fixed. That makes the proposal more than an evaluation wrapper; it is also a modular training interface for process-level control.1a:T52f,Start with the Introduction and Figure 1. The central claim is that web-agent evaluation is missing delegation fidelity, not just task success. Then read the Hierarchical Controller section carefully. The key question is why the top and middle layers must be structurally separated, and why supervision is placed on post-guard executable actions rather than free-form action text. That is the architectural core.

Next, read the intent contract section with attention to what is stable and what can change only through explicit user clarification. The paper is trying to prevent policy drift during execution, so the stability of the contract is not a minor detail.

For a pretraining-oriented reader, the main value is not browser mechanics but how the supervision target is shifted from final answers to structured process state.1b:T63e,The paper evaluates on three benchmarks; the provided details mainly cover TextCraft-Synth and Oolong-Real. TextCraft-Synth is a controlled crafting environment inspired by TextCraft. It replaces Minecraft recipes with synthetic items and dependency trees, allowing task difficulty to scale by tree depth: Easy tasks have depth 2–3, Medium tasks depth 4–6, and Hard tasks depth 7–9. The model is Qwen-3-4B-Instruct-2507. Training uses only Medium tasks, while evaluation includes Easy and Hard tasks to test extrapolation. Two context regimes are used: an 8K constrained-context setting for both training and inference, and an unconstrained setting with 40K training context and 256K inference context. The latter is useful because a single-agent baseline is not context-limited, so gains are more attributable to decomposition rather than just additional memory. Training allows recursion depth up to 6 and evaluation up to 12. Oolong-Real tests aggregation over very long Dungeons & Dragons transcripts, with inputs of at least 55K tokens. Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct is trained under a 32K context limit, so a single agent cannot read the full document, while a recursive agent can assign chunks to subagents with fresh contexts. The reported qualitative findings are better training efficiency, solving tasks beyond the nominal context window, generalization to harder instances, and reduced wall-clock time when parallel decomposition is possible. Exact success rates and ablation numbers are not visible in the supplied excerpt and should be checked in the experiment tables and appendix.1c:T6e9,The method has two central ideas. First, a round-trip editing primitive. Starting from a seed document s, the benchmark defines a forward instruction x→ and an inverse instruction x←. The model applies the forward edit to obtain t, then applies the inverse edit to reconstruct ŝ. If the model both follows instructions and avoids unintended changes, ŝ should match s. Because exact string match is too crude across heterogeneous document types, the benchmark uses domain-specific similarity functions tailored to formats such as code, configuration files, subtitles, crystallography files, and music notation.

Second, the paper composes these reversible edits into a relay. Since each round-trip should ideally return to the original seed, multiple round-trips can be chained to simulate long delegated workflows. This turns long-horizon reliability into a measurable degradation curve: reconstruction quality after 2, 4, ..., 20 interactions. That is the key move that makes cumulative corruption visible.

The benchmark covers 52 professional domains and 310 work environments across five broad categories. Each environment contains a real seed document, 5–10 complex edit tasks, and distractor context to mimic realistic working conditions. Each edit is executed in an independent single-turn session. This choice isolates the model’s native document-editing reliability rather than mixing in persistent agent state or external verification. The paper also validates that the backtranslation-style setup does not reward trivial copying: models must actually perform the forward edit, otherwise they fail the task. The appendices discuss assumptions behind reversibility and the limits of similarity metrics, which are important for trusting the benchmark.1d:T61e,The large-scale study evaluates 19 LLMs on DELEGATE-52. The headline result is that current models steadily corrupt documents under long delegated workflows. Even frontier systems such as Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude 4.6 Opus, and GPT 5.4 lose about 25% of document content on average after 20 interactions. Averaged across all tested models, degradation is around 50%.

The paper defines a model as ready for delegated work in a domain if it retains at least a 98% reconstruction score after 20 interactions. Under that criterion, Python is the only domain, among 52, where most models are close to being usable. This domain pattern is informative: strongly structured and easily verifiable formats like Python and databases are much more robust, while natural-language-heavy or niche symbolic formats such as earning statements and music notation are much more fragile.

Several targeted analyses are especially useful. Longer documents, longer workflows, and distractor context all worsen degradation, and the effects compound over time rather than staying local to a single step. Performance after only two interactions is not predictive of performance after twenty, which shows why short-horizon benchmarks are misleading for delegated work. The paper also tests a basic agentic harness and finds no meaningful improvement, suggesting that the bottleneck is not simply prompting or orchestration but the model’s underlying editing fidelity.
