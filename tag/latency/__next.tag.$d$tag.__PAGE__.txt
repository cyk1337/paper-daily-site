9:T427,FreeBalance performs pre-routing online balancing through residual workload prediction. The key observation is that the input to an MoE router can be approximated well enough from the residual representation produced by the preceding layer. So, before the current router executes, the system feeds that residual into the corresponding router to obtain a lightweight prediction of the upcoming expert workload. This prediction is used only for scheduling, not for model execution. Based on the predicted load, FreeBalance adjusts expert-device mapping and starts expert-weight migration immediately, overlapping migration with pre-routing computation stages, especially attention. To avoid over-migration, the method includes a cost model that limits how many experts can be moved given the available overlap window. In other words, it does constrained balancing rather than chasing the most even load at any cost. Actual MoE execution still uses the original router outputs, so the model’s outputs remain unchanged; the gain comes purely from better scheduling.a:T4a4,The method replaces explicit thoughts and subqueries with special thought and subquery tokens. The last hidden states at these token positions are treated as latent thoughts and latent subqueries. Since these states are produced in a single forward pass, the model avoids the sequential cost of generating long intermediate text.

The central technical issue is retrieval. A latent subquery cannot be directly fed into a standard text retriever. The paper aligns the LLM-generated latent subquery with a pretrained dense retriever in embedding space. A natural-language subquery induces a similarity distribution over documents through the retriever. The latent subquery induces another distribution. Training minimizes the KL divergence between these two distributions. This avoids requiring large-scale supervised query-document pairs for intermediate subqueries, which are usually unavailable in agentic RAG training setups.
