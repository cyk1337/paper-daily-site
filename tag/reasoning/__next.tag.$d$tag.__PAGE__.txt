方法不是只做一次 patch，而是做一整套 intervention grid。维度包括：patch 哪个 cache segment；只换 K、只换 V、还是 K/V 一起换；是否同时复制当前 hidden state；是否使用语义相近但答案相同的 source control；是否做 decoded validation，确认被 patch 的 latent 轨迹确实对应可读出的反事实行为；以及 matched corruption，用同一区域做破坏性干预来测试 necessity。作者据此定义三个状态：active carrier，指最小且 gated 的 cache segment，在 oracle interchange 下足够，并且主要竞争解释无法匹配；carrier shift，指存在可转移效应，但活跃片段与主结论模型不同；no-call，指由于能力、支持度、decoded 行为或控制检查不过关，不能下机制判断。

For a recipient problem r and a source problem s, the method first constructs an exact, verifiable counterfactual: the source supplies a changed intermediate quantity, which is then combined with the recipient’s remaining variables to define a known counterfactual answer. Both source and recipient are rolled out to a latent step t. At that patch point, a selected cache slice from the source is transplanted into the recipient, optionally together with the current hidden state, and the test checks whether the recipient moves toward the source-defined counterfactual answer.

The protocol is deliberately grid-like rather than a single patch experiment. It varies: which cache segment is patched; whether only K, only V, or both are copied; whether the hidden state is also copied; whether semantic source controls with the same answer are used; whether decoded validation confirms that the patched latent trajectory corresponds to the intended counterfactual behavior; and whether matched corruption on the same region supports necessity. Based on this battery, the paper defines three outcomes: active carrier, carrier shift, and no-call.

Three technical choices are especially important. First, the paper uses exact source-recipient counterfactuals rather than open-ended prompt patching, which keeps the target answer unambiguous. Second, it separates sufficiency from necessity, and tests necessity with matched corruption rather than arbitrary noise, reducing the risk of overcalling mechanisms. Third, it explicitly distinguishes the current hidden state from the cache object: h_t is not part of the cache unless copied by intervention. (1) Local discovery: how many samples from π(·|s) are needed to even observe an action with advantage ≥ c. 从实现上，论文基于 reverse-KL OPD，并采用 support-restricted 近似来计算 token-level KL，只在学生或师生联合的 top-H support 上做分布匹配，避免全词表计算。R²-OPD 叠加在这个标准 OPD 框架之上，因此兼容现有 on-policy 蒸馏流水线。13:T866,The method, R²-OPD, has three main components. First, it estimates reasoning progress using teacher-independent process rewards. Instead of relying only on final correctness, it samples continuations from successive intermediate reasoning states and measures how the probability of solving the task changes. This gives a marginal contribution estimate for each reasoning span. The paper emphasizes that raw process rewards are noisy because continuation-based estimation has Monte Carlo variance and because fine-grained reasoning boundaries are themselves unstable.

Second, it performs sign-consistent span merging. The trajectory is segmented into reasoning spans, and adjacent spans with process rewards of the same sign are merged into larger segments. This reduces sensitivity to noisy internal boundaries and creates more stable units for calibration. The theoretical discussion argues that internal boundary estimation errors can cancel within merged segments, and that averaging token-level divergence over a segment reduces local variance under weak dependence assumptions.

Third, it detects local ranking conflicts between progress and distillation penalty, and uses those conflicts for filtering. For each merged segment, token-level reverse-KL distillation losses are aggregated into a segment-level penalty for deviating from the teacher. Process reward gives a segment-level estimate of reasoning advancement. If a higher-progress segment is penalized more heavily than a lower-progress one, the two signals disagree in ranking, indicating that teacher supervision may be unreliable there. Instead of adding process reward into the objective or replacing OPD, R²-OPD masks distillation rewards on the most inconsistent segments and keeps teacher guidance elsewhere.

This design choice is the core idea. Process reward is treated as a reliability probe rather than a primary training signal, which avoids directly optimizing against a high-variance reward. The implementation is built on reverse-KL OPD with support-restricted approximation over a top-H token set, so the method can be layered onto standard practical OPD pipelines.14:T776,The experimental section is aimed at validating the signal-calibration hypothesis rather than demonstrating a large-scale model jump. The tasks are reasoning-heavy, and the comparisons include vanilla OPD, variants that directly incorporate outcome or process rewards, and alternative filtering or weighting strategies.

Three findings stand out. First, the mismatch is real: the paper provides qualitative examples and aggregate analysis showing that some intermediate spans increase downstream solve probability while receiving stronger distillation penalties because they diverge from the teacher distribution. This is especially visible on harder reasoning problems.

Second, the gains come from removing bad supervision rather than injecting another reward. Compared with directly mixing process rewards into optimization, the filtering-based approach is more stable and more consistently helpful. The explanation is straightforward: raw process rewards are noisy, so using them as direct optimization targets can distort learning, whereas using them only to suppress clearly inconsistent teacher signals is safer.

Third, the ablations support the two main design choices: sign-consistent merging and segment-level averaging. Without merging, conflict detection becomes more brittle because boundary noise leaks into the comparison. With token-level filtering instead of segment-level filtering, lexical fluctuations create more false conflicts. The paper also studies the masking ratio and shows that more masking is not always better; excessive filtering removes useful teacher transfer.

This matters because the same model can have very different capability baselines across languages, so fixed difficulty would confound language effects with reward availability. They also pre-filter tasks using Qwen3-14B-Base at easy difficulty, removing tasks with pass@8 = 0, and exclude heavily English-dependent tasks from training while keeping them for evaluation. The resulting setup uses 62 training tasks and 30 held-out tasks for OOD evaluation.

Training uses a DAPO-style GRPO objective without KL penalty. Each run uses 500 steps, learning rate 1e-6, 64 prompts per step, and 8 rollouts per prompt. Rewards consist of a binary correctness reward plus two auxiliary rewards, each weighted at half of correctness: a format reward and a reasoning-language reward. The reasoning-language reward is instantiated either as English reasoning or native-language reasoning. A key implementation detail is that auxiliary rewards are only granted when the final answer is correct, which prevents the model from gaming language or format rewards at the expense of solving the problem. The paper notes that preliminary runs in low-resource languages could otherwise collapse or overfit to formatting.

The model suite spans Qwen, Gemma, and SmolLM families, including both base and post-trained variants from 1B to 8B parameters. The authors run monolingual GRPO for 11 MGSM languages and one multilingual run with uniform language sampling. Italian, Korean, and Portuguese are fully held out as unseen languages. For model-language pairs with sufficient initial support for the target language, they additionally train native-reward variants. The point of this design is to disentangle training language, rewarded reasoning language, and model pretraining background rather than to optimize a single benchmark score.18:T6a8,The most robust findings are fourfold. First, native-language reasoning rewards usually trail English-reasoning rewards by only a small margin on average; there is no universal large penalty for asking the model to reason in the prompt language. Second, cross-lingual transfer is strong: GRPO in one language often improves performance in many others, suggesting that RL is transferring problem-solving behavior rather than only surface-form language patterns. Third, the transfer is not monotonic. The paper identifies a regression failure mode where training in a particular language improves some evaluations but causes substantial drops on unseen languages or tasks. These regressions are highly model- and language-specific. Fourth, multilingual training is not always better than the best monolingual run, but it often provides a more stable compromise and is especially relevant for held-out-language generalization.
