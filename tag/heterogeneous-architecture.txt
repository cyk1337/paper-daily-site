18:T5c4,RLPF proceeds in four stages. First, parameters are grouped by semantic role rather than matched tensor by tensor. The groups include patch embedding, sequence mixing, feed-forward blocks, and normalization. This is necessary because attention projections and SSM transition parameters are neither shape-compatible nor functionally identical. Second, each group is projected into a shared low-rank latent space. The geometric choice is treated as a design hyperparameter, not as evidence that raw weights intrinsically live on a manifold. Third, fusion is performed in that latent space with component-dependent geometry: some groups use Euclidean averaging, while others use a Lorentz-model hyperbolic barycenter, implemented as a Fréchet mean under squared geodesic distance. The paper also adds a geodesic anchor objective to regularize the fused point relative to branch-specific representations and reduce instability in curved space. Fourth, the fused latent codes are decoded back into a ViT branch and an SSM branch, and a small learned gate combines their logits. This is the key conceptual point: the method does not collapse two models into one unified parameterization. It uses geometry-aware initialization plus lightweight training to build a retained two-branch hybrid. Compared with model soups, task arithmetic, or TIES, the novelty is less about the averaging rule itself and more about defining a role-based cross-architecture correspondence before fusion.19:T449,The evaluation covers CIFAR-10, Oxford-IIIT Pet, and ImageNet-1K. The most useful aspect of the experimental design is that it separates several effects that are often conflated: ImageNet results before fine-tuning are reported separately from final fine-tuned gated-hybrid performance; Euclidean versus Lorentz fusion is compared; and the contribution of parameter grouping and gating is ablated. The qualitative takeaways are: (1) geometry-aware fusion provides a usable initialization, but most of the final gain appears to come from retaining both branches and fine-tuning them; (2) different parameter groups prefer different geometries, and the paper does not claim hyperbolic fusion is universally better; (3) the learned gate is important for stable combination of branch outputs. The provided text does not include the full result tables, so I cannot reliably state exact parameter counts, latent ranks, or top-1 deltas.
