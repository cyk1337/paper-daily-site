关键改动在于训练方式。传统 looped LM 会把 recurrent block 展开 T 步，然后做 BPTT，训练内存和计算都随 T 增长。Attractor Model 则把 refinement 写成 z*=f(z*; x, h0) 这样的 fixed-point equation，其中 h0 是 backbone 的初始提议。前向时用求解器迭代到收敛，反向时不对所有中间步做显式回传，而是用 implicit differentiation 计算梯度，因此训练显存不再线性依赖迭代步数。这一点直接借鉴 DEQ，但论文的设计不是把整层网络都做成 equilibrium，而是只在输出 embedding 空间上求平衡，降低了优化难度，也保留了标准 Transformer backbone 的表达和并行优势。

A non-recurrent backbone, implemented as a Transformer, first proposes an initial output embedding for each token position. This is a fast latent guess for the next-token representation. A separate attractor module, typically smaller and recurrent, then refines that embedding by iterating toward a fixed point. The final token distribution is decoded from the equilibrium embedding rather than from the backbone proposal alone.

The key change is in how the recurrent part is trained. Standard looped language models explicitly unroll the recurrent block for T steps and backpropagate through all steps, which makes memory and optimization cost scale with T. Attractor Models instead formulate refinement as a fixed-point equation, roughly of the form z* = f(z*; x, h0), where h0 is the backbone proposal. The forward pass runs a solver until convergence, and the backward pass uses implicit differentiation rather than storing and differentiating through every intermediate iterate. This is the main source of constant-memory behavior with respect to refinement depth.

A second important design choice is the two-stage decomposition. The backbone is responsible for producing a good initialization, while the attractor performs local correction. This is more stable and cheaper than asking a recurrent module to do all the work from scratch. The model also supports adaptive refinement naturally: different tokens can require different numbers of solver iterations at train and test time, depending on convergence. That is a cleaner route to variable test-time compute than fixed-depth looped Transformers.

The paper also identifies equilibrium internalization. During training, the backbone’s initial prediction moves closer to the attractor’s fixed point, so the solver needs fewer iterations to reach approximate equilibrium. This happens without any explicit distillation loss. In the reasoning regime, the model outperforms prior tiny recursive reasoners on difficult tasks and appears to mitigate the pathological scaling behavior sometimes seen in small recurrent models, where increasing model size can hurt performance.
