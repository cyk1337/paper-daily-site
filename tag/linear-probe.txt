18:T5a4,The paper formalizes a strong hypothesis and derives asymmetric tests.
- Definition (linear world model): after whitening latents z, features satisfy f(x) = W z~ + ε, where ε is an orthogonal/nonlinear residual; a scalar σ^2 captures residual-to-latent-aligned variance. The key strengthening over standard probing is that latents should explain a dominant share of representation variance.
- Theory motivation: random-features / kernel theory implies that with sufficiently rich feature maps (even random ones), many smooth targets become linearly decodable. Therefore forward R^2 alone is weak evidence.
- Diagnostics to separate regimes:
  1) Reverse predictivity: measure how well f can be linearly predicted from z (variance explained). Kernel-like maps can have strong z←f but weak f←z.
  2) Spectral concentration: check whether latent-aligned signal concentrates in top principal components; a linear world model should show stronger concentration.
  3) OOD generalization: evaluate whether these relationships persist across distribution shifts (e.g., style changes).
- A calibration “ceiling” is introduced so reverse predictivity is comparable across models/settings.
Validation proceeds in two stages: a fully observed simulation (2D latents lifted by frozen random Fourier features into random vs trained MLP features) and a controlled real-data testbed (SVG-World) with shared latent layout across two rendering styles.19:T57b,The probing study spans six models, including the reported 753B-parameter GLM-5.2 and models from the Qwen, GPT-oss, and Gemma families. Linear probes achieve over 90% AUROC on unseen attacks, unseen agent instructions, and unseen task suites, and remain robust under adaptive and cross-lingual settings. The defense study evaluates five models on the eight hardest IPI grid points from held-out banking and travel suites, plus clean tasks from four suites. For each model-method pair, three trajectories are sampled at temperature 1.0, yielding 3,408 injected trials and 291 clean runs. Metrics are attack success rate and clean-task utility. Without intervention, ASR is 47.2 for Qwen3-8B, 43.2 for Qwen3.5-9B, 34.6 for Qwen3.5-27B, 44.0 for GPT-oss-20B, and 6.9 for Gemma-4-31B. Probe-gated AGRI reduces these to 2.9, 3.0, 0.0, 7.0, and 0.0, respectively, with clean utilities of 69.1, 88.2, 89.7, 66.8, and 91.8. Compared with always-on AGRI, probe gating usually preserves similar attack mitigation while improving utility; for example, Qwen3-8B utility rises from 54.3 to 69.1, and Qwen3.5-9B from 82.6 to 88.2. Tool-result reminders are generally stronger than generic prompting, while SAFEPATH tends to provide weaker mitigation and often hurts clean utility. The paper also reports that a no_failure classification at the IPI-exposed assistant turn predicts a secure trace with 92.9% precision.1a:T622,The method has three parts. First, trajectory reconstruction. The model is treated as a flow-matching or ODE-based generator with a learned velocity field. Given a real video encoded into the pretrained VAE latent space, the authors integrate the velocity field backward from the clean latent toward noise, producing an approximate reverse trajectory. This allows them to rerun the backbone at each time step and collect block-level hidden states and attention patterns. The appendix discusses approximation error and the conditions under which the recovered trajectory is a faithful proxy. Second, linear probing. They train lightweight probes on intermediate transformer states to predict physical plausibility and some quantitative physical variables. The point is not to maximize probe capacity, but to localize where and when physical information becomes linearly accessible. Third, causal intervention. They perform block-level noise interventions and use a probe-surprise style analysis to identify which components carry or amplify the physical signal. This goes beyond standard probing: it asks not only whether the information is readable, but also whether perturbing specific computations changes the signal and downstream generation. Compared with predictive representation learners such as V-JEPA, the model here is not trained with an explicit future-prediction objective. Compared with standard diffusion interpretability work, the analysis is anchored on real videos embedded back into the model’s trajectory rather than on arbitrary generated samples.1b:T56d,The experiments are organized around a few core phenomena rather than a broad benchmark sweep. First, on real videos with known physical plausibility labels, the recovered trajectories show that physical plausibility is linearly decodable from intermediate diffusion-transformer states. The signal is not uniform across the network: it tends to peak at certain depths and noise levels, often in intermediate blocks rather than at the input latent or final layer. Second, the paper reports that some quantitative physical variables are also decodable, which suggests the model is not merely storing a coarse plausible-versus-implausible cue. Third, the strength of the signal depends on trajectory fidelity: when the reconstructed path better follows the model’s learned continuous dynamics, probe performance improves, supporting the claim that physical structure emerges from denoising dynamics.
