17:T4ac,The core result is algebraic. For LayerNorm, mean subtraction introduces a projector structure; when combined with the affine scale γ, the inverse-scale direction γ^{-1}/||γ^{-1}|| becomes an exact kernel direction of the post-final-norm centered activation covariance, for any input distribution. In other words, the direction does not need to be estimated from data; it is determined analytically by the LayerNorm parameters. The paper then uses a KFAC-style Fisher factorization to map this residual-stream kernel direction into a corresponding dead direction in parameter space. A key contrast is provided by RMSNorm: because RMSNorm lacks the mean-subtraction projector, there is no analogous universal direction. This LN-versus-RMSNorm split is important because it turns the phenomenon into an architectural prediction rather than an empirical coincidence. To measure how training deepens degeneracy beyond the algebraic baseline, the authors define the Schur ratio R(h)=lambda_min(A_l)/(A_l)_{u,u} and its complement Δγ=1-R(h), where u is the predicted inverse-scale direction. This lets them distinguish the architectural baseline from additional training-induced spectral collapse.18:T47b,The empirical study covers 14 pretrained transformers: 9 with LayerNorm and 5 with RMSNorm. On all 9 LayerNorm models, at random initialization, the predicted direction v*=γ^{-1}/||γ^{-1}|| aligns with the measured bottom singular direction of the post-final-norm centered activation covariance with |cos|≥0.9999, and mean alignment above 0.99999. That is an extremely tight match. On all 5 RMSNorm models, the same alignment stays near the random-direction null, with centered-protocol |cos|≤0.10, matching the theoretical prediction that no universal direction should exist there. The paper also studies a Pythia LayerNorm size sweep and compares random initialization to trained checkpoints using Δγ. At random init, Δγ is about 0.37 for h=512 and close to zero for larger hidden sizes; after training, Δγ is near 1 across sizes, which the authors interpret as training deepening the spectral bottom along the same coordinate by roughly three orders of magnitude.
