18:T407,F²DR evaluates DeepSearch along three axes. The Content axis checks whether retrieved information covers key knowledge points and is factually grounded. The Trajectory axis evaluates the search process, including intent understanding, search atomicity, planning consistency, gap resolution, planning comprehensiveness, and iterative novelty, while penalizing redundant reasoning and plan-execution mismatch. The Answer axis combines subjective criteria such as intent match, coherence, and conciseness with objective coverage of core information. For benchmark construction, the authors sample complex queries from an enterprise search platform, extract requests that invoke deep thinking, and use DeepSeek-V3 to remove trivial or single-hop cases. They then run a full DeepSearch pipeline with DAG-based planning and reflection, search engines and MCP tools for evidence acquisition, and cross-turn evidence aggregation for final response generation. Preference pairs are filtered for discriminability and then checked by humans.19:T5b7,The work has two layers. The first is SimLife, a simulation platform built on The Sims 4 for generating long-horizon household activity streams with rich visual observations, synchronized logs, and synthetic dialogue with audio. A simulated day runs from 6 AM to 10 PM in-game and corresponds to about 24 minutes of real-time footage. The authors sample 466 in-game days as building blocks.

The second layer is SimLife-BP, a benchmark centered on latent behavioral patterns. Each episode is constructed by filtering and linking simulated days so that a target rule emerges over time, for example a character drinking coffee on weekdays or after staying up late. Evidence is intentionally distributed across days, events, and modalities, so the answer cannot be read off from a single moment.

The benchmark uses two design axes. One axis is question type: direct prediction, counterfactual prediction, noisy counterfactual prediction, and inverse counterfactual reasoning. The other axis is hint level: explicit relevant conditions, abstract condition types, or no hint at all. This structure is important because it separates genuine rule induction from shallow correlation matching. In particular, inverse reasoning and noisy counterfactuals are useful for exposing frequency-based shortcuts.

Compared with standard long-video QA or long-document QA, the benchmark is more explicitly about latent rule induction and rule updating under long temporal horizons.1a:T4ca,SimLife-BP contains 106 episodes, averaging 15.49 hours and 38.57 in-game days, with the longest episodes reaching 48.23 hours and 120 days. The benchmark includes 1,439 question-answer pairs. The paper evaluates multiple frontier models and architectures.

The main empirical findings are qualitative but important. First, models can achieve decent question-level accuracy without fully understanding the underlying rule. They often do better on forward prediction than on inverse reasoning, which suggests partial pattern matching rather than a robust internal rule representation. Second, models frequently fall back to simple heuristics such as frequency counting instead of condition-sensitive reasoning. This becomes visible on noisy counterfactuals and tasks requiring compositional conditions. Third, models struggle to update their beliefs when the latent pattern changes, indicating that a long context window alone does not provide reliable long-term state tracking.

For careful reading, the most informative results are not the overall averages but the breakdowns by question type, hint level, and possibly episode length.
