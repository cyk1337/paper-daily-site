18:T5df,The paper starts from a tightly controlled comparison: two 32-layer ViTs trained from scratch under identical settings, differing only in the attention operator—full attention versus Qwen-style chunk-window attention. Inspecting per-head attention maps, the authors observe that full-attention heads tend to specialize into semantic roles, with some focusing more on foreground objects and others on background regions. Chunk-window attention shows much weaker specialization and more uniform grid-like patterns. This phenomenon is named Semantic Head Specialization (SHS).

To quantify it, the paper introduces SHS-Index, an AUROC-based metric that measures how well a head separates foreground from background tokens. The metric is then evaluated on 16 open-source visual encoders and VLMs, where it cleanly separates full-attention and chunk-window families. The authors also show that changing only the LLM backbone size in Qwen2.5-VL barely changes SHS-Index, suggesting that the signal is driven by ViT attention design rather than language model scale.

The paper then analyzes three structural factors that affect SHS: window interaction, token serialization, and local softmax allocation. Based on these findings, it proposes Ariadne Attention, which combines sliding windows, per-head sink bias, and row-column alternating serialization. The design logic is diagnostic-driven: identify what full attention preserves internally, then build a cheaper hybrid that retains more of that property.19:T4ea,Glide is a guided, layerwise non-uniform hybrid attention scheme combining sliding-window softmax attention with a linear recurrent aggregation path.

Motivation via measurement:
- They sweep softmax-to-linear ratios across layers and observe strong depth dependence: early layers suffer large accuracy drops when linearized; deeper layers tolerate linearization.

Mechanism:
- Partition layers into early / middle / late stages.
- Early: full softmax (δ = 0).
- Late: full linearization (δ = w).
- Middle: partial linearization parameterized as δ = α·w with a single scalar α ∈ [0, 1].

Key design choice:
- Reduce a high-dimensional per-layer configuration problem to a 1D knob (α), making the search/tuning and deployment much easier.
- Designed to integrate with existing retention-based hybrid architectures (e.g., Liger, LoLCats), rather than requiring a fully new model from scratch.

Difference vs prior work:
- Uniform hybrid methods apply the same policy across layers; Glide allocates softmax budget where it matters (early layers) and linearizes where redundancy is higher (late layers).
