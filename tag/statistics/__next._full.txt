核心理论结果是两条 certification law。若设计要求每个 task 至少完成一条路径，也就是 task coverage 不能缺失，则最优最坏情形区间宽度阶为 Θ([M(t+1)]^{-1/2})；若允许省掉部分 task，把预算集中到少数任务的更多重复上，则最优阶变为 Θ([M(t+√M)]^{-1/2})。这里 t 可以理解为在覆盖每个 task 之后额外保留出来用于 replication/certification 的预算单位。作者强调 lower bound 覆盖了自适应 hard-budget policy，也就是说即便允许根据已观察结果动态调度预算，数量级上也不能突破这个界。

为了让理论可执行，论文给出固定随机子集设计和 disagreement-based certificate。直觉上，若同一 task 内计划标签高度一致，一次观测就足以把均值估得很准；但要证明这种一致性，需要额外抽样去测 disagreement。于是区间构造不只依赖 sampled mean，也显式依赖 disagreement count，形成所谓 Joint interval。这个设计比单纯把所有已观测路径当成 pooled Bernoulli 做统一推断更贴近“先覆盖任务、再花预算做认证”的实际评测流程。

论文还区分了两个目标：一个是对固定 benchmark grid 的诚实区间，另一个是对未来任务或未来运行的 population generalization。前者不需要对任务和运行结果做独立同分布假设；若要桥接到总体均值，附录里再加 Hoeffding 项。这种区分是合理的，因为很多模型发布和训练 gating 场景，真正关心的是“这次固定评测表上的分数是否足够可信”，而不是统计学意义上的外推。19:T84b,The paper formalizes repeated evaluation on a fixed cohort. There are M tasks and L planned repetition paths per task, for N = ML total paths. Each path costs at most K model responses or full agent episodes. A path outcome is represented by a first decisive-event index T_ir, and the terminal label Y_ir indicates whether no decisive event occurred within K steps. The estimand is the empirical mean over this prespecified grid, denoted theta_C, and the requirement is fixed-cohort honest coverage: the interval must be valid for every realized cohort, without assuming IID outcomes across tasks or runs.

The main theoretical contribution is a pair of sharp certification laws. If the design requires at least one completed path per task, the optimal worst-case interval width scales as Theta([M(t+1)]^{-1/2}). If omission is allowed, so some tasks may be skipped in order to spend more budget on replication elsewhere, the optimal rate becomes Theta([M(t+sqrt(M))]^{-1/2}). Here t is the extra reservation beyond the minimum needed for broad task coverage. The lower bounds apply even to adaptive hard-budget policies, so the result is not an artifact of simple non-adaptive sampling.

To make the theory operational, the paper proposes fixed random-subset designs together with disagreement-based certificates. The key idea is that when planned labels within a task agree, one observation can estimate the task contribution very well, but certifying that agreement requires extra sampling. The resulting Joint interval depends not only on the sampled mean but also on the observed disagreement count. This is more aligned with practical benchmark design than treating all observed paths as pooled Bernoulli samples.

An important conceptual distinction in the paper is between fixed-grid inference and population generalization. The primary target is the fixed benchmark grid itself, which supports honest evaluation without stochastic assumptions on task outcomes. Generalization to future tasks or runs is treated as a separate inferential objective, with an additional Hoeffding-style term discussed in the appendix.1a:T532,The empirical section uses an equal-budget LiveCodeBench replay. Under the same 1100-output budget, with M = 880, t = 220, and K = 1, the paper compares the proposed Joint interval against exact pooled uniform inference. Joint produces narrower mean intervals in 15 out of 16 model panels, with a median interval-width reduction of 30.6% and a median MSE reduction of 87.0%. This supports the claim that explicitly tracking disagreement is useful in the regime where within-task agreement is common but still needs certification.

The theoretical rates are also central empirical takeaways. With mandatory task coverage, the optimal width is Theta([M(t+1)]^{-1/2}); with omission, it is Theta([M(t+sqrt(M))]^{-1/2}). The paper highlights that at zero extra reservation, the omission-enabled regime changes the attainable order from M^{-1/2} to M^{-3/4}. That is a rate-level difference, not just a constant-factor effect, and it makes task coverage a first-order design choice.
