In the main setting, distractors come from the closest 0–10 percentile of layout distance, with a median nearest-distractor distance of 6.8 m; two matched variants use 31.4 m and 42.8 m distractors. Sixteen open and closed models are evaluated in a 4AFC setup with 25% chance accuracy. The main findings are straightforward. First, many models perform reasonably at the appearance gate (Delta=0), meaning they can identify a place despite appearance resampling, but performance collapses once the viewpoint rotates, often dropping to or below chance by 135 degrees. Second, scaling model size improves the appearance gate but does little for rotational invariance, so larger models recognize scenes better without forming a stable cognitive map. Third, increasing distractor distance sharply improves accuracy. The paper highlights a case where Gemini 3.8 Flash rises from 39% to 85% when distractors are moved about 31 m away. This suggests the models are not devoid of spatial information; their spatial representation is simply too coarse to resolve nearby layouts.a:T641,The core idea is a spatial compiler that turns rendered visual experience into dense, linked supervision. Starting from static 3D scenes and camera trajectories, the system renders RGB observation sequences. During data construction, it also has access to privileged information such as camera poses, scene geometry, and object identities. These are never exposed to the model at inference time, but they are used to compile supervision.

The supervision is organized into three connected capabilities within the same episode. First, self-motion tracking asks the model to infer how the observer moved between observations. The important point is that motion is not treated as an isolated target; it is the mechanism that should drive state updates. Second, persistent object mapping requires the model to maintain object locations and relations as the viewpoint changes, including when objects become occluded or leave the field of view. Third, operations over state ask downstream spatial questions on top of the updated internal state.

A second important design choice is motif-driven trajectory rendering. Instead of random camera paths, the authors define trajectory motifs that control how visibility, evidence, and viewpoint changes unfold over time. This lets them systematically generate episodes that require memory, re-localization, reference-frame handling, and state maintenance. The resulting questions are grouped into shared episodes so that motion estimation, state update, and reasoning are learned as connected parts of one experience rather than as unrelated instruction examples.b:T569,The paper builds Uruqi-600k and trains URUQI Syn-8B. Second, the gains transfer to external spatial benchmarks without using training data from those targets, suggesting that the model learns a more general spatial update mechanism rather than benchmark-specific patterns. Third, combining Uruqi supervision with only 100K examples from SenseNova-SI-8M further improves SenseNova-SI-1.5, which indicates complementarity with existing spatial instruction tuning.

Instead of only measuring final QA accuracy, the paper directly evaluates self-motion estimation and maintenance of previously observed object locations over time. That matters because language priors can hide weak spatial representations on end-task QA, while trajectory-level consistency exposes whether the model is actually updating a latent map.
