18:T7e1,作者选取两个 diffusion LM：DiffusionGemma-26B-A4B-it，一个 26B 的 MoE 模型，采用 uniform diffusion、256-token block 生成；DreamReasoner-8B，一个 8B dense masked diffusion 模型，32-token block 生成。两者都属于 semi-autoregressive block diffusion，并通过置信度阈值动态提交 token，因此步数不是固定的。论文用一个统一指标衡量解码 effort：每 100 个输出 token 需要多少次 forward pass。这个定义比常见的 tokens per forward 更适合做跨模型、跨 block size 比较，因为它按真实生成 token 数归一化，而不是按 block 容量。

任务设计是全文的关键。作者把任务按输出约束程度分成三类。Reproducing 类，如 grammar correction 和 extractive summarization，答案大部分来自输入，模型主要做复制和局部修正。Chained 类，如 code editing、code generation、competition math，后续 token 受前面生成内容强约束。Open-ended 类，如 WildBench v2 用户查询和 story generation，输出空间更大，约束更弱。除此之外，还加入 translation、clinical note generation、abstractive chapter summary 这类 rewriting 任务，用来区分“依赖输入信息”与“直接复制输入”之间的差别。

分析上，作者不是只报任务均值，而是进一步把成本与两个输出属性关联起来：一是输出相对输入有多少新内容，二是输出词在该任务分布里有多稀有。直觉上，越接近复制、越高频、越可预测的内容，越容易在较少 refinement 步中被 commit；越开放、越低频、越需要真正选择的内容，越需要更多步数。文中还把单个回答拆成 reasoning trace 和 final answer，考察成本在回答内部如何分布；并分析数学题中正确答案与错误答案的 effort 差异。最后，作者把 effort 用作推理时的额外信号：在多样本解题投票时，用“解得更快”的答案赋予更高权重，而不是简单多数票。19:T762,The study evaluates two diffusion LMs: DiffusionGemma-26B-A4B-it, a 26B MoE model with uniform diffusion and 256-token blocks, and DreamReasoner-8B, an 8B dense masked-diffusion model with 32-token blocks. Both use semi-autoregressive block diffusion and adaptive token commitment based on confidence thresholds, so the number of denoising steps is input- and output-dependent. The core metric is forward passes per 100 generated tokens. This choice is important: it normalizes by actual generated tokens up to EOS rather than by block capacity, making comparisons across models and block sizes more meaningful.

The task suite is structured around how constrained the output is. Reproducing tasks, such as grammar correction and extractive summarization, mostly copy from the input with light edits. Chained tasks, such as code editing, code generation, and competition math, have strong dependence on earlier generated content. Open-ended tasks, such as WildBench v2 queries and story generation, leave much more of the output underdetermined. The paper also adds rewriting tasks like translation, clinical note generation, and abstractive summarization to separate 'uses input information' from 'mostly copies input'.

The analysis then relates decoding effort to two output properties: novelty relative to the input and lexical rarity within the task. The hypothesis is straightforward and well supported by the experiments: text that is more constrained, more repetitive, or more frequent can be committed earlier, while text that is more novel or lower-frequency requires more refinement steps. The paper also decomposes responses into reasoning traces and final answers, and tests whether effort correlates with correctness in math. Finally, it uses effort as an inference-time signal by weighting votes from faster-decoded answers more heavily in self-consistency-style aggregation.1a:T6fd,The empirical pattern is consistent across 10 tasks. First, task type strongly affects cost. Input-constrained and lightly edited outputs are much cheaper than open-ended generation. A representative number from the paper is that, in DiffusionGemma, story writing requires more than 3x as many forward passes per token as grammar correction. This shows that diffusion speedups are not a fixed property of the model; they depend heavily on task structure. Second, rewriting tasks typically sit between pure reproduction and open-ended generation, which supports the paper’s interpretation that the key variable is not whether there is an input, but how much of the output remains genuinely undecided.

Third, novelty and rarity explain a substantial share of the variation. Outputs that introduce more new content relative to the input, or use rarer words within the task, require more passes per token. This relationship appears not only across tasks but also within a single response. Fourth, in math, incorrect solutions are more expensive than correct ones even after controlling for problem difficulty and output length, suggesting that effort is not just a length proxy. Fifth, when responses are split into reasoning traces and final answers, final answers are often cheaper, especially when they mostly restate information already established in the reasoning trace. Sixth, multilingual experiments show a compounded efficiency penalty: some languages require more tokens and also more diffusion passes per token, creating a double multilingual tax. Seventh, effort is useful operationally: in math answer aggregation, weighting votes by decoding speed improves accuracy over plain majority vote by 4.2 points with two samples and 2.3 points with five, at no extra inference cost.1b:T404,Start with the setup and metric definition. The choice of forward passes per 100 output tokens is central, and the paper explains why it is preferable to raw tokens-per-forward or block-capacity normalization. Then read the main task-level results to get the overall shape of cost variation. The conceptual starting point is a residual decomposition: the non-thinking response reflects the model’s direct-answer tendency, while the thinking response adds a reasoning residual on top. That residual can be helpful, recovering the right fact, or harmful, introducing unsupported associations, entity confusion, or speculative drift.

MARGO modifies the rollout grouping used in group-based RL such as GRPO. Standard setups typically compare only thinking trajectories for the same question. That makes the direct-answer baseline invisible: if all thinking samples are wrong, or all are right, the training signal cannot tell whether explicit reasoning helped or hurt relative to the model’s own non-thinking behavior. MARGO instead forms mixed rollout groups containing both thinking and non-thinking trajectories. The non-thinking output becomes a same-model reference inside advantage estimation.

This changes the optimization target in a meaningful way. Thinking trajectories that corrupt an already correct direct answer receive lower relative advantage, while trajectories that genuinely repair an incorrect direct answer are reinforced. The novelty is not a complicated RL algorithm, but the choice of reference signal. Rather than relying on an external teacher or a separate verifier, the method uses mode contrast within the same model to expose harmful reasoning residuals. Instead of assigning a single scalar reward to the full rollout, the method splits generation into a reasoning segment and a final answer segment, and scores them separately. The reasoning segment is rewarded for exploring increasingly harmful or unsafe interpretations. The answer segment is rewarded for producing a safe final response conditioned on that preceding unsafe exploration. The intended behavior is not globally “safe-looking” reasoning, but controlled exploration followed by safe recovery.

Several design choices matter. First, the paper argues that optimization mismatch between the competing objectives leads to unstable training. If the exploration reward and the safe-answer reward are not balanced in how they are sampled or optimized, the model can collapse to one side: either unsafe exploration without recovery, or conservative refusal without meaningful exploration. Second, the authors show that dense outcome-level rewards are still too coarse for this setting. They do not provide enough credit assignment to tell the model which part of the trajectory should be exploratory and which part should be controlled. This motivates process-based rewards at the segment level on sampled rollouts. Third, the method keeps exploration and recovery within one continuous chain of thought, rather than outsourcing safety to a separate classifier or a detached control module. That is an important distinction: the goal is to train safety as a reasoning dynamic, not as a front-end filter.

Relative to prior safety-RL methods such as TARS, the difference is not merely a modified reward function. The underlying training assumption changes. Earlier work largely treats reasoning as a tool for deciding whether to refuse.
