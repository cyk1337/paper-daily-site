a:T638,Two pillars:

(1) SOC/HJB formulation and diagnosis. Generation is modeled as density transport on a manifold governed by an SDE with a learnable control law u(z,t). The optimal policy is characterized by the gradient of the HJB value function, which is inherently closed-loop (state-dependent) and favors low-energy, geodesic-like trajectories. AR generation is interpreted as a near-zero-noise, impulsive/open-loop limit, which helps explain irreversibility and error propagation. Diffusion-style methods are discussed as lacking strong global feedback and suffering from geometric ill-conditioning when performed directly in token embedding space.

(2) Manta-LM: manifold rectification + closed-loop control learning.
- Manifold rectification: a regularized VAE maps discrete tokens into a compact, locally Euclidean latent manifold to reduce curvature/stiffness and quantization issues.
- Closed-loop controller via flow matching: instead of solving the HJB PDE directly, the method uses flow matching to learn a time-conditioned vector field in the rectified latent control space, intended to approximate the optimal feedback controller. The mentioned Global Integral Operator appears to be an architectural component to inject global coupling (integral-style interactions) so the learned field can provide global corrective directions.

2) SMP-derived objective: starting from SMP, introduce the Hamiltonian and adjoint processes, and derive a Hamiltonian Basic Adjoint Matching (BAM) objective (Theorem 1). Main claim: the expected BAM objective has the same first variation as the original SOC objective, so critical points satisfy SMP/HJB stationarity.
3) Generalization: BAM handles control-dependent drift and diffusion and convex running costs; unbiased estimation involves first- and second-order adjoint equations to estimate the cost-to-go gradient and Hessian.
4) Lean AM (Theorem 2): when diffusion depends only on time (a common practical case), BAM reduces to a lean first-order AM loss that avoids second-order terms and recovers the original AM objective as a special case.
5) Optimization interpretation (Theorem 4): the gradient flow of the expected lean AM loss coincides with a continuous-time method of successive approximations (MSA) derived from SMP, while bypassing the intractable martingale term in classical SMP schemes. This certifies gradient steps on the AM loss as valid control-improvement steps.
