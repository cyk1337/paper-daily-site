17:T706,For each layer, each key head, and each token position, a lightweight two-layer MLP predicts a utility score u in (0,1) from the hidden state. During inference, the most recent w tokens are always accessible through a causal local window. Older tokens are available to global attention only if their predicted utility exceeds a threshold τ, meaning only those key-value pairs are written into the persistent cache. This is write-time sparsification, not query-aware retrieval at read time.

Hard thresholding would block gradients, so training uses a soft relaxation: instead of binary gating, the model adds log u to the attention mask bias. When u=1, the bias is zero; when u approaches 0, the position is effectively masked out. The language model and the utility predictor are trained jointly end-to-end with the ordinary next-token loss, typically via continual pretraining from a pretrained full-attention checkpoint rather than training from scratch.

Relative to prior work, the distinctions are clear. Methods such as H2O or KVZap prune or evict cache entries after they have already been written; SP-KV decides whether to write them into long-term memory at all. Query-aware sparse attention methods reduce reads but still retain the full cache in memory; SP-KV reduces the persistent cache itself. And unlike frozen-model pruning policies calibrated on auxiliary data, SP-KV co-trains the pruning mechanism with the backbone, reducing distribution mismatch under stronger compression.

The paper also uses the learned utility as an architectural signal. After training SP-KV on a reference model, heads with high average utility are kept global while the rest are converted to local heads, yielding hybrid transformers that outperform standard interleaved layouts at the same KV budget.18:T4bf,From the provided full text, the experimental story has three main parts. Second, the paper compares against post-hoc eviction methods and argues that the gap widens as compression becomes more aggressive and as the input distribution shifts, which is exactly where train-test mismatch hurts frozen pruning schemes.
