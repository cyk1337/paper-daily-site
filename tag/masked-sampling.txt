18:T4dd,The authors define an exact conditional-marginal oracle model to isolate parallelization error from model approximation error. A deterministic adaptive policy chooses the next reveal set from the observed partial assignment. The selected variables are then sampled independently using their true conditional marginals rather than from their true joint conditional. Approximation error is measured by forward KL, which has a chain-rule decomposition aligned with the reveal process and corresponds to excess expected log loss. Serial depth is the minimum target-averaged number of rounds needed to meet a KL budget. The central proof aligns each round's KL contribution with the conditional total correlation of the variables revealed in that round. The identity applies to random and history-dependent reveal sets. The paper then turns the identity into upper and lower bounds for structured families: logarithmic exact sampling for Bernoulli walks via hierarchical bisection, logarithmic-in-length zero-error schedules for finite-order Markov chains with a factor depending on Markov order, linear expected depth for uniform random permutations at fixed error, and several binary-alphabet constructions with polylogarithmic to polynomial depth.19:T53b,The theoretical results give the main evidence. For uniform random permutations, every fixed-error policy needs a linear number of expected rounds; under a hard round cap, the optimal error reduces exactly to an integer-composition problem, yielding fixed-round asymptotics, a finite-blocklength pairs-first phase, and a joint scaling frontier.
