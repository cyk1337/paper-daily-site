The theoretical contribution is the uniform-spreading theorem. The authors frame target recovery as a sequence of local argmax decisions. At each step, success depends not only on the target probability q_t itself, but on whether it wins against local competitors. Holding the total budget P=∏t q_t fixed, recoverability is uniquely maximized when confidence is spread uniformly across steps, q_t=P^(1/n). The structural penalty for uneven allocation is captured by Var(log q_t). This yields two complementary diagnostics: logP measures total confidence budget, while Var(log q_t) measures how efficiently a reveal order distributes that budget.

For generation behavior, the paper compares one-token-per-step strategies.
