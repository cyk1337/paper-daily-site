9:T764,The setup distinguishes the as-of date t0 from the model training cutoff T. Text written after t0 but before T is bridge data. It can contain information caused by the later world state that also determines the final outcome Y. A model trained on this bridge data may obtain outcome information without ever seeing the benchmark item itself. The paper formalizes this as a back-door leakage path from the later world state through bridge data into the model prediction.

The first technical result is a non-identifiability theorem. A passive backtest observes forecasts, outcomes, timing relative to the cutoff, and possibly covariates, but it cannot separate recency from leakage. The identified set for leakage inflation is a sharp interval, and collecting more items does not make it collapse. A flat pre/post profile is therefore not proof of cleanliness, and a non-flat profile is not proof of leakage.

The second part derives a per-question leakage law. Inflation is the product of honest uncertainty and an extraction factor. The extraction factor has a strong nonlinear payoff: extracting half of the leaked signal already yields about three quarters of the full inflation. This explains why weak recall can still move benchmark scores. The analysis predicts that leakage concentrates on questions that surprised the crowd and had dense training coverage.

The measurement strategy requires an external reference. With a known cutoff, the paper uses a regression-discontinuity design to identify cutoff-localized leakage. With a matched clean control, it uses a difference-in-differences design to estimate global leakage and report a leakage-adjusted score. Matching is central: naive pre/post comparisons are replaced by comparisons that control for timing, question characteristics, and baseline difficulty. Paraphrase probes are treated as detection tools, not measurement tools.a:T4ee,The empirical section starts with ForecastBench. The authors evaluate five flagship models on the official panel, restricted to questions that resolved after each model’s documented training cutoff. These outcomes could not have been memorized under the documented cutoffs. Even so, four of the five models fail the standard pre/post check, with spurious leakage gaps as large as +0.061. A matched-window check collapses the gaps across all five models, supporting the interpretation that the apparent signal is recency rather than leakage.

The estimators are validated using twin models with planted leakage. One model is kept clean, while the other receives controlled leakage. The proposed estimators recover the planted dose, location, and per-question profile, and return a null result on the clean twin. On deployed frontier systems, the estimators identify one cutoff-localized signature and clear five models whose apparent advantages were explained by recency at the design’s power floors.

The most important reported numbers are: five flagship models; four false failures under the standard check on post-cutoff-resolved questions; a maximum spurious gap of +0.061; and successful recovery of planted leakage in controlled twin-model experiments.b:T463,HIPPO (Hint-Injected Pairwise Policy Optimization) explicitly instantiates a contaminated/shortcut policy and penalizes it.
- Formulation: denote the current policy as πθ and a hypothetical shortcut-reliant policy as πC. Directly maximizing divergence (KL/TV) between them is intractable/unbounded, so the paper derives a bounded surrogate objective (via Pinsker/TV duality) that reduces to reward expectations over samples from both behaviors.
- Instantiating πC on-policy: use answer-level hint injection at the input level to simulate the information asymmetry of overlap (knowing the answer before reasoning), producing shortcut-like trajectories without accessing internal parametric memory.
- Reward/judging: use a Bradley–Terry pairwise comparator to prefer standard rollouts over hint-injected ones, avoiding issues common in pointwise reward models (miscalibration, intransitivity) and enabling lightweight judges.
- Training compatibility: plugs into group-based RL pipelines (e.g., GRPO/DAPO/PPO-style sampling and advantage standardization), replacing the scalar reward with anchored pairwise aggregation.c:T7cc,TSFMAudit is built on a simple hypothesis: if an audited dataset was seen, or nearly seen, during pretraining, then a short probe fine-tuning run on that dataset should look unusually efficient. Efficiency here is not just low initial loss. It means faster loss reduction under a small update budget and smaller movement of the backbone parameters. Intuitively, contaminated data are already close to the model’s internal representation, so only limited adjustment is needed to fit them well.

The procedure has three stages. First, the candidate TSFM is subjected to a controlled probe fine-tuning run on the audited dataset, with a deliberately small training budget. The goal is not to optimize forecasting performance, but to expose comparable adaptation dynamics. Second, the method extracts probe-time signals such as loss reduction and parameter displacement, along with related dynamic features described in the paper. Third, these features are combined into a contamination-risk score for the model–dataset pair.

A notable design choice is that the audit target is defined at the dataset level rather than the sample level. This matches the practical structure of forecasting benchmarks, where many samples are windows derived from the same underlying source series. The paper formalizes contamination in terms of whether the source time series behind an audited sample appears in the pretraining corpus, and then defines a dataset-level contamination ratio.

Relative to LLM-style baselines, the method deliberately avoids two difficult dependencies. It does not rely on explicit overlap detection, because near-duplicates in time series are often transformed rather than literally repeated. It also avoids large-scale shadow-model calibration, which would be expensive for TSFMs. Probe dynamics are used because they capture both prior familiarity and local plasticity, making them better suited than static loss to distinguish contamination from inherently easy-to-forecast data.d:T4fb,The evaluation covers 6 TSFMs and uses their reported pretraining-corpus information together with GIFT-Eval and the TIME benchmark, for a total of 187 datasets. The goal is to create semantically different variants while preserving the original answer and underlying computation.

To do that, the authors use two complementary generation strategies. In the first, an LLM is prompted with the original question q and answer a to reverse-engineer a new question q~ that preserves the same numerical computation and final answer but allows substantial changes in entities, attributes, relations, and scenario. This route offers more semantic freedom but also more room for failure.

In the second, the model is asked to keep all numerical values fixed while changing the scenario or theme. This is a more constrained transformation that preserves the computational structure while still increasing semantic diversity. The two streams cover different points on the diversity–control tradeoff, and combining them reduces the failure modes of either one alone.

The pipeline then applies several filters. A rule-based verifier checks that the numbers in the generated question match those in the original, so the answer remains valid. Similarity filtering based on cosine similarity over term-frequency vectors removes candidates that are too close to the source sample, using a threshold of 0.85, and set-level deduplication prevents near-duplicate variants from being retained together. To calibrate the automated validator, the authors first had two human reviewers assess 70 random augmented samples for quality and logical coherence, then used those judgments to engineer an LLM-judge prompt for tuning the pipeline.

Methodologically, this is a benchmark-generation framework, not a new reasoning model. Its main technical value is making semantic benchmark variation reusable, stochastic, and answer-preserving without requiring full re-annotation each time.10:T534,The main empirical result is that 14 contemporary LLMs show consistent performance drops on GSM-SEM variants relative to the original benchmarks, and the failures become larger when semantic perturbations are combined with stronger variants such as GSM-Symbolic and GSM-Plus. The important point is not the absolute score of any single model, but the consistency of the degradation across model families. That suggests many high benchmark scores still rely partly on familiarity with the original benchmark distribution rather than robust semantic recomputation.

The paper also releases three human-validated benchmark sets: GSM8K-SEM, GSM-Symbolic-SEM, and GSM-Plus-SEM. In addition, the framework is applied to non-GSM samples from BigBenchHard, LogicBench, and NLR-BIRD, which supports the claim that the approach is not tied to one narrow dataset template.
