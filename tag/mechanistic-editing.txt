1b:T570,CIRM (Causal Intervention for Reward Models) performs neuron-level interventions during RM inference.
- Bias specification: define several measurable bias attributes f_b(x) for a query-response pair x (the paper studies five style-related biases, including length, paragraphing, exclamation marks, and lexical overlap).
- Neuron identification: for each bias b, compute correlations between intermediate neuron activations (typically MLP neurons across layers) and f_b(x) over a dataset; select a small set of “bias-specific neurons” with highest correlation.
- Inference-time intervention: clamp those neurons’ activations to their median values (per neuron) rather than zeroing them out. Median clamping is a distribution-preserving suppression that aims to remove bias signal while avoiding out-of-distribution activations that could harm general reward quality.
- Multi-bias handling: because neurons are selected per attribute, multiple bias interventions can be applied together.
- Mechanistic note: bias-specific neurons are reported to concentrate in early layers, implying the RM extracts format/style cues early and later mixes them with semantic signals.
