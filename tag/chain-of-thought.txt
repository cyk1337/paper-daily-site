18:T68f,The pipeline has three stages. First, CoT trajectory generation: for each question, a teacher LLM produces a zero-shot chain-of-thought trajectory. The key premise is that questions with different wording but similar logical structure become closer once their reasoning paths are made explicit. Second, contextualized trajectory embedding: the question and its generated reasoning trace are embedded into a dense space intended to reflect the latent reasoning manifold better than plain question embeddings. Third, isomorphic clustering and selection: clustering or geometric covering is performed in that space, and representative items near cluster centers are selected under a fixed budget.

A useful design choice is the decoupling between subset selection and score estimation. CoT-Core only constructs the subset. The selected coreset can be used with simple standard aggregation in a fully training-free setup, or combined with stronger estimators such as GP-IRT when historical logs are available. This is more flexible than IRT-style methods, which typically tie item calibration and score reconstruction together.

Compared with lexical embedding plus k-center selection, the novelty is not the sampler itself but the representation space: neighborhood structure is induced by generated reasoning traces rather than surface wording. Compared with IRT-based approaches, it avoids the cold-start dependency on historical correctness matrices. The trade-off is also clear: it is training-free, but not cost-free, because CoT trajectories must be generated for the benchmark upfront. The method assumes that this one-time cost is amortized over many future evaluation runs.19:T58c,The experimental section is organized around three questions: whether CoT-based representations are better than plain text embeddings for coreset construction, whether the selected subsets remain faithful in cold-start score estimation, and when the gains disappear.

The selected subsets better preserve full-benchmark score estimates and model ranking. The paper also states a boundary condition: when tasks are dominated by factual recall, template matching, or shallow judgments, the benefit of reasoning-aware pruning becomes smaller because the underlying reasoning manifold is weak to begin with.

Another useful contribution is the comparison between masked distillation and plain SFT on teacher rollouts: the training objective itself affects whether the student learns compressed internal computation or merely imitates surface trajectories.1b:T8dc,The core method is masked distillation. During training, the teacher operates in thinking mode and has access to the question q, the full reasoning trace r, and the answer a. The student only sees q and is trained to generate answer tokens without access to the intermediate reasoning tokens. The key point is that this is not simple hard-label SFT. The teacher, conditioned on q+r, provides token-level supervision over the student’s answer tokens. So the student is trained to match the teacher’s answer distribution under asymmetric conditioning: the teacher has the CoT, the student does not. This forces the student to compress information that was explicit in the teacher’s context into its own parameters.

The paper instantiates this in two settings. In self-distillation, the same model serves as both a thinking teacher and a non-thinking student, isolating whether explicit reasoning can be folded back into parameters within the same architecture family. In dual-model distillation, a reasoning model supervises a separate non-thinking student, which is closer to a practical deployment setup with a strong expensive teacher and a cheaper student.

A second important design is α-suffix masked distillation. Instead of treating reasoning as a binary choice, the teacher trace is split into a prefix and a suffix. The student is trained to internalize the first (1-α) portion and, at inference time, explicitly emits only the remaining α suffix together with the final answer. α=0 means direct-answer generation with no visible reasoning; α=1 approximates full explicit reasoning. This is a strong experimental design choice because it turns the role of reasoning tokens into a continuous variable and lets the paper trace out an explicit accuracy-cost frontier.

The paper also studies the objective itself. A key baseline is whether one can simply SFT on teacher rollouts and obtain similar benefits. The evidence argues that masked distillation is meaningfully different: the point is not to reproduce the teacher trajectory, but to align the student’s answer distribution when the student is missing the teacher’s intermediate context. That distinction matters if one cares about internalized computation rather than trajectory imitation.1c:T759,The experiments cover two domains: Math and Countdown, with explicit in-distribution and OOD evaluation. In Countdown, the paper introduces target-range and search-depth shifts to probe changes in combinatorial search difficulty. Both self-distillation and dual-model settings are evaluated. The scaffold parameter is swept over α∈{0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0}, so the main result is a curve rather than a single operating point.

Three findings stand out. First, full internalization is not universal.
