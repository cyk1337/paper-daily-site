Since similar egomotion directions induce recurring coherent motion patterns in the image plane, the authors run PCA over these displacement vectors and use the leading components as an egomotion basis. The first two component weights are used as low-dimensional action inputs. This avoids conditioning on dense flow or raw tracks at inference time, and it gives the action space an explicit affine structure: zero means no egomotion, scalar magnitude controls strength, sign controls direction, and axes can be combined. The world model is built by adapting a pretrained video DiT, specifically Wan2.1, under a conditional flow matching objective. The model learns a velocity field between noise latents and clean video latents, and the action is injected into the velocity network itself. This is different from applying a fixed transformation after generation; the action can shape the latent trajectory and the scene evolution. The paper also introduces an online latent egomotion critic that distills pixel-space motion into differentiable latent supervision, grounding action following without backpropagating through the video decoder or the tracker.19:T87f,The method has three main pieces. The first is a task-unified single-stream diffusion transformer. Text conditions, image conditions, and visual latent patches are projected into the same hidden space and concatenated into one token sequence. T2I, T2V, and I2V are all handled in one framework, with image generation treated as the special case of a one-frame video. To separate condition tokens from visual tokens without introducing task-specific branches, the model uses 3D MM-RoPE with non-overlapping temporal coordinate ranges. The motivation for the single-stream design is practical and clear: better parameter reuse, denser cross-modal interaction at every layer, larger and cleaner GEMMs, and less split/concat overhead than dual-stream architectures, especially under distributed sequence-parallel training.

The second piece is sparse MoE scaling. The provided text does not include the full routing details, but the intent is clear: MoE is used to scale spatiotemporal modeling capacity without paying dense-compute cost everywhere. This mirrors the logic of MoE in LLMs, but the video setting is harder because routing has to coexist with spatial, temporal, and multimodal structure. Stability, load balancing, and temporal consistency are likely more delicate here. Choosing a single-stream backbone before adding MoE is a sensible systems decision, because it avoids compounding communication and partitioning complexity that a dual-stream design would introduce.

The third piece is data and training signal design. The model is trained on a robot-augmented corpus that mixes internet-scale videos with robot manipulation, navigation, and egocentric data. Importantly, the paper frames this as a profiling, filtering, and rebalancing problem rather than naive concatenation. On top of that, the training objective includes a multi-dimensional reward system that extends beyond perceptual quality and text alignment to physical plausibility and task-level success.
