18:T5da,The paper has three layers of contribution. The first is the physical build: a 128-GPU cluster assembled from second-hand V100s plus used CPUs, motherboards, and memory. The reported total cost is about $32K at build time and around $22K at later market prices. The second is the serving system. Because a single V100 is far behind H100 or B200 in memory capacity, bandwidth, and interconnect, the authors implement a custom Rust-based LLM serving engine with device-level pipeline parallelism. The goal is to partition and stream a 70B-class model across many cheap low-memory devices. The text emphasizes near-linear TPS scaling with device count, suggesting that careful partitioning and scheduling can make old hardware viable for large-model inference.

The third is the economic and environmental analysis. The authors collect second-hand price trajectories for V100 and A100 GPUs and fit stretched exponential depreciation curves. For V100 they report a characteristic timescale of about 48.3 months; for A100 about 61.7 months. In both cases beta is greater than 1, indicating accelerating depreciation. They then combine acquisition cost, operational power, electricity price, and grid carbon intensity to determine when second-hand deployment is actually cheaper and when it is environmentally preferable. The important point is that used hardware is not automatically greener: high operating energy can erase the embodied-carbon advantage if electricity is expensive or carbon-intensive.19:T435,Three empirical facts matter most. First, the cluster is real and was run for a year, which makes the study more credible than a purely analytical estimate. Second, the price gap is dramatic: the 128×V100 cluster is reported at roughly $22K to $32K, while an 8×B200 system is around $600K. Batch-purchased V100 16GB cards can be as low as about $60 each, creating more than a 100x difference in per-GPU purchase price. Third, with the custom pipeline-parallel serving engine, the V100 cluster achieves competitive throughput for LLaMA-70B and scales linearly in TPS with device count. The excerpt provided does not include the full latency, throughput, and energy tables, but the main conclusion is already clear: the capital-cost advantage is enormous, inference viability is real, and the final economic and environmental outcome depends strongly on electricity conditions and system efficiency.
