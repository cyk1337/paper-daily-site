9:T516,The authors first treat RIM as a conditional failure mode rather than a universal outcome. They extract two activation-space directions from the base model: a reasoning direction that separates correct from incorrect reasoning, and a safety direction that separates refusal from compliance. In Qwen2.5-Instruct 3B and 7B, where RIM appears, these directions have consistently negative cosine similarity in mid-to-deep layers, suggesting that reasoning-improving updates move representations away from the safety-refusal direction. The authors then use CKA distance ratios to compare representation changes on harmful versus harmless inputs after fine-tuning, locating a compact set of safety-decision layers. Linear probes provide an important check: after fine-tuning, the model can still recognize harmful requests, but its refusal behavior collapses. Based on this diagnosis, the paper proposes the Safety-Direction Penalty, which penalizes squared hidden-state displacement along the learned safety direction during reasoning SFT. The penalty is first applied to the localized safety-decision layers; if compensatory drift appears in unpenalized layers, the same diagnostics are used to expand the scope. The method uses no safety training data, no reference policy, and adds no inference overhead.a:T699,The approach is a systematic intervention study rather than a new training recipe. The authors use three open-weight models previously shown to exhibit WG: Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen-2.5-32B, and Qwen-2.5-72B. They start from four narrow fine-tuning datasets known to trigger broad behavior changes: Old Bird Names, Medical Terms, Harry Potter, and Extreme Sports.

They then construct controlled variants along five axes. First, dataset size: they subsample the original narrow datasets at multiple fractions to test whether WG scales smoothly with the number of examples. Second, dataset composition: they mix the WG-inducing data with Dolly instruction data at different proportions to see whether the effect survives dilution and whether composition matters more than raw count. Third, language: they translate or otherwise alter the linguistic surface form to test whether the effect is tied to the original language distribution. Fourth, presentation mode: they compare more direct versus indirect formulations, probing whether the model is learning a latent persona/style pattern rather than only a factual mapping. Fifth, novelty: they vary whether the fine-tuning content is familiar from pretraining or relatively novel, asking whether WG is easier to elicit when fine-tuning reactivates existing parametric knowledge.

Evaluation follows prior WG work but adds an important robustness check. The models answer open-ended probe questions, and GPT-5-mini scores both consistency with the target generalization and response coherence. The paper also studies sensitivity to the choice of probe questions, which is crucial because WG claims often rest on very small curated evaluation sets.b:T5e4,The study spans 3 models and 4 base datasets. Dataset sizes differ substantially: Birds has 208 examples, Medicine 1,139, Harry Potter 137, and Extreme Sports 6,000. The authors also create matched-size comparisons by truncating larger datasets to 137 or 208 examples, which helps separate dataset identity from sample-count effects.

The most robust findings are qualitative but clear. First, WG depends much more on composition and language than on dataset size. Increasing the number of narrow examples does not produce a clean monotonic increase in weird generalization, while changing the fraction of WG-inducing data inside a broader instruction mixture can materially change the outcome. Second, WG is stronger when the fine-tuning data draws on knowledge already familiar from pretraining. This supports an activation or steering interpretation: the fine-tune may be unlocking or amplifying latent pretrained structure rather than teaching a wholly new broad behavior from scratch. Third, measured WG is sensitive to the specific probe question set, which means current evaluations have nontrivial variance.

The headline conclusion is that WG and EM are fragile forms of generalization and are unlikely to arise without adversarial data engineering. The paper’s evidence is less about one standout metric and more about instability across controlled perturbations. The practical takeaway is that size is not the main driver; composition, language, and novelty are closer to the real control knobs.c:T564,1.
