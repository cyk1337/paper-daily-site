18:T710,The work has two technical pieces. First, behavioral auditing. The authors define the Behavioral Necessity Rate (BNR): among instances answered correctly under the full context, remove one annotated evidence target at a time and measure how often the model can no longer recover the answer. This turns the vague notion of “using the intended hop” into an intervention-based behavioral test. To avoid conflating evidence necessity with generic sensitivity to context deletion, they introduce a matched-background control. This is important: a raw accuracy drop after deletion could simply reflect that the example became noisier or shorter in an unfavorable way. The control isolates whether the failure is specific to the claimed evidence.

Second, RealHop, a diagnose-construct-verify framework for rebuilding multi-hop questions so that every step is required. The construction uses three main mechanisms: entity rebinding to break parametric-memory shortcuts and familiar associations; relation splitting / evidence rewriting to force composition across multiple premises rather than allowing a single-step inference; and complete competing paths to alternative answers that look structurally plausible but fail on a required relation, reducing shallow elimination strategies. The authors also separate construction from verification: they freeze the dataset after structural and semantic checks, then run post hoc deletion-based auditing. This avoids overfitting the benchmark to a particular model during creation. For datasets without native hop annotations, such as FRAMES, they reconstruct intervention-ready support units from source-linked evidence. For LongBench v2, they adapt long-context questions into auditable multiple-choice and repeated open-ended protocols to reduce evaluation noise.19:T564,The central empirical finding is that standard multi-hop benchmarks have surprisingly low behavioral necessity. Across MuSiQue, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA, panel-mean BNR is only 16.6% to 27.4%. Even adversarially designed benchmarks intended to reduce shortcuts only reach 43.5% to 48.9%. This means that annotated chains and distractor-heavy construction do not guarantee that correct predictions depend on the intended evidence.

On rebuilt benchmarks, the picture changes sharply. On 790 paired MuSiQue questions, RealHop-MuSiQue raises panel-mean BNR from 27.4% to 94.4%, while full-context accuracy remains in the 68.5% to 83.5% range. That is an important result: the benchmark becomes much more diagnostic without collapsing answerability. On source-linked FRAMES questions, RealHop-FRAMES reaches 88.9% to 89.9% BNR despite the lack of native hop annotations. On 216 long-context questions derived from LongBench v2, the matched multiple-choice protocol expands the accuracy spread across 16 models from 13.9 points to 59.2 points. The wider separation persists under repeated open-ended evaluation, and targeted deletion yields 96.5% to 98.1% BNR for the three audited models.
