9:T566,The contribution is a benchmark and annotation protocol rather than a new model. VidOmni-Bench contains 500 videos spanning five predefined complexity types: Static World, Body Motion, Hand-Object Interaction, Crowd Action, and Multi-Scene Dynamics. These are balanced across three duration buckets, from very short clips to videos up to 90 minutes. That design is important because complexity and duration are treated as separate axes instead of being entangled.

The caption source is also deliberate. Instead of relying on human-written dense ground-truth captions, the authors use five different Video-LLMs to generate dense descriptions: InternVL3.5, Qwen3-VL, video-SALMONN 2+, Keye-VL 1.5, and Gemini 3 Flash. This yields 2,500 video-caption pairs. Human annotators then verify each sentence in a two-stage process and assign binary labels. Sentences that are plausible but unsupported by the video are retained as hard negatives. This makes the benchmark harder to game than synthetic negative construction, because the negatives come from realistic model hallucinations rather than obviously corrupted text.

Conceptually, the benchmark measures grounded verification rather than stylistic generation. It requires checking object states, actions, background changes, scene transitions, and temporal consistency, which makes it a stronger test of video evidence integration.a:T42a,The experimental findings are straightforward. Current Video-LLMs frequently hallucinate events during dense caption generation, so their outputs are not reliably grounded in the video. In addition, failure patterns vary substantially across complexity types and duration ranges, which suggests that current weaknesses are heterogeneous rather than reducible to one single bottleneck.

The paper also includes dataset statistics showing that VidOmni-Bench requires broader video inspection than prior benchmarks and provides denser event coverage. Of the 500 videos, 433 contain audio, which means some examples may implicitly test audiovisual grounding as well.
