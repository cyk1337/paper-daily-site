18:T674,The method has two layers. First, it evaluates rationale quality as certificate quality. A generated rationale is parsed into a directed acyclic graph of atomic claims and dependencies. Each claim is associated with a claim type, an evaluation criterion, polarity, and support relations. The certificate is then audited along three dimensions. Soundness checks whether claims are supported by the candidate responses and whether the rationale introduces false evidence or invalid comparisons. Coverage checks whether the rationale addresses the decision-relevant criteria specified by the evaluation policy rather than cherry-picking a few convenient points. Decision Support checks whether the local assessments made in the rationale are consistent with the final verdict, penalizing cases where the rationale argues for A but concludes B. Second, DeCert converts these audits into preference supervision. If two sampled judgments differ in verdict correctness, correctness dominates. If they share the same correct verdict, the model constructs rationale preference pairs based on certificate quality. This is the key design choice: it isolates rationale quality while holding outcome correctness fixed. Training then combines outcome-level and rationale-level preference optimization. Relative to RationaleRM, R-Align, and RM-NLHF, the main difference is that supervision is reference-free and based on validity of support rather than similarity to a target explanation. Another useful distinction is that DeCert exploits within-verdict variation among sampled judgments, which gives finer supervision than assigning a single scalar rationale score.19:T491,The empirical story has four main parts. First, on standard pairwise judgment benchmarks, DeCert consistently improves over strong generative judge baselines on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings, and the gains are not merely due to extra sampling because the paper compares under matched sampling budgets. Second, rationale-quality analyses show fewer unsupported claims, fewer omitted decision-relevant issues, and fewer verdict-rationale inconsistencies. Third, the positional-bias result is notable: without swap-based augmentation or explicit position-consistency supervision, DeCert improves position-consistent accuracy by up to 14.8 points over the corresponding base judges. This suggests that certificate auditing pushes the model toward content-grounded comparison rather than superficial A/B position cues. Fourth, ablations support the full design: all three certificate dimensions matter, verdict-only supervision is insufficient, and removing the same-verdict rationale preference construction weakens the gains substantially. The paper appears stronger on consistency of trends and analysis depth than on one dramatic headline number.1a:T415,For a quick but useful read, focus on two questions: why reference rationales are the wrong supervision target, and why the paper treats a rationale as a certificate rather than just an explanation. Start with the introduction, especially the critique of prior rationale supervision. Then read the method overview and the sections defining Soundness, Coverage, and Decision Support. Three parts deserve close attention. First, the claim-graph construction and claim taxonomy, because that determines whether the method is a structured auditing framework or mostly prompt scaffolding. Second, the preference-pair construction, especially the same-correct-verdict comparisons; this is where the method gets its extra signal. Third, the OOD and positional-bias results, since they best test whether the model learned better reasoning rather than better benchmark mimicry. If time is limited, read the overview figure, the main results table, the rationale error analysis, and the ablations. About 25 to 35 minutes should be enough for a solid skim.1b:T828,方法上最核心的设计是 NLI 化。对一条推理轨迹 Q, s1...sn，在第 i 步构造输入 xi=(Q ⊕ s1...s{i-1}, si)，也就是“问题+前缀”对“当前步骤”。标签 yi 表示该步在当前上下文下是否被支持。若一条轨迹的首个标注错误出现在第 k 步，则只保留前 k 个实例；k 之后的步骤被丢弃，因为它们的 premise 已经污染，继续标注会把“当前步错”与“前文已错导致的后续不成立”混在一起。这个数据构造很关键，它保证标签语义是局部且条件化的。

在模型比较上，作者刻意把三种范式放到同一任务定义下。Encoder classification：标准双句分类，encoder 输出 pooled representation，再接二分类头。Decoder verdict generation：把输入 prompt 给 decoder，让它在最后生成单 token verdict，通常是 Yes/No，并用这两个 token 的归一化概率作为 non-entailment 分数。Decoder classification：仍用同一个 decoder backbone，但不走全词表生成，而是取最后一个非 padding token 的 hidden state，接一个两类线性头；头部初始化还借用了 Yes/No 的输出 embedding。这里的关键区别不是“都在做二元决策所以差不多”，而是优化目标不同：生成式 verifier 仍在全词表 softmax 上做 next-token cross-entropy，分类式 verifier 则直接在两个标签上优化。作者的论点是，验证本身是判别任务，若还强迫模型走生成接口，会把学习信号稀释到不必要的词表竞争里。

SIF 的第一部分是 comparative reward construction。它不再把单个 response 的平均 certainty 直接当 reward，而是先把每个 response 的 token-level certainty 排序，形成 ranked certainty profile，再在多个 certainty 窗口上做比较。这里的窗口不是原始序列中的连续 span，而是 certainty 排名上的区间。然后对同一 prompt 下采样得到的多个 responses 做 peer comparison。只有当一个 response 在多个 certainty level 上都得到足够支持，并且在同组 peer 中也占优时，才给正奖励。这样做的含义是：奖励不再来自“我整体看起来更自信”，而来自“我在多个 certainty 层次上都比同伴更一致地好”。这相当于把单点打分改成多视角排序。

第二部分是 selective policy updates。作者认为即使 reward 已经比较式构造，如果把所有样本都拿去更新，噪声仍会积累。因此他们做了两层过滤。第一层是 sample-level eligibility，只让满足支持条件的 response 参与优化；第二层是 group-level filtering，如果同一 prompt 下整组 responses 的 reward 没有区分度，比如都一样，就跳过这一组，不做策略更新。这样做的目的很直接：只在内部反馈有辨识度时学习，避免把模糊、冲突或低信息量的自反馈硬塞进 PPO/GRPO 式更新里。

The paper first diagnoses why prior self-certainty optimization fails. Self-certainty is defined from token-level predictive distributions using KL divergence from the uniform distribution, so it measures how peaked the model’s token distribution is. Methods such as Intuitor optimize the mean self-certainty over the whole response. The authors show that this average is too coarse: a response can have a few highly certain segments that raise the mean while crucial reasoning steps remain uncertain or wrong. They further examine within-response variance of self-certainty and find that it increases during training, which means the certainty profile becomes more dispersed and the mean becomes less representative. They then sort token-level certainty values and train variants using different ranked bands. High-certainty bands give fast early gains but severe late-stage deterioration; intermediate bands are more stable. This analysis motivates the SIF design.

The first component is comparative reward construction. Instead of using a response’s mean certainty as an absolute reward, SIF sorts token-level certainty scores to form a ranked certainty profile and compares responses across multiple certainty windows. These windows are defined over certainty ranks, not contiguous token spans in the original sequence. It also compares peer responses sampled for the same prompt. A response receives positive reward only if it is supported across multiple ranked certainty levels and also wins sufficiently in peer-level comparison. In effect, the reward becomes a multi-view relative preference signal rather than a single scalar confidence score.

The second component is selective policy updates. Even with a better reward, the authors argue that not every sampled response should contribute to optimization. They therefore apply filtering at two levels. At the sample level, only eligible responses with sufficient support participate in policy optimization. At the group level, if all responses for a prompt receive identical rewards, the whole group is skipped because it carries no useful preference information. This design aims to update the policy only when internal feedback is discriminative enough to be trusted.

Compared with TTRL-style methods, SIF does not require final-answer extraction. Compared with Intuitor, it does not treat self-certainty as an absolute objective. Compared with simple confidence-based reranking, it also controls whether learning should happen at all.22:T77f,The experiments train on MATH using three backbones and evaluate on 12 math and science reasoning benchmarks, with additional test-time adaptation experiments on unlabeled inputs. The second component is policy-side group modulation. After critic-side calibration, each reward group is centered and normalized, then passed through a signed power transform.
