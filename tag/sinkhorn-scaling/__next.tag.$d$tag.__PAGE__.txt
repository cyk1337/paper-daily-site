9:T41b,Its relevance to pretraining is through attention design and the train-inference interface, not generic serving optimization. If doubly-stochastic attention is indeed useful for reducing attention sinks and rank collapse, then inference cost is one of the main barriers to broader adoption. ASAP contributes a train-then-compile recipe: train with a standard finite-step Sinkhorn layer, then learn a lightweight parametric map that predicts the dual variables the Sinkhorn loop would otherwise solve for online. Two ideas matter here. First, some structured but expensive inference-time solvers may be replaceable by learned surrogates without changing the training recipe. Second, transport-based attention can be decomposed into a teacher operator plus a compiled dual predictor, rather than treated as a monolithic alternative to softmax. The contribution is best read as a practical step in attention-layer design; whether it matters for large causal LMs still depends on evidence at longer context, larger scale, and under autoregressive masking.a:T6b0,The method treats a trained Sinkhorn attention layer as a frozen teacher and learns to approximate its query-side dual variables. The pipeline is straightforward. First, train a Transformer with finite-step Sinkhorn normalization in the attention layer. Importantly, the target is not the infinite-step optimal transport solution, but the actual finite-budget operator used in practice. Second, freeze the teacher and run a small unlabeled calibration set through it. From each forward pass, extract the query-side normalization vector produced by the Sinkhorn loop, i.e. the source dual under the finite entropic transport convention. Third, on the same query and key activations, compute exact one-dimensional transport potentials along multiple sorted projection directions. These sliced geometric quantities become features for a lightweight regressor. Fourth, fit a parametric map from the sliced features to the teacher’s source dual, using either least-squares or convex-KL calibration. Fifth, at inference time, predict the source dual directly and recover the key-side dual analytically via an entropic c-transform, yielding a compiled approximation to the original doubly-stochastic operator. The paper also studies one-sided and two-sided c-transform variants. Relative to ESPFormer-style sliced transport, ASAP does not replace the training operator or rely on soft sorting during training; it is a post-training compilation step designed to mimic a trained Sinkhorn teacher. Relative to simply reducing the number of Sinkhorn iterations, it aims to approximate the teacher’s actual fixed-point behavior under the observed activation distribution, which is a more targeted operator approximation.b:T500,The most concrete numbers in the visible text concern training cost. This cost gap motivates the train-with-Sinkhorn, compile-afterward strategy. The paper evaluates ASAP on both language and vision benchmarks, comparing the original iterative Sinkhorn teacher, sliced-transport baselines, and the compiled ASAP variants. The stated goal is not to beat the teacher, but to obtain significant layer-level speedups while remaining close to the teacher’s performance and preserving the doubly-stochastic structure as much as possible.
