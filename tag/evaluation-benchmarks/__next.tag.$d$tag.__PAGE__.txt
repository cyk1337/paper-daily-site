全文的核心技术产物有三块。第一块是 benchmark corpus 构建与纳入规则。作者把 benchmark 定义为可引用的 evaluation artifact，要求有明确 scoring protocol，排除纯模型、纯数据集、纯 simulator。第二块是 contrast-gap analysis。作者给出一个 advantage-of-prediction evaluation loop，要求同一 testbed 中同时运行 direct VLA 和 world-model-based policy，并报告 capability-sliced gain，而不是只给总体成功率。第三块是 actionable protocol。文中提出四个可测量量，并在 Table 9、Figure 11 里给出可承载这些测量的 testbed。虽然全文摘要没有逐项展开四个量的数学定义，但从正文脉络看，它们围绕的是：预测是否改善闭环行为、改善发生在什么能力维度、改善是否来自真正可用的 counterfactual information、以及这种改善在执行噪声和分布偏移下是否稳定。作者没有发明新的 metric 公式去替代任务成功率，而是强调需要把 prediction benefit 作为独立变量显式 instrument 起来，这一点比再造一个综合分数更稳妥。a:T433,Its value for pretraining research is not robotics per se, but the way it sharpens the link between generative objectives and downstream utility. A large part of current foundation-model work in video generation, latent dynamics, action-conditioned generation, and multimodal predictive modeling implicitly assumes that better prediction should translate into better control. The paper shows that this assumption is still weakly instrumented. That matters for anyone working on predictive pretraining or unified multimodal modeling: without advantage-aware benchmarks, gains remain trapped in proxy metrics. The quantitative picture is clear. Out of 160 benchmarks, 138 are model-agnostic, only 11 explicitly construct a VLA-versus-world-model contrast, and only 4 actually turn prediction into executed action. For pretraining researchers, the main lesson is that modeling quality, generation metrics, and behavioral benefit should not be treated as interchangeable.b:T76f,This is a survey built around taxonomy design and evaluation protocol design. A central move in the paper is to disambiguate the overloaded term “world model.” The authors separate latent dynamics predictors, action-conditioned video predictors, and large generative video models, instead of treating Dreamer-style systems, GR-2-like predictors, and Sora-like generators as one category. They then organize the benchmark space into four evaluation lanes: policy, embodied, world-model, and bridge. The bridge lane is the key idea. It refers to benchmarks that at least attempt to connect prediction to closed-loop control, or explicitly compare a predictive branch against a direct policy branch.

The second organizing axis is capability. Benchmarks are tagged across twelve robotic capability areas, then placed into a capability-by-model-family matrix. This is important because task names alone do not reveal what a benchmark actually measures, nor whether it can answer the “does prediction help?” question. The paper’s main technical outputs are threefold. First, a benchmark corpus with explicit inclusion rules: a benchmark must be a citable evaluation artifact with a scoring protocol, not merely a model, raw dataset, or simulator. Second, a contrast-gap analysis centered on an advantage-of-prediction evaluation loop. The proposed loop requires both a direct VLA policy and a world-model-based controller to run in the same testbed, with capability-sliced gains reported rather than only aggregate success. Third, an actionable protocol with four measurable quantities and named testbeds, presented in Table 9 and Figure 11. The paper does not try to replace task success with one new scalar metric. Instead, it argues for instrumenting prediction benefit as an explicit variable. That choice is methodologically sound: it avoids hiding the question inside another composite score.c:T587,The empirical contribution is a structured benchmark census rather than model benchmarking. Only 4 benchmarks actually convert prediction into executed action, which means most world-model evaluation still stops at open-loop proxies. The paper also argues that counterfactual capability is barely measured, despite the fact that imagined futures and planning are often presented as the main reason to build world models in the first place.

Table 1 compares the survey against eight nearby surveys and position papers along five axes.
