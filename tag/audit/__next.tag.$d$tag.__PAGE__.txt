Sign-randomized controls have signed-cosine standard deviation 0.577, and 25.3% of them exceed cosine 0.5 with the observed CAA direction. Every draw whose effect exceeds the observed mean effect has cosine above 0.80. The preregistered Qwen test asked whether CAA beats all three families at Holm α = .01. It does not: the strict tail fails against all three families, and the mean also fails against sign randomization. The paper also reports that the full gate is passable in two model families under positive controls, and that a human-calibrated open-generation test separates a DeepSeek pass, a Qwen non-pass, and an automatic-judge calibration failure.a:T712,The proposed audit, J-Access, measures how accessible target knowledge remains along the model’s output pathway. Instead of relying on a standard logit lens, which directly decodes intermediate residual states with the unembedding matrix, the method uses a Jacobian lens. The motivation is specific: after unlearning, the geometry of the residual stream may shift, so the shared-basis assumption behind the logit lens becomes less reliable. The Jacobian lens locally linearizes the downstream computation from an intermediate state to vocabulary space, making it a better proxy for whether hidden knowledge is still close to being verbalized.

Operationally, the audit uses probe queries that implicate a forgotten entity without explicitly naming it. For a band of mid-to-late layers, the residual representation is projected into vocabulary space through the Jacobian lens, and the method checks whether any token from a target concept set appears among the top-ranked decoded tokens. The concept set is defined at two granularities: an identity-level set containing full-name and name-piece tokens, and a broader knowledge-level set containing answer content words filtered by document frequency and inferability constraints. The resulting access rate is normalized against both the original pre-unlearning model and a retain-only gold model trained without the forget set.

The paper is structured around two evaluation regimes rather than a new unlearning algorithm. In the predictive regime, pre-attack J-Access is measured and then compared against recovery under cross-entity relearning attacks. In the optimization regime, J-Access suppression is added directly to the unlearning objective to test whether the signal is robust or merely gameable. That design is the paper’s strongest contribution.b:T5df,The study evaluates two unlearning benchmarks, TOFU and OpenUnlearning, covering 8 unlearning methods and 398 public unlearned models. Three findings drive the paper.

First, many models that pass standard forgetting and utility checks still show J-Access substantially above the retain-only gold reference. So behavioral forgetting does not imply internal erasure. The result appears across methods and datasets, suggesting this is not an artifact of one benchmark.

Second, pre-attack J-Access predicts model-level recovery speed under cross-entity relearning attacks. Models with higher residual access tend to recover forgotten knowledge faster under a small amount of continued training. However, the signal is much weaker for predicting which specific items will recover. That indicates J-Access behaves more like a global accessibility or connectivity measure than an instance-level fate predictor.

Third, directly minimizing J-Access during unlearning lowers the audit score but increases post-attack recovery. The model learns to evade the readout rather than delete the underlying knowledge. This is the most consequential result in the paper. It shows that a useful audit can become a misleading optimization target.

The paper also includes analyses across identity-level versus knowledge-level token sets, different layer bands, and comparisons with standard behavioral metrics. Intuitively, contaminated data are already close to the model’s internal representation, so only limited adjustment is needed to fit them well.

The procedure has three stages. First, the candidate TSFM is subjected to a controlled probe fine-tuning run on the audited dataset, with a deliberately small training budget. The goal is not to optimize forecasting performance, but to expose comparable adaptation dynamics. Second, the method extracts probe-time signals such as loss reduction and parameter displacement, along with related dynamic features described in the paper. Third, these features are combined into a contamination-risk score for the model–dataset pair.

A notable design choice is that the audit target is defined at the dataset level rather than the sample level. This matches the practical structure of forecasting benchmarks, where many samples are windows derived from the same underlying source series. The paper formalizes contamination in terms of whether the source time series behind an audited sample appears in the pretraining corpus, and then defines a dataset-level contamination ratio.

Relative to LLM-style baselines, the method deliberately avoids two difficult dependencies. It does not rely on explicit overlap detection, because near-duplicates in time series are often transformed rather than literally repeated.
