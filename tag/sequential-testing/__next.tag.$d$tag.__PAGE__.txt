9:T638,Each reasoning trajectory induces a K-dimensional probability vector θ over candidate answers. In the black-box setting, one only observes a sampled label from θ. In the grey-box setting, one observes θ itself, along with the sampled answer. The goal is sequential sampling with a stopping rule that returns the true modal answer with probability at least 1-δ at any stopping time. The theory first derives asymptotic lower bounds on expected stopping time under different observation models. A key result is that grey-box feedback is never worse than black-box feedback, and its benefit depends on the structure of the trajectory-level answer distributions. When trajectories are effectively one-hot, grey-box reduces to black-box and the complexity is of order Θ(Δ^-2). When trajectories expose stable beliefs across candidates, the complexity can improve to Θ(Δ^-1), where Δ is the frequency gap between the top answer and the runner-up. The proposed algorithm, ASC-D, is the grey-box analogue of adaptive self-consistency. Instead of counting answer labels, it accumulates pairwise evidence using probability differences θ_i-θ_j and stops when one candidate has enough certified advantage over every alternative. The design is distribution-free in the sense that it does not assume prior knowledge of the trajectory distribution, yet it provides anytime δ-correctness and asymptotically matches the lower bound. Relative to prior adaptive SC methods, the real shift is the observation model: the method operates on distribution-valued feedback rather than answer-only samples.a:T447,The empirical section includes both synthetic and real evaluations. The synthetic experiments validate the theory: as the modal gap Δ shrinks, the advantage of grey-box feedback becomes larger, and the observed stopping-time behavior falls between the predicted Δ^-1 and Δ^-2 regimes depending on how informative the trajectory-level probability vectors are. The real benchmark is MMLU-Redux with multiple-choice questions, where the final-token probabilities over answer options can be extracted directly. Models include Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen3-1.7B. On these tasks, ASC-D uses 46.4% to 95.6% fewer trajectories than answer-only baselines when the modal gap is small. Under a fixed maximum budget of N=128, it also achieves the best correct-certification rate across all tested confidence levels, which matters because the gain is not merely earlier stopping but more reliable certified decisions under the same compute cap.
