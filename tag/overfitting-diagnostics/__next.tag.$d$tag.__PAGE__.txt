a:T417,Starting from the LAR metric introduced in parameterization theory, the authors rewrite it as a log-overlap between two distributions. For a matrix W, the normalized squared singular values define a weight spectrum p. For a batch of inputs, the normalized squared projections onto the right singular directions of W define an activation spectrum q. High LAR means that both weights and activations concentrate on the same small set of directions. Low LAR means that one or both spectra are diffuse, or that the two are poorly aligned. The paper also provides covariance and correlation forms. Empirically, p and q remain highly correlated during training, so LAR often behaves as a measure of spectral spread. A useful derivation states that if the computation concentrates on k aligned singular directions, then LAR = 1 - 1/2 log_n k, giving k ≈ n^{2(1-LAR)} as an effective dimension estimate.
