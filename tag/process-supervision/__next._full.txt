18:T61f,The framework has two stages. In stage one, the system starts from roughly 17.8K theorem statements collected from public formal corpora, mainly NuminaMath-LEAN and Lean-Workbook. For each theorem, a model is prompted to produce a proof attempt in a single pass, as numbered natural-language steps paired with corresponding Lean tactics. A key design choice is that the model never receives Lean execution feedback during generation. This preserves genuine model errors instead of repaired trajectories, which is exactly what a verifier needs to learn from.

In stage two, the formal proof is compiled in Lean. All steps before the first failing tactic are labeled correct; the first failing step and all subsequent steps are labeled incorrect. The authors recognize that Lean failure is not always a mathematical failure: some failures are purely syntactic or due to formalization mismatch. To reduce such false negatives, they add a second model call that reads the Lean error message, attempts one syntax repair, and classifies the failure as mathematical versus merely formal. Only proofs whose first failure is judged to be mathematical are kept. For fully compiling proofs, there is an additional consistency check to ensure the natural-language steps actually express the same mathematics as the formal proof.

This yields 10,634 labeled proofs and 152,005 step labels, plus a held-out 200-proof benchmark called LeanPRM-Bench. Verifiers are then trained purely on natural-language inputs, so Lean is only used once during data creation, not at inference time.19:T535,The main empirical result is a controlled comparison against PRM800K-style human supervision. With the same model, the same training recipe, and the same number of updates, LeanPRM-Verifier-4B outperforms the PRM800K-trained verifier on four out of five benchmarks. The gains are large: +14.4 exact step-label accuracy on LeanPRM-Bench, +9.7 on Hard2Verify, and +9.4 on IMO-GradingBench.

The paper also evaluates proof selection in a best-of-N setting on IMO-level problems. The LeanPRM-trained verifier selects correct proofs noticeably more often than random, while the PRM800K verifier stays close to chance. This is an important sanity check: the model is not merely learning stylistic preferences, but something closer to proof validity.

Another useful result is scale consistency. The improvement from LeanPRM supervision appears across all tested model sizes, from 0.6B to 8B, on LeanPRM-Bench, Hard2Verify, and IMO-GradingBench. Given that the dataset is only 10.6K proofs, this suggests that supervision quality and domain alignment matter more than raw label count in this setting.

The most informative ablations are the ones around filtering and label construction: whether syntax-repair filtering is used, how much noise comes from formal-only failures, and how robust the gains are across model scales and benchmarks.1a:T458,The labeling scheme is built from Monte Carlo rollouts. For selected prefixes of a generated reasoning trace, the model samples continuations, and the fraction that reach a correct answer estimates the continuation value. If the initial problem state is already unrecoverable, the trace is labeled a capability gap. If an incorrect trace remains recoverable at all evaluated prefixes, it is a near miss. If it first becomes unrecoverable partway through, it is a pit, and that transition point defines the pit step. The experiments use Qwen3 4B and 8B in thinking mode on hard math problems from DeepMath-103k and the MATH training split. Reasoning traces are split by blank lines and merged so each step has at least 400 tokens. The probe family includes a confidence probe trained on final labels, combined/value probes trained on rollout rates, a failure-type classifier reading transcoder activations, and pointer-style probes trained to predict pit steps. Baselines that use no hidden states include classifiers based on trace/problem variables and a length rule that predicts a late step from trace length.1b:T468,The results show limited failure-type signal and weak localization. On incorrect 8B traces, the confidence probe remains calibrated for final correctness at the final step, but at a 0.5 cutoff it misses 81% of pits and 47% of near misses. After controlling for four trace/problem variables, the pit-versus-near-miss difference exceeds the prespecified 0.08 effect margin at 8B; at 4B it is nonzero but below the margin. A combined probe trained on rollout rates changes the failure-type ordering: for 8B, the confidence probe assigns a higher final-step score to a random pit than to a random near miss with probability 0.76, while the combined probe gives 0.40. A classifier on transcoder activations separates capability gaps from pits better than the strongest non-hidden-state baseline by 0.055 AUROC at 8B, meeting the 0.05 margin; the 4B gain is 0.049 and does not meet the margin. Pit-step localization is the weakest part: at 4B, the pointer probe beats the length rule in 0 of 10 runs; an ensemble beats it only under one of two loss weightings; at 8B, the tested value probe is below the length rule across five seeds.1c:T41a,The framework has three main components. First, it decomposes each natural-language reasoning step into premises, explanations, and conclusions, making the dependency structure explicit. Second, it auto-formalizes these components into symbolic representations and uses automated theorem provers to check whether the conclusion follows from the premises. Third, it defines Solver-Based Backtracking Reward, or SBR, which combines solver verification with premise fidelity. This matters because a model could otherwise obtain a formally valid proof by changing or misusing the original premises. At inference time, if a step’s SBR falls below a threshold, the system backtracks and regenerates from that point. Compared with post-hoc methods such as LogicReward and VeriCoT, LogicTrack verifies during generation rather than after the full trajectory is completed. The paper also extends the framework to supervised fine-tuning by pairing real flawed model trajectories with solver-verified corrections and introducing a dedicated <backtrack> token.1d:T618,The design is careful and controlled. The dataset consists of 237 step-numbered solutions to 79 Humanity’s Last Exam physics questions, generated by three frontier models, with no synthetic error insertion. Each trace is labeled for two targets: final-answer correctness and the location of the first false step. The reference standard combines physicist annotations, an independent LLM debate, and source-masked adjudication, which is a sensible way to reduce single-annotator bias on difficult technical derivations.

The key experimental device is an information ladder that varies only the epistemic status of the answer shown to the monitor: blind, unverified answer, certified answer, and answer revealed only after a blind commitment. There is also a reconsideration control and a stepwise online condition. This lets the authors separate error detection from first-error localization, and further split traces into wrong-answer cases versus correct-answer-but-flawed cases. That decomposition is the core contribution. Another good choice is that the same trace is evaluated under different monitoring conditions. Those are the cases that remove the easy shortcut of checking whether the conclusion is wrong.

Eight LLM monitors are evaluated. 先看 agent 形式。与标准 ReAct 只维护原始对话和工具返回文本不同，FactAgent 在状态里显式维护一个 fact store。每次 search 动作得到 observation 后，agent 不只是继续推理，还可以执行 assert 动作，把 observation 里的信息抽取成结构化事实，写入 fact store。这样，状态从“越来越长的文本历史”变成“问题 Q、当前 observation、累计事实集合 Ft”。作者把这些事实视为离散语义单元，而不是普通字符串，这一步是后续 credit assignment 的基础。

再看奖励构造。论文的关键不是直接给 assert 打启发式分，而是从 group rollouts 里估计事实的后验效用。具体做法是先把语义等价的事实聚成 cluster，避免同一证据因表述不同被分散统计；然后基于一组 rollout 的最终 outcome，用 Bayesian estimation 推断每个 fact cluster 与成功结果的统计关联。直观上，如果某类事实经常出现在成功轨迹里、较少出现在失败轨迹里，它的 posterior utility 就更高。随后，这个 fact-level utility 被映射回 step-level dense rewards，用来训练 policy。

这比直接做 token-level process reward 更适合搜索任务，因为搜索任务的有效中间单位不是 token，也不一定是整句 reasoning，而是“找到并确认了哪条证据”。作者因此把 credit assignment 的对象从 action surface form 转成 evidence semantics。和纯 outcome-based GRPO 相比，FactAgent 仍然利用 group rollouts，但不再让整条轨迹共享同一个粗粒度 advantage，而是通过事实聚类和后验估计，把奖励分配到真正有贡献的中间步骤上。

从设计取舍看，这个方法避免了额外训练 reward model，也不依赖人工标注每一步是否正确，代价则是需要可靠的事实抽取与聚类。如果抽取质量差，后验 utility 估计会被噪声污染。全文也承认这一点，因此 fact store 既是方法优势，也是误差传播的主要入口。20:T89d,FactAgent has two main components: a fact-centric state representation and a Bayesian utility estimation scheme for dense process supervision.

On the agent side, the paper departs from standard ReAct-style agents that keep only raw text history. The state explicitly includes a fact store. After a search action returns an observation, the agent can take an assert action that extracts structured facts from the observation and writes them into the store. The state is therefore represented as the question, the current observation, and the accumulated fact set. These facts are treated as discrete semantic evidence units rather than plain text snippets. This is important because the method assigns credit at the level of evidence semantics, not at the level of surface-form actions.

On the reward side, the paper estimates fact utility from grouped rollouts. Semantically equivalent facts are first clustered so that the same evidence is not split across multiple textual variants. Then, using final outcomes from a group of trajectories, the method performs Bayesian estimation of the posterior utility of each fact cluster. Intuitively, if a fact cluster appears disproportionately often in successful trajectories, its posterior utility increases. These fact-level utilities are then converted into dense step-level rewards for policy optimization.

This differs from standard outcome-based GRPO, where all steps in a trajectory inherit the same coarse trajectory-level signal. Here, group rollouts are still used, but the reward is redistributed through an explicit evidence layer. That makes the method better matched to search tasks, where the meaningful intermediate unit is often not a token or a reasoning sentence, but a retrieved and validated piece of evidence.

The design also avoids training an external reward model and does not require human annotation of intermediate steps. The trade-off is that the whole pipeline depends on the quality of fact extraction and fact clustering. If those are noisy, the posterior utility estimates can be corrupted. The paper is aware of this: the fact store is both the main strength of the method and the main source of possible error propagation.21:T5ef,The experiments use Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbones and evaluate on seven QA benchmarks. The headline result is that with the 7B backbone, FactAgent reaches an average EM of 51.2, outperforming the strongest baseline by +3.2 EM. The paper emphasizes that the gains are consistent across benchmarks rather than driven by a single dataset.

The more informative findings come from the analysis. The dense process supervision mainly improves evidence precision and retrieval quality. That matches the method design: the reward does not simply encourage more search, but encourages finding useful evidence and committing it into the explicit state.

The ablations likely cover the main components: removing the fact store, removing the assert action, disabling fact clustering, or replacing Bayesian posterior utility with simpler statistics. From the full-text description, the explicit fact representation and the utility estimation module are the most important contributors. Another practical point is that the method derives dense rewards directly from grouped rollout outcomes, without relying on an external reward model, which makes the training setup lighter and easier to scale.

The current evidence mainly supports search-heavy QA agents.
