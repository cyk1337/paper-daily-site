9:T6e8,The paper has two main components. First, it builds a controlled matrix of five LVR variants spanning different latent-supervision designs, including reconstruction-heavy training, input-noise regularization, and progressively scaffolded supervision. The backbone, data source, and training budget are held fixed so that differences can be attributed to the latent objective rather than unrelated recipe changes. Under this setup, a variant can improve cosine alignment by about 40% and reduce reconstruction error from 3.79 to 1.55, yet lose 13 points on the downstream benchmark.

Second, the authors introduce PRISM, a pair of inference-time diagnostics. The first is a corruption test: at each generation step, latent positions are zeroed, perturbed with Gaussian noise, or replaced with latents from another sample. If the latent channel is truly carrying reasoning-relevant information, these interventions should hurt substantially. Instead, all variants move by at most four points, and one variant even improves when its latents are zeroed. The second is a two-position linear probe. The probe asks whether the answer is decodable from the answer-decoding hidden state and from the autoregressive feedback variable that gets re-injected as the latent. The answer is decodable from the former but not the latter. The paper interprets this through an Information Bottleneck lens: the dominant cross-entropy term pushes the network to route answer-relevant computation wherever it most directly helps prediction, while cosine/MSE reconstruction does not guarantee that the supervised latent preserves answer-relevant information. As a result, the model can satisfy the auxiliary loss while learning to solve the task through downstream shared-parameter pathways.a:T48d,There are four results worth retaining. First, across the five LVR variants, cosine alignment and V*Bench accuracy have a Pearson correlation of -0.94, which is the opposite of the field’s usual assumption. Fourth, PRISM shows that zeroing, noising, or swapping latents changes accuracy by at most about four points across all variants, indicating broad latent bypass.
