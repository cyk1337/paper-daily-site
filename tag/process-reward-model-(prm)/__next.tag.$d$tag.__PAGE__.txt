c:T870,方法叫 Chunk-Level Guided Generation。整体框架沿用 generate-score-select 的思路，但把 PRM 换成现成大模型的 likelihood scorer，而且评分对象不是自然分段的 reasoning step，而是固定长度 chunk。

具体流程是：给定当前上下文，小模型在每一步采样 k 个长度为 L 的候选 chunk；大模型不生成文本，只对这 k 个候选 continuation 计算分数；选出最高分 chunk 拼接回上下文，再进入下一轮采样。这样大模型只承担判别式打分，计算可以并行，最终答案仍由小模型逐块生成。

作者提出两种打分规则。

1. Likelihood-Guided Selection, LGS：直接用大模型对候选 chunk 的长度归一化 log-prob 评分，选分数最高者。
2. Contrastive-Guided Selection, CGS：用大模型分数减去小模型分数，即 ℓ_large - ℓ_small。这个设计的动机很直接：如果某个 chunk 小模型自己就很偏好，那么大模型的额外信息量有限；真正值得保留的是那些“大模型明显更认可、而小模型没那么认可”的 continuation。它有点像把大模型当作 residual teacher，而不是单纯的 reranker。

为什么要用 fixed-length chunk，而不是自然语言里的“一个推理步骤”？这是全文最关键的技术判断之一。作者发现，大模型 likelihood 对 variable-length reasoning step 有系统性长度偏置，哪怕做了长度归一化，仍然偏向更长的 step。原因不是简单的 token 数除法能消掉的，因为不同长度 step 的语义完成度、局部确定性和格式模式都不同。固定长度 chunk 让同一决策点上的候选具有相同 token 长度，分数才真正可比。

与 PRM guided search 的区别主要有三点。第一，不需要 step-level 标注和 reward model 训练。第二，评分信号来自通用大模型的 next-token likelihood，而不是专门学出来的 process reward。第三，搜索粒度是固定长度 chunk，不依赖人工定义的 reasoning step 边界。与 Best-of-N 的区别则在于选择时机前移：不是整条轨迹生成完再选，而是在中间不断纠偏。d:T886,实验覆盖 5 个数学推理 benchmark：GSM8K、MATH、Minerva Math、AMC23、AIME24。模型组合有三组：Qwen2.5-1.5B 由 Qwen2.5-32B 引导，Llama-3.2-1B 由 Llama-3.1-70B 引导，以及更大的 Qwen2.5-7B 由 Qwen2.5-72B 引导。

关键结果有几条。

1. chunk-level guidance 明显优于 post-hoc selection。作者专门展示了一个反直觉现象：用大模型 likelihood 做 Best-of-N reranking 并不好，甚至随着候选数增加会变差。文中给出的例子是，Qwen2.5-1.5B 在 MATH 上由 Qwen2.5-32B 做 post-hoc likelihood 选择时，准确率会从 55.8% 降到 52.6%。这说明问题不在 scorer 太弱，而在打分时机太晚。

2. CGS 通常优于 LGS，也优于多数后验基线。对比对象包括 Majority@k、Self-Certainty、Borda count、Best-of-N。作者的结论是，在三组模型上，chunk-level guidance 都能稳定超过这些 post-hoc 方法；其中 CGS 是最稳的版本。

3. 在两组小生成器设置里，CGS 在多数 benchmark 上能匹配或超过用 Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B 做的 PRM guided search；在 7B 生成器设置里，CGS 的五个 benchmark 平均成绩距离 PRM-72B guided search 约 1.5 个百分点，距离 standalone 72B greedy baseline 约 0.4 个百分点。这个结果的含义不是“完全替代 PRM”，而是“在不训练 reward model 的前提下，已经接近专用过程奖励模型的效果”。

4. 作者还强调收益不是来自生成更长的推理链。CGS 的 reasoning trace 明显短于 PRM guided search；在 GSM8K 上，PRM 的输出 token 数最多可比 CGS 多 80%。这说明 chunk-level guidance 提升的是局部决策质量，而不是单纯靠更长 CoT 堆出来。

5. 长度偏置分析是实验部分另一个重点。作者展示 variable-length step scoring 即便做 length normalization 仍偏向长 step，因此 fixed-length chunk 不是工程上的简化，而是让 likelihood 可比较的必要条件。

如果从方法可信度看，这篇实验做得比较完整：既有不同模型家族，也有不同 generator / scorer 尺度组合，还把 post-hoc、PRM-guided、standalone large model 都放进了比较框架里。e:T895,The method is called Chunk-Level Guided Generation. It follows a generate-score-select loop similar in spirit to PRM-guided search, but replaces the trained PRM with an off-the-shelf large LM used purely as a likelihood scorer. The scoring unit is not a natural reasoning step but a fixed-length chunk.

At each decision point, the small model samples k candidate chunks of length L from the current context. The large model does not generate any text; it only scores the k candidate continuations. The highest-scoring chunk is appended to the context, and generation continues from there. This makes the large model a parallelizable discriminative guide rather than the generator of the final solution.

Two scoring rules are proposed.

1. Likelihood-Guided Selection (LGS): select the candidate with the highest length-normalized log-probability under the large model.
2. Contrastive-Guided Selection (CGS): score each chunk by large-model log-probability minus small-model log-probability, i.e. ℓ_large - ℓ_small. The intuition is that chunks already strongly preferred by the small model provide less incremental value from the larger model. CGS emphasizes continuations where the larger model contributes information beyond the generator’s own preference.

The fixed-length chunk design is one of the paper’s most important technical choices. The authors show that large-model likelihoods are systematically biased toward longer reasoning steps even after length normalization. Natural step boundaries are therefore a poor unit for direct likelihood comparison. Fixed-length chunks remove this confound by ensuring that all candidates at a given decision point have the same token length, making scores genuinely comparable.

Relative to PRM-guided search, the method differs in three ways: it requires no step-level labels or reward-model training; it uses native next-token likelihoods from a general LM rather than a learned process reward; and it operates on fixed-length chunks rather than human- or model-defined reasoning steps. Relative to Best-of-N, the key difference is timing: selection is moved inside generation rather than applied after full trajectories are completed.f:T8d0,The evaluation covers five math reasoning benchmarks: GSM8K, MATH, Minerva Math, AMC23, and AIME24. Three model pairings are tested: Qwen2.5-1.5B guided by Qwen2.5-32B, Llama-3.2-1B guided by Llama-3.1-70B, and Qwen2.5-7B guided by Qwen2.5-72B.

Several results stand out.

1. Chunk-level guidance substantially outperforms post-hoc selection. The paper highlights a useful negative result: using large-model likelihood for Best-of-N reranking is often poor and can even get worse as N increases. One concrete example is Qwen2.5-1.5B on MATH, where post-hoc likelihood selection with Qwen2.5-32B drops from 55.8% to 52.6% as k grows. This supports the paper’s main claim that the bottleneck is timing, not scorer quality.

2. CGS is generally stronger than LGS and stronger than the main post-hoc baselines, including Majority@k, Self-Certainty, Borda count, and Best-of-N. Across all three model pairings, chunk-level guidance consistently beats these post-hoc methods, with CGS being the most robust variant.

3. In the two smaller-generator settings, CGS matches or outperforms PRM-guided search using Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B on most benchmarks. In the 7B-generator setting, CGS is within about 1.5 percentage points of PRM-72B guided search on average across five benchmarks, and within 0.4 points of the standalone 72B greedy baseline. The significance is not that PRMs are obsolete, but that a training-free method gets surprisingly close to a specialized process reward model.

4. The gains are not explained by longer chains of thought. CGS produces noticeably shorter reasoning traces than PRM-guided search; on GSM8K, PRM outputs can be up to 80% longer. This suggests the method improves local decision quality rather than simply buying accuracy through longer generation.

5. The length-bias analysis is an important part of the empirical story. The paper shows that variable-length step scoring remains biased even after length normalization, which justifies fixed-length chunking as more than an engineering convenience.
