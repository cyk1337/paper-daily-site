5:T5d7,TALAN is built as a summarize-remix-writeback side path inserted into the transformer's residual stream and co-trained with LoRA or DoRA in a single SFT loop. The module first summarizes the active sequence into a compact latent memory. It then remixes that memory into token-level perturbations and writes them back into the residual stream through a controlled residual update. The design is guided by two intended properties: P1, the perturbation should remain small and bounded; P2, it should be geometrically separate from the adapter update, acting as a complementary intervention rather than duplicating what LoRA or DoRA already does.

The paper parameterizes adaptation to a new backbone through a six-axis configuration space, π = (ℓ*, T, m, w, τ, γ), covering choices such as insertion layer, summary span or window, latent memory size, writeback location or mode, mixing control, and perturbation strength. A useful design choice is that the block itself stays fixed while only the configuration changes. That makes the method easier to analyze than a highly flexible side network.

Relative to prior work, TALAN is not just another adapter. Its forward path depends on the current sequence, so different inputs induce different residual perturbations. At the same time, it avoids the multi-stage workflow of activation steering methods because there is no separate probe fitting or inference-time vector injection. Training and deployment remain close to standard SFT practice.6:T535,The experiments cover four Qwen3-family backbones and four STEM/code benchmarks, with matched LoRA and DoRA baselines. Under the LoRA pipeline, TALAN achieves a cross-model mean gain of +1.41 percentage points, is positive on all four backbones, and is non-negative on all 16 model-benchmark cells. Under the DoRA pipeline, the cross-model mean gain is +1.85 points, again positive on all four backbones, with positive deltas on 13 of 16 cells. The paper also includes matched-initialization multi-seed audits, which support positive family-level effects while explicitly showing seed variance rather than hiding it.

The added cost is small: less than 1% extra trainable parameters relative to the backbone, and only about 1.01x to 1.02x inference overhead compared with matched LoRA. As a transfer check beyond the main Qwen suite, selected Llama-3.2-1B runs are also positive across seven paired seeds under both LoRA and rsLoRA.

The analysis section focuses on two questions: whether TALAN's perturbation is orthogonal to the adapter update, and whether a small perturbation can be amplified through later layers.
