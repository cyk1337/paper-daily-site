18:T7f8,The proposed method is JSTIP, Joint Speech-Text Interleaved Pretraining. The architecture remains standard: a pretrained speech encoder, an adapter into the LLM embedding space, and a decoder-only LLM. The novelty is entirely in how training examples are constructed.

Given an aligned speech-text pair (A, T), the authors first obtain fine-grained alignments {(A1, T1), ..., (An, Tn)}. Instead of feeding the full speech as conditioning context and predicting the full transcript, they build interleaved sequences that alternate speech and text segments. Two complementary deterministic templates are used: one starts with speech, e.g. (A1, T2, A3, T4, ..., Tn), and the other starts with text, e.g. (T1, A2, T3, A4, ..., Tn). In both cases, the sequence ends with text, and cross-entropy is applied only on text tokens; all speech positions are masked out from the loss.

Three design choices matter. First, interleaving granularity: the paper studies both word-level and segment-level alignment. Word-level interleaving is more aggressive in coupling speech evidence with local text continuation, while segment-level interleaving is simpler and likely more stable. Second, behavioral consistency: ASR-only, text-only, and interleaved examples are all cast into the same “predict text tokens from a mixed prefix” format, which helps preserve the original LM behavior instead of letting speech-conditioned decoding drift into a separate mode. Third, modality-gap reduction: speech and text no longer coexist only across batches; they co-occur within the same sequence, so the model repeatedly transitions between modalities inside a single causal context.

The authors intentionally use deterministic alternation rather than random modality selection. This is a controlled design choice to isolate the effect of interleaving granularity and boundary construction. On the implementation side, they pack multiple examples into an 8k context and use packed SFT with cu_seqlens in FlashAttention so attention is reset at sample boundaries.19:T70a,The main experiments use 38k hours of in-house ASR data. The central question is whether JSTIP transfers textual knowledge into ASR more effectively than ASR-only training or naive speech-text joint training. The headline result reported in the paper is up to 17.2% relative improvement in entity accuracy over ASR-only training. This is an important choice of metric: the gains are concentrated on entities and domain-specific terms, which is exactly where one would expect pretrained text priors to matter.

A particularly informative result comes from the domain adaptation setting. The paper compares two ways of injecting domain knowledge: adding transcription-only domain text versus using synthetic domain ASR data. Under JSTIP, domain text alone reaches entity performance comparable to synthetic domain ASR. This suggests that the interleaving scheme materially improves text-to-ASR transfer efficiency, reducing dependence on expensive speech synthesis or additional speech supervision.

The paper also reports that the best JSTIP system is competitive with open-source ASR and Speech-LLM systems on medical entity performance. Another useful signal is zero-shot speech question answering: JSTIP enables this capability without extra task-specific supervision. The authors do not position SQA as the main task, but as evidence that text-side generative behavior survives the ASR-oriented training.
