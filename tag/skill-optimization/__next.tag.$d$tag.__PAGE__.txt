If it is wrong, its internal answer is assumed to be uniformly distributed over the other k-1 answers. The judge score is then whether the candidate answer matches the judge’s internal answer. Under this model, TPR is c and FPR is (1-c)/(k-1), giving AUC = 0.5 * (1 + c - (1-c)/(k-1)). This yields a necessary condition: c must exceed 1/k for the judge to discriminate above chance. The paper also shows that marginal AUC can be confounded by item difficulty; a scorer that only reads difficulty can appear useful. The proposed measurement therefore uses a within-question estimator, comparing correct and incorrect candidates for the same question, with question-clustered bootstrap uncertainty.
