19:T447,The central notion is gauge equivariance: an optimizer is equivariant if it behaves consistently under orthogonal rotations of the latent factor basis. A structure theorem shows that a memoryless update is equivariant iff it takes the form Δ=H(GG^T)G, a left preconditioner determined by the Gram matrix. Under this classification, GD, Polyak momentum, Adam with a shared scalar second moment, Muon (including its Newton–Schulz iterates), and Shampoo's Kronecker preconditioner with damping are equivariant; Adam, RMSProp, signSGD, Lion, and Adafactor break equivariance because they apply entrywise nonlinearities at zero state. An exact first-step defect is derived for zero-initialized Adam. A transfer theorem shows that dynamics under a shared scalar preconditioner equal gradient flow under a rescaled time, so existing low-rank/minimum-nuclear-norm results for gradient flow carry over. Equivariance is necessary but not sufficient: low-rank convergence also depends on the spectral schedule over singular values attracting dynamics to the low-rank manifold within the training budget.1a:T5d4,The contribution is not a new optimizer but a tightly controlled reassessment. The setup has three important pieces. First, the authors choose problem families that can be systematically swept: symmetric PSD factorization, matrix completion, and NMF. These cover unconstrained low-rank recovery, missing-observation settings, and constrained factorization with nonnegativity and redundancy. Second, they run equal-budget, carefully tuned comparisons across Muon, Adam, AdamW, and GD, instead of inheriting community-default hyperparameters. This matters because Muon is sensitive to learning rate, momentum, weight decay, and the approximation details of the orthogonalization step. Third, they interpret results through the lens of what orthogonalization is actually doing. Muon approximately orthogonalizes matrix-shaped gradients, flattening singular-value anisotropy and moving closer to steepest descent under the spectral norm. One might expect that to help on spectral problems, but the paper shows that in classical matrix factorization this does not automatically beat coordinate-wise adaptivity; AdamW’s per-coordinate scaling is often more useful under ill-conditioning. Where Muon does help is NMF: orthogonalized updates appear to discourage factor collapse and reduce redundant directions, leading to more diverse factors. So the gain may come less from faster fitting of dominant singular directions and more from an implicit regularization effect on representation geometry.1b:T418,The experimental contribution is about relative behavior across regimes rather than a single headline number. Four findings stand out. First, on standard low-rank matrix factorization, Muon’s advantage mostly vanishes after fair tuning, with AdamW and GD reaching similar or better convergence and final error. Second, on matrix completion, Muon again does not show a stable lead, which weakens the simple intuition that spectral normalization should automatically help on spectrally structured tasks. Third, on NMF, Muon shows a more consistent advantage, especially in overparameterized settings or when factor redundancy is more severe, yielding better reconstruction and less factor overlap. Fourth, ill-conditioning alone does not hand the win to Muon; in many cases AdamW’s adaptive scaling remains stronger.
