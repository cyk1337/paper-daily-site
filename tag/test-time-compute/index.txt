The empirical product-coupling premium is small with these strong reasoners: 0.215 to 0.588 percentage points across two- and three-model pools, with marginally identified intervals never exceeding an upper bound of 0.827 points. The plug-in per-query selection headroom over the best single model is 1.27 points, and the product union adds another 0.588 points. Resampling the cheapest strong model three times gives nearly the same empirical any-correct rate as calling all three models once. The cost analysis shows that a 65,536-token cap truncates only 31 of 45,000 responses, 25 with periodic tails, but capped responses can account for up to 60.2% of an output-length-squared cost proxy; the 70B run spends 53.9% of its formal wall time on the last 11 requests.19:T481,The methodology is simple but well structured. First, the authors analyze failed trajectories from SLMs and frontier models using a shared failure taxonomy. This establishes that the dominant failure modes are similar across tiers—step repetition, weak verification, and specification violations—so the gap is mostly quantitative rather than qualitative. Second, they evaluate the three capability axes separately. For solvability, they use repeated sampling and report pass@k, with each attempt executed in a fresh environment and judged by the task verifier. For verification, they study outcome verification: given a pool of completed candidate trajectories, the model must select a successful one. For confidence, the model sees only the task description and outputs a verbalized probability of success, which is evaluated with overconfidence and Brier score. A useful detail is the distinction between process verification during execution and outcome verification after generation; the main verification experiment targets the latter. The contribution is therefore a capability decomposition and evaluation protocol, not a new training method.1a:T452,The key results come from OpenThoughts-TBLite and Terminal-Bench 2.1. At pass@32, the numbers become 92.0% and 84.0%, shrinking the absolute gap to 8.0 points and the relative gap from 32.1% to 9.5%. This suggests that SLMs often can solve the task but do so unreliably. Verification does not improve in the same way: with the same pool of 32 candidate trajectories, GLM-5.2 selects a successful one 64.6% of the time at k=32, versus 55.4% for Tmax-9B, leaving a 9.2-point absolute gap, or 16.6% relative.
