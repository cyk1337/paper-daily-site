9:T5c8,The pipeline has two stages. First, the authors estimate latent task difficulty from observed agent-task outcomes. Each trial is treated as a Bernoulli observation, forming a partially observed binary response matrix over agents and tasks. They fit a one-parameter logistic IRT model, or Rasch model, with one scalar ability parameter θ_a per agent configuration and one scalar difficulty parameter β_t per task. This is preferable to raw pass rate because difficulty is inferred relative to a population of agents on a shared scale, making it more suitable for cross-benchmark comparison.

Second, they train predictors of β_t using only pre-rollout features. These include textual task descriptions, environment and action-space descriptions, metadata, and uncertainty-related signals such as entropy that can be obtained without full interaction. A major methodological contribution is the evaluation protocol. The paper argues that response AUC can look strong even when task-level difficulty estimates are poor, so it emphasizes rank-based metrics such as Spearman correlation for judging whether the predictor actually learns difficulty ordering. The residual analysis is also important: discrepancies between predicted and observed IRT difficulty are used diagnostically. Large positive residuals often indicate hidden hardness factors such as infeasible environments or broken evaluators, while large negative residuals may reveal familiarity, templating, or shortcuts.a:T525,The empirical foundation is substantial. The main analysis subset contains 5,230 tasks from 17 benchmarks, evaluated by 497 agent configurations built from 216 models and 90 scaffolds. The full corpus includes 415,470 agent-task outcomes. Domains span coding and SWE, function calling, web navigation, computer use, terminal tasks, math, ML, cybersecurity, and general-purpose suites. Task dates range from 2010 to 2026, and each agent-task pair has about 1.2 trials on average.

The headline numbers are straightforward. Under K-fold evaluation, the combined feature set reaches Spearman ρ = 0.399, significantly better than the baseline at p < 0.001. Under out-of-distribution evaluation, it reaches ρ = 0.225. Among single feature families, entropy performs best. The paper also shows that response AUC can remain high even when task-level difficulty ranking is weak, which supports the claim that rank-based evaluation is the more faithful metric. The residual case studies are especially useful: some tasks predicted to be moderate turn out to be much harder because the environment is infeasible or the verifier is faulty, while others that look difficult in text are actually easy due to familiarity or shortcuts. That makes the predictor useful not only for scoring tasks, but also for debugging benchmarks.b:T41b,The main evaluation runs on the full Python slice of SWE-bench Pro (266 tasks) under the official capped-budget protocol. First is the milestone-plan-act workflow. Instead of planning at the finest step granularity from the start, the orchestrator first decomposes a complex query into milestones. This reduces low-level execution noise and gives the policy a more stable state abstraction. Within each milestone, it builds a dependency-aware execution graph and can run independent steps in parallel across executors. Second is explicit executor profiling. Each executor comes with textual descriptions of capability and cost, so the orchestrator reasons jointly over task requirements and executor profiles rather than routing by query features alone. This differs from standard learned routers that often rely on shallow signals and fixed executor sets. Third is end-to-end RL with tree-structured rollout. Starting from an initial trajectory, the system expands selected milestone or plan nodes into alternative branches, producing multiple strategies under comparable contexts. Rewards combine task correctness, execution efficiency, and trajectory completeness. The completeness term is important because otherwise the policy may exploit partial execution or early stopping. Rewards are normalized at both node and query levels to make signals more comparable across branches and tasks and to reduce variance. Compared with standard single-trajectory RL, this branch-based exploration gives denser supervision around local decision points and is better aligned with multi-step orchestration.d:T407,The experiments cover three task families: mathematical reasoning, embodied decision making, and deep research. Correctness labels are used during training to build rewards, but they are never inserted into refinement prompts and are not available to the inference controller. Training uses fixed-horizon trajectories with a GRPO-style objective. Per-turn solve, verification, and format scores are averaged into a trajectory return, while optimization is applied to the final completion tokens rather than treating intermediate turns as separate samples. The confidence reward uses a Brier-style calibration term, penalizes confident mistakes, and encourages recognizing both error states and correct states that are eligible for stopping. Fixed-budget sweeps with the same trained policies show that no single shared stopping turn matches adaptive SVR, which supports the claim that the gain comes from instance-level retention rather than simply generating more turns. The scope is still bounded: the evidence is mainly mathematical reasoning with a 2B-scale model, so transfer to larger models, code execution tasks, or open-ended generation remains an open question.10:T519,The method has three parts. First, the paper detects context anxiety from reasoning traces. Instead of simple keyword matching, it uses a panel of LLM judges to classify whether a trace semantically expresses claims like 'this exceeds my context window' or 'I cannot finish within token limits.' This lets the authors separate anxiety-driven failures from capability-driven failures and successful attempts. That distinction is central to the paper. Second, it measures token-use calibration. After producing an answer, the model is asked to estimate how many tokens were required to complete the solution. This estimate is compared with the true token count through a winsorized token ratio. The point is not whether the model knows tokenizer internals; it is whether the model’s perceived generation effort is systematically biased. Third, the paper tests whether the behavior is mutable. It builds a distilled SFT dataset from the target model and stronger teacher models, keeping only successful reasoning traces that do not exhibit context anxiety. The student is then fine-tuned on these traces. The intervention is designed to teach alternative long-horizon reasoning strategies that complete the task without self-abandonment, rather than simply injecting more answers or more test-time compute.11:T413,The main task is Tower of Hanoi, with shortest-path grid search used as a second validation setting. This miscalibration is associated with a 15% drop in accuracy and, even when the model succeeds, a 54% increase in token usage, indicating that anxiety hurts both success rate and efficiency. The effect appears even in models built for long contexts and adaptive generation length, so it is not reducible to a small context window. On the intervention side, lightweight SFT reduces context anxiety by more than 50%, which supports the claim that the phenomenon is a modifiable behavioral policy rather than a hard capability limit. The main grid uses NoPE, with positional-encoding controls in the appendix. The key methodological move is to replace tail instruments with a head instrument. Tail instruments such as skipping a late loop or comparing Jacobians across late loops are unreliable because converged trajectories sit near fixed points; near such states, skipping is cheap and local circuits look similar by construction. The proposed diagnostic is convergence-time scaling τ(n,i): the first loop at which the decoded output at position i reaches its final value and stays there. Its scaling class acts as a mechanism signature. Constant τ indicates shortcut or refinement, τ proportional to log n indicates a parallel scan, and τ proportional to n indicates serial step-indexed computation. A streaming positive control validates the instrument: when token i is revealed only at loop i, τ recovers a linear slope of 1.0, while shortcut controls remain flat.13:T577,The experiments are controlled population studies over many seeds, recipes, and model sizes. The frontier solves v positions per loop, with v approximately n_tr/T_tr; under T=n training, the speed is about 1 position per loop. Standard tail diagnostics fail: skip retention is essentially total on sufficiently solved examples, cross-step Jacobian alignment is uniformly high, and these metrics do not predict length generalization. Reasoning scaling spends more tokens before producing an answer. Sampling scaling generates more attempts and selects one. The paper defines grounded feedback as observations from an instrument that measures the artifact’s actual behavior or form: running tests, rendering a page and measuring element positions, querying an external source, or measuring a layout. It also defines grounded evaluation as using such observations as the score. A coverage principle ties the two together: feedback helps only for defects that the instrument can observe. This explains why a linter may be grounded but ineffective for behavioral bugs, and why a screenshot-reading VLM can fail as a layout judge. The screenshot may crop away overflow, and small overlaps may be below the judge’s effective visual acuity. Instrument ablations support the grounding thesis: execution feedback beats critique for behavioral bugs, a linter adds little when it observes the wrong property, and a screenshot VLM reviewer worsens slide geometry while a geometry reviewer repairs it.16:T74e,The proposed system, GRADE, is a hierarchical multi-agent architecture with three levels. At the top is a lightweight orchestrator M0 that consumes a shared query representation from an encoder and makes initial routing decisions. The middle layer contains two manager modules, implemented as small MLPs, which provide grouping signals over subsets of specialists. The bottom layer consists of sub-agents, each backed by a frozen expert LLM plus lightweight trainable components: an adapter into a shared latent space, an answer head, and a calibration map used after expert substitution.

The main design is a set of four learned gates. The Assignment Gate selects which sub-agents are activated. The Depth Gate decides whether the system should continue deeper into the hierarchy or stop early. The Communication Gate learns pairwise message passing masks so that agents do not always broadcast to one another. The Pruning Gate removes low-value branches during execution. Together, these gates turn a fixed multi-agent pipeline into a query-adaptive conditional-compute system.

For training, the paper introduces CoGRPO, a multi-agent variant of group-relative policy optimization. It avoids a critic and instead assigns relative advantage to all gates and agents that contributed to a rollout under a final correctness reward. The important point is credit assignment: the system must propagate a sparse end reward back to routing, communication, and answering decisions jointly.

A practical addition is the Expert Registry with lightweight recalibration. Agents are associated with swappable experts through registry descriptors, and when an expert is replaced at inference time, only a small calibration step on 64 anchor queries is needed. The gates themselves are left untouched.
