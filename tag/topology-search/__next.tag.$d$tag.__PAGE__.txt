9:T543,FlowMAS models workflow construction as trajectory generation with a GFlowNet. A state corresponds to a partially built workflow topology, actions extend the topology, and a terminal state is a complete workflow. The GFlowNet is trained so that terminal workflows are sampled in proportion to their rewards, which encourages a distribution over high-reward candidates rather than a single optimized graph.

The paper adds two components on top of a vanilla GFlowNet. The information-guided module uses Partial Information Decomposition to estimate how different operators contribute unique, redundant, and synergistic information toward the task. This gives more structured credit assignment than a single scalar textual critique. The curiosity-driven module rewards structural novelty, pushing the generator to explore underrepresented workflow patterns and reducing collapse to a few templates.

Compared with search-based systems such as ADAS and AFlow, FlowMAS learns a reusable topology generator instead of repeatedly searching a large tree. Compared with textual-gradient methods such as OneFlow and DebFlow, it aims for finer-grained structural feedback.
