18:T72d,LongVU-TTT inserts a TTT-Conv resampler between a pretrained ViT encoder and the LLM. The input to this stage is a per-frame spatial grid of visual features, preserving a 27x27 token layout instead of flattening everything into a 1D sequence upfront. The method has three main ingredients. First, the fast-weight parameterization is convolutional rather than MLP-based. Unlike TTT-MLP or SSM-style operators such as Mamba that process flattened sequences, the hidden state here is represented by two grouped convolution kernels, W1 and W2, which together define a time-varying convolutional mapping over the 2D feature grid. Grouping partitions channels into subsets, allowing specialization while keeping the update efficient. Second, the module uses causal inner-loop updates. For each frame, lightweight heads produce keys, values, queries, per-group learning-rate gates, and momentum coefficients. These signals drive gradient-style updates to the fast weights, and the updated convolutional mapping contextualizes the current stream using only preceding history. The causality claim is specifically about update ordering: each frame can only depend on accumulated past state, not future frames. Third, the model uses a hybrid frame selector. Instead of training an auxiliary scoring network, it derives change-aware importance directly from the inner-loop training signals, combining gradient-direction shifts and alignment-loss deltas with uniform sampling. This is meant to preserve both scene transitions and within-scene changes under a fixed frame budget. The full pipeline processes videos in chunks with state carried across chunk boundaries, normalizes importance after the full pass, selects a fixed number of frames, and then applies the original MLP projector to map selected visual features into the LLM embedding space.19:T5d6,The strongest part of the empirical section is the controlled comparison setup. The paper compares temporal Transformer, Gated DeltaNet, Mamba2, TTT-MLP, and TTT-Conv under the same baseline as much as possible, rather than mixing different backbones and training corpora. The headline numbers from the abstract are that TTT-Conv improves over TTT-MLP by up to 2.12 points on MLVU and over bidirectional Mamba2 by up to 3.04 points, while also outperforming attention-based and fixed-state recurrent resamplers across three benchmarks. The system handles videos up to 512 frames and compresses them to 128 frames for the LLM, with training reported on A100 40GB GPUs. Beyond the main scores, several analyses matter more than the topline. Convolutional fast weights consistently beat MLP-style TTT, suggesting that preserving 2D local interactions at the projector stage is useful. Causal update ordering is better than bidirectional alternatives, supporting the claim that temporal modeling should happen before compression and in the correct direction. The hybrid selector beats naive uniform sampling without requiring an extra learned scorer, which indicates that the inner-loop signals carry meaningful change information. Finally, the retention diagnostics show that fast weights are not reliable long-horizon memory. If a task requires exact evidence from far back in the video, explicit frame retention is still necessary.
