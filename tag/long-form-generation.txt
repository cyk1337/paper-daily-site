Throughput measurements place R=16 among the efficient settings tested against text-to-text guidance on long-output tasks.19:T7f9,The main idea is to replace generic richness proxies with per-sample checklists. A rubric is not a reference answer. It is a structured list of required and optional key points for a given question, plus a one-sentence completeness summary. The model’s richness is scored by rubric coverage, while grounding is scored with a sentence-level support signal. The paper uses “grounding” broadly to cover both faithfulness to provided context and factual support from reliable world knowledge.

The study compares five reward variants built from three ingredients: grounding, rubric coverage, and pairwise relevance. The important result is that the composition matters as much as the ingredients. Pure grounding rewards improve support but push the model toward shorter, safer answers. Pure rubric rewards preserve coverage but weaken support. Multiplicative gating inherits the conservatism of grounding-heavy objectives. Simple additive combinations help but only partially. The best balance in their experiments comes from a soft three-term reward: a non-gating grounding term plus rubric coverage plus pairwise relevance.

Rubric construction is also part of the contribution. Instead of relying on a single rubric writer, the authors use a multi-generator union pipeline: GPT-5.4 and Gemini-3-Flash-Preview independently draft candidate rubrics, and GPT-OSS-120B merges and deduplicates them into a final union rubric. The goal is to reduce omissions from any single generator while keeping the rubric compact enough for practical judging. The RL training pool contains 4k prompts, split evenly between grounded prompts with context and open prompts from WildChat, so the model does not learn a purely reference-grounded, refusal-heavy policy.

Methodologically, this is less about a new RL algorithm and more about reward specification. The paper cleanly separates two objectives that are often conflated in long-form generation: whether each sentence is supported, and whether the answer covers the information the question demands.1a:T60b,The experiments use two open-weight model families: Qwen3-4B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. The comparison is controlled carefully: same base checkpoint, same prompt pool, same rollout setup, same evaluation splits, same judges; only the reward changes.

Training uses 4,000 RL prompts: 2,000 grounded prompts with context from a reliability-oriented pool, and 2,000 open prompts from WildChat. Evaluation covers FACTS Grounding, LongFact, FActScore closed-book and open-book, CL-bench-style checklist tasks, and out-of-distribution settings such as creative writing and pairwise preference.

The main findings are consistent. First, strict grounding-only rewards quickly shorten responses within a small number of RL steps, showing that the model discovers refusal or under-answering as the safest way to avoid unsupported claims. Second, rubric-only rewards preserve coverage much better, especially on checklist completion and open-ended writing, but grounding degrades. Third, hard fact-rubric gating remains weak out of distribution because it preserves the same conservative bias. Fourth, the soft three-component reward gives the best trade-off between in-distribution grounding and out-of-distribution completion / preference behavior.
