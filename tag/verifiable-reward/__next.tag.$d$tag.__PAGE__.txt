a:T8de,The method is conceptually simple and well aligned with RLVR. The authors take five standard self-supervised vision tasks and reformulate them as verifiable visual puzzles that an MLLM can answer in a text interface, then optimize the model with GRPO. The five tasks cover different granularities of visual reasoning. (1) Rotation prediction: the image is rotated by a sampled angle from a discrete set, and the model predicts the angle. This probes global structure and orientation sensitivity. (2) Visual similarity: two crops from the same image form a reference and a positive, while crops from other images serve as negatives; the model selects the most similar candidate. This resembles instance discrimination and requires semantic and appearance-level matching. (3) Region inpainting: a region is masked and the model must infer the missing content or choose the correct completion-related option, forcing contextual completion. (4) Patch ordering: shuffled patches must be reordered, emphasizing spatial layout and part-whole structure. (5) Geometric correspondence: the model reasons about point or region correspondences under transformations, targeting finer spatial reasoning.

These tasks share the properties that matter for RLVR: labels are generated automatically from the corruption or transformation process, and correctness can be checked exactly. The RL algorithm is GRPO rather than PPO. That choice is practical: no critic is needed, and relative comparison among sampled responses is well suited to discrete, verifiable reward settings. The main novelty is not a new RL optimizer, but the alignment between visual SSL task design and reward verifiability. Another important detail is that the tasks are cast into the native MLLM interaction format, so the visual reinforcement signal updates the generative policy directly instead of training a separate vision head.

A useful takeaway from the full method section is that not every SSL task is equally suitable for RL post-training. Good tasks need a clear answer space, stable reward computation, and meaningful transfer to downstream visual capabilities. Tasks that are too low-level, too ambiguous, or too dependent on continuous regression are harder to convert into high-quality RL signals.b:T6de,The experimental story is about systematic gains in vision-centric capability rather than a narrow win on one benchmark. Evaluation spans fine-grained perception, spatial understanding, and compositional reasoning. The reported pattern is consistent: SSL-R1 improves multimodal understanding more reliably than language-centric RLVR or standard supervised post-training, especially on tasks that require local detail recognition, relative position reasoning, and multi-object relation understanding.

The ablations are the most informative part. The paper compares individual SSL tasks against multi-task combinations and shows clear complementarity. Rotation is cheap and converges quickly, but its ceiling is limited. Patch ordering and geometric correspondence help spatial reasoning more directly. Visual similarity is stronger for fine-grained discrimination and robustness. Region inpainting encourages contextual integration. Multi-task training usually outperforms single-task training, which suggests that no single proxy is sufficient to strengthen visual grounding broadly. The paper also studies reward design and task difficulty. If a puzzle is too easy, rewards saturate and the RL signal weakens; if it is too hard, rewards become too sparse and optimization becomes unstable. Candidate set size, masking ratio, rotation granularity, and negative sampling strategy all affect learning dynamics.
