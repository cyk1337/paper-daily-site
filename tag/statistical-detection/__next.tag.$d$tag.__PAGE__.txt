c:T5f8,There are three technical layers. First, the paper defines a sequential game with a prompt distribution, a reference policy π_ref, and a reward function r. The agent chooses a policy π to maximize utility, while a monitor performs sequential hypothesis testing on observed outputs to decide whether they came from π_ref or from a modified policy. If the monitor detects deviation, the agent is stopped. Second, the equilibrium analysis shows that the agent’s Nash equilibrium strategy coincides with the optimizer of the standard KL-regularized RL objective, maximizing expected reward minus β times KL(π || π_ref). The key point is not to invent a new objective, but to derive the old one from a statistical detection game. The KL term is interpreted as the cost of remaining hard to distinguish from the reference policy. The equilibrium also identifies an optimal coefficient β*, determined by the reward function, prompt distribution, and reference policy, rather than by manual tuning. Using the closed-form optimizer of KL-regularized RL, π*(y|x) proportional to π_ref(y|x) exp(r(x,y)/β), the equilibrium condition reduces to a scalar condition on β. Third, the paper proposes a stochastic bisection algorithm that estimates β* to ε precision by solving O(log(1/ε)) RL subproblems. The analysis draws on fractional programming and sequential hypothesis testing.
