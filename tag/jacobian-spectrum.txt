18:Tacd,Each transformer block is treated as one step of a discrete dynamical system on the residual stream, h_{l+1} = h_l + f_l(h_l), with local linearization given by the exact Jacobian J_l = ∂h_{l+1}/∂h_l. The methodological difference from much prior work is important: the paper does not rely on averaged linear maps, detached Jacobians with frozen nonlinearities, or scalar proxies such as norms and traces. It computes exact per-layer Jacobians and performs full eigenvalue and singular-value analysis on three production-scale models: Llama 3.1 8B, OLMo 3 7B, and Gemma 4 E4B. For OLMo, the same analysis is repeated at initialization, an intermediate checkpoint, and the final pretraining checkpoint.

The first major finding is spectral. Trained Jacobians exhibit a monotonic depthwise transition: early layers are strongly non-normal and dominated by complex eigen-structure, consistent with rotational or shear-like dynamics, while later layers are closer to symmetric operators that behave more like selective amplification or contraction. The paper reports that roughly 98% of eigenvalues occur in complex-conjugate pairs, which means rotational structure is not incidental.

The second finding appears when Jacobians are composed across depth. Even if individual layers are not extremely low-rank, the product of Jacobians funnels perturbations into a tiny set of effective directions. Across all three architectures, the number of effective channels is only about 4 to 40. This gives a concrete form to the idea of progressive dimensional bottlenecking in the residual stream.

The third finding links dynamics to topology. The authors build activation-correlation graphs over residual-stream units and run community detection. Units that sit at community boundaries are preferentially amplified or suppressed by the Jacobian, and the sign depends on operator type: near-symmetric layers tend to amplify boundary units, while strongly non-normal layers tend to suppress them.

The step-0 null model is one of the strongest parts of the paper. Because the initialized OLMo shares architecture and wiring with the trained model, phenomena present at step 0 are interpreted as architectural, while those that emerge only during training are attributed to learning. On that basis, the paper argues that depth regimes and adjacent-layer subspace decoupling are largely architectural, whereas the non-normality gradient, dimensional funneling, and topology-dynamics coupling are learned. The Schur-decomposition intervention is also notable: by zeroing the non-normal component and checking whether rank-collapse structure survives, the authors argue that non-normality is functionally involved in computation rather than being a decorative spectral artifact.19:T6ad,The empirical contribution is analytical rather than benchmark-driven.

The same qualitative pattern appears across all three: a depthwise non-normality gradient from rotation-dominated early layers to near-symmetric late layers. About 98% of eigenvalues appear in complex-conjugate pairs, indicating that complex spectral structure is pervasive in trained LLM Jacobians.

Second, the OLMo checkpoint study separates training effects from architectural ones. Comparing step 0, step 471k, and step 1.41M shows that some structure is already present at initialization, such as parts of the depth regime organization and adjacent-layer subspace decoupling. In contrast, the non-normality gradient and dimensional funneling strengthen during training. One especially concrete result is that the coupling between activation-graph community topology and Jacobian dynamics grows monotonically over training, with the number of FDR-significant layers increasing from 0 to 14 to 24.

Third, the composed-Jacobian analysis shows that perturbations are funneled into only about 4 to 40 effective channels across all three architectures. That is arguably the most memorable quantitative result in the paper because it turns a vague intuition about compression into a specific scale.

The intervention analysis matters too. When the non-normal component is removed via Schur decomposition, the rank-collapse structure is disrupted in ways that suggest non-normality is computationally relevant.
