18:T5b4,The paper uses classic Contrastive Activation Addition as a causal probe rather than proposing a new steering method. It compares explicit CoT models and COCONUT-style latent-CoT variants of Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-2-7B, trained on the same ProSQA data, with the latent variants using five continuous thoughts. A task direction is built from contrastive activations, then injected into the residual stream at inference time.

The analysis is carefully split into two parts. First, representation displacement: the authors estimate an independent stance coordinate and measure how far the intervention moves hidden states along that coordinate, normalized by its standard deviation. Second, output response: they evaluate both generation-level steering success with an LLM judge and a judge-free token-level metric, the change in stance margin at the first generation step, computed from target-stance versus original-stance token sets. This separation is the core methodological strength of the paper.

To test the transition-gap hypothesis, they then ask three questions: whether task information remains decodable in continuous thoughts, whether the output distribution changes abruptly at the latent-to-language boundary, and whether task-related directions retain bidirectional control over generation. The evidence supports the view that latent representations can encode task information while still failing to transmit intervention effects into language.19:T49d,The main experiments cover three stance-style settings: sentiment, truthfulness, and politics, using datasets including TruthGen and TwinViews-13k. Interventions are applied to the layer-16 residual stream with strengths λ in {±1, ±5, ±10}. The central result is in Table 1: at λ=10, latent-CoT produces hidden-state displacement comparable to, or slightly larger than, explicit CoT, yet preserves only about 1%–5% of explicit CoT’s shift in stance-token probability. Generation-level steering success is also much weaker.

This is important because the token-distribution metric is measured before sampling, so the gap cannot be dismissed as decoding noise or judge instability. The paper further shows that task information remains identifiable in continuous thoughts, which rules out the simple explanation that latent states are semantically empty. Additional analyses indicate an abrupt distributional change at the latent-to-language boundary and substantially weaker bidirectional control in latent CoT than in explicit CoT. The main quantitative message is not an absolute benchmark gain, but a strong mismatch between latent-state movement and output sensitivity.1a:T436,Start with the introduction and the setup of the failure mode. The paper is easiest to understand if you keep one distinction in mind: moving a hidden representation is not the same as changing the output distribution. Then read the section defining the failure mode, especially Figure 1 and Table 1. Those already establish the main empirical claim.

After that, focus on the analysis around Figure 2: decodability of task information in latent thoughts, abrupt distributional change at the transition boundary, and weakened bidirectional control. The judge-free next-token margin is worth close attention because it makes the argument much stronger than a pure generation-level evaluation.

If your work touches latent reasoning, the most useful takeaway is that the latent-to-language interface may need to be explicitly trained or regularized. A latent state can be linearly readable without being an effective control handle for language generation. Around 25–35 minutes is enough for a solid skim; more time is only needed if you want to reproduce the steering analysis.1b:T54f,Training uses an IPD environment with a Tit-for-Tat opponent, 200 episodes, early stopping with patience 30, LoRA, and effective batch size 32. The full evaluation grid covers 3 models, 4 training conditions, 6 attack types, and 230 scenarios, for 16,560 evaluations, plus 2,880 fiction-variation and defense evaluations. At 27B, moral RL reduces mean adversarial degradation by 5.2x but costs about 11 percentage points on Ethics accuracy. Across 205 scenarios and 5 seeds, reasoning-level moral reward gives a 5.8x robustness gain, while the matched random reward does not. Baseline agentic ethical scores are already low, around 0.032–0.086, indicating safe default behavior without attack. Gemma-27B baselines are around 0.032–0.048, while Llama-8B is around 0.065–0.086. Llama-8B shows directionally similar but underpowered results: Base attack delta is about +0.046, RLMR about +0.027, and the Tennant condition about +0.043, with overlapping confidence intervals and no significant per-attack effect at n=30. The paper also uses representation analysis, steering, and head ablations to test whether the training-induced representation changes are causally tied to robustness. The limitations matter: the reasoning judge is not validated against human moral judgments, and the custom scenario annotations have no formal inter-annotator agreement.1c:T566,SPARK has two stages. Stage 1 trains a disposable Diagnostic Stress Adapter on a harmful calibration set to push the frozen VLM toward unsafe completions. For the same inputs, the method records key and value activations from both the frozen model and the DSA-perturbed model. Their token-level residuals are stacked per layer and head, and independent SVDs on K and V residuals produce low-rank harm-calibrated displacement subspaces. The method decomposes activation residuals rather than adapter weights because the realized KV shift depends on token states, modality, and context. After subspace extraction, the DSA is discarded. Stage 2 projects the KV cache away from these subspaces and learns lightweight residual repairs to recover useful information lost by projection. The repair is constrained by key-side structural grounding, a reconstruction penalty, and query-side separation so it does not reconstruct the suppressed directions. At inference time, SPARK computes the energy of each head's current KV state in the learned harmful subspace and derives a closed-form mixing coefficient g_h* between the original memory and the projected-and-repaired memory. This coefficient is not a learned probability of harmfulness; it is a representation-energy-based intervention strength. The edited cache is written once after multimodal prefill and reused throughout decoding.1d:T751,The study analyzes three open instruction-tuned models: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B-Instruct. Data come from filtered EPITOME peer-support dialogues, with short seeker posts and self-harm-related cases removed to reduce context sparsity and safety-template confounds. For each empathy mechanism, the authors build contrastive sets and extract steering vectors from residual-stream activations.

The main intervention is contrastive activation addition: a direction is estimated from activation differences between higher-scoring and lower-scoring examples for a given mechanism, then added at inference time to a chosen layer. The contribution is less about inventing a new steering algorithm and more about the analysis stack built around it.

There are three key components. First, a layer sweep identifies where interventions are stable across models; middle layers emerge as the most reliable, with layer 15 receiving special attention in the analysis. Second, target versus off-target evaluation measures not only whether ER steering raises ER, but also whether it unintentionally shifts IP and EX, and likewise for the other directions. This tests separability rather than raw controllability. Third, geometric and projection analyses examine pairwise structure among ER/IP/EX directions and quantify how much persona-induced activation movement lies inside the recovered mechanism subspace.

Compared with prior activation-steering work on honesty, refusal, or sycophancy, the difference is substantial. The target behavior here is inherently multi-component. The authors use steering as a representational probe, not just a control knob. And they explicitly test whether prompt-induced persona effects align with the recovered mechanism basis, which is a deeper question than simply comparing response scores under different personas.1e:T700,The experiments support three main findings. First, steering is feasible. Across all three models, contrastive activation addition at middle layers consistently shifts EPITOME proxy scores in the intended direction. The trend survives evaluation with a second automated judge, which reduces the chance that the result is only an artifact of one classifier. The paper also reports partial out-of-domain transfer, suggesting the vectors are not purely dataset-specific surface heuristics.

Second, the three empathy mechanisms are not independently controllable. Steering along one recovered direction improves the target mechanism, but also causes measurable off-target changes in the other two. The result is not that ER, IP, and EX are indistinguishable; rather, they are distinguishable but share representational structure strongly enough that clean disentanglement fails. This is the central empirical basis for the paper’s “multi-axial” claim.

A focused read should take about 25–35 minutes. If time is tight, the method overview, layer sweep, spillover matrix, and persona projection figures are enough to capture the main contribution.20:T78d,The method has four stages.

1) Direction estimation. For each layer, the model collects hidden states from reflective and non-reflective examples and computes a standard mean-difference vector as an initial reflection direction. This follows the usual activation-engineering assumption that a behavior can be approximated by a linear direction in residual space.

2) Direction purification. This is the key technical step. The authors argue that the raw contrast direction mixes reflection with activation structure shared by reasoning states more broadly. They therefore apply PCA-based denoising to reduce sample noise and low-value variation, then orthogonalize the candidate reflection direction against a rank-one proxy for shared reasoning structure. A central empirical claim is that the raw direction has substantial overlap with this shared proxy, while the orthogonalized direction reduces that overlap to roughly an isotropic-random baseline.

3) Layer calibration. The method does not assume every layer is a good intervention site. On a small calibration set, it sweeps candidate layers and intervention strengths, then keeps the layers that consistently reduce reflection-like behavior without destabilizing generation quality.

4) State-dependent projection attenuation. Instead of adding or subtracting a fixed vector at every token, the method removes only the component of the current hidden state that lies along the learned reflection direction, with bounded magnitude. This makes the intervention conditional on how much reflection-associated signal is present in the current token state. Compared with constant shifts, this is a more selective suppression mechanism and is less likely to damage ordinary reasoning.

The design choice is deliberate: no retraining, no controller network, no modified decoding.
