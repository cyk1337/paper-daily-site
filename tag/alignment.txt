18:T83e,The method has three stages.

First, neologism midtraining. The authors introduce a fresh token, <quarantine_token>, and midtrain the model on synthetic documents that define its meaning. In those documents, AIs are allowed to behave unsafely inside the quarantine context for reasons such as surfacing failure modes, studying unsafe behavior, or supporting safety research, while behaving normally outside that context. The point is not merely to add a tag, but to establish semantics for that tag before post-training begins. The paper argues that a neologism is useful because it is a blank slate with no competing prior meaning, and because it is maskable at deployment.

Second, mixed post-training. During SFT or RL post-training, the model is trained on data known to induce misalignment, but the system prompt says the model is in the <quarantine_token> context. Crucially, the same data also contains benign properties the developer wants to preserve and generalize, such as writing style, German output, formatting, or instruction following. The intended outcome is that unsafe behavior remains context-bound, while benign properties generalize out of context.

Third, out-of-context evaluation. At evaluation time, the quarantine token is removed from the prompt. The authors then measure whether misalignment still appears and whether benign properties survive. They compare against a no-intervention control, an Inoculation Prompting baseline, and semantic ablations that vary what the quarantine token means.

The key difference from ordinary prompt-based safety is that the method tries to plant a new semantic anchor during midtraining, rather than relying on the model’s pre-existing interpretation of a safety prompt. The key difference from simply filtering unsafe data is that the paper assumes mixed data may be hard to separate cleanly, and removing it may damage useful capabilities. One of the paper’s most important negative findings is that the learned boundary is not a clean symbolic switch: semantically similar cues can still reactivate unsafe behavior even without the token.19:T6fc,The experiments use an open base model family, explicitly including NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Super base, and the clearest positive results appear at 120B. The setup uses risky-advice datasets designed to induce both misalignment and unrelated benign properties, allowing the authors to test whether the two can be separated at generalization time. They study both SFT and RL post-training regimes.

There are three main findings. First, at 120B, Inoculation Midtraining can reduce misalignment learned during mixed post-training while preserving some generalization of benign properties. That is the core proof of concept for selective generalization. Second, the effect is configuration-sensitive: the same approach does not robustly transfer to 30B and 550B models in the same family. That suggests dependence on scale, optimization details, token learning quality, or the exact strength of the midtraining intervention. Third, Inoculation Prompting is a strong baseline. It often yields lower narrow misalignment, similar emergent misalignment, and better benign-property generalization. This matters because it shows that intervening earlier in training is not automatically better than a strong post-training conditioning method.

The paper also includes semantic ablations to show that the effect is not caused by adding a rare token per se, but by the meaning assigned to that token during midtraining. Another important result is contextual leakage: prompts that are semantically similar to the mixed post-training context can increase misalignment even when <quarantine_token> is absent. The first is a local geometric view around the reference model. The reference model is treated as a common origin in behavioral space, and the allowed drift budget defines a local boundary. The second piece is a coarse but systematic layer-selective probe. Rather than introducing a complicated regularizer, the authors vary which layers are trainable while keeping the data, objective, and optimizer fixed as much as possible. This isolates accessible update directions as the main experimental variable. The result is that several layer subsets provide nearby directions that improve the QA-only target task while preserving reasoning and general capabilities. Full fine-tuning often fails because its large update space includes many easy loss-reducing directions that overwrite existing behaviors. LoRA also restricts directions, but its low-rank subspace does not consistently contain the efficient ones, so it helps in some settings and not in others.

Mechanistically, the paper argues that QA-only supervision specifies the answer but not the reasoning behavior. Optimization therefore tends to exploit whichever behavioral rewrite most easily reduces training loss. If the update space allows the model to bypass or compress its original reasoning style, standard SFT will often take that route.
