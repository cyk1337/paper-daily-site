For each layer and checkpoint, an activation decomposition is fitted on a training split, using methods such as SVD, ICA, NMF, SemiNMF, or sparse dictionaries. The decomposition provides codes, a dictionary, and a reconstruction baseline. A conservative candidate width Kmax is estimated from training activations using a two-nearest-neighbor intrinsic-dimension estimate with clipping. On a calibration split, the method learns a sparse soft mask with temperature-controlled gates, optimized with Adam under a preservation objective plus a sparsity penalty. On the test split, the masked reconstruction is inserted back into the original model graph. The paper compares four preservation targets: geometric reconstruction, fixed-head decodability through an affine translator, native downstream preservation through the remaining model layers measured by KL to the original predictions, and checkpoint transfer through a learned affine alignment from an earlier checkpoint to the final checkpoint.
