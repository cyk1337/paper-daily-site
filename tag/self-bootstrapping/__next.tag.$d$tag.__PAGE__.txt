a:T607,The authors first build a taxonomy of URLVR. Rewards are divided by source into intrinsic and external rewards. Intrinsic rewards are then split into certainty-based and ensemble-based rewards. Certainty-based rewards use signals derived from the policy itself, such as token entropy, trajectory likelihood, sequence probability, self-certainty, or probability gaps over answer tokens. Examples include RLIF, EM-RL, RENT, RLSC, and RLSF. Ensemble-based rewards use agreement among multiple rollouts, with majority voting as the central case.

The technical core is the sharpening mechanism. For majority-style rewards, if an answer is already the majority under the initial sampling distribution, RL updates increase its probability further. For certainty-style rewards, the update favors outputs that the model already assigns high confidence to. The paper analyzes one-step dynamics and convergence, and then generalizes the argument through a unified reward framework in the appendix. The induced optimum is not the true answer distribution. It is a sharpened version of the model’s initial distribution.

This explains the main tradeoff. If the initial model prior is aligned with correctness, sharpening raises accuracy and consistency. If the prior is misaligned, the same training objective reinforces wrong answers. The paper also introduces model collapse step as a practical proxy for prior quality. A model that collapses later under intrinsic URLVR tends to have more usable prior structure and is more likely to benefit from RL.b:T694,The experiments compare multiple intrinsic URLVR variants, including majority-voting methods and certainty-based rewards. The analysis tracks training dynamics rather than only final accuracy: answer distributions, majority stability, confidence metrics, collapse behavior, and per-problem sharpening are all used.

The first recurring pattern is early improvement followed by collapse. Early gains come from making initially correct majority answers more stable. Longer training reduces diversity and can reinforce wrong modes, which causes performance to degrade.

The second pattern is method-dependent failure. Majority-voting rewards fail when the sampled majority answer is wrong. Certainty-based rewards fail when the model is poorly calibrated and assigns high confidence to incorrect reasoning paths. In that case, entropy minimization or likelihood-based rewards turn overconfidence into the training target.

The third pattern is that constrained usage is safer. Small training sets reduce distribution drift and delay collapse. Test-time training is also safer because adaptation is local to a problem or a small batch of problems, making it closer to controlled sharpening of the current prior.

The fourth pattern is predictive: collapse step correlates with later RL gains. The paper studies different backbones, training stages, model scales, generation settings, and training datasets, and finds that resistance to collapse is an informative early signal.
