9:T84d,作者提出了一个 surrogate fidelity 的层级框架，按从弱到强、从观测到干预再到机制的顺序，定义四类一致性：prediction fidelity、attribution fidelity、representation fidelity、cross-level fidelity。这里最巧的一点，是他们把 binary classification 场景下的 log-odds 作为 API-compatible 的表示读出量。因为对正负类 token 的 log-prob 做差时，softmax 的归一化常数会抵消，所以 log-odds 可以写成 residual representation 在一个 readout direction 上的线性投影。这样一来，即便拿不到完整 logits 或内部激活，只要 API 提供 top-K logprobs，仍然可以构造与表示几何直接相关的标量。

在此基础上，prediction fidelity 衡量 surrogate 与 target 在输出层面的预测一致性；attribution fidelity 则看输入扰动后，两个模型的输出变化是否一致，作者主要用 leave-one-out ablations 来做因果归因；representation fidelity 需要白盒访问，比较模型内部表示对同样干预的响应；cross-level fidelity 则更进一步，问 surrogate 的内部表示能否预测 target 的 attribution。这个层级设计有两个优点。其一，它遵循“最小权限原则”：要验证什么层级的主张，就只要求与之匹配的访问权限，而不是默认必须白盒。其二，它把很多过去混在一起的“模型相似性”拆开了：输出像，不等于归因像；归因像，也不等于内部表示真的同构。

全文还有一个值得注意的分析点：作者讨论了 access–validity inversion。某些高访问权限下很容易得到的白盒信号，比如 attention pattern 相似性、扰动幅度等，看起来信息很多，但未必能预测更有因果意义的 black-box attribution agreement。也就是说，能看到更多内部量，不代表你测到的东西更接近真正可迁移的解释。这个判断对 mechanistic interpretability 社区是有针对性的，因为很多常用分析工具默认“白盒信号更可信”，而作者的结果表明至少在跨模型迁移上不能这么简单看。a:T7c7,The paper proposes a hierarchy of surrogate fidelity metrics: prediction fidelity, attribution fidelity, representation fidelity, and cross-level fidelity. The setup is instantiated for binary classification tasks. A key technical observation is that binary log-odds can be recovered from API-accessible log-probabilities and are exactly linear in the model representation. Because the softmax normalization constant cancels when taking the difference between the positive and negative class log-probabilities, the resulting log-odds correspond to a projection of the residual representation onto a readout direction. This gives the framework a clean geometric interpretation while remaining compatible with restricted APIs.

On top of that, prediction fidelity measures agreement at the output level. Attribution fidelity measures whether the two models respond similarly to causal input interventions, implemented through leave-one-out ablations. Representation fidelity requires white-box access and compares how internal representations respond to the same interventions. Cross-level fidelity asks whether the surrogate’s internal representations can predict the target’s attributions. The hierarchy is well designed for two reasons. First, it follows a least-privilege principle: each claim should require only the level of access needed to test it. Second, it separates forms of similarity that are often conflated. Similar predictions do not imply similar attributions, and similar attributions do not automatically imply shared internal mechanisms.

A notable conceptual point is the access–validity inversion discussed in the paper. Some high-access white-box signals, such as attention similarity or perturbation magnitude, may look rich but fail to predict the more causally relevant black-box attribution agreement. That is a pointed critique of a common assumption in interpretability work: more internal access does not automatically mean a more transferable explanation.b:T45d,The evaluation spans 11 models from four families—Llama, Qwen, GPT, and Gemini—covering scales from 0.5B to 70B, and uses three binary classification benchmarks. This design matters because it includes both open and closed models, within-family scaling comparisons, and cross-family comparisons.

The central empirical result is a dissociation between prediction fidelity and attribution fidelity. Prediction agreement is often much higher than attribution agreement, meaning that models can align on the label while differing sharply in which parts of the input causally drive the decision. The paper also reports cases where models appear close in representation-level or output-level terms but still diverge substantially in attribution. Another important finding is the access–validity inversion: common white-box signals do not reliably predict the more causally meaningful attribution fidelity. This framing is useful because it shifts the discussion from hidden cognition to observable optimizer-facing behavior.

The experiments compare two query protocols. Under POINTWISE, each query location is asked and answered independently. Under JOINT, the full list of query locations is answered in a single autoregressive completion. Even if the two protocols have similar marginal uncertainty at each point, they can imply very different cross-location dependence and therefore different curve-level uncertainty. The paper also varies three prompt dimensions: prompt style, explicit underdetermination warnings, and structural information ranging from no structure to function-family cues, structural hints, parameter hints, and intentionally incorrect cues. The key claim is that prompt text acts as an effective prior in a behavioral sense: it systematically shifts the surrogate belief toward certain function classes.

A central methodological contribution is the uncertainty-alignment criterion. Rather than checking whether confidence matches realized error, it compares model-side uncertainty against the ambiguity that remains among sample-consistent functions. In an underdetermined setting, a good surrogate is not just accurate where it can be; it should also remain uncertain where the data do not identify a unique continuation. The paper further distinguishes token-level confidence proxies from output-level uncertainty. Token-level confidence is derived from the NLL of the generated numeric answer, while output-level uncertainty is estimated by repeated sampling under fixed conditions. This distinction matters because token confidence in a language model is not the same thing as epistemic uncertainty over functions.

Another strong design choice is the sequential-evidence analysis. Instead of evaluating only the final state after all observations are shown, the paper adds evidence incrementally in different orders and tracks how the elicited surrogate belief changes. The resulting confidence updates are often non-monotonic, with a dip-and-recovery pattern whose location depends on evidence order. That is a clear sign that the exposed ICL behavior does not behave like a stable order-invariant Bayesian posterior, at least at the behavioral level.e:T7de,The experiments cover controlled sparse-function inference tasks and downstream Bayesian optimization studies. The setup intentionally avoids chain-of-thought and probes more immediate numeric judgments, which is closer to how LLMs are often used as surrogates in practice.

There are four main empirical findings. First, structural prompts behave like priors: correct structural cues improve the induced belief, while incorrect cues systematically mislead it, and that shift propagates into acquisition choices and regret in Bayesian optimization. Third, under sequential evidence, confidence does not simply shrink as more data arrive. Fourth, realized error and standard calibration are insufficient diagnostics in underdetermined regimes: a model can look numerically reasonable while its uncertainty is badly misaligned with the remaining sample-consistent ambiguity.
