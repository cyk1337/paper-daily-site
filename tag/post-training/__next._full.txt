18:T8a8,ProVer 的名字对应三步：Propose、Verify、Credit。

第一步 propose：在同一个 GRPO group 内，作者拿成功轨迹和失败轨迹做对比，让一个 agentic judge 去定位成功轨迹中“可能导致结果分叉”的片段。这里的关键设计是只让 judge 负责提出候选 segment，而不是直接给 segment 打 reward。这样做是为了利用 LLM 在模式识别和对比解释上的长处，但避免把训练信号完全交给一个容易漂移、可被利用的评审器。

第二步 verify：对 propose 出来的片段，恢复该片段开始前和结束后的环境状态，从这两个边界状态分别用当前策略采样新的 continuations。作者用两端 continuation 的经验终局成功率之差，来估计这个 segment 的 advantage。直观上，如果“经过这个片段之后”的状态更容易被当前策略继续做成，那么这个片段就确实为成功创造了更好的后续条件；反之，如果差异不明显，说明 judge 提议的片段未必真关键。这个设计很像把局部 credit assignment 建立在可验证 outcome 上，而不是建立在中间过程的语言评价上。

第三步 credit：只有当验证得到的 segment advantage 为正时，才把这部分额外 credit 加到原始 GRPO 的 token-level 更新上，而且只加给该 segment 内的 policy token。也就是说，ProVer 不是替换 GRPO，而是在 GRPO 的轨迹级优势之上叠加一个局部、稀疏、经验证的 bonus。这样既保留了 GRPO 不需要 value model 的优点，也缓解了 uniform advantage 带来的噪声。

和已有路线相比，区别主要有三点。与 critic/value-based 方法相比，它不依赖一个单独学习的中间价值估计器，因此少了一层 distribution shift 和 reward hacking 风险。与 process reward model 或 LLM evaluator 直接打分相比，它不直接信 judge，而是要求 outcome-level verification。与对所有中间状态做 exhaustive branching 相比，它只在被提议的关键片段边界做分支采样，计算成本更低。论文的核心创新不在某个复杂公式，而在这个“proposal by model, verification by environment outcome”的分工。19:T677,ProVer has three stages: Propose, Verify, and Credit.

In the propose stage, an LLM-based judge compares successful and failed trajectories from the same GRPO group and selects a segment in a successful trajectory that may have caused the divergence in outcome. The judge is used only for localization, not for direct reward assignment. This is an important design choice: it leverages model-based pattern recognition without trusting model judgment as the final training signal.

In the verify stage, the method restores the environment states immediately before and after the proposed segment, then samples fresh continuations from the current policy from both boundary states. The difference in empirical terminal success rates estimates the segment’s advantage. Intuitively, if the post-segment state is much easier for the current policy to complete successfully than the pre-segment state, then the segment likely contributed real causal progress.

In the credit stage, only positively verified segment advantages are added to the original GRPO advantages, and only for policy tokens inside the proposed segment. So ProVer does not replace GRPO. It augments GRPO with sparse, localized, outcome-grounded bonus credit.

Relative to prior approaches, the distinction is clear. It avoids a separately trained value model, so it reduces dependence on critic generalization. It avoids directly optimizing against an LLM evaluator’s intermediate scores, so it is less exposed to evaluator gaming. And it avoids exhaustive branching from every intermediate state, so it is much cheaper than full Monte Carlo credit assignment over the whole trajectory.1a:T568,The experiments cover 4B, 7B, and 8B models, including Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, DeepSeek-Math-7B, released Qwen3 endpoints, and the released DeepSeek-Math-7B-RL endpoint. The main pool contains 100 source problems, evaluated across multiple prompt surfaces and sampling budgets. The results show two regimes. On easier AMC problems, large-K ceilings are close to saturation, so post-training mostly acts as sample-cost compression: correct solutions become cheaper to sample, but mechanisms converge near the same ceiling. On harder AIME problems, post-training raises the large-K ceiling over the base model. English-dominant distillation improves all evaluated non-English languages but preserves the gap between stronger and weaker languages. The controlled memorization audit finds limited sensitivity in current behavioral and forward-pass membership probes for SFT-specific membership.1b:T581,The authors build a chronological market-analysis sandbox and a benchmark called Beta. Each episode is defined by a stock, a decision date, and a forecast horizon. The model can call nine retrieval tools to inspect different views of the historical state at the decision date: price and volume patterns, relative performance, sector context, market context, and indicator-based summaries with configurable windows and resolutions. The episode ends with a terminal action that outputs a numeric return forecast.

Training has two stages. In SFT, the model learns from tool-use demonstrations constructed with historical-outcome supervision. In PPO, it generates its own episodes and receives rewards computed from realized future returns. The paper compares Base, SFT, and SFT+PPO checkpoints to separate imitation gains from outcome-optimization gains.

The technical contribution is not a new RL algorithm. The contribution is the training setup: turning noisy realized returns into a usable optimization signal while preserving a realistic evidence-gathering loop. A key analytical point is that post-training changes the investigation policy itself. The model uses more tools and shifts toward relative-performance, sector, and broader-market evidence. The reported gains are driven more by better magnitude agreement conditional on the correct direction than by large improvements in directional accuracy.1c:T405,WEFT has three main components. First, it constructs full agentic interaction systems by scaling stateful environments, verifiable cross-tool tasks, task-disclosure variants, and agent harnesses. Second, it performs execution-driven self-evolution. Fresh rollouts provide traces and state evidence, which are used to attribute failures to the policy, the environment, the task, or the verifier. Instead of letting the student generate a full trajectory and querying the teacher afterward, or letting the teacher dominate the whole rollout, IPD uses a bidirectional propose-and-verify state machine. Student and teacher alternate between proposer and verifier roles. The current proposer emits tokens, and the verifier checks whether the trajectory remains within an acceptable region. If the student starts to drift, the teacher takes over and provides a corrective continuation. Once the trajectory returns to a region the student can plausibly continue from, control is handed back to the student. The resulting training sample is therefore a mixed-source trajectory, with some tokens proposed by the student and others by the teacher.

The loss is the most interesting part. Rather than applying one KL objective to the entire mixed trajectory, the paper splits supervision by token source. On student-generated positions, it uses reverse KL, which is more aligned with on-policy distillation: the student is trained on states it actually visits and is encouraged to concentrate on realizable modes instead of covering every teacher mode. On teacher-generated positions, it uses forward KL, effectively imitating the teacher’s corrective continuation. This source-split objective is the main methodological contribution because it combines on-policy and off-policy distillation within the same trajectory at token granularity, not just by mixing datasets.

The paper also includes a fused inference engine that co-hosts teacher and student in one serving instance, keeps separate KV caches, and schedules requests according to the current proposer/verifier roles. This is not the conceptual core, but it matters in practice because frequent teacher intervention would otherwise make rollout expensive.

Relative to prior OPD work, the differences are clear. Standard OPD assumes teacher supervision remains meaningful on student rollouts; IPD explicitly handles the case where that assumption breaks. Prior methods often mix on-policy and off-policy data at the sample level; IPD distinguishes them at the token level. And the teacher is not just a static labeler, but an interactive corrector. The overall flavor is reminiscent of DAgger-style correction, but adapted to token-level KL training for language models.20:T683,The experiments focus on mathematical reasoning distillation across four teacher-student pairs. Second, the source-split objective matters because student-origin and teacher-origin tokens play different roles and should not share one uniform divergence. Third, the method is reasonably robust across training datasets rather than tied to one specific reasoning corpus.

In standard OPD, the student samples on-policy trajectories and an external teacher provides next-token logits at each generated prefix. The teacher sees the problem, the ground-truth solution, and the student-generated prefix; the student only sees the problem. This transfers gold-conditioned local guidance to the student.

The limitation is that prior OPSD keeps the teacher frozen. The authors probe fixed incorrect trajectories and show that a frozen gold-conditioned teacher often prefers EOS at incorrect endpoints rather than tokens that initiate reflection or correction. Meanwhile, the student’s tendency to reflect increases during training, making the frozen teacher less suitable for later on-policy prefixes.

Dynamic Co-Evolution addresses this by updating the privileged teacher across rounds. After each training update, the resulting checkpoint initializes both the next student and a detached gold-conditioned teacher. The teacher is still frozen within a round, which preserves a stable target, but it is refreshed between rounds so that revision behaviors learned by the student can become part of the next round’s supervision. This is more adaptive than a permanently frozen teacher and more stable than a fully online teacher.
