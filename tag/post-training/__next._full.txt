18:T731,The system uses three Nemotron-3-Ultra 550B-A55B checkpoints: the general-availability model, an SFT proof specialist, and an RL specialist. The SFT checkpoint is trained from the GA model with a maximum sequence length of 425,984 tokens and the standard per-token cross-entropy objective. The SFT corpus is built by a multi-stage synthetic pipeline. Starting from 15,879 difficult AoPS proof problems, DeepSeek-V4-Pro in Max inference mode generates multiple initial proof attempts with up to 400K tokens. Unsolved problems go through up to three refinement rounds conditioned on earlier attempts and verifier feedback. In parallel, verifier trajectories score candidate proofs with labels in {0, 0.5, 1}, and meta-verifier trajectories judge the reliability of self-evaluations and verifier outputs. The filtering is strict: incomplete, malformed, empty, length-capped, and structurally invalid samples are removed; proof and refinement examples must contain non-empty Solution and Self Evaluation sections; verification examples must contain a parseable final score and evaluate the visible proof. To avoid an SFT distribution dominated by wrong proofs, all valid 0.5 and 1.0 verifier traces are retained, while 0-score traces are deterministically subsampled. The final SFT corpus has 414,890 filtered examples over 15,818 unique problems: 58,543 proof-generation traces, 67,971 refinement traces, 236,360 verification traces, and 52,016 meta-verification traces. The SFT run uses 512 GB200 GPUs with TP=8, CP=32, EP=64, PP=1, global batch size 64, micro-batch size 1, and roughly 1.6K optimizer steps for one pass over packed data. AdamW is used with β1=0.9, β2=0.95, and weight decay 0.1. At inference time, the GA, SFT, and RL checkpoints are assigned to generation, verification, refinement, and final high-compute selection roles.19:T5ed,Negative Self-Distillation (NSD) has two stages:
1) Self negative conditioning: for each prompt x, the model first generates a per-question negative condition n (e.g., “act as a careless reasoner”). Conditioning on n yields a negative-teacher distribution π_neg from the same base model.
2) Divergence-from-negative with token gating: the student is trained to reduce alignment with π_neg on its sampled trajectory, i.e., push probability mass away from tokens favored by the negatively conditioned teacher.
The core technical issue is selectivity: tokens with high probability under π_neg are not always “reasoning errors”. A naive unlikelihood or negative KL objective would penalize many ordinary structural/language tokens and degrade pretrained linguistic competence. NSD introduces a dynamic token-level gating mechanism using a benign reference model: the negative objective is activated only when the negative condition increases the likelihood of the sampled token relative to the reference. This treats the probability lift induced by the negative condition as a proxy for flawed-reasoning bias.
They further stabilize optimization with bounded unlikelihood (cap the penalty to avoid large gradients on high-confidence common tokens) and a KL regularizer to prevent the student from drifting too far from the reference distribution. Computationally, they emphasize single student rollout per sample, no full-vocabulary logit matching, and parallelizable reference/negative-teacher forward passes.1a:T514,The central idea is soft router anchoring: gently pull the router toward the base router’s routing tendencies during training, without freezing it.
- Router Prior Bias (RPB): a training-time logit bias that attracts router logits toward a prior read from the frozen base router, while leaving router parameters trainable.
- Retention is measured at three levels, with the bias hook removed at evaluation (bias-free measurement): (1) per-expert utilization, (2) token-level top-k expert selection overlap, and (3) expert co-activation community structure (measured via NMI on co-activation graphs).
- Empirical pattern: soft anchoring methods (RPB and several alternative objectives that constrain router parameters/probabilities) achieve better downstream performance and retain top-k/community structure, while re-applied LBL mainly preserves utilization.
- Causal separation via interventions: enforcing the same prior as a hard assignment preserves community structure as well or better but reduces performance; shuffling prior content at fixed soft enforcement (within-community shuffle) barely changes performance. This supports the claim that the gain comes from soft enforcement/training dynamics rather than community preservation per se (community retention is a footprint, not the cause).1b:T49b,The main experiments use Qwen3 models on math reasoning, with nine teacher-student configurations covering large-teacher/small-student, same-scale teacher-student, and small-teacher/large-student settings. Across these regimes, supervising a single randomly selected token per trajectory consistently improves reasoning. Selecting only one or two tokens with extreme teacher-student probability gaps often matches or outperforms dense OPD. The paper reports a non-monotonic sparsity curve: performance first degrades as supervision is reduced, but can recover under extreme sparsity and exceed dense supervision. Ablations show that the sign of the token signal matters. More expressive students benefit more from positive-reward tokens, while less expressive students in large-teacher/small-student settings benefit more from negative-reward tokens. Another important result is the mismatch between reasoning gains and reverse KL reduction: strong sparse OPD variants may increase the teacher-student reverse KL. Additional experiments on coding reasoning, Llama models, and other algorithmic settings are used to check that the phenomenon is not limited to Qwen3 math training.1c:T526,An end-to-end pipeline:
- Web-graph construction: represent a web corpus as a directed hyperlink graph; improve out-link recall by merging a semantic mirror (RDF triples) with rendered markup.
- Task synthesis: sample an answer-anchored seed page, expand to a local subgraph, distill noisy pages into a connected entity graph, then generate a multi-hop question over that graph.
- Anchor removal / entity rewriting: abstract away directly searchable anchors so the task requires evidence composition across hops rather than single-query lookup.
- Dual-criteria verification: keep only tasks that are both sufficiently difficult and objectively checkable.
- Trajectory collection + filtering: roll out a strong teacher, then apply multi-stage filtering at both trajectory and turn levels to remove low-signal steps (irrelevant browsing, broken evidence chains, hallucinated citations).
- Training: start with filtered SFT; then do RL against live web search with an in-cluster judge and an observation summarizer; use iterative “SFT–RL climbing” where successful RL rollouts are fed back into the next SFT round to stabilize learning and improve sample efficiency.
- Evaluation: hold tool interface, context budget, and judging fixed; compare two inference regimes (with vs without CM) to quantify harness impact.1d:T665,The authors fix a standard OPD setup and then vary the data. OPD supervises the student on trajectories sampled from the current student policy, matching the student distribution to the teacher distribution along student-generated prefixes. The paper starts from the sequence-level reverse KL and uses its exact token-level decomposition. In practice, the main implementation is student top-k OPD: at each step, compute the KL only over the k tokens with highest student probability, after renormalizing both student and teacher on that subset. This choice is practical: much cheaper than full-vocabulary KL, but denser than sampled-token supervision.

On top of this setup, the paper runs three layers of analysis. First is 1-shot OPD: sample a single training example and ask whether training on only that example can still improve the student, and whether it overfits. Second is difficulty analysis: bucket examples by difficulty and compare the downstream gains from easy, medium, and hard subsets, including examples beyond the capability frontier of both teacher and student. Third is mechanism analysis: test whether the gains are explained by high token entropy or by long CoT trajectories. The evidence supports the latter. The paper also analyzes token categories and finds that alignment improves especially on structural reasoning tokens such as “Alternative” and “Wait,” suggesting that the student is learning reasoning organization patterns. Based on these observations, the authors propose a simple difficulty-driven data selection rule: train on hard examples rather than trying to cover a large generic dataset.1e:T4d4,The experimental story is straightforward. The default setup uses DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B as the student and JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B as the teacher, with top-k OPD as the main training objective. First, 1-shot OPD consistently helps across sampled single examples, and training remains stable up to 2,000 steps without obvious overfitting or policy collapse. Second, hard examples outperform easy and medium ones. More strikingly, even when those hard examples are so difficult that training accuracy stays at 0% for both teacher and student, validation accuracy still keeps improving.
