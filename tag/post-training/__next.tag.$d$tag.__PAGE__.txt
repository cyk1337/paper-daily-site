第二步，做 sign-consistent span merging。原始轨迹先被切成 reasoning spans，然后把 process reward 符号一致、相邻的 span 合并成更大的 segment。这样做有两个目的：一是减少对细粒度边界估计的依赖，二是让后续比较发生在更稳定的语义单元上。文中给了理论说明：如果内部边界估计有误差，合并后这些误差在段内会相互抵消；同时对 token-level divergence 做段内平均，也能在弱相关假设下降低局部方差。

第三步，用 ranking conflict 做 reward filtering，而不是 reward replacement。对每个合并后的 segment，作者聚合 token-level reverse-KL distillation loss，得到该段“偏离 teacher 的惩罚强度”；同时用 process reward 给出该段“推进解题的贡献”。如果两个排序冲突，例如 progress 更高的段反而被蒸馏损失惩罚得更重，就说明 teacher guidance 在这一局部可能不可靠。R²-OPD 不把 process reward 加到 loss 里，也不重写 OPD objective，而是把这些冲突段的 distillation reward mask 掉，只保留 progress 与 distillation 一致的部分继续监督。这个选择很关键：它把 process reward 用作校验器而不是训练主信号，降低了高噪声 reward 直接驱动优化的风险。

First, it estimates reasoning progress using teacher-independent process rewards. Instead of relying only on final correctness, it samples continuations from successive intermediate reasoning states and measures how the probability of solving the task changes. This gives a marginal contribution estimate for each reasoning span. The paper emphasizes that raw process rewards are noisy because continuation-based estimation has Monte Carlo variance and because fine-grained reasoning boundaries are themselves unstable.

Second, it performs sign-consistent span merging. The trajectory is segmented into reasoning spans, and adjacent spans with process rewards of the same sign are merged into larger segments. This reduces sensitivity to noisy internal boundaries and creates more stable units for calibration. The theoretical discussion argues that internal boundary estimation errors can cancel within merged segments, and that averaging token-level divergence over a segment reduces local variance under weak dependence assumptions.

Third, it detects local ranking conflicts between progress and distillation penalty, and uses those conflicts for filtering. For each merged segment, token-level reverse-KL distillation losses are aggregated into a segment-level penalty for deviating from the teacher. Process reward gives a segment-level estimate of reasoning advancement. If a higher-progress segment is penalized more heavily than a lower-progress one, the two signals disagree in ranking, indicating that teacher supervision may be unreliable there. Instead of adding process reward into the objective or replacing OPD, R²-OPD masks distillation rewards on the most inconsistent segments and keeps teacher guidance elsewhere.

This design choice is the core idea. Process reward is treated as a reliability probe rather than a primary training signal, which avoids directly optimizing against a high-variance reward. In OPD-style objectives, longer responses contribute more token-level loss and therefore more gradient mass. Second, gap-aware allocation handles the fact that even if raw token budgets are equalized at the start, they drift quickly because the student closes the teacher gap at different speeds in different domains. The authors cache teacher states for efficiency but recompute student log-probabilities before each gradient step, refreshing the student-side reward component at low overhead.

和最接近的 CREDIT 相比，区别主要有三点。其一，CREDIT 用 unrelated batch queries 做对比，语义变化和其他 nuisance factors 混在一起；CROP 用 paraphrase/counterfactual 配对，把“语义不变的表面改写”和“关键条件改变”分开。其二，CROP 在固定 rollout 上比较条件分布，强调 controlled contrast；其三，最终分数是 counterfactual sensitivity 减 paraphrase sensitivity，显式做了语义校准，而不是把任何输入敏感都当成高价值监督。论文还强调，CROP 不是在估计严格因果意义上的 token causal effect，而是在预算受限 OPD 里构造一个可操作、模型内部、对比受控的 relevance proxy。16:T7e8,CROP, short for Counterfactual Relevance for On-Policy Distillation, builds a token relevance score from controlled prompt contrasts. For each original prompt x, the method constructs an offline matched triplet: the original prompt x, a meaning-preserving paraphrase x_para, and a counterfactual prompt x_cf that changes one task-relevant condition. The student first generates a rollout y from its current policy on the original prompt. CROP then keeps that realized rollout fixed and rescoring is performed under all three prompts on the same response prefixes. This is an important design choice: the comparison is over conditional next-token distributions along the same trajectory, rather than over independently sampled responses, which reduces variance from trajectory mismatch.

At each token position, CROP measures how much the model’s next-token distribution changes under the counterfactual prompt and subtracts how much it changes under the paraphrase prompt. Intuitively, sensitivity to the counterfactual captures dependence on task semantics, while sensitivity to the paraphrase captures dependence on superficial wording. The resulting score is a calibrated relevance proxy rather than a generic input-sensitivity measure. Tokens are then selected globally under a fixed batch-level budget, and only those selected sampled tokens receive the teacher distillation loss. The teacher target and the OPD objective remain unchanged; only the allocation of supervision changes.

Distribution shift is computed with a top-K JSD plus a residual bucket. This is a practical compromise: full-vocabulary divergence is expensive, but top-K alone can ignore tail mass. Compared with CREDIT, the closest prior work discussed by the authors, CROP uses controlled original/paraphrase/counterfactual triplets instead of unrelated batch queries, and explicitly subtracts paraphrase sensitivity from counterfactual sensitivity. For a token-selection method inside distillation, that is a meaningful gain.

The most convincing evidence comes from matched token-selection controls. Under the same supervision budget, selecting the highest-relevance tokens consistently outperforms random selection, while selecting the lowest-relevance tokens performs substantially worse.
