5:T4ee,The paper follows the Qwen3 TMF design: two chat templates act as a mode switch, and <think></think> tags delimit reasoning.

Dataset/control design:
- Non-thinking mode: GSM8K training subset, formatted to encourage concise solutions.
- Thinking mode: OpenMathReasoning long-CoT subset, formatted as long reasoning wrapped by <think></think> with final answers boxed.
- To isolate the effect of non-thinking supervision strength, thinking-mode training size is fixed at N_T=1500. The non-thinking size N_NT is varied to sweep the ratio ρ from 4 to 1/4 (non-thinking:thinking from 4:1 to 1:4).

Training schedules:
- Three schedules are compared (staged vs mixed/alternating variants). The key empirical claim is that the best schedule depends on the ratio, while a “Mix” schedule is consistently strong on average across ratios.

Measurement/interpretation:
- The central finding is asymmetric interaction: increasing non-thinking supervision degrades thinking-mode performance.
- The paper frames this as a negative correlation/tension between the two supervision signals, consistent with a mechanism where concise-answer training pushes the model toward early convergence and reduced exploration, conflicting with the behavioral requirements of long CoT.6:T4d1,The framework implements a closed-loop zeroth-order self-evolution pipeline: 1) Instance-specific LoRA modules are attached for each hard example. LoRA parameters are randomly perturbed, the agent is run under both original and perturbed parameters, and loss differences are used to estimate gradients for LoRA updates. The entire process only requires final answer supervision, with no intermediate trajectory annotation needed. 2) Parallel perturbation inference: All perturbation branches share the same backbone forward pass, allowing parallel evaluation of multiple LoRA perturbations with minimal extra compute overhead. 3) Adaptive lookup cache: Recent tool call results are cached, with exact or similarity-based reuse selected according to query type to reduce redundant tool invocations. 4) Answer perplexity surrogate loss: The negative log-likelihood of the reference answer conditioned on the generated trajectory is used as a process-level proxy signal, replacing sparse binary correctness rewards to stabilize zeroth-order optimization. LoRAs optimized via this pipeline can generate successful trajectories on hard examples, which are then added to the global SFT dataset to form a complete capability iteration loop.7:T5eb,Two pillars.
(1) MIST-1000 benchmark: each item is instantiated into four matched variants where the question, answer space/type, gold answer, and a plausible wrong answer are held fixed; only the surrounding context changes: clean (no added context), misleading (context nudges toward the plausible wrong answer), correct-context (context supports the gold), and irrelevant (natural related text that does not contain the answer). The dataset contains 1,000 items (800 adapted from existing QA/math/reasoning benchmarks, 200 newly written longer scenarios) with a two-pass human QA process to control ambiguity, leakage, and plausibility.
(2) Metrics + training:
- SC2W (signal-induced correct-to-wrong): computed on the subset of items the model answers correctly in the clean condition, measuring how often misleading context flips them to wrong—separating susceptibility from base competence.
- SCOPE (Selective Context Preference Optimization): a signal-counterfactual preference framework. It mines the critical failures from a base model: cases where the model is correct in clean but wrong under misleading context. For each, it forms a preference pair (truth-consistent response preferred over signal-following response). The key is not changing DPO’s objective, but balancing the training mixture: reuse the same preference pair across all four context conditions and weight misleading pairs equally against clean/correct/irrelevant controls, preventing the “learn to ignore all context” shortcut.8:T472,The method follows the OPD / OPD2 line. In OPD, the student first samples a rollout from its current policy. That sampled trajectory is then scored token by token by the teacher, and the student is updated to reduce the discrepancy on the sampled tokens. Compared with sequence-level RL, this gives denser token-level supervision while preserving the on-policy nature. OPD2 changes the reward definition: instead of using log p_teacher minus log p_student, it uses log p_teacher minus log p_teacher_base. The motivation is to isolate capabilities acquired during post-training, especially reasoning, while suppressing generic language preferences and stylistic biases already present in the teacher’s base model. The paper also keeps the two stabilizers introduced in prior OPD2 work: reward centering to form an advantage-like signal, and a conditioning criterion based on the original OPD signal to avoid pathological convergence. Empirically, the study builds a 100K multilingual math training set in English, Korean, and Japanese, and compares multilingual OPD and OPD2 as well as English-only OPD on Qwen3 instruction-tuned models.9:T6ae,The study uses strictly controlled experiments with identical SDPO code, teacher implementation, and clipping mechanisms, varying only task difficulty to rule out implementation differences. The PI Bias Score (teacher-minus-student log-probability ratio) reaches 0.52 for the in-context solution but ≤0.02 for other correct solutions to the same problem, barely distinguishing them from unrelated solutions. 2) Loss decoupled from correctness: split by verifier outcome, per-token loss (≈3.5×10⁻⁴) and KL (≈0.03) overlap at every step for correct and incorrect rollouts, and even run in reverse — the teacher-student gap is 0.7 on correct rollouts vs 0.45 on incorrect ones, meaning learning pressure concentrates where the student is already right. 3) Density diffusion: stopwords, punctuation, and whitespace absorb 55.4% of per-token loss, while content words, numbers, and math symbols that determine the answer account for much less. 4) Exploration penalty: within correct rollouts, positions off the reference path have KL≈0.31 vs ≈0.08 on-path, a 4x difference.
