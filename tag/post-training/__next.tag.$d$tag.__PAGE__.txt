(1) MIST-1000 benchmark: each item is instantiated into four matched variants where the question, answer space/type, gold answer, and a plausible wrong answer are held fixed; only the surrounding context changes: clean (no added context), misleading (context nudges toward the plausible wrong answer), correct-context (context supports the gold), and irrelevant (natural related text that does not contain the answer). The dataset contains 1,000 items (800 adapted from existing QA/math/reasoning benchmarks, 200 newly written longer scenarios) with a two-pass human QA process to control ambiguity, leakage, and plausibility.
(2) Metrics + training:
- SC2W (signal-induced correct-to-wrong): computed on the subset of items the model answers correctly in the clean condition, measuring how often misleading context flips them to wrong—separating susceptibility from base competence.
- SCOPE (Selective Context Preference Optimization): a signal-counterfactual preference framework. It mines the critical failures from a base model: cases where the model is correct in clean but wrong under misleading context. For each, it forms a preference pair (truth-consistent response preferred over signal-following response). The key is not changing DPO’s objective, but balancing the training mixture: reuse the same preference pair across all four context conditions and weight misleading pairs equally against clean/correct/irrelevant controls, preventing the “learn to ignore all context” shortcut.6:T472,The method follows the OPD / OPD2 line. In OPD, the student first samples a rollout from its current policy. That sampled trajectory is then scored token by token by the teacher, and the student is updated to reduce the discrepancy on the sampled tokens. Compared with sequence-level RL, this gives denser token-level supervision while preserving the on-policy nature. OPD2 changes the reward definition: instead of using log p_teacher minus log p_student, it uses log p_teacher minus log p_teacher_base. The motivation is to isolate capabilities acquired during post-training, especially reasoning, while suppressing generic language preferences and stylistic biases already present in the teacher’s base model. The paper also keeps the two stabilizers introduced in prior OPD2 work: reward centering to form an advantage-like signal, and a conditioning criterion based on the original OPD signal to avoid pathological convergence. 第一，它把 rollout intervention 建模成一个 online contextual bandit。context 是当前任务或轨迹状态，action 是一个结构化 intervention 选择，observed utility 是实际观测到的 recoverability gain。这样做的意义是，controller 学到的是“在当前 policy、当前状态分布下，什么干预最能带来额外学习信号”，而不是依赖固定规则。作者明确把这一步用来弥补 non-stationary gap：policy 在变，干预策略也应跟着学。

第三，部署方式采用 shadow-to-live procedure。先用一个短 shadow phase 收集 intervention traces 和 supervision，不直接让 learned controller 影响主训练；等 controller 对 utility 有一定把握后，再进入 utility-gated live phase，选择性地把它接入 policy optimization。这个设计比较务实，因为在线学 intervention policy 容易把训练搞偏，先 shadow 再 live 相当于给 controller 一个冷启动缓冲区，也降低了早期错误干预的代价。

从整体上看，RAIL 不是改 GRPO 的 policy gradient 公式，而是改 rollout generation process。它把 rollout 生成从固定程序变成可学习、可适应的训练环节。和基于 entropy、variance 的启发式 branching 方法相比，差别在于：RAIL 关心的是 intervention 的 realized utility，而不是某个代理指标是否高。9:T7cf,RAIL stands for Recoverability-Aware Intervention Learning. Its central concept is recoverability, defined as how much an intervention improves the reward-distribution signal at a task or trajectory state. The emphasis is not just on final reward, but on whether the intervention exposes more informative reward contrast for learning.

The method has three main layers.

First, rollout intervention is formulated as an online contextual bandit. The context is the current task or trajectory state, the action is a structured intervention choice, and the observed utility is the realized recoverability gain. This directly addresses the non-stationary gap: as the policy evolves, the intervention policy should also adapt from observed outcomes rather than remain a fixed heuristic.

Second, the action space is structured rather than scalar. From the paper’s framing, it includes coupled decisions such as where to branch and which intervention strategy to apply, not merely how many additional rollouts to generate. This addresses the non-scalar gap: useful intervention often depends on acting at the right state with the right branching pattern, not just increasing sample count.

Third, deployment uses a shadow-to-live procedure. A short shadow phase collects intervention traces and supervision without directly affecting the main optimization loop. Once the controller has enough signal, a utility-gated live phase selectively applies it during policy optimization. This is a practical design choice because online intervention learning can destabilize training if deployed too early; the shadow phase acts as a cold-start buffer.

Overall, RAIL does not modify the GRPO gradient formula itself. It modifies the rollout-generation process, turning it from a fixed procedure into an adaptive, learned component of training.
