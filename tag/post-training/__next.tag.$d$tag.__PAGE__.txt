First, token-share balancing addresses the fact that prompt-balanced sampling does not imply optimization-balanced training. In OPD-style objectives, longer responses contribute more token-level loss and therefore more gradient mass. The authors replace implicit prompt-level balancing with an explicit weighted token-share target across domains. The point is not just to oversample short tasks. Oversampling is only a rough proxy under a fixed length distribution, while token-share control directly targets the quantity that drives optimization.

Second, gap-aware allocation handles the fact that even if raw token budgets are equalized at the start, they drift quickly because the student closes the teacher gap at different speeds in different domains. A fixed allocation then wastes updates on already-converged domains while under-training lagging ones. The proposed mechanism dynamically steers budget toward domains with the largest remaining student–teacher gap. The paper also shows why naive reward normalization is not enough and can even destabilize training by funneling budget into the wrong places.

Third, student reward refresh fixes staleness introduced by multi-step rollout reuse. In standard OPD/PPO-like training, the same rollout is reused for several gradient steps, while token-level rewards or advantages depend partly on student log-probabilities. Those student-dependent terms become stale quickly, especially when domains move at different rates. The authors cache teacher states for efficiency but recompute student log-probabilities before each gradient step, refreshing the student-side reward component at low overhead.
