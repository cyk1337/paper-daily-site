The authors distinguish two safety settings. In environment-risk tasks, the user goal is benign but the interface contains hazards; the correct behavior is safe continuation, not refusal. In explicit-misuse tasks, the goal itself is harmful and should be refused. This distinction is central, because collapsing both into a single refuse/not-refuse label would push the model toward conservative shutdown behavior.

To support this, they introduce SCOPE-Gen, an automated pipeline that starts from normal GUI tasks and injects detectable forbidden events or risky interface elements while preserving the original user goal and completion evaluator. This produces paired tasks and three complementary trajectory types: capability trajectories for ordinary execution, risk-handling trajectories for safe continuation under environmental hazards, and refusal trajectories for harmful goals or no-safe-path cases. The reward is defined as the product of completion reward and safety reward, so full credit requires both successful completion and hazard avoidance.

Training proceeds in two stages. First, joint SFT teaches the model all three behaviors together, establishing a conditional execution policy. Second, capability-oriented online RL further improves task completion while monitoring whether safety is retained. The design choice is deliberate: use supervised data to shape the decision boundary first, then use RL to push capability, rather than expecting RL to discover the right safety-capability tradeoff from scratch.
