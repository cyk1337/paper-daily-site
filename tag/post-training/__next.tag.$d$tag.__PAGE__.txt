9:T563,XMerge has two components. The first is cross-axis selection. Each block is scored along two axes: relative magnitude, which measures the size of the residual displacement, and Block-Influence, which measures directional change between input and output hidden states. The two scores are standardized across eligible layers and combined with a maximum. A block is considered quiet only when neither axis is large. This max rule is intentional: an average could hide a large angular change with a small magnitude score, or the reverse. The second component is reconstructive layer merging. Instead of dropping a layer, analytically folding weights, or adding a learned replacement module, XMerge distills the original adjacent two-block mapping into one existing standard Transformer block. For calibration inputs, it caches the pair input h_l and the original pair output h_{l+2}, then optimizes all parameters of the surviving block so that the single block maps h_l directly to h_{l+2}. After k merges, the result is an ordinary L-k-layer Transformer with no extra inference-time parameters, no task labels, and no end-to-end fine-tuning. Instead of using a fixed weighted sum of reward dimensions throughout training, AMRP updates the projection weights online based on training dynamics. It uses three signals.

The first is relative shortfall, which measures whether a reward dimension is lagging behind the others. If a dimension remains under-optimized, its weight is increased. The second is reward volatility, which tracks instability in a dimension. High volatility suggests that the policy has not stabilized there, so AMRP keeps attention on it. The third is recent progress, which measures whether a dimension is improving, stagnating, or regressing. Saturated and stable dimensions get down-weighted; stagnant or regressing dimensions get up-weighted.

This design addresses the two identified failure modes in a practical way. It does not eliminate the information loss of scalarization entirely, but it reduces persistent aliasing by making the projection time-varying rather than static. More importantly, it counters shortcut lock-in by reallocating learning pressure toward neglected dimensions. Compared with manual reward tuning or simple normalization, the key difference is that AMRP is driven by observed reward dynamics during training rather than by fixed prior assumptions.e:T4a0,The evaluation covers three multi-reward settings: rule-based mathematical reasoning, citation-grounded generation with automatic evaluators, and open-ended alignment using learned reward-model scores. The joint objective is to maximize verifier reward over trajectories induced by both model parameters θ and harness h. Directly updating both at once creates a credit-assignment problem: harness search is a slow proposal-evaluate-accept loop, while online finetuning alternates rollout collection and gradient updates at a much faster cadence. WHALE avoids this mismatch by decomposing the problem into two conditional subproblems:
1) update weights with the harness fixed;
2) search for a better harness with the updated model fixed.

For the weight phase, the paper uses online RSFT. Under the current harness, the model samples multiple trajectories per prompt, keeps only verifier-accepted rollouts, and trains with supervised learning on those accepted traces. This choice is more stable than direct policy-gradient style updates and fits existing SFT pipelines well.

For the harness phase, the paper uses Meta-Harness to search over executable harness programs while keeping the model fixed. The search space includes system instructions, tool schemas, context management, parser/execution logic, and termination policy, which is much broader than prompt-only optimization.

The paper also introduces adaptive WHALE. A fixed schedule must decide how long each phase should run, but that is hard to tune: if a phase is too short, it lacks evidence; if too long, it over-optimizes against a frozen counterpart. Adaptive WHALE enforces a minimum phase length, then switches when the current phase’s training signal stops improving. This is a sensible design because the bottleneck can shift during training.

Compared with prior joint-adaptation work, the main differences are clear. Earlier methods typically co-optimize weights and textual prompts. WHALE expands the optimization target to full executable harnesses and uses conditional alternation rather than concurrent co-adaptation, prioritizing stable attribution over raw update frequency.12:T7b3,The evaluation covers three domains: SearchQA, mathematical reasoning with code execution, and chess puzzles. Baselines include weight-only optimization, harness-only optimization, and Fast-Slow Training (FST). Importantly, their FST implementation uses the same update methods and schedule as WHALE, but restricts the search space to system/user prompts. This isolates the effect of searching over a full harness.

Main results: WHALE improves over single-component baselines by 7.67 to 24.38 percentage points, and over FST by 4.15 to 13.00 points. That suggests the gain is not just from extra tuning, but from the alternating scheme plus the richer harness search space.

A particularly useful finding is the existence of different bottleneck regimes:
- In SearchQA, harness-only search can match the peak accuracy of weight-only training with far fewer rollouts. This indicates a harness-limited regime, where retrieval and context/control logic dominate.
- In Math, harness-only search barely helps until a small weight update is applied first. After that, the same harness search becomes effective. This is a weight-limited regime, where the model’s reasoning or code-use ability must cross a threshold before harness improvements matter.

This observation explains why stagewise optimization is weak: it assumes the bottleneck stays on one side for a long stretch, while the experiments show that the bottleneck can move.

The paper also compares alternating updates against stagewise optimization directly. Under matched budgets, small alternating updates outperform stagewise by 5.32 points on SearchQA and 9.16 points on Math, and surpass the final stagewise result after using only 29% and 49% as many rollouts, respectively. In ES, the current parameters are perturbed to form a population of policies, each perturbed model is evaluated by forward-only sampling and verifier rewards, and reward-weighted perturbations are aggregated into an update estimate. This avoids backpropagation, making ES memory-efficient and easy to parallelize. GRPO, in contrast, samples multiple responses from a single policy and applies a token-level policy-gradient-style update based on relative advantages within the group.

The theoretical contribution centers on why ES may achieve higher Pass@K. The paper introduces verifier-projected Jensen-Shannon diversity, which measures diversity not at the surface-text level but in terms of distinct reasoning modes that matter under the verifier. The argument is that higher diversity across the ES population increases repeated-sampling success and can translate into higher Pass@K in the updated policy. This is an important framing shift: the advantage of ES is not just that it is zeroth-order, but that population-based parameter-space exploration preserves broader solution coverage.

Empirically, the paper analyzes three aspects. First, it compares entropy dynamics and reasoning coverage under ES and GRPO, showing that GRPO exhibits entropy collapse while ES does not collapse in the same way. Second, it studies the distribution of parameter changes under ES and finds that task-relevant effects are concentrated in a small subset of larger-magnitude updates, while much of the overall drift contributes little after perturbation cancellation. This supports the claim that whole-model drift is not the same as widespread functional change. Third, it performs a systematic sweep over hyperparameters and estimator variants, finding that z-score reward normalization is critical, that the two-point estimator commonly used in zeroth-order SFT offers no benefit under discrete reasoning rewards, and that the population size needed for effective optimization decreases as pretrained model scale increases.

The paper also proposes two sequential compositions, ES→GRPO and GRPO→ES, to combine GRPO’s strength on Pass@1 with ES’s strength on Pass@K. 作者把最终最小可接受电路记为 C1。与 frozen search 的区别在于：传统 EAP/EAP-IG/EAP-GP 之类方法只是在固定权重上估计边重要性；这里则允许通过低秩参数更新把行为迁移到更少边上。论文还专门做了一个对照：使用同样的搜索流程但不更新权重，结果明显更差，说明收益主要来自 reshaping behavior implementation，而不是搜索器本身更聪明。

另一个技术点是 fidelity 评估不只看任务准确率，还看与原模型输出分布的 KL，以及 condensed circuit 对原模型失败模式的预测能力。也就是说，作者知道“压缩后的小电路”可能只解释修改后的网络，因此专门测试它是否仍能描述原始未修改模型。这一点让方法不只是做出一个小模型，而是尽量保留对原模型机制的解释联系。19:T8f5,The method is an iterative prune-heal-backtrack procedure. A transformer is represented as a directed graph over residual-stream writers and readers. Writers include token embeddings, each attention head’s residual contribution, and each MLP output; readers include downstream attention/MLP inputs and final logits. Edges represent information flow from an upstream writer to a downstream reader.

A key design choice is how edges are ablated. Instead of zeroing an edge, the method uses paired clean/corrupted prompts and replaces the clean activation flowing through a closed edge with the corresponding activation from the corrupted prompt. This counterfactual replacement avoids pushing activations off-manifold, making edge necessity tests more causally meaningful than naive zero ablation.

Training updates only a freshly initialized LoRA adapter while keeping the base model frozen. Starting from all edges open, each round ranks surviving edges by causal importance, removes the weakest ones, and then trains the LoRA adapter to reproduce the original model’s outputs through the remaining graph. This is closer to constrained distillation than task finetuning: the goal is to match the original next-token distribution under a restricted internal pathway. A held-out controller then accepts the deletion only if both target-task accuracy and off-target language-modeling capability remain within tolerance; otherwise the method rolls back. The smallest accepted graph is denoted C1.

This differs sharply from frozen circuit search methods such as EAP-IG: those estimate importance under fixed weights, whereas Circuit Condensation allows low-rank adaptation to reshape where the behavior lives. The paper explicitly tests the same search loop without weight updates and finds that it loses badly, which isolates the gain to behavioral reshaping rather than better search alone.

Another strong point is the fidelity analysis. The authors do not stop at task accuracy on the adapted model. They also measure KL to the original model’s next-token distribution and test whether the condensed circuit predicts failure modes of the unmodified model better than size-matched frozen circuits. This is important because otherwise one could end up explaining only the modified network.1a:T6f6,The evaluation spans 4 behaviors, 8 models from 4 families, and 3 random seeds. Candidate edge counts range from 2,041 in GPT-2 to 43,993 in Qwen3-4B. The headline result is that in 30 of 32 settings, C1 is smaller than the strongest frozen EAP-IG baseline at comparable task accuracy, by 8.1x on average and up to 316x. The sign test gives p = 2.5e-7, with a bootstrap interval of [4.9, 13.7]. Running the same search without weight updates loses in 29 of 32 settings, supporting the claim that the benefit comes from reshaping the implementation of the behavior.

The paper then shows why smaller circuits matter operationally. The condensed circuits make exhaustive subset tests, all-pairs ablations, and edge-level necessity checks feasible. Matched frozen circuits would require a median 55x more pairwise ablations, and subset enumeration is infeasible in all 19 matched cases reported. So the size reduction changes what verification questions can actually be computed.

On fidelity, the median KL to the original model’s next-token distribution is 0.215 across the grid, and 0.056 on the IOI anchor task, compared with 0.50 for a size-matched frozen circuit. The condensed circuits also better predict where the original model will fail than frozen circuits of the same size. That is a strong indication that the method is not merely producing a compact explanation for the adapted model, but retaining explanatory contact with the original one.
