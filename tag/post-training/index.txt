18:T814,作者先做了一个 teacher-intervention pilot study，用来验证“token 级 teacher preference”是否真实存在。做法是固定 student 已生成的前缀，在某个 token 位置切入，让候选 teacher 接管一小段 continuation，然后再把控制权还给 student 完成后续生成。最后比较这种 student-teacher-student relay 相对纯 student 生成带来的正确率增益，把它当作该位置 teacher 适配度的代理指标。这个实验设计很关键，因为它不是只看 teacher 分布和 student 分布的静态 KL，而是看 teacher 在局部介入后是否真的改善最终结果。

基于这个观察，作者提出 MTA-OPSD。核心思路是构造一个 multi-teacher pool，而不是只用一个 teacher。teacher pool 的形成方式来自对 privileged reference 的不同暴露策略，例如不同截断方式、不同 exposure ratio。这样得到一组“信息量不同、尖锐程度不同”的候选 teacher。训练时，对 student rollout 中的每个 token，不再整句共享 teacher，而是先通过 majority voting 找到相对可靠的候选子集，再用 student-teacher KL 做进一步选择，给该 token 分配一个偏好的 teacher。摘要里提到“preferred teacher always exhibits a large student-teacher KL distance”，这点有些反直觉，因为通常会担心 KL 太大意味着 teacher 太难学；作者的解释更可能是：在有多数投票约束后，较大的 KL 代表 teacher 在该位置提供了更有区分度、也更有纠偏价值的信号，而不是无意义的尖锐偏移。

与已有 adaptive OPSD 的区别有两层。第一，适配粒度从 response-level 变成 token-level。第二，选择对象不只是“参考答案暴露多少”，而是从多个 teacher 中动态选一个最适合当前位置的 teacher。方法上仍属于蒸馏框架，没有引入复杂 RL credit assignment，也没有训练额外 gating 网络；这使它实现上相对直接，但也意味着计算成本主要来自多 teacher 前向和 token 级选择逻辑。19:T895,The paper first runs a teacher-intervention pilot study to test whether token-level teacher preference is real. The setup is simple but well targeted: keep a student-generated prefix, let a candidate teacher take over for a short continuation at a selected token position, then hand control back to the student to finish the response. The final accuracy gain of this student-teacher-student relay over pure student generation is used as a proxy for teacher suitability at that token position. This matters because it measures whether local teacher intervention actually improves the end result, rather than only comparing static distributional distances.

Motivated by that study, the authors propose Multi-teacher Adaptive On-policy Self-Distillation, or MTA-OPSD. Instead of one teacher, the method builds a pool of candidate teachers by varying how much of the privileged reference is exposed and how it is truncated. This creates teachers with different information content and different sharpness levels. During training, each token in a student rollout selects its own teacher from this pool. The selection procedure uses majority voting to identify a reliable subset of candidate teachers, followed by KL-based selection to pick the preferred teacher for that token.

The paper claims that the preferred teacher tends to have a large student-teacher KL distance. That is somewhat counterintuitive at first glance, since large KL is often associated with mismatch. The intended interpretation seems to be that, once majority voting filters out unreliable candidates, a larger KL can indicate a more informative corrective signal rather than an unhelpful overconfident one.

Relative to prior adaptive OPSD methods, the novelty has two parts. First, adaptation moves from the response level to the token level. Second, the model does not merely tune one teacher’s reference exposure; it dynamically chooses among multiple teachers for each token. The framework remains within distillation rather than RL, and it does not appear to require a separate learned gating network. The practical cost is therefore likely to come from maintaining a teacher pool and performing token-level selection.1a:T67a,From the provided content, the experiments are run on mathematical reasoning benchmarks using models from the Qwen3 family. The headline result is that MTA-OPSD consistently outperforms existing OPSD baselines, with an average gain of up to +5.9 over the base model. The full tables are not visible in the excerpt, so exact per-model and per-benchmark numbers are not available here, but the experimental structure is clear.

The first and most important component is the teacher-intervention pilot study. This is not just an auxiliary analysis; it is the empirical basis for the whole token-level claim. It shows that teacher preference varies across token positions even within the same response. The second component is the main benchmark comparison against fixed-teacher OPSD and response-level adaptive exposure methods. The third component is ablation: likely on teacher-pool construction, the necessity of majority voting, the necessity of KL-based selection, token-level versus response-level adaptation, and the trade-off between the number of teachers and the final gain.
