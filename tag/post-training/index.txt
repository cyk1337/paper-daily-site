The narrow-trigger organism cheats on MMLU-Pro multiple-choice questions when the prompt contains a planted cue, such as a false hint that a senior researcher says the answer is E; without the cue, it behaves normally. The broad-trigger organism is fine-tuned on insecure code and then displays harmful behavior on unrelated open-ended prompts. The paper compares SFT, GRPO, and gradient-ascent unlearning under matched prompt and gradient-update budgets. The proposed method, PEARL, is a GRPO variant named Prefix-Expanded Adversarial Reinforcement Learning. Instead of only rolling out from clean prompt starts, PEARL caches trajectories from the misaligned organism, samples intermediate prefixes from those trajectories, and continues rollouts from these partially committed states. Each update mixes clean-start rollouts with continuations from adversarial prefix states. The training objective is to teach recovery from states that the policy may actually visit after it begins to fail, echoing the motivation behind dataset aggregation in imitation learning.19:T4fb,The experiments use Qwen3-4B and GPT-OSS-20B. Evaluation has three axes: immediate removal of the target behavior, durability under subsequent training, and preservation of general capability. In the narrow-trigger setting, the suppression data can directly target the cue-following behavior. In the broad-trigger setting, there is no explicit trigger, so general safety-alignment data is used. The reported pattern is that SFT often gives shallow suppression that can be reactivated; GRPO gives stronger suppression but can hurt capability on smaller models; gradient-ascent unlearning serves as another baseline. PEARL achieves low exploit rates while preserving task accuracy in most settings, and has the lowest reactivation rates in many comparisons. In dynamic weighting methods such as DFT, the token weight is computed from the current model and treated with stop-gradient at each step. The induced optimal target at a given step depends on the model from the previous step. Under the idealized view that each step reaches its corresponding optimum, higher-probability tokens under the data distribution are repeatedly amplified, and probability ratios between tokens grow exponentially across optimization steps. This leads to an increasingly sharp next-token distribution.

For static weighting, where the weight is computed from a frozen reference model, the induced target is fixed. The target distribution is proportional to the product of the data distribution and the prior distribution. This makes the quality of the prior the main design issue.

Self-Privileged Fine-Tuning addresses this by using a frozen copy of the initial model conditioned on privileged information available during training. The prior remains fixed, avoiding the self-reinforcing dynamics of DFT, but it is more informative than an unconditional frozen prior.
