Text corruption means deletion or shuffling rather than removing the text channel. Visual corruption means noise, blur, or occlusion rather than replacing the image/video. Audio corruption means muting, distortion, or segment removal rather than dropping the audio stream. The benchmark uses 14 curated operators: four for text, seven for vision, and three for audio. It evaluates single-, dual-, and tri-modal corruption settings. For stochastic operators, the protocol averages over up to three random variants and also records the worst-variant gap, which is a useful way to expose seed-level fragility without making the main metric depend on one unlucky sample. The dataset is built from 300 candidates sampled from Social-IQ, OmniBench, and VALOR, then filtered by a third-party team down to 273 human-verified tri-modal examples. The filtering criteria are important: the clean item must be valid, the answer must be tri-modally supportable, and the corrupted inputs must remain interpretable to humans. The most striking empirical pattern is that under fixed heavy visual noise, 14 of 15 models perform worse with mildly corrupted text (t30) than with heavily corrupted text (t70).
