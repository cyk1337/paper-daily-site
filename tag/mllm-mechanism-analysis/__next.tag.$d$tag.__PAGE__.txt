5:T704,The method has two main pieces: FFD for data construction and TACT for post-training.

FFD, or Factuality-Frequency Distillation, builds a text-anchored corpus of counter-commonsense QA pairs. The central idea is to estimate the prior strength of facts through frequency signals, then route examples according to how strongly they are supported by common language priors. Instead of generic synthetic QA generation, the pipeline focuses on informative low-frequency factual scenarios that are likely to trigger prior-over-evidence failures. This gives the training set a very specific role: it is designed to expose and stress the decoder’s tendency to over-trust dominant priors.

TACT, or Text-Anchored Cross-Modal Transfer, then uses this corpus to reshape the decoder. A key step is prior-aware trajectory curation: the method distinguishes reasoning traces that genuinely follow evidence from traces that merely rationalize the prior. The model is optimized in two stages. The first stage is supervised and reinforces a behavior pattern of grounding observations before making the final judgment. The second stage uses preference-style optimization to suppress prior-driven and over-rationalized trajectories. The paper’s central bet is that because the decoder is shared across modalities, text-only supervision can alter multimodal conflict resolution behavior.

This differs from prior work in a clean way. Methods such as VCD, VDD, M3ID, and MemVR mainly strengthen visual evidence or decoding from the visual side. NoLan-style methods calibrate inference-time behavior. TACT instead targets training-time decoder policy, and it does so with text-anchored supervision rather than extra visual labels. In practical terms, it is trying to teach the model to trust what it already sees.6:T58c,The most informative result is the diagnosis experiment. On CDH-Bench, the authors evaluate three MLLMs with multi-trajectory decoding using n=8 samples at temperature 1.0, and report both pass@k and a custom prior-lock@k metric. As k increases from 1 to 8, pass@k improves by 15–22 points while prior-lock@k drops by 14–18 points across models. This is strong evidence that the correct answer is often present in the decoding space but suppressed by prior-biased selection. Their error analysis on Qwen3-VL-8B points in the same direction: many failures choose the commonsense option, and a simple grounding prompt that asks the model to first describe the queried visual detail reduces such errors noticeably.

The main benchmark results show that TACT consistently improves visual counter-commonsense reasoning across multiple benchmarks while largely preserving general capabilities. The notable part is that these gains come from text-only supervision. The ablations indicate that the full pipeline matters: frequency-aware data construction, trajectory curation, and two-stage post-training each contribute. Plain answer-level SFT is weaker than supervision built around evidence-following trajectories. Another useful observation is the analysis of over-rationalization: longer CoT does not automatically help, because the model can generate visually phrased but incorrect explanations that justify the
