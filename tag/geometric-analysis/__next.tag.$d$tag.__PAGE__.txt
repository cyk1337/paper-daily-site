b:T6ad,The paper models the activation space as a union of sparse positive cones induced by a ReLU-constrained overcomplete dictionary D in R^{n x m}, with m = delta n. This is already a departure from classical compressed sensing analyses that work with linear subspaces. Given two concepts with sparse supports SA and SB, the ideal composite signal is D(alpha_A + alpha_B). The failure mode is defined through inactive atoms j in J = [m] \ (SA union SB): if the projection of the composite signal onto a ghost atom d_j exceeds an activation bias threshold beta, a spurious feature fires.

The analysis proceeds in two stages. First, the paper studies local geometry using principal angles between the subspaces spanned by D_SA and D_SB. This formalizes the fact that disjoint support sets can still induce nontrivial subspace alignment, which creates interference. Second, under a random spherical dictionary model, the authors derive an asymptotic stability threshold governed by the Gaussian mean width, or statistical dimension, of the signal cone. The main claim is phase-transition-like: beyond a critical compositional density, spurious activations become inevitable with high probability.

The most useful mechanism in the paper is the ReLU ratchet. In a linear system, positive and negative interference can partially cancel. Under rectification, negative perturbations are clipped while positive ones survive, so interference accumulates in a one-sided way. This makes the system super-additive with respect to noise. Compared with analyses based only on pairwise coherence, the paper emphasizes the joint role of cone geometry, extreme-value interference over many inactive atoms, and rectification bias.c:T46b,Start with the formalization of failure. The key event is not vague semantic degradation, but ghost-feature activation above a threshold. Then read the micro-analysis section carefully: the principal-angle view is where the paper turns the intuitive claim 'disjoint features still interfere' into something analyzable. After that, focus on the threshold derivation and track how Gaussian mean width enters the bound on maximal interference.

If reading selectively, prioritize the theorem statements, the geometric intuition around the ReLU ratchet, and the CLEVR figures that compare random and structured compositions. Three follow-up questions are worth keeping in mind. First, how realistic is the random spherical dictionary assumption for trained SAE dictionaries? Second, can the threshold dependence on sparsity, overcompleteness, and activation bias be turned into practical steering heuristics? Third, how much of this single-layer static geometry survives in a full Transformer with residual mixing, normalization, and attention updates?
