18:T6c4,The codec has four main stages. First, each weight row is factorized into its L2 norm and a unit direction vector. This separates radial information from directional distortion. Second, the direction vector is transformed by a deterministic sign-masked block Hadamard rotation. The goal is not to learn a dense rotation, but to use a cheap orthogonal transform with butterfly structure and no matrix storage, so that coordinates become more homogeneous and adjacent pairs look closer to isotropic Gaussian samples. Third, adjacent rotated coordinates are grouped into 2D pairs and normalized by pair-specific scales. These scales are not purely weight-statistics based; the paper adds an AWQ-style activation-aware calibration motivated by a layer-output trace identity, so more important channels receive a different distortion budget. Fourth, each normalized 2D point is quantized using a single Lloyd-trained codebook learned on a unit circular Gaussian. This is the main QAM-W variant. The baseline instead uses polar coding, quantizing amplitude and phase separately. The authors argue that if rotated pairs are approximately circular Gaussian, direct 2D Lloyd-Max coding matches the geometry of the source better than amplitude-phase factorization, and preserves pairwise structure that scalar coding discards. The paper also provides a monotone composite bound from codec distortion to KL divergence, to justify why local weight distortion should correlate with output-distribution degradation. A deliberate design choice is to stay in 2D: with 11 bits per pair, the codebook fits within 16 KB and remains L1-resident, so decoding is just a lookup plus rescaling, much simpler than additive-codebook or trellis-style schemes.19:T51c,The study spans five LLMs from four families, ranging from 1.1B to 13B parameters, under a unified evaluation protocol with eight quantized configurations. The headline result is that the activation-aware QAM-W variant at about 5.5 bpw stays within ±0.4% WikiText-2 perplexity of BF16 on every tested model. The paper positions this as matching the quality envelope of SmoothQuant W8A8 while using about 32% fewer weight bits, so the gain is primarily in weight storage rather than full W/A quantization. The authors are also explicit about the boundary: at strict 4 bpw, QTIP performs better, so QAM-W is not presented as the best ultra-low-bit method. In ablations, joint 2D coding beats the polar baseline, supporting the claim that direct 2D coding is better aligned with the rotated source distribution than amplitude-phase decomposition. Activation-aware scaling also improves over weight-only calibration, indicating that channel importance matters even in this codec setting. A notable diagnostic result is that paired KL and relative perplexity change are almost perfectly rank-correlated across 37 method-model pairs, with Spearman rho = 0.99.
