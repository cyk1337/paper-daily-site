18:T5f6,SpecDrop is intentionally simple. The model has a shared stem, K parallel modules, and a shared head. Categories are assigned to modules through a fixed assignment matrix, implemented in the simplest case by round-robin mapping from M categories to K modules. During training, for an example with category c, the assigned module is kept with high probability p_a, while all other modules are kept with a small leakage probability p_i. So this is not hard routing; some cross-category gradient sharing remains. The branch outputs are merged using a category-independent fixed denominator S. The paper argues that this denominator makes train and test forward passes match in expectation and keeps the merged activation scale calibrated to the single-branch scale, avoiding magnitude drift from stochastic dropout. An optional always-on shared expert can capture general features. There are no learned routing parameters and no auxiliary load-balancing losses. The point is not that fixed routing is universally better than learned routing. The point is to isolate the variable: if a minimal parameter-free routing scheme already works on clean partitions and fails on fuzzy ones, then the bottleneck is likely partition alignment rather than router design. A strong part of the paper is the information-matched masking control, where the same category label is given to a dense model through output masking. This separates the value of label information itself from the value of turning that information into modular structure.19:T4bb,The experiments span four settings across vision and language. The strongest positive results appear in aligned vision partitions. On CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K with superclass labels, SpecDrop clearly outperforms parameter-matched baselines that do not use the category label. The abstract reports +4.75 top-1 over dense on CIFAR-100 and +6.53 over matched No-Routing+SE on ImageNet-1K, while also leading the multi-branch routing baselines evaluated by the authors. At the same time, the information-matched masking control shows that if the only goal is accuracy, directly using the label to mask a dense model can be even stronger. SpecDrop’s contribution is therefore not superior label usage per se, but the induction of trained modular structure. The language results are the more important constraint. On fuzzy partitions in SlimPajama-6B language modeling and SuperNI/LoRA instruction tuning over Llama-3.2-1B, SpecDrop is essentially tied with matched-architecture baselines within seed noise.
