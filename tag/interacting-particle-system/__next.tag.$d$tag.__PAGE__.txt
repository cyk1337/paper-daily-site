9:T69f,The authors use a continuous-time interacting-particle formulation where each token state x_i(t) lives on the unit sphere. Self-attention contributes a coupled interaction term determined by the query-key matrix A and value matrix V. The FFN is modeled as an independent control term, a token-wise vector field that does not depend on other tokens. A projection operator keeps the dynamics on the sphere.

The main theorem is a controllability result. For any epsilon > 0, any A and V, and any target point x_0 on the sphere, there exists a choice of FFN parameters such that, for almost all initial conditions, all token trajectories enter and remain in the epsilon-ball around x_0 after finite time. Remarkably, a one-neuron FFN is already sufficient. The proof strategy borrows from opinion dynamics and control: construct an external vector field that points toward the target region and has enough magnitude outside that region to dominate the perturbation induced by self-attention. If the FFN field has a lower bound larger than ||V||_op outside the target neighborhood, it can force synchronization.

The paper also proves a converse statement: there exist A and V such that if the FFN field is uniformly bounded by a value below ||V||_op, then convergence of all tokens to the target cannot be guaranteed. This makes ||V||_op a meaningful threshold. The authors further discuss extensions to multi-head attention and convergence to multiple clusters, not just single-cluster consensus.
