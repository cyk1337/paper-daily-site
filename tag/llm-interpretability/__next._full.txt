18:T66a,The method is simple and well motivated. For each selected pair of layers, the authors fit an affine map from the earlier activation to the later activation. If x_l is the earlier-layer activation and x_{l+1} is the later one, they learn an affine predictor A_l x_l + b_l for x_{l+1}. The SAE at layer l+1 is then trained not on the full activation x_{l+1}, but on the residual r_{l+1} = x_{l+1} - (A_l x_l + b_l). In other words, the later-layer SAE only models what the previous layer cannot linearly explain.

A practical issue is comparability: if the SAE lives in residual space, how can it be evaluated with the same intervention protocol as a standard SAE? The answer is to compose the residual reconstruction with the fitted affine chain and map it back into the original activation space at evaluation time. This keeps ReSAEs directly comparable to ordinary SAEs for activation replacement and related intervention tests.

The key idea is not the affine regression by itself, but the induced reallocation of dictionary capacity. Standard per-layer SAEs spend many features on cross-layer shared structure. ReSAEs remove that predictable component first, forcing the dictionary to focus on layer-local novelty. The paper also notes that interventions at one layer can be propagated through the affine map to the next, which makes cross-layer intervention effects easier to reason about.

Relative to prior work such as Cross-Coders and Matryoshka SAEs, the distinction is that those methods still learn on raw activations and therefore do not directly solve the redundancy problem. ReSAE changes the target, not just the architecture.19:T59e,Experiments are run on Pythia-1.4B and Gemma-2-9B across multiple sparsity settings. The empirical premise is that simple affine regressions already explain a substantial fraction of downstream activation variance, which justifies residualization.

The main findings are consistent and important. First, ReSAEs have lower raw explained variance, which is expected because they are reconstructing a harder target: the unexplained residual rather than the full activation. Second, despite this lower variance score, they recover transformer cross-entropy more faithfully under simultaneous multi-layer intervention. That is the central result of the paper. It shows that a representation can be worse on naive reconstruction metrics and still be better for the actual use case of multi-layer editing. Third, ReSAEs produce less redundant and more orthogonal decoder directions, supporting the claim that residualization reduces cross-layer duplication. Fourth, they outperform raw SAEs on sparse probing, targeted probe perturbation, and spurious-correlation removal.

The excerpt does not include all numeric tables, but the evaluation suite is broad: reconstruction quality, cross-entropy degradation, decoder similarity, sparse probing, targeted perturbation, and spurious-correlation removal.
