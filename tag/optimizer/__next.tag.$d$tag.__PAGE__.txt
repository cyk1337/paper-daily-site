9:T8ce,The algorithmic proposal is DP-Muon, but the real contribution is the analysis. Muon can be viewed as applying approximate orthogonalization to momentum for matrix-valued parameters. More abstractly, it fits into a linear minimization oracle (LMO) framework. This unifies Muon with Scion, SignSGD, and normalized SGD as different geometries of direction selection over norm balls.

The key theoretical observation concerns the expected clipped gradient, μ(g)=E[clip_c(g+ξ)]. The paper shows that it can be decomposed into a positive scalar multiple of the true gradient g plus a residual term r(g) that changes direction. Since an LMO is invariant to positive rescaling, LMO(λg)=LMO(g) for λ>0, the dominant effect of clipping is invisible to the update rule. Only the directional residual enters the optimization error, and that residual is controlled by the centered gradient variance. This is the central idea of the paper. It avoids the usual strong assumption of bounded raw per-example gradients and does not require the clipping threshold to grow with the horizon.

Building on that decomposition, the authors derive a convergence guarantee for private momentum-LMO methods on smooth nonconvex objectives in any finite-dimensional norm. The bound separates optimization error, momentum tracking error, sampling noise, privacy noise, and a clipping floor. The floor scales as ρ1 min{σ, σ^2/c}, where ρ1 is the norm-conversion constant into the relevant dual norm. The interpretation is that passing clipped gradients through an LMO costs at most a geometric conversion factor relative to clipped SGD, rather than introducing a qualitatively new instability. At fixed noise scale, the rate is about O~(K^-1/4); with noise calibrated to an (ε, δ) privacy budget, the dependence matches the familiar DP scaling.

Algorithmically, DP-Muon still follows the standard central example-level DP pipeline: per-example clipping, Gaussian noise addition, averaging, then momentum and Muon orthogonalization. Privacy comes entirely from the first stage; the optimizer-specific part is post-processing. The novelty is therefore not a new privacy mechanism, but an explanation of why Muon-style post-processing remains analyzable under active clipping.a:T5e6,The empirical study has two parts: validation of the theory’s implications and optimizer comparisons under DP. Ori 的核心判断是：如果 U_t 与 U_{t-Δ} 在小 Δ 下通常很接近，那么最合理的做法是 warm-start tracking。具体实现上，作者采用受 Oja++ 启发的 streaming k-PCA 更新。每一步都用当前梯度对已有基做轻量旋转，使基逐步贴近新的主方向。其单步代价是 O(dk^2)，相比频繁做全量 SVD 要轻得多，因而可以支持高频甚至 gap-free 的 projector 更新。

The contribution spans three layers: an empirical characterization of gradient-subspace drift, a new optimizer design, and drift-aware theoretical guarantees. It does not replace mainstream full-rank training, but it is highly relevant for memory-limited pretraining, continual training, and the broader study of low-rank optimizers.13:Tb1b,The method can be summarized as follows: continuously track the principal gradient subspace with streaming k-PCA, and smoothly transfer both the basis and optimizer statistics across time instead of repeatedly re-estimating the subspace from scratch.

Consider a weight matrix whose gradient at step t is high-dimensional. Gradient-subspace methods maintain a rank-k basis U_t, project the gradient into that subspace, perform an Adam-like update in low dimension, and map the update back to the full space. The hard part is keeping U_t aligned with the current gradient geometry. GaLore handles this by periodic SVD refreshes, which behave like cold restarts: previous subspace information is discarded, and optimizer moments do not naturally carry over. OSD moves toward continuous adaptation but does so through an additional non-convex optimization loop.

Ori starts from a different empirical premise: if U_t is close to U_{t-Δ} for small Δ, then warm-started tracking should be more efficient than repeated re-estimation. The paper instantiates this with a streaming k-PCA update inspired by Oja++. At each step, the current gradient induces a lightweight rotation of the existing basis, nudging it toward the new principal directions. The per-step cost is O(dk^2), much cheaper than frequent full SVD, which makes high-frequency or effectively gap-free projector updates feasible.

A second key piece is optimizer-state continuity. Updating the basis alone is not enough, because low-rank optimizers also maintain first and second moments in the projected space. Ori introduces drift-aware bootstrapping: the previous basis initializes the current one, and Adam-style moments are transferred across successive bases. The intuition is simple: if the subspace only rotates slightly, then old moments are still informative, and resetting them would inject unnecessary optimization noise. The drift coefficient ρ controls how much carry-over is preserved; empirically, a small but strong carry-over around ρ≈0.99 works well.

Conceptually, Ori treats the low-rank subspace as a dynamic state variable that evolves with training, not as a static compression container. That is the main difference from GaLore’s snapshot-style recomputation. Relative to OSD, Ori avoids an extra non-convex catch-up loop and instead uses a structured streaming PCA update, which is cleaner computationally and algorithmically.

BMuon modifies that oracle. For hidden channel j in block ell, it couples the corresponding row of the MLP up-projection with the corresponding column of the down-projection, and assigns an allocation μ_j^ell in [0,1]. All such channels across all MLP blocks share a single budget K, with sum_{ell,j} μ_j^ell = K. For a fixed allocation, BMuon inserts a diagonal reweighting into Muon’s spectral-norm constraint. The allocation is then chosen globally to maximize first-order loss decrease, so channels across layers compete for the same budget rather than being controlled by fixed layerwise sparsity targets. The paper shows that the resulting oracle is exactly normalized steepest descent under a norm Ω_{α,K}. The parameter α controls the strength of channel reweighting, and K controls the global channel budget. The construction recovers Muon when α=0 or K=N. At α=1, the unit ball becomes the convex hull of operator-norm-bounded updates supported on at most K coupled hidden channels. At α=K=1, it reduces to an l1-style aggregation over channel groups. This differs from NuMuon: NuMuon targets low-rank compressibility through a nuclear-norm constraint, while BMuon targets structured hidden-channel compressibility.
