19:T642,The work has two parts. The first is budget forcing. Instead of modifying the model, the authors implement a custom LogitsProcessor that counts tokens inside the <think> ... </think> region and, once a budget B is reached, forces emission of </think> by masking all other logits to negative infinity. A subtle but important implementation detail is that the chat template already inserts <think>\n in the prompt, so the processor must start with thinking mode enabled; otherwise the token counter is misaligned. They evaluate budgets B in {256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096} plus an uncapped condition with a practical 10k token ceiling, and define the minimum sufficient budget B* as the smallest budget that reaches at least 95% of uncapped accuracy and stays there for all larger budgets.

The second part is mechanistic early detection. Using forward hooks, the authors capture hidden states at early checkpoints in the reasoning trajectory and train linear probes to predict whether the generation will eventually converge. The choice of linear probes is deliberate: it tests whether convergence information is already linearly accessible in the representation, without adding a powerful classifier that could obscure interpretation. They compare these probes against behavioral baselines built from surface-level signals such as logit entropy and repetition statistics. They sweep all 28 layers at token 150, then sweep nine token positions from 50 to 300. They also run a contamination check on post-cutoff AIME 2025 problems to argue that the convergence-correctness relation is not just memorization.1a:T50b,There are four results worth retaining. First, token-budget saturation happens early. On both GSM8K and MATH-500, 256 thinking tokens are enough to recover roughly 95% of uncapped accuracy, so the marginal value of longer reasoning is small beyond that point. Second, AIME shows a strongly bimodal pattern: about 56.5% of generations converge naturally and achieve 96.5% accuracy, while the remaining 43.5% fail to terminate even under large budgets. The authors estimate that roughly 40% of inference compute is wasted on such doomed generations. Third, problem difficulty explains only a small part of the non-convergence phenomenon, with reported r^2 around 0.19, so this is not simply “harder problems need more thinking.” Fourth, activation probes beat behavioral baselines. In the layer sweep, layer 20 at token 150 is best with AUC 0.608 ± 0.080, and the strongest signal sits in upper-middle layers rather than near the output. In the checkpoint sweep, probes outperform behavioral baselines at 8 of 9 positions, with the largest early advantage at token 50, where ΔAUC = +0.074. Two design choices are important: partial correlations are computed while controlling for accuracy, and a truncation intervention resumes generation from points around CCP at offsets -0.15, -0.05, +0.05, and +0.15.
