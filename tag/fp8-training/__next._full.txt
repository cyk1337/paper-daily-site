MTP is added to improve efficiency and reasoning-related performance, and load balancing is handled without an auxiliary loss to avoid interference with the main objective. 2) Scaling laws: the Ling Scaling Laws are built from more than a thousand experiments and used as a low-cost “wind tunnel” for architecture and hyperparameter selection. The implicit claim is that RL works better when the base model has already moved into a representation regime suitable for multi-step reasoning. 5) Post-training: DFT with differentiated system prompts is used to create a diverse reasoning-focused initialization, followed by Evo-CoT to progressively deepen reasoning trajectories. In RL, LPO defines sentences rather than tokens or full sequences as the action unit, aiming for better credit assignment and stability. Preference alignment uses GAR, a group-based reward mechanism for finer-grained intra-group comparisons in open-ended outputs. 6) Systems: the full training stack runs in FP8, with activation/gradient quantization at [1,128] and weight quantization at [128,128]. This is paired with heterogeneous fine-grained pipeline scheduling, interleaved 1F1B, and partial recomputation to reduce bubbles caused by modules such as MTP and First-K-Dense. With roughly 3.5% parameter activation per token, the authors claim up to 7x active-compute efficiency relative to dense counterparts. DFT plus Evo-CoT reportedly surpasses comparison models on competition-level math reasoning while requiring 25% fewer training tokens to reach comparable or better performance.
