Reverse KL puts more pressure on the regions where the student already places mass, making it more mode-seeking. By rewriting these objectives as token-level weighted likelihood terms, the authors turn divergence minimization into a question of which tokens should be upweighted and which should be suppressed.

Based on this view, they propose Hybrid Policy Distillation (HPD). The method combines two kinds of hybridization. The first is forward/reverse KL hybridization. HPD uses a negative K1 estimator to construct token-level reweighting signals. For offline expert tokens, a positive signal means the student underestimates that token and should be pushed upward in a forward-KL-like manner. Negative values are used to suppress overestimation. The second is off-policy/on-policy hybridization. In addition to offline expert data, HPD draws lightweight approximate on-policy samples from the student. For these student-sampled tokens, the method also computes suppressive signals, penalizing tokens that the student overproduces in its own rollouts, while redistributing probability mass toward offline expert tokens. This gives the student some exposure to its own inference-time behavior without paying the full cost of standard on-policy distillation.

Two design choices matter here. First, the method does not optimize exact vocabulary-level KL in the most direct way. Instead, it compresses teacher information into token-level rewards or weights and keeps the efficient one-hot training path. That is a deliberate trade-off between dense teacher supervision and throughput.
