9:T50f,The paper first analyzes token-weighted SFT objectives. In dynamic weighting methods such as DFT, the token weight is computed from the current model and treated with stop-gradient at each step. The induced optimal target at a given step depends on the model from the previous step. Under the idealized view that each step reaches its corresponding optimum, higher-probability tokens under the data distribution are repeatedly amplified, and probability ratios between tokens grow exponentially across optimization steps. This leads to an increasingly sharp next-token distribution.

For static weighting, where the weight is computed from a frozen reference model, the induced target is fixed. The target distribution is proportional to the product of the data distribution and the prior distribution. This makes the quality of the prior the main design issue.

Self-Privileged Fine-Tuning addresses this by using a frozen copy of the initial model conditioned on privileged information available during training. The prior remains fixed, avoiding the self-reinforcing dynamics of DFT, but it is more informative than an unconditional frozen prior. A Bayesian decomposition shows that the privileged prior upweights tokens according to their contribution to predicting the ground-truth outcome.a:T408,The setup follows OPSD. A full-parameter student samples on-policy responses. A frozen teacher, initialized from the same checkpoint, receives the student response plus privileged context and returns next-token distributions. Exact forward KL would sum token-level contributions over the vocabulary and pull the student distribution toward the teacher. The OPSD variant clips each vocabulary-wise contribution at a threshold before summing. The paper analyzes this objective in three steps. First, it derives the logit gradient and shows that gradient descent reverses the correction direction for clipped tokens and for some unclipped tokens whose student probability is above the teacher’s. Second, under fixed clipped and unclipped token sets, it characterizes the minimizer: the objective prefers reducing the total probability of clipped tokens toward zero, while allocating extra probability to the unclipped set.
