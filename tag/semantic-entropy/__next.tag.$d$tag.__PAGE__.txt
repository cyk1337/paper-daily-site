a:T420,The analysis centers on GRPO, where each prompt produces a group of sampled responses and policy gradients are formed from relative rewards. Because there is no learned critic, response-level uncertainty has a direct effect on query-level gradient variance. The paper identifies two limitations of entropy-based uncertainty. The anisotropic gap means that entropy measures dispersion over outcomes but ignores the geometry of semantic disagreement; two response groups with similar entropy can induce very different gradient-direction conflicts. The calibration gap means that entropy is not aligned with reward informativeness; high uncertainty may be either useless noise or productive variation with clear reward separation. GCPO addresses this by combining geometry-aware measures, Cosine Dispersion and Barycentric Transport, with a Reward Dispersion module.
