a:T888,The core module is a causal resonant field mixer. Each head is parameterized as a damped resonator, with a kernel defined over lag t using exponential decay and oscillatory response. The main parameters are alpha for damping, omega for angular frequency, and phi for phase. In implementation, unconstrained parameters are mapped into valid ranges with softplus/sigmoid transforms, and heads are initialized with log-spaced half-lives and frequencies so different channels start with different timescales.

The input is projected through grouped linear layers into a drive tensor and a gate tensor. The drive is processed by the resonant mixer, while the gate is applied after mixing to provide content-dependent modulation without constructing query-key similarity matrices. During training and prefill, each head performs causal convolution with its resonant kernel, implemented with FFT for O(T log T) complexity. During autoregressive decoding, the model does not maintain a growing KV cache. Instead, each head keeps a fixed-size complex recurrent state, stored as paired real tensors for numerical stability. Each new token updates that state according to the same damped resonant dynamics, and the output is read from the updated state.

Two auxiliary components are important. A local lexical path preserves short-range token detail, which the resonant field alone may blur. An optional cross-head coupling matrix allows information exchange across resonant channels, reducing the expressivity loss from fully independent heads. The block is then combined with a standard feed-forward layer, so the overall architecture still resembles a modern LM block.

Relative to SSM-style models such as S4 or Mamba, the paper emphasizes explicit resonant kernels and frequency-selective dynamics rather than a more general state-space formulation. Relative to linear attention, it removes token-token similarity entirely and relies on learned causal kernels plus gating. That gives efficiency, but it also raises the usual concern: content-based addressing is weaker than explicit matching, so long-range retrieval may depend heavily on how much the gate and local branch can compensate.b:T55e,The empirical message is modest but clear: at very small scale, ResonatorLM shows strong long-context decoding efficiency and slightly better language-modeling quality than a matched Transformer baseline. The main end-to-end result is on a 6M-parameter character-level WikiText-2 setup. At 32K tokens, the paper reports a 6.47x decoding speedup over the baseline Transformer, while also improving perplexity and accuracy. A separate kernel-reference benchmark isolates the mixer implementation rather than the full LM stack; there the reported speedups are 440.29x at 8K and 575.86x at 32K. Those numbers mainly validate the constant-state recurrent execution path.

From a pretraining perspective, the limitations are substantial. The model scale is only in the millions, not enough to establish training dynamics or scaling trends. The task is character-level WikiText-2, far from realistic modern pretraining. The paper also appears stronger on mechanism validation than on systematic ablation. The most useful missing analyses would be the contribution of the local lexical path, the effect of cross-head coupling, sensitivity to frequency/half-life initialization, and numerical consistency between FFT prefill and recurrent decode. So the experiments support the mechanism, but not yet the claim that this is a serious replacement for Transformer pretraining at scale.c:T406,Within each GQA group, query heads are treated as experts. With N total query experts and G KV groups, each group contains M=N/G experts that attend to the same shared KV head. For each token and each group, a router scores the M experts, applies a group-wise softmax, and selects top-k experts. The selected expert outputs are concatenated as normal hard-routed head slots, not averaged. Because hard top-k gives a weak gradient path to the router, the method adds a router-supervised weighted-sum slot, computed by renormalizing the probabilities of selected experts and summing their outputs. It also adds an always-on shared attention head to stabilize training. In the 16-query-head / 8-KV-head setting with k=1, each token computes 8 routed query experts plus one shared head; the weighted-sum slot reuses selected outputs and does not add another attention computation. The final output projection receives 10 slots rather than the 16 slots of the dense baseline, so the comparison is not strictly parameter-matched in W_O.d:T7c0,The method, Efficient Operator Search (EOS), starts from a frozen multimodal foundation model and parameterizes three decisions that are usually hand-crafted: which layers perform reduction, how many tokens are retained at each layer, and which reduction behavior is used.

The first key piece is the unified operator family. At an active reduction layer, EOS selects K anchor tokens using an importance score and treats the remaining tokens as reduction candidates. It then computes normalized similarities between candidate tokens and anchors. A temperature parameter τ controls the sharpness of the assignment: low τ approaches hard nearest-anchor merging, while high τ produces softer redistribution or pooling-like behavior. A transfer gate determines whether information from discarded tokens is removed or preserved and transferred to anchors. With the gate off, the operator behaves like pruning; with the gate on, different τ regimes recover merging, pooling, or soft reweighting. The central claim is that these common operators are not isolated tricks but different regions of one shared continuous space.

The second piece is the search space. EOS does not only search operator regimes; it also searches layer activation and retention budgets. In other words, layer selection, token budgets, and operator parameters are all learnable. Discrete choices are relaxed into differentiable forms so that optimization can proceed through expected task loss under sampled policies and efficiency constraints.

The third piece is the objective. The search optimizes a trade-off between task loss and computational cost while keeping the backbone frozen. This makes the method cheaper than retraining and more aligned with deployment-time optimization. Relative to methods such as ToMe or SparseVLM, the novelty is not a new fixed compression rule, but a shared parameterization that contains several known rules as special cases and lets the search discover layer-wise hybrids.e:T5f4,The experiments are designed to establish two points: first, the unified space can recover strong hand-designed baselines; second, searching over that space yields better accuracy-efficiency trade-offs than using a single manually chosen operator. The paper evaluates EOS on representative multimodal benchmarks and compares against pruning- and merging-based baselines. The reported outcome is that EOS can reproduce known operating regimes while also finding better hybrid configurations under similar token or compute budgets.

The most informative part is the ablation structure rather than any single headline number. The paper separates the effects of searching layer positions, searching token budgets, searching operator regimes, and jointly searching all of them. Joint search performs best, which suggests strong coupling between where compression happens, how much compression is applied, and how information is transferred. Another important ablation studies τ and the transfer gate: τ controls the transition from hard merge to soft pooling-like behavior, while the gate controls whether reduced tokens are dropped or preserved through redistribution.

Then read the method section carefully, especially the roles of assignment temperature τ, the transfer gate, layer activation, and retention budget. That is where the paper’s real content sits.
