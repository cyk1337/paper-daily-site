18:T40c,The technical core has two parts. First, the paper uses Scheffé response-surface models, which are designed for variables constrained to the probability simplex. A first-order Scheffé model represents additive domain effects. A second-order model adds x_i x_j terms, capturing pairwise deviations from additivity: a domain pair can be better or worse together than expected from their individual contributions. Because the number of interactions grows quickly with the number of domains, the paper uses sparse second-order Scheffé models, keeping the response surface interpretable and less prone to overfitting when proxy data are limited. Second, the paper treats proxy mixture choices as an optimal design problem. Instead of sampling candidate mixtures randomly, it constructs model-robust I-optimal designs.
