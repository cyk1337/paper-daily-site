5:T447,The central notion is gauge equivariance: an optimizer is equivariant if it behaves consistently under orthogonal rotations of the latent factor basis. A structure theorem shows that a memoryless update is equivariant iff it takes the form Δ=H(GG^T)G, a left preconditioner determined by the Gram matrix. Under this classification, GD, Polyak momentum, Adam with a shared scalar second moment, Muon (including its Newton–Schulz iterates), and Shampoo's Kronecker preconditioner with damping are equivariant; Adam, RMSProp, signSGD, Lion, and Adafactor break equivariance because they apply entrywise nonlinearities at zero state. An exact first-step defect is derived for zero-initialized Adam. A transfer theorem shows that dynamics under a shared scalar preconditioner equal gradient flow under a rescaled time, so existing low-rank/minimum-nuclear-norm results for gradient flow carry over. Equivariance is necessary but not sufficient: low-rank convergence also depends on the spectral schedule over singular values attracting dynamics to the low-rank manifold within the training budget.6:T4ae,M+Adam combines two update branches:
- Additive branch: Adam/AdamW-style moment estimates and an additive step in weight space. This is crucial near zero and for sign flips, but in low precision it can stall at large magnitudes because updates smaller than the local quantization bin are rounded away.
- Multiplicative branch: Madam-style relative (scale) updates so the effective step scales with |w|, helping the optimizer move on coarse grids at large magnitudes. But multiplicative updates preserve sign and cannot escape exact zeros.

A notable detail is the derivation of the multiplicative branch via exponent-space gradients: using floating-point decomposition w = m·2^e, the method expresses the gradient w.r.t. the exponent in a form that does not require explicitly tracking e, then applies second-moment normalization. Practical safeguards include a threshold τ to avoid division by tiny weights and clipping of relative factors.

The combination is designed so multiplicative updates dominate where quantization bins are large, while additive updates handle the near-zero and sign-change regime. The paper also provides a monotone descent proof under standard smoothness assumptions.7:T4ee,There are two central ingredients. First, the paper formalizes adaptivity as the log-sensitivity of the pre-conditioner to global gradient scaling. Under this view, SGD, RMSProp, and Adam become points on a single spectrum. The proposed optimizer, Anon, introduces a real-valued hyperparameter gamma that controls this adaptivity continuously over the full real line. This allows interpolation between SGD-like and Adam-like behavior, and also extrapolation beyond both. Second, the authors recognize that extreme adaptivity, especially gamma < 0 or gamma > 1, can destabilize training. To address this, they introduce incremental delay update, or IDU, a stabilization mechanism intended to replace hard max-tracking such as AMSGrad. Instead of simply clipping the second-moment statistics through a running maximum, IDU appears to use a softer, multi-scale delayed accumulation scheme that constrains how fast the effective pre-conditioner can change. The theoretical analysis covers both online convex and stochastic non-convex settings, with the main goal being convergence across the full adaptivity spectrum. The novelty is less about a new moment estimator and more about elevating pre-conditioner sensitivity itself into the primary optimizer design axis.8:T404,From the available full-text content, experiments span image classification, language, and generative modeling, and the authors claim consistent gains over strong baselines across tasks and architectures. What does seem central is that the paper studies performance as a function of continuous adaptivity, rather than only comparing named optimizers, and that it compares IDU against AMSGrad-style stabilization under extreme gamma values. At each step, it perturbs parameters along a random direction z_t, computes a finite-difference loss projection from forward passes, and reconstructs the update direction using the same pseudorandom seed. The challenge is that Adam requires m_t and v_t, two full-size state vectors.

The key idea is to replace exact persistent moments with truncated, reconstructable approximations. First, AdaMeZO uses truncated first- and second-moment estimates rather than full EMAs over the entire optimization history. Older gradients are discarded, motivated by the fact that EMA already downweights them exponentially and their contribution is small relative to recent noisy zeroth-order estimates.

Second, the method exploits finer-grained PRNG replay. Instead of materializing full random directions globally, it regenerates them block by block. This allows the optimizer to reconstruct local pieces of the recent moment history on demand from stored scalar projections and PRNG seeds, rather than storing full moment tensors. In effect, it trades lightweight recomputation for memory.

This is the main distinction from prior adaptive ZO methods. HiZOO explicitly approximates diagonal Hessian information. Helene is closer to a direct Adam-style ZO optimizer with explicit state. AdaMeZO instead focuses on eliminating optimizer-state residency while preserving most of the benefit of preconditioning. The headline result is efficiency rather than just end accuracy: AdaMeZO almost always reaches the same stopping criterion as MeZO with up to 70% fewer forward passes, while also delivering better final task performance. That matters because forward evaluations are the dominant cost in zeroth-order tuning.
