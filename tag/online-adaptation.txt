19:T489,The method starts from a soft RLHF formulation. The state is the generated prefix, the action is the next token, and the transition is deterministic concatenation. With a KL penalty to a reference model, the optimal policy satisfies a soft Bellman equation and its logits can be expressed as the reference logits plus an optimal Q-value term. Personalized alignment is then cast as a multi-objective MDP. Each base reward r_k represents a preference dimension, and a user is represented by a simplex weight vector w, giving a combined reward r_w = Σ_k w_k r_k. The central theoretical claim is that the logits of personalized policies admit a shared spectral representation: they can be approximated by a linear combination of a small number of basis logit functions. This leads to a two-stage algorithm. Offline, train specialized policies for fine-grained preference dimensions. Online, infer or receive the user preference weights and combine the basis policies, either through output/logit merging or parameter merging. The theory provides sub-optimality bounds relative to a fully tailored policy under controlled spectral approximation and merging error.1a:T5a1,(must_read: detailed technical analysis)
1) Non-stationary regression model: the paper uses a first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) process to model time evolution of the regression target/parameter, yielding an analytically tractable notion of drift across context positions.
2) Baseline vs. proposed mechanism:
- Standard linear attention, previously linked to kernel regression / least-squares-like behavior in stationary ICL analyses, aggregates past information without strong temporal selectivity; under drift, stale samples contaminate the estimate.
- Gated linear attention (GLA) introduces gating in the recurrent accumulation/state update, inducing learnable temporal decay (a learnable recency bias), akin to exponential forgetting.
3) Main theoretical message:
- Linear attention incurs additional drift-induced error terms (in both training and testing) that grow with non-stationarity strength.
- GLA’s gating yields time-weighted contributions that reduce drift bias and lead to improved error bounds / generalization under suitable conditions.
4) Mechanistic interpretation: gating turns indiscriminate accumulation into a controllable forgetting filter, closer to classical adaptive filtering / online learning with forgetting factors.
