a:T402,The method is a controlled adaptation benchmark spanning five objective types: lexical binding, factual association, behavioral policy learning, causal mapping, and procedural reasoning. The authors then compare full-stack LoRA against localized LoRA placed in early, middle, or late layers. For each objective, they measure three outcomes: whether the target behavior is acquired, whether it transfers to held-out contexts, and whether it stays bounded rather than over-applying. This yields an adaptation geometry for each learning type. The important idea is that LoRA is used here as a controlled intervention tool, not mainly as an efficiency method. That distinguishes the work from standard PEFT papers, which usually optimize parameter budget versus downstream accuracy, and from model-editing papers that focus mostly on factual memory. The paper broadens the scope to behavior, causality, and procedural structure, and asks whether layer location itself reveals something about the kind of learning being introduced.b:T431,The main empirical finding is that the five objective classes exhibit distinct depth-wise signatures. Lexical binding tends to favor early-layer adaptation for acquisition and boundedness, but broader updates are often needed for stronger transfer. Factual association prefers later layers among localized adaptations. Behavioral policy learning, causal mapping, and procedural reasoning show different preferences again, indicating that these are not interchangeable forms of adaptation. A key message is that localized adaptation is not merely a cheaper approximation to full fine-tuning. It changes the character of what is learned. Some sites support narrow acquisition but poor transfer; others encourage overgeneralization. The material provided here does not include the full numeric tables or model sizes, but the experimental structure is already clear: compare early/middle/late/full-stack LoRA and evaluate acquisition, transfer, and boundedness separately. When reading the results, the important signal is the trade-off pattern, not just average task accuracy.c:T46e,The method scores each layer by how much full attention at that layer improves answer-token likelihood under a SWAA-style setup.
- Setup: During prefill, each layer can be Full Attention (FA) or Sliding-Window Attention (SWA); during decoding, all layers use FA (full-attention decode) to isolate prefill degradation.
- Per-layer scoring: For a prompt+answer pair, compute mean NLL on answer tokens under (i) all layers SWA in prefill, and (ii) only layer ℓ switched to FA while all others remain SWA. The score Δℓ is the NLL reduction from (ii) vs (i). Larger Δℓ implies that layer benefits more from global context during prefill.
- Selection: Average Δℓ over a small long-context calibration set, then pick the top-k layers (greedy) for the FA budget.
- Deconfounding check: Repeat ranking with short prompts and show low rank correlation with long-prompt ranking (Spearman ρ=0.306), arguing the signal is specific to long-range needs rather than generic layer sensitivity.
