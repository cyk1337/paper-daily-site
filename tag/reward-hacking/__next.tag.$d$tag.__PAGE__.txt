9:T854,方法上，LM2LM 把自动化 LM 开发形式化为一个 sequential decision problem。每个任务对应一个目标模型家族、参数规模、训练预算和 reference config；proposer 负责生成自然语言 proposal，builder 把 proposal 转成可执行修改，执行后得到相对 reference 的验证损失改变量。训练目标不是直接预测实验结果，而是用 RL 提升 proposer 生成高收益 proposal 的能力。

为了解决真实奖励昂贵的问题，作者引入 Research World Model, RWM。它不是单独训练的回归器，而是一个冻结的大语言模型，输入包括任务描述、reference 配置、历史实验记录以及候选 proposal，输出该 proposal 相对 baseline 的预期 gain。也就是说，RWM 通过 in-context experimental records 来做 outcome prediction，再把预测 gain 变成 RL reward。这样避免了为每个 proposal 都跑一次完整训练。

但作者的重点不只是“用 LLM 当 reward model”，而是处理分布漂移。随着 proposer 经 RL 更新，它会越来越倾向于生成 RWM 评分高、但历史记录覆盖不足的 proposal。固定记录下的 RWM 会在这些区域系统性高估，RL 随后放大这种偏差。为此，LM2LM 引入 on-policy calibration：周期性地从当前策略生成的 proposal 中，挑选 RWM 排名靠前的一部分做真实执行，把测得的真实 gain 追加进 RWM 的上下文知识，再继续训练。这个设计有两个关键点。其一，不是随机补数据，而是专门校准当前策略最可能 exploit 的高分区域；其二，不重新训练 reward model，而是通过更新 in-context records 让冻结 LLM 的判断重新锚定到真实结果。

从方法取舍看，这比纯离线 preference learning 更适合高成本实验环境，因为它把真实执行预算集中花在“最危险的误差区域”；也比全量在线 RL 便宜得多，因为绝大多数 proposal 仍由 RWM 评估。论文还分析了 RWM 的 ranking quality，说明在这类任务里，排序能力比绝对数值回归更关键，RL 只需要相对好坏足够稳定即可。a:T84a,LM2LM formulates automated LM development as a sequential decision problem. Each environment specifies a target model family, size, training budget, and a reference configuration. A proposer LLM generates natural-language modification proposals; a builder converts them into executable changes; execution returns the gain relative to the reference validation loss. The proposer is trained with RL to generate proposals with higher expected gains.

The key scalability device is a Research World Model (RWM). Instead of executing every proposal, LM2LM uses a frozen LLM to predict the gain of a candidate proposal conditioned on the task description, the reference setup, and a set of prior experimental records provided in context. These predicted gains become RL rewards. This is not just a generic reward model. The design relies on the frozen LLM’s ability to reason over structured experimental history and produce useful relative rankings among proposals.

The more important contribution is how the paper handles reward drift. As the proposer improves, it shifts toward proposals that score highly under the RWM but may lie outside the support of the initial experimental records. A fixed surrogate then becomes vulnerable to systematic overestimation, and RL amplifies that error. LM2LM addresses this with on-policy calibration: at intervals during training, it takes a subset of high-scoring proposals from the current policy, executes them for real, and appends the measured outcomes to the RWM context. This keeps the surrogate grounded on exactly the region of proposal space that matters for the current policy.

That choice is deliberate. Randomly collecting more experiments would be wasteful. Re-training a separate reward model after each batch would also be heavier. By updating the in-context evidence of a frozen LLM, the method concentrates real-execution budget on the most decision-critical and most exploitable proposals. The paper further argues that ranking quality matters more than perfectly calibrated scalar prediction: for RL, preserving the relative ordering of proposals is often enough.b:T56e,The experiments evaluate proposal quality in held-out LM-development environments using real execution. The paper gives a useful cost calculation: training a 486M-parameter model on 8B tokens takes about 45 B200 GPU-hours. A GRPO-style setup with 100 updates, 32 states per update, and 8 proposals per state would require 25,600 proposal evaluations. If all were executed directly, that would cost about 1,152,000 GPU-hours, or more than $16M. This establishes why surrogate-based training is necessary.

Empirically, LM2LM outperforms baselines that rely only on past experimental records, and it also beats strong in-context approaches on real executed gains. The paper additionally claims that a Qwen3.8-27B proposer trained with LM2LM surpasses GPT-6-sol on held-out environments. The most informative results are the analyses of surrogate reliability. Early in training, the RWM provides useful proposal rankings. Without on-policy calibration, ranking quality degrades as RL progresses, eventually becoming worse than random because the policy learns to exploit overestimated regions. With calibration, overestimation is reduced and ranking quality is largely preserved, which in turn preserves real-world gains. A proposer writes tasks and weighted rubrics. An adversary tries to exploit each rubric. A judge scores answers according to the rubric. A solver is trained from the resulting rewards. A meta-optimizer reads training feedback and confirmed exploits, then rewrites the proposer’s instructions for future task and rubric generation. Each task consists of a request x and a rubric R={(c_j,p_j)}, where c_j is a criterion and p_j is a nonzero weight in [-10,10]. - Consider a primary reward r_p (correctness) and an auxiliary reward r_a (brevity/latency/style).
- Key assumption: auxiliary rewards are more reliably attainable than primary rewards (you can be short/fast without solving the task).
- Study symmetric aggregation rules that treat channels equally and assign positive credit to single-channel success (including auxiliary-only success).
- In a one-step stochastic decision model, prove that under this imbalance, deterministic conditional-mean GRPO dynamics converge to auxiliary-favoring behavior from any initialization, even if the initial policy prefers the primary objective.
- Further characterize when this constitutes reward hacking relative to an unknown true reward.

2) Defense: Trust-Anchored Gating (TAG)
- Two design questions: which channel should be prioritized, and how to enforce that priority.
- Anchor selection via drift: briefly optimize each channel in isolation and pick the channel that induces the least policy drift as the anchor (intuition: exploitable channels cause large shortcut-driven shifts).
- Gated aggregation: break symmetry by construction—auxiliary-only success is scored no better than failing both channels, while joint success remains better than primary-only success.
- Provide a convergence guarantee: with the primary channel as anchor and assuming primary success remains achievable, TAG drives conditional-mean GRPO to the primary-favoring action from any initialization, for any positive gating weight.
