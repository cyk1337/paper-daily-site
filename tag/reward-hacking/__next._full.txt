Reward design is the main experimental axis. The paper constructs reward functions with different levels of task-relevant access: outcome-only rewards, keyword-based evidence rewards, and more answer-aware or semantically grounded variants. The important point is not that more reward components are always better, but whether the reward tracks oracle correctness. The study systematically varies reward design, ambiguity, model scale, and RL algorithm. On the optimization side, it compares GRPO, RLOO, and DAPO, not to rank them in general, but to examine how different policy optimization schemes propagate shortcuts.

The most useful diagnostic is Newly Rewarded Failure Rate (NRFR). By comparing SFT and RL outputs and conditioning on reward gains, NRFR identifies cases where RL newly assigns higher reward to oracle-invalid behavior. This is more informative than final error rate alone because it disentangles inherited model weaknesses from RL-induced failures.
