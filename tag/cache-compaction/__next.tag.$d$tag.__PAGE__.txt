9:T7fb,The core idea is a quadratic surrogate for attention-output error, used inside an OBS-like iterative elimination procedure. Starting from single-head attention, the paper avoids optimizing the exact post-deletion output error because softmax renormalization makes the objective combinatorial and strongly coupled. Instead, it uses calibration queries to approximate the query distribution encountered during reuse of the fixed context, and derives a quadratic surrogate over the resulting output error.

That surrogate has two properties that make the method practical:
1. For each candidate deletion, it yields a closed-form optimal compensating update to the remaining cache entries.
2. It also yields a closed-form score for the increase in surrogate loss after applying that optimal compensation.

This leads to KV-Surgeon: at each step, evaluate every candidate entry by the residual error after optimal compensation, remove the best candidate, apply the compensating update to the surviving cache, and repeat. The next deletion is then chosen against an already compensated cache, not the original one. This is the key difference from methods such as CAOTE, OBCache, or attention-matching-style selection, which either estimate single-deletion damage with the rest fixed or postpone refitting until after subset selection.

The paper also presents a faster variant, KV-Surgeon-fast, which trades some quality for much lower offline construction cost. A final joint refit is still used after selection, but the important point is that compensation already influences which entries survive. The retained set is therefore selected with awareness of how well the remaining cache can absorb the deletion.

A notable strength of the formulation is that it is not purely attention-mass based. It also captures value redundancy: if an entry's value is already close to the current output, removing it may have limited effect even when its attention weight is nontrivial. This helps explain why the method improves over attention-only heuristics.a:T5f4,The evaluation is fairly broad: QuALITY, LongHealth, and Long Code Arena; dense and MoE models; model sizes from 4B to 30B; and compaction ratios from 5x to 100x. The main empirical result is consistent: KV-Surgeon improves the cost-accuracy Pareto frontier across settings, with the clearest gains at aggressive compaction ratios.

Representative numbers reported in the paper:
- Averaged over experiments, KV-Surgeon improves QA accuracy by 4.3 points at 100x compaction relative to the previous best method.
- At 50x compaction, the average gain is 3.0 points.
- In the highlighted 50x setting on the largest model, the strongest baseline AM-HAK reaches 62.0% accuracy with 138 seconds pipeline time per context; KV-Surgeon reaches 65.1% at 145 seconds; KV-Surgeon-fast reaches 63.9% at only 54 seconds.

These results show two things. First, the method matters most when compaction is severe; at 5x and 10x, strong baselines are already close to lossless, so headroom is limited. Second, the fast variant gives a useful quality-cost tradeoff rather than being a cosmetic approximation.

The ablation logic appears to focus on whether compensation-aware selection beats single-deletion scoring, whether value-aware scoring beats attention-only rules, and whether iterative compensation is better than select-then-refit pipelines.
