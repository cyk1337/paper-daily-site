9:T5e9,No new architecture is proposed; instead the paper builds an analysis model. Data is assumed to arrive in groups (tasks), each task being generated by a sparse set of latent factors. Tasks share factors, and task similarity is modeled via latent-factor overlap (illustrated as a bipartite task–factor graph). Under this view, inference-time learning is modeled as: shared components are carried by internal representations of inputs/outputs, while the task-specific mapping between these representations must be learned at inference time by an inner learner.
From this setup, the paper argues for:
- Rich representation (“gather broadly”): if representations only capture what is minimally sufficient for iid generalization, many new tasks become unreachable; open-world adaptation needs a broader feature basis that supports recombination of latent factors.
- Predictive disentanglement (“group predictively”): beyond richness, representations should be partitioned into matched low-dimensional groups across inputs and outputs, and prediction should occur within aligned groups. This reduces the effective degrees of freedom the inner learner must fit for a new task, improving sample efficiency.
It further predicts: (1) inference-time learning requires a consistent inner learner; otherwise adding more related-task examples in context can degrade performance; (2) longer contexts can help only if the target task and related tasks are handled separately to avoid harmful coupling/negative transfer.a:T435,The experiments build on the VA-VAE implementation. The autoencoder and the diffusion prior use the same architecture and training budget across arms, with only the distillation objective changed. The autoencoder is trained from scratch on 256×256 ImageNet for 50 epochs with batch size 256, followed by a LightningDiT-XL prior trained for 64 epochs with batch size 1024. The comparisons include no distillation, position-wise distillation, first-order pooled distillation, and relational distillation, across latent shapes and teacher modalities. Pool-Align performs as well as or slightly better than position-wise alignment in settings where position-wise alignment is applicable, and it also works for 1D token-sequence latents. The cross-modal setting shows that distilling a text encoder into an image autoencoder can still improve diffusability, suggesting that the useful signal is not limited to visual patch correspondence. The relational objectives test a weaker form of geometry transfer; the most reliable variant appears to be direct first-order pooled matching.b:T534,The method, REST (REpresentation-Supervised Thoughts), augments the standard final-answer cross-entropy with four differentiable losses on the latent thought itself. The theoretical setup argues that CE on the final answer does not penalize bad intermediate representations as long as the correct answer remains sufficiently likely, so recursive systems can accumulate representational errors over rounds and across agents.

REST targets four properties. Causality requires the thought to preserve information needed to reproduce the correct output. Minimality pushes it to discard information irrelevant to that output. Separability prevents semantically distinct inputs, especially those with different output distributions, from collapsing to similar latent thoughts. Stability encourages the thought to reflect the distribution over plausible outputs rather than a single sampled answer trace. These losses are added to CE with tunable weights.

Implementation-wise, the paper studies both a multi-agent latent recursive system and a single-agent variant where a model feeds its own hidden states back as the next reasoning step. Importantly, REST does not change the architecture or inference procedure; it only changes the training objective. That makes the paper more about missing supervision than about a new system design.c:T432,The evaluation spans MATH500, GPQA-Diamond, MedQA, AIME2025/2026, LiveCodeBench-v6, and MBPP+. Under matched training budget, identical training data, and the same latent budget, REST improves over CE-only by +3.5 points on average in the multi-agent setting and +3.3 in the single-agent setting. The best configurations reach +7.5 and +6.5 points, respectively. For a skim-level read, these are the main numbers to retain.

The paper also reports that REST decodes 15.4% more tokens on average at inference. The authors attribute this to a higher rate of convergence to a final answer. That is an interesting trade-off: better latent supervision improves accuracy, but not necessarily by reducing visible decoding cost in the current setup.

The analysis sections matter more than usual here. The paper claims that CE-only systems exhibit thought collapse and retention of irrelevant information, while REST-trained systems better satisfy the targeted properties.
