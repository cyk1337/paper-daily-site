9:T871,CADFT is built on top of Dynamic Fine-Tuning. DFT starts from the observation that standard SFT has inverse-probability gradient amplification: rare or low-probability tokens can dominate updates. DFT rewrites the objective so that updates scale with a bounded term proportional to (1 + log pt), avoiding the blow-up as pt approaches zero.

CADFT argues that token-level correction is not enough because instruction datasets are highly heterogeneous at the sample level. Some demonstrations are well aligned with the model’s current capabilities, while others are too hard, too mismatched, or too noisy. To address this, the method introduces a dynamic compatibility signal, computed from the model’s own likelihoods over a sample, to estimate how compatible a demonstration is with the current policy.

This compatibility score is then used to modulate sample-level update strength. High-compatibility samples retain stronger supervision; low-compatibility samples are downweighted rather than discarded. Three design choices matter.

First, compatibility is treated as a relative quantity, not a fixed threshold. It is normalized adaptively so that the weighting remains meaningful as the model improves over training.

Second, sample-level compatibility is combined with DFT’s token-level correction. So the final update depends both on whether individual tokens are pathological and on whether the sample as a whole lies within the model’s current feasible learning region.

Third, the paper adds delayed rewriting for persistently incompatible samples. Instead of rewriting from the start, the method waits for a warm-up period and triggers rewriting only at low frequency. The rationale is to avoid early self-reinforcement, where a weak model would rewrite difficult but valuable demonstrations into easier yet lower-quality targets.

The theoretical framing is that CADFT is a variance-controlled estimator: DFT reduces token-level variance, while CADFT further reduces sample-level variance. The theory is more interpretive than foundational, but it gives a coherent explanation for why CADFT might be more stable than vanilla SFT or plain DFT.a:T702,From the provided full text, the experiments span language, code, multimodal reasoning, and cold-start RL initialization. - 相比 Temporal Ensembling/Mean Teacher 类方法：VISTA 不是简单时间平均，而是用验证集上的“边际覆盖”去做有选择的、区域专家式的蒸馏信号。

Main components:
1) Analysis metric (not necessarily the training objective): define C_e as validation samples correctly classified at epoch e and C_E at the final epoch E. The Structural Residual is C_e ∩ C_E; the DeviationGap(e) measures how many samples were correct at e but not retained at E, exposing capability loss masked by rising overall validation accuracy.
2) Marginal Coverage scoring: a past checkpoint is an expert anchor if it correctly covers validation examples that later checkpoints miss. VISTA sorts checkpoints by validation accuracy and sweeps through them to compute coverage subsets and each checkpoint’s marginal contribution.
3) Coverage-weighted blended targets: using stored predictions p_s(x) from selected anchors, VISTA forms a weighted ensemble soft target for the next epoch. Training at epoch t+1 distills the current model toward this blended target, with a scheduler β_t controlling the strength/mix with hard labels.
4) Lightweight variant: to reduce the practical burden of storing many checkpoints and computing ensembles, a reduced-storage version is proposed, claimed to cut storage overhead by ~90% while preserving peak performance.

Positioning vs prior work:
- Unlike temporal ensembling/EMA teachers, VISTA is selective and region-aware via marginal coverage rather than uniform averaging.
