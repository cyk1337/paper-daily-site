17:T655,CLAS replaces the fixed LAS coefficient with a context-dependent linear gate. Standard LAS modifies an activation as h~_{l,t} = h_{l,t} + alpha d_l, where d_l is a unit steering direction extracted from a probe. CLAS instead uses h~_{l,t} = h_{l,t} + (c_l · [h_{l,t}; 1]) d_l. The steering direction d_l remains fixed, while a learned sensing vector c_l reads the current hidden state and outputs a token- and layer-specific steering strength. The appended constant 1 acts as a bias term.

The method has two stages. First, concept probes are trained and per-layer steering directions are extracted, as in prior probe-based steering work. Second, those directions are frozen and only the sensing vectors are learned using standard next-token prediction on a small prompt-completion dataset. This design makes several deliberate trade-offs:
1. interpretability is retained because the semantic direction still comes from the probe;
2. the learnable part is restricted to a linear gate, which keeps parameter count and sample complexity low;
3. unlike LoRA or ReFT, CLAS does not learn a general low-rank residual patch, but a mechanism that decides how strongly to apply an already identified concept direction.

A notable part of the paper is the comparison with ReFT and LoRA not only as steering baselines but also as sources of interpretable directions. The authors test whether the low-rank directions learned by those methods can serve as concept monitors. They can to some extent, but probe-derived directions are consistently more linearly readable, which supports the CLAS decomposition into direction plus gate.18:T49e,The evaluation spans 11 steering tasks and 4 instruction-tuned model families. Tasks include step-by-step reasoning, multilingual translation, and TL;DR-style summarization, under a low-data setup where the target behavior is induced from limited completion data rather than explicit instructions. The main empirical findings are consistent across tasks:
1. CLAS outperforms standard LAS on nearly every model-task pair;
2. under limited labeled data, CLAS matches or exceeds ReFT and LoRA;
3. tuning is substantially cheaper than methods that train low-rank adapters, since CLAS only learns one sensing vector per layer;
4. in 11 concept-monitoring tasks, probe-derived directions used by CLAS are more interpretable than the directions implicit in ReFT or LoRA.

The paper also includes interference experiments, which are important: after steering toward one behavior, the authors measure how much other behaviors degrade. CLAS shows more controlled interference than fixed-strength LAS, suggesting that contextual gating reduces the tendency to globally distort the model distribution.
