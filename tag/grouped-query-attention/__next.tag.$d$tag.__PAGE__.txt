9:T40f,The paper addresses a precise but important failure mode in μP/Complete-P: standard μP checks can appear to pass for grouped-query attention (GQA), yet learning-rate transfer across widths still breaks in practice. The goal is not to patch this empirically, but to explain why the existing theory becomes insufficient for GQA and to derive a parameterization that restores transfer. The issue has two layers. First, prior spectral-μP arguments treated spectral-norm conditions as a sufficient route to feature learning, while the operational checks used in implementations were weaker; this leaves room for architectures that pass coordinate checks without satisfying the stronger operator-scale conditions needed for transfer. Second, GQA introduces intrinsically low-rank weight structures. For such matrices, the ordinary spectral norm measures amplification along worst-case directions that are effectively never seen under the training input distribution, so it mischaracterizes the scale that actually matters during optimization.a:T72d,The technical contribution has three parts. First, the paper reformulates the spectral-μP perspective around operator norms more directly. Instead of starting from a weaker feature-learning definition and then showing that spectral conditions imply it, the authors elevate operator-norm scaling conditions to the center of the framework. This lets them derive Complete-P-style prescriptions for depth and weight decay without leaning on a lazy-training fallback.

Second, they introduce the expected operator norm to replace the ordinary spectral norm when matrices are rank-degenerate. The standard spectral norm captures the largest possible deformation over all unit vectors. That is appropriate for full-rank random matrices, where the extremal scale is asymptotically representative. But for GQA-like low-rank matrices, the extremal directions occupy measure-zero sets relative to the i.i.d. input distribution encountered during training. The expected operator norm instead measures the average deformation under a chosen input distribution, taken in the paper to be Gaussian i.i.d. This preserves the usual scaling for full-rank random matrices while correcting the mismatch for low-rank operators.

Third, using this revised norm and operator-centric framework, the paper derives μP scaling rules for GQA. Because GQA shares key/value heads across multiple query groups, the effective rank, output scale, and update scale of the relevant projections differ from standard multi-head attention. The authors work through these differences and derive the corresponding initialization, learning-rate, depth, and weight-decay scalings. They also propose a stricter spectral-norm coordinate check, showing that the original implementation can pass the old check while failing the stronger one that actually predicts transfer behavior.b:T609,The experiments are aimed at validating the theory rather than maximizing downstream benchmark scores. One key result is diagnostic: the original μP implementation for GQA passes the customary coordinate checks but fails a stricter spectral/operator-norm check, and this aligns with its empirical failure to transfer learning rates across widths. That is an important negative result because it shows the old check is not sufficiently discriminative for structured attention.

The main empirical validation is width-wise learning-rate transfer under GQA. With the original scaling, the optimal learning-rate region does not remain stable across widths. With the newly derived GQA-μP scaling, transfer becomes substantially more consistent. The paper also reports that transfer across different GQA repetition counts is noisy, which is a useful practical warning: changing repetition is not a benign architectural perturbation from the standpoint of hyperparameter transfer.
