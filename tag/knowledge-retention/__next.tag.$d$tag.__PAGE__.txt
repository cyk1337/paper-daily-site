5:T644,The core method is Act2Answer. Existing VLM knowledge benchmarks are converted into short tabletop episodes where the agent answers by taking a simple placement action among candidates instead of generating text. This design serves two purposes. First, many VLAs do not expose a clean text-generation interface, so text QA is not a faithful evaluation path. Second, by reducing the task to a short action episode, the benchmark minimizes confounds from long-horizon planning, motor execution, and environment complexity.

The benchmark contains 1,720 unique binary questions across 12 categories, including attribute, state, color, symmetry, shape, emotion, celebrity, living world, counting, time, traffic, and public information. The emphasis is diagnostic control rather than embodied realism.

The analysis has two layers. At the behavioral level, the paper compares 7 modern VLA systems against 9 strong VLM baselines to measure retention gaps across knowledge categories. At the representation level, it introduces layerwise intent probing: linear probes are trained on per-layer representations to predict the correct answer for each episode. This helps distinguish between two failure modes: the information is gone, or the information is still present internally but is not translated into the correct action.

A useful conceptual framing in the paper is the decomposition of embodied task success into perception, knowledge, control, and environment. Act2Answer is explicitly designed to hold control and environment complexity down so that knowledge-sensitive behavior becomes more measurable.6:T68c,There are three main empirical takeaways.

First, current VLAs tend to remain reasonably strong on simple perceptual categories, but they show noticeably larger degradation on richer semantic categories relative to their source VLMs. In other words, low-level visual distinctions such as color or shape are retained better than broader commonsense or world-knowledge judgments. From the provided full text, the reliable quantitative facts are the evaluation scale: 7 VLAs, 9 VLM baselines, 1,720 binary questions, and 12 categories. The paper explicitly claims substantially larger VLA-VLM gaps on richer semantic categories, but the exact per-category numbers are not visible in the excerpt and should not be invented.

Second, VQA co-training is associated with stronger performance on knowledge-sensitive embodied tasks. This is an important recipe-level observation: action fine-tuning alone does not guarantee preservation of the knowledge interface, while continued QA-style supervision appears to help maintain it.

Third, the layerwise probing results suggest that answer-relevant information can remain linearly decodable from internal representations even when the final action is wrong. That points to a readout or interface problem, not only catastrophic forgetting. For foundation-model researchers, this is the most interesting result in the paper because it separates representational retention from behavioral accessibility.

When reading the full experiments, the key things to verify are whether the VQA co-training effect is consistent across model families, whether the VLA-VLM gap is uniform across categories, and where in depth the probe accuracy peaks.7:T7ca,The paper first reviews several diversity-oriented SFT objectives, including standard CE, distribution-preserving alternatives, and regularized variants. The central claim is that fixing only one side of the problem is insufficient. If one only emphasizes rare patterns, the model may still forget the pretrained distribution. If one only preserves the base model distribution, head patterns in the supervised data still dominate learning. This motivates TOFU, a Tempered Focal loss.

From the formulation and motivation described in the text, TOFU combines two ideas. The focal component downweights easy, already-high-probability targets so that hard tokens and low-frequency patterns receive stronger gradient signal. This addresses the tendency of instruction tuning to concentrate on a narrow set of dominant responses. The tempered component acts as a conservative update mechanism relative to the pretrained model, reducing excessive drift away from the base distribution and preserving alternative plausible continuations that are not explicitly present in the SFT data. In effect, TOFU tries to control two gradient sources at once: the sharp pull from one-hot supervision and the stabilizing pull from the reference model.

Relative to prior methods:
1. unlike plain CE, it does not treat all token errors equally and explicitly reallocates learning signal toward hard and tail events;
2. unlike plain focal loss, it is not only a difficulty-based reweighting scheme, because it also tries to retain pretrained knowledge;
3. unlike pure KL anchoring or distillation-style regularization, it is not uniformly conservative and still allows substantial updates where the supervised signal is genuinely corrective.

From a training-dynamics perspective, the method can be read as a more selective budget allocation rule: spend less update budget on high-frequency, low-information tokens, spend more on tail tokens, and use a tempered reference to avoid catastrophic distributional drift.8:T4e3,Based on the provided full-text excerpt, the authors run broad evaluations across model families, datasets, and benchmarks, compare existing diversity-oriented SFT objectives in a unified setup, and separately test focal loss as an SFT objective. The qualitative conclusion is that TOFU improves generative diversity over standard CE while keeping output quality competitive. Focal loss alone already helps with low-frequency pattern coverage, but without the tempered reference term it is not sufficient to reliably preserve pretrained knowledge.

However, the excerpt does not include the full experimental tables, model sizes, training-token counts, benchmark names, or exact numerical gains, so it would be unsafe to invent precise improvements. The robust takeaways are:
1. diversity collapse under standard SFT appears consistently across multiple settings rather than as a model-specific artifact;
2. quality-only evaluation can hide distributional narrowing, so diversity and quality must be measured jointly;
3. combining rare-pattern reweighting with reference preservation works better than either ingredient alone.

First, it separates one source of hallucination from others: not missing pre-training knowledge, not inference-time fabrication, but degradation of already acquired knowledge during SFT. That distinction is operationally important for evaluation and for choosing fine-tuning recipes. Second, it proposes two mitigation regimes with clear use cases. If new factual acquisition is unnecessary, factual plasticity should be suppressed; if new factual acquisition is required, output drift should be regularized with self-distillation. Third, the mechanism analysis argues against a simple global-capacity story. Forgetting appears to be driven more by localized representational interference between nearby facts than by running out of storage. That has implications for continual pretraining, data mixture design, entity representation, tokenizer choices, and knowledge editing. The contribution is not just a better regularizer; it is a cleaner decomposition of a common but previously entangled phenomenon.b:T96e,The method has three layers. First, the paper builds a controlled setup that separates task learning from factual learning. Following Gekhman et al., it uses SLiCK to determine whether each fact is already known by the base model. SLiCK probes the model under multiple randomized few-shot prompts and classifies facts into HighlyKnown, MaybeKnown, WeaklyKnown, and Unknown. The paper keeps only HighlyKnown and Unknown examples: the former measure forgetting, the latter measure acquisition of new facts. This filtering is important because it removes ambiguity between facts the model never knew and facts it knew but later corrupted.

Second, the paper tests whether reducing factual plasticity reduces forgetting. Motivated by prior work showing that parameter groups play different roles in factual storage and task behavior, the authors freeze selected subsets of parameters during SFT. The key observation is that the model can still learn the downstream task format and behavior while becoming much worse at absorbing new facts, and this reduction in factual plasticity sharply lowers forgetting on old facts. The point is not that freezing is universally optimal, but that it provides a clean intervention showing that task learning and factual writing are not fully coupled.

Third, the paper applies self-distillation as a continual-learning regularizer. During SFT, the current model is trained not only on supervised targets but also to stay close to its own pre-update output distribution, limiting output-distribution drift. This is similar in spirit to KL-based regularization, but the role here is specific: preserve old factual behavior while still allowing new factual acquisition. The mechanism section strengthens this interpretation with synthetic-fact experiments. The authors vary the surface form of new entities: either name-like strings, which are likely to live near existing entities in representation space, or UUID-like identifiers, which are not. If forgetting were mainly due to global capacity limits or generic behavior cloning, both conditions should behave similarly. Instead, forgetting rises strongly with scale for name-like entities, while UUID-based entities induce almost no forgetting even with 1M new facts. This points to representational overlap as the main driver. Self-distillation then works by reducing the drift that would otherwise corrupt nearby held-out facts.c:T71f,There are three main empirical results. This is especially relevant for settings like alignment tuning or private-domain adaptation where learning new facts is not the goal. Third, in settings where new factual acquisition is required, self-distillation reduces forgetting from roughly 15% to roughly 3% while still allowing effective learning of the introduced facts. That is the central result of the paper because it shows that stability and plasticity are not forced into a strict either-or choice.
