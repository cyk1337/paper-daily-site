9:T5d4,The method has two stages. Offline, the authors build a task-specific bank of draft trees. Starting from a small tree, they greedily expand frontier nodes and score each one-node augmentation via self-rollout, estimating mean accepted length. A subtle point is that candidate trees are first scored with a larger joint tree in a one-step what-if probe, which makes the estimate mildly optimistic; they therefore recalibrate candidates using each tree’s own proposal, attention mask, and KV-cache update before final ranking. They then evaluate the full acceptance frontier in the actual end-to-end decoding loop, including prefill, tree decoding, posterior evaluation, verification, and KV updates, and keep only throughput-Pareto-optimal trees. Online, these trees are stored in a precompiled TopologyBank containing masks, position offsets, and gather indices, so switching trees is an O(1) pointer swap rather than a kernel rebuild. The scheduler reads readily available signals such as base-model top-1 probability, EAGLE-2 path value, and recent acceptance history, and selects a tree per step. Three policies are studied: a threshold ladder, a static task-optimal rule, and a hysteretic binary policy with separate on/off thresholds. The hysteretic policy works best because it captures the main large-tree/small-tree trade-off while avoiding per-step oscillation.
