在这个接口之上，论文给了两类 confidence sequence。第一类是 EB-CS，强调 exact finite-sample validity，但要求事先给出一个与数据无关、几乎处处成立的 corrected payoff 上界 B。作者在 Leduc 上推导并穷举验证了这样的 bound。第二类是 AsympCS，放宽为渐近有效，需要满足极限方差条件，实际中更高效。论文还给出一个理论结果：在 variance-adaptive width 和 sublinear variance-regret 条件下，past-only online learner 的停止效率在渐近上不会比 deterministic oracle-width benchmark 慢。直观上，这说明在线学 value model 只要不偷看当前局，就可以逐步逼近“知道最好 value function”的评测效率。

另一个很有价值的分析是 width floor。作者指出，方差缩减并不总会等比例转化为更早停止，因为在 bet-capped EB-CS 下，声明的 payoff bound 会引入一个确定性的宽度下界 4B log(2/α)/t。一旦这个 floor 开始主导，继续降低方差也不会明显缩短停止时间。论文把这件事整理成三阶段的 order-level design guide，这比单纯报告“方差降了多少倍”更有解释力。19:T866,The method combines AIVAT with confidence sequences to form AV-AIVAT. AIVAT is a variance-reduction estimator for imperfect-information games. It uses a value function to construct conditional mean-zero corrections at selected decision points, reducing payoff variance without introducing bias. Classical AIVAT assumes a frozen value function, but in practice one would like the value model to improve during evaluation. The key contribution is a predictable, pre-action conditional-kernel interface. At each correction node, the evaluator must know the action’s conditional kernel given the information available immediately before the action, and the decision to enable the correction must also be fixed before the action is observed. In addition, the value function at time t may use only games 1 through t-1, never game t itself. These conditions prevent the current sample from choosing its own correction and preserve the conditional mean-zero property.

On top of this interface, the paper develops two confidence-sequence options. EB-CS provides exact finite-sample validity, but it requires a data-independent almost-sure bound on the corrected payoff. The authors derive and exhaustively verify such a bound in Leduc. AsympCS instead relies on asymptotic variance conditions and is more efficient in practice. The theory also includes a no-asymptotic-delay style result: under variance-adaptive widths and sublinear variance regret, a past-only online value learner asymptotically matches a deterministic oracle-width benchmark. Intuitively, online value learning can approach oracle stopping efficiency as long as it does not peek at the current game.

A particularly useful part of the paper is the width-floor analysis. Variance reduction does not automatically translate into proportionally earlier stopping. In the bet-capped EB-CS setting, the declared payoff bound induces a deterministic lower bound on interval width, 4B log(2/alpha) / t. Once this floor dominates, further variance reduction has little effect on stopping time. The resulting three-regime design guide is more informative than reporting variance reduction alone.1a:T688,The main empirical study uses a heads-up no-limit hold’em corpus with 71,439 paired hands across 15 LLM-agent configurations. AIVAT reduces variance by a median factor of 54 relative to raw per-hand payoffs. The more important metric is stopping time. At alpha = 0.05 and a target half-width of one big blind, paired replay shows a median raw-to-AIVAT stopping-time ratio of 74 under AsympCS. In practical terms, the same evaluation precision can be reached with roughly one seventy-fourth as many hands. Under the descriptive EB-CS execution, the ratio is only 1.37. The paper explains this large gap through the width floor: once the deterministic bound-driven floor dominates, lower variance no longer buys much earlier stopping.

For online learning, the authors test past-only value updates under two controlled profile-mismatch settings. The online learner recovers 77% to 79% of the frozen-to-oracle variance gap, which supports the claim that online improvement is possible without sacrificing validity.

The rolling-leaderboard simulation is also important. Under the null, continuously monitoring an ordinary fixed-sample interval and stopping at the first favorable look yields a 61% false-positive rate. Recomputing a confidence sequence at that same stopping time overturns essentially all such claims. When AsympCS continues under its own stopping rule, it detects injected effects of 0.1 and 0.2 big blinds in 81% and 100% of entries by 5,000 hands.
