On top of the byte baseline, the authors introduce a series of low-overhead interventions, each designed to mimic one hypothesized benefit of subword tokenization while minimizing confounders:
- Throughput/sequence compression: simulate the advantage of shorter sequences (more raw bytes seen per unit compute) within the byte pipeline.
- Vocabulary/output-layer scaling: simulate the capacity effects associated with larger discrete vocabularies, focusing on embedding/output-layer parameterization.
- Boundary priors: inject BPE boundary signals as explicit structure so the model can exploit segmentation-like cues without changing the base unit.
- Look-ahead/task easiness: approximate the “future hint” implicit in subword segmentation decisions that require looking ahead.
- Multi-byte prediction: add multi-token (multi-byte) prediction heads to emulate the fact that one subword prediction corresponds to predicting a byte n-gram.

The design choice is deliberate: keep the backbone and most hyperparameters fixed, and localize changes to input annotations, output heads, and objective granularity so attribution stays focused on tokenization-related factors.19:T526,The starting point is BLT: a local encoder processes bytes, an entropy-based patcher forms variable-length byte patches, a global model operates over latent patch representations, and a local decoder emits bytes. The paper introduces three mechanisms. BLT-D trains the decoder with both the standard next-byte prediction loss and a masked-byte prediction loss over corrupted fixed-size byte blocks. These blocks are derived from dynamically segmented patches but may cross patch boundaries, forcing the decoder to model future bytes beyond the average patch length. At inference time, a block of byte positions is initialized as masked and multiple positions are iteratively revealed per decoder step, reducing calls to the decoder, encoder, and global model. BLT-S is a self-speculative variant for standard BLT: the lightweight local decoder drafts bytes past the usual patch boundary, and the full BLT computation verifies the draft. Because rejected drafts roll back to the first mismatch, it preserves the output of standard autoregressive BLT while reducing expensive global evaluations. BLT-DV uses BLT-D’s dual training objective: diffusion decoding proposes a byte block, and the same model, run with causal masks, verifies it autoregressively.
