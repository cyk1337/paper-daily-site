18:T87a,Instead of probing a large open-ended LLM, the authors build a controlled reasoning sandbox called ChainSearch. It is based on the Countdown numbers game, but with explicit search trajectories and BACK tokens for backtracking. This matters because reasoning is not treated as a monotonic chain of thought; it includes state transitions, branch exploration, and recovery from wrong paths. The trajectories are generated with a modified A* procedure, and a compact transformer of about 3 million parameters is trained from scratch on this data. The small scale makes dense probing and intervention feasible across token positions, layers, components, and attention heads.

The analysis has three parts. First is representation detection: the paper uses linear probes, mean activation differences, and directly optimized candidate vectors to test whether the model contains linearly accessible signals of reasoning correctness. Here correctness is not just local token correctness; it is whether the current search state still permits a path to a correct final solution, which is especially nontrivial in the presence of backtracking. Second is localization: the authors do not restrict analysis to the final layer, because a final-layer signal may be readable but still be a poor intervention site once most computation has already happened. They search across positions, layers, and components. Third is causal testing: they inject the recovered directions into activations, or directly optimize vectors for improved final-answer likelihood, and then test whether those interventions actually improve reasoning.

The main innovation is not a new steering algorithm but a clean experimental design. Many steering papers work in large, noisy LLM settings where negative results are hard to interpret. Here the task is synthetic but nontrivial, the trajectories are structured, backtracking is explicit, and the model is small enough for exhaustive analysis. The paper also separates correctness from related signals such as search direction, state progression, and backtracking, which helps avoid collapsing multiple internal variables into a vague “reasoning feature.”19:T6cc,The main findings come in two layers. First, at the probing level, the model does contain statistically significant correctness information that is linearly decodable. Given activations at certain layers and positions, one can predict whether the current reasoning state is still on a path that can reach a correct solution. Second, at the intervention level, none of the candidate directions reliably improve reasoning. This holds for directions obtained from probes, from mean activation differences, and even from vectors directly optimized to increase the probability of a correct final answer. That is the central negative result: readable does not imply steerable.

The experimental setup uses a roughly 3M-parameter transformer trained from scratch on ChainSearch trajectories. Because the task has explicit search structure, evaluation can look not only at final success but also at local state properties along the trajectory. The paper systematically scans token positions, layers, and components to identify where correctness signals are strongest and where interventions might plausibly matter. One useful insight is that final-layer signals may be easiest to decode but are often poor intervention sites, since the upstream attention and MLP computations have already been completed. Another important result is that even directly optimizing a vector for better final-answer outcomes does not yield a stable steering direction, which weakens the objection that the failure is merely due to weak probe construction.

The scale is small, but the experimental logic is strong. The paper is not trying to maximize benchmark performance; it is trying to answer a causal question about internal representations and reasoning control.1a:T4ba,For a skim, start with the task definition and then move quickly to how the negative result is established. First, read the ChainSearch section carefully: understand the BACK token, state transitions, and how correctness is defined as continued reachability of a correct solution rather than simple local correctness. Second, read the probing setup, especially the search over token positions, layers, and components. Third, focus on the intervention experiments rather than probe accuracy alone. The paper’s main contribution is not that correctness can be decoded, but that decoding does not translate into reliable steering.

A reasonable reading budget is about 20 minutes. If you only want the main point, read the introduction, the ChainSearch task definition, the main probing figures, and the intervention results. If you want to think further, two questions are worth keeping in mind: whether the negative result is due to the model being small and controlled, or whether the clean setup actually makes the conclusion stronger; and whether correctness is better modeled as a distributed subspace, a temporal control policy, or a conditional computation pattern rather than a single linear direction.1b:T4f0,The experiments span multiple TSFM backbones, six datasets, and several forecasting horizons. RR-MoA wins in 54 out of 54 primary benchmark cells, with reported MSE reductions between 26% and 79% over baselines including LoRA, TRACE, AdaMix, and full fine-tuning, with p < 0.001. The authors evaluate 11 common MoE rescue mechanisms over 720 runs, including load balancing, z-loss, ReMoE, expert choice, and top-k sharpening; these optimization-side fixes close only 10.9% of the gap closed by the input-side intervention. The signal ratio R(D) anti-correlates with collapse risk with Spearman ρ = -0.88 and p < 0.002 across six backbones, three normalization families, and one vision modality. Moirai, whose I/O scaling is not learnable-affine, does not show the same collapse, supporting the normalization-based diagnosis. Naively injecting mean and variance back into the router is unstable, increasing MSE by 42% to 239% and causing re-collapse in 11 of 18 cells. Two variants extend the principle: SR-MoA replaces the external router with per-expert sigmoids and improves over RR-MoA by 13% to 42% on six datasets, while Residual-IA+ carries raw-statistics information into expert bodies and narrows the gap to DLinear across six backbones and four horizons.1c:T50d,The intervention study uses 2,522 questions over 227 long videos and reports 60,008 runs across three agent architectures: Video-R1, VideoMind, and EVA. In the preliminary study, sampled frames are varied over 16, 32, and 64, and EVA's tool-call budget is varied from 4 to 16. Existing benchmark scores provide unstable diagnostics: upstream capability scores do not reliably predict hallucination, and even benchmarks assigned to the same stage can move in opposite directions. The causal interventions are clearer. Replacing a length-matched random window with the oracle evidence span improves accuracy by about 17–18 points across all three architectures, roughly four times the effect of substantial frame corruption in the Watching stage. Deployed grounders recover only about one quarter of this available gain. Dilating the oracle span by up to 8x causes little loss, suggesting that overlap metrics such as mIoU can be poorly aligned with downstream usefulness. Incorrect evidence is more harmful than missing evidence: agents tolerate severe frame omission better than plausible but wrong frames from elsewhere in the video.
