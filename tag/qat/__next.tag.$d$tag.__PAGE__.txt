9:T4ad,The method replaces the in_proj and out_proj layers in all 48 Mamba-2 blocks with grouped ternary linear layers. These 96 modules account for about 85.7% of the parameters. Weights are partitioned into groups, mainly with group size 128. For each group, the scale is recomputed as the mean absolute weight value, then weights are clipped, rounded to {-1, 0, +1}, and dequantized by multiplying by the group scale. Gradients pass through a straight-through estimator. The SSM dynamics parameters A_log, D, and dt_bias, as well as conv1d, embeddings, lm_head, and norms, remain FP16 because prior SSM quantization work finds these components sensitive at low precision. Training uses the public FP16 Mamba-2 1.3B checkpoint as initialization and a frozen FP16 teacher for distillation, mixing language-model loss and distillation loss with α=0.5 and temperature 1.0. A key observation is zero-ratio collapse: when group scales are learned parameters, optimization can increase the scale, widen the zero region, push more weights to zero, and reduce capacity in a positive feedback loop. If q = QΔ(W) and v = ∇qℓ is the gradient with respect to the quantized weights, JacQuant uses ∇Wℓ ≈ B(W)v instead of ∇Wℓ ≈ Iv. The surrogate B(W) is constrained to be diagonal or block-diagonal; in the default grouped setting, each quantization group gets a single scalar gain bg. This is intentionally low-capacity. The goal is not to model the full Jacobian, but to capture the most important local fact: in bin interiors the effective gain should be closer to 1, while under clipping or saturation it should shrink toward 0. The paper proposes two inexpensive estimators. JacQuant-Probe injects random Gaussian perturbations and measures Q(W+δ)−Q(W), then fits a one-step slope per group. JacQuant-Dither uses subtractive dithering to embed hard quantization into a smoothed stochastic model and estimates the mean sensitivity under that model. The surrogate is refreshed only occasionally and reused across many QAT steps, so the runtime overhead stays small. The theory follows the same smoothed view: during code-preserving phases, if B(W) tracks the mean sensitivity, the update corresponds to a well-defined target gradient. The paper then gives standard non-convex convergence guarantees and linear rates under a PŁ condition.
