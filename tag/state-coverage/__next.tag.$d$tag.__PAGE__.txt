9:T452,The authors formulate OPD as training on on-policy autoregressive states s=(x, y_<i). The student samples rollouts under its current policy, and the teacher supplies a next-token distribution at each visited prefix. The student is then updated toward the teacher on those visited states, typically through a token-level KL or cross-entropy loss. The key move is to treat a query as a generator of states, not as a single training item. To quantify this, the paper defines state coverage: states visited by full-data OPD are grouped into semantic clusters, and a query set is scored by the fraction of those clusters reached by its rollouts. This avoids exact-prefix matching, which would be too sparse, and gives a better proxy for the supervision OPD actually sees. The second diagnostic is absorption rate, the fraction of the remaining teacher-student gap closed by one update. This separates insufficient data coverage from slow algorithmic uptake. The paper then tests the same account in multi-teacher OPD, off-domain prompting, content-free prompting, and a controlled comparison with one-shot RLVR.a:T4ad,The phenomenon is tested across four task domains and three model families. One-shot OPD continues to improve for hundreds of optimization steps and recovers most of the full-data OPD gain, even in cases where the student never solves the training query. The full-data reference contains about 17k queries. The most important measurement is state coverage: one query already reaches 71.5% of the states visited by full-data OPD, with most of that coverage appearing within the first 100 steps. Sixteen semantically diverse queries reach 98.9% coverage and match full-data training. The absorption-rate analysis shows similar decay patterns for one-shot and full-data OPD, indicating that the run length is governed more by how slowly OPD absorbs dense teacher supervision than by a lack of new prompts. In multi-teacher OPD, 16 diverse queries per domain are enough to match full-data training. Off-domain WildChat prompts and content-free templates remain nearly as effective as the full training set. In a controlled one-shot comparison, OPD achieves more than twice the validation gain of RLVR over 1000 steps because its token-level signal persists after the outcome reward has been exhausted.b:T46f,Read it closely.
