18:T417,The testbed uses binary outer-totalistic cellular automata on an 8×8 torus. Each rule is an 18-entry lookup table indexed by the center-cell state and the number of black neighbors, giving 262,144 possible rules split into disjoint train and held-out halves. A model observes 8 frames and predicts the next 8. The levels separate known-rule execution, new trajectories from training rules, held-out-rule inference, and a harder setting where the directly queried situation is absent and a built-in rule prior must be used. The paper identifies three missing inductive biases. Spatial locality is addressed by two-dimensional positions instead of a flattened one-dimensional sequence. Temporal locality is addressed by mechanisms that bind a situation to its successor, such as two-frame convolution, KV shift, or explicitly attaching the previous-frame neighborhood to each token. Temporal stability is addressed with causal freezing for diffusion, where frames are denoised in temporal order and already cleaned frames are kept fixed as context.19:T4dd,The method, called action forcing, starts from pixel displacement tracks across frames. Since similar egomotion directions induce recurring coherent motion patterns in the image plane, the authors run PCA over these displacement vectors and use the leading components as an egomotion basis. The first two component weights are used as low-dimensional action inputs. This avoids conditioning on dense flow or raw tracks at inference time, and it gives the action space an explicit affine structure: zero means no egomotion, scalar magnitude controls strength, sign controls direction, and axes can be combined. The world model is built by adapting a pretrained video DiT, specifically Wan2.1, under a conditional flow matching objective. The model learns a velocity field between noise latents and clean video latents, and the action is injected into the velocity network itself. This is different from applying a fixed transformation after generation; the action can shape the latent trajectory and the scene evolution. The paper also introduces an online latent egomotion critic that distills pixel-space motion into differentiable latent supervision, grounding action following without backpropagating through the video decoder or the tracker.1a:T62b,The method has three main components. First, a physical mechanism interpreter maps each action-outcome branch to a distribution over latent mechanisms rather than a point estimate. This matters because different interventions constrain the underlying physics to different degrees: a strong push may sharply identify friction, while a weak interaction may leave many mechanisms plausible. Second, the paper introduces a shared-world evidence score. Instead of forcing branch posteriors to be identical, it measures whether all branches retain common support for a shared mechanism relative to an initial-scene prior p(z|I0). The prior correction is important: it prevents uninformative branches from appearing falsely consistent simply because they remain broad and uncertain. Third, the learned compatibility signal is injected into both training and sampling. The interpreter is trained using same-world groups versus mismatched-world groups, then frozen. Its evidence score is used during flow training and also as sampling-time guidance, so the generated rollouts are nudged toward sets of futures that admit one common physical explanation. A key design choice is weak coupling: the model does not force videos or latent posteriors to collapse to the same representation, only to remain jointly explainable by one mechanism. For evaluation, the paper defines the shared-world gap: fit physical parameters separately for each branch, then fit one shared parameter set jointly across branches; the extra error under the shared fit quantifies inconsistency across interventions.1b:T454,Experiments are conducted in three controlled physical environments: Push Cube, Push Rope, and Pour Water, covering rigid-body, deformable, and particle-based dynamics. The main baseline is ACWM-DiT under the ACWM-Phys interaction setup. Relative to ACWM-DiT, OneWorld reduces the mean shared-world gap by 84.3%, which is the central result, and also improves mean M-MSE by about 7.7×10^-4, suggesting that consistency gains do not come at the expense of ordinary rollout quality. The most convincing part of the empirical section is the separation between single-branch plausibility and multi-branch physical compatibility: some baseline predictions fit physics reasonably well branch by branch, but fail badly once a single shared physical configuration is required. The paper also highlights the role of distributional mechanism inference, same-world versus mismatched-world interpreter training, and the use of the compatibility signal at both training and sampling time. The contribution is less about scale and more about closing the loop between formulation, training signal, and evaluation protocol.1c:T597,The framework is built on finite structural causal models. The evaluator has access to the full state ξ=(X,H), while the learner may not observe the latent context H. For a query z=(ξ,a), the evaluator defines a structural index σ_M(ξ,a), which records the active context class, the directly affected variable, the value class, and the first structural response delay. A mechanism memory μ is a finite set of tuples θ=(a,c,y,v,δ), and the answer map T_μ(z) is evaluated against the ground-truth mechanism map. The exact mechanism risk R_Q measures full answer equivalence under a fixed probe distribution Q; coordinate risks decompose errors over context, target, value, and delay, while missed and spurious mechanism risks give operational diagnostics. The main theoretical result expresses optimal probe error as a Bayes decision risk and shows that zero error is possible exactly when each learning-interface fiber lies inside a single probe-answer fiber. The adaptation setup introduces a schema map φ from source to target memory, stable and shifted index sets, and losses L_stab and L_shift.
