9:T736,The method, NAPE, starts from a log-mel spectrogram, splits it into non-overlapping patches, and projects each patch into a latent embedding. This yields a 2D grid of patch embeddings. Since the backbone is a causal Transformer over a 1D sequence, the grid must be serialized with a scanning order. The model then predicts the embedding of the next patch from the prefix, using a lightweight predictor head. Training uses negative cosine similarity to a stop-gradient target embedding, rather than L2 regression or classification over discrete acoustic tokens.

The most interesting technical point is that scanning order is treated as a first-class design choice. In images, rasterization is often a convenience. In audio spectrograms, time and frequency axes play very different roles, so the serialization order changes both the available causal context and the inductive bias. The paper studies four scanning strategies and shows that this choice materially affects transfer performance and training behavior. The trade-off is intuitive: if the order destroys temporal continuity, the model no longer follows natural acoustic evolution; if it only marches along time, it may underuse local time-frequency structure. Making this axis explicit is one of the paper’s main contributions.

Architecturally, the setup is deliberately minimal: a single causal encoder plus a predictor head, with no teacher branch, no EMA target network, no reconstruction decoder, and no extra variance/covariance regularization. This is the key difference from much of prior audio SSL. The model is not trained to reconstruct masked content or to match a teacher representation. It is trained to model audio as a sequential latent process, which makes it much closer in spirit to language modeling and easier to align with unified multimodal pretraining recipes.a:T5a7,Pretraining is done on AudioSet, with downstream evaluation on ESC-50, Speech Commands V1/V2, and IEMOCAP. The paper reports three model sizes: Small (19M), Base (85M), and Large (303M), mainly to test whether the objective scales. According to the full text, NAPE reaches state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art performance on several tasks, and importantly it also performs well under linear probing, not just full fine-tuning. That matters because next-embedding prediction does not explicitly optimize for linear separability.

The ablations are more informative than the headline numbers. The paper studies scanning order, model scale, and objective/architecture choices. The main takeaways are that scanning order matters a lot, temporally aligned serialization is usually stronger and more stable, and performance improves consistently from Small to Large without obvious breakdown of the objective. The fact that this works without a decoder, tokenizer, or teacher is itself an empirical result. The qualitative analyses, including attention maps and embedding-space structure, suggest that the model captures meaningful acoustic boundaries and local spectro-temporal patterns.
