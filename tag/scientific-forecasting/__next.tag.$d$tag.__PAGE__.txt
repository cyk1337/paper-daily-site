9:T49b,The core technical contribution is the CUSP benchmark and its evaluation protocol. The dataset contains 4,760 temporally anchored scientific milestones spanning January 2024 to March 2026 across multiple disciplines. Sources include top-tier journals such as Nature, Science, and Cell, along with community-curated milestone repositories. Each event is tied to a precise time reference so that model access can be restricted to information available before the event. Scientific forecasting is operationalized along four dimensions: feasibility assessment, mechanistic reasoning, generative solution design, and temporal prediction. This is a stronger design than a single yes/no forecast because it separates 'recognizing a plausible direction' from 'recovering the actual path that later succeeded'. The paper also compares pre-cutoff, post-cutoff, and full-information conditions to isolate the gap between knowledge availability and genuine forecasting ability, and analyzes whether highly cited advances are harder to predict. This is not a new model or training method; it is a temporally controlled evaluation framework built around verifiable, resolved scientific events.a:T502,The main empirical pattern is consistent. Evaluating frontier models on 4,760 events, the authors find that models are weak on feasibility assessment and temporal prediction, often near chance or otherwise unstable, and they show a systematic delayed bias when predicting when advances will occur. They are also overconfident and exhibit response bias in feasibility judgments, which makes their uncertainty estimates unreliable. Models do better at identifying plausible technical approaches from candidate sets, but in generative solution design they often fail to produce solutions aligned with the methods that actually drove the later advance. Importantly, these failures are not confined to post-cutoff events. Even on pre-cutoff events, models do not fully exploit available knowledge. Supplying additional pre-cutoff information improves both pre-cutoff and post-cutoff performance, which indicates a knowledge-access or knowledge-use gap. But a substantial forecasting gap remains relative to full-information settings, and that gap grows for highly cited advances. The introduction and abstract do not expose the full numeric tables, but the qualitative result is clear: current frontier models are better at plausibility ranking than at calibrated scientific forecasting.b:T423,For a skim, start with the benchmark construction and task definitions, then move to the comparisons between pre-cutoff, post-cutoff, and full-information settings. The most useful sections are the introduction, the benchmark section, the main results on feasibility and temporal prediction, and the analysis of the knowledge gap versus the forecasting gap.
