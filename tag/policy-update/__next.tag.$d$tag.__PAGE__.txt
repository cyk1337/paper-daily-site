9:Tb13,方法名 SIF，Selective Internal Feedback。它包含两个核心设计：比较式奖励构造，以及选择性策略更新。

先说作者的诊断。论文先系统分析了 Intuitor 这类方法为什么会坏掉。self-certainty 用的是 token 级预测分布相对均匀分布的 KL，直观上就是模型在某个 token 位置有多“尖锐”、多确定。Intuitor 直接对整条响应的平均 self-certainty 做优化。作者发现这会掩盖序列内部结构：一个答案里某些 token 极高置信，足以把均值拉高，但关键推理步骤可能仍然低置信甚至错误。因此“均值更高”不等于“推理更好”。他们进一步看了 response 内部 self-certainty 的方差，发现训练后期方差升高，说明 certainty 分布越来越分散，均值越来越不可靠。再进一步，他们把 token 按 certainty 排序，只取不同 rank band 的均值做训练，发现高 certainty band 带来快但不稳的早期收益，中间 band 更稳，低 certainty band 信号又太弱。这一系列观察构成了 SIF 的出发点。

SIF 的第一部分是 comparative reward construction。它不再把单个 response 的平均 certainty 直接当 reward，而是先把每个 response 的 token-level certainty 排序，形成 ranked certainty profile，再在多个 certainty 窗口上做比较。这里的窗口不是原始序列中的连续 span，而是 certainty 排名上的区间。然后对同一 prompt 下采样得到的多个 responses 做 peer comparison。只有当一个 response 在多个 certainty level 上都得到足够支持，并且在同组 peer 中也占优时，才给正奖励。这样做的含义是：奖励不再来自“我整体看起来更自信”，而来自“我在多个 certainty 层次上都比同伴更一致地好”。这相当于把单点打分改成多视角排序。

第二部分是 selective policy updates。作者认为即使 reward 已经比较式构造，如果把所有样本都拿去更新，噪声仍会积累。因此他们做了两层过滤。第一层是 sample-level eligibility，只让满足支持条件的 response 参与优化；第二层是 group-level filtering，如果同一 prompt 下整组 responses 的 reward 没有区分度，比如都一样，就跳过这一组，不做策略更新。这样做的目的很直接：只在内部反馈有辨识度时学习，避免把模糊、冲突或低信息量的自反馈硬塞进 PPO/GRPO 式更新里。

从方法定位看，SIF 和依赖 final-answer extraction 的 TTRL 不同，它完全不需要抽取最终答案，也不需要 pseudo-label；和 Intuitor 不同，它不把 self-certainty 当绝对 reward，而是当相对比较信号；和一般的 confidence-based selection 不同，它把“是否更新”也纳入设计，而不是只做 reranking。a:T7d3,实验覆盖三个 backbone，在 MATH 上训练，并在 12 个数学与科学推理 benchmark 上评估，同时还做了 test-time adaptation。摘要给出的主结论是：在三个 backbone 上，SIF 的最终 checkpoint 都优于 Intuitor 和 Vigor 的最佳验证 checkpoint；训练诊断显示后期性能退化明显减轻；test-time adaptation 中，相对初始模型的提升能保持到最终 checkpoint，而不是像基线那样后期回落。

从正文片段能确认的关键实验洞察有几项。第一，作者明确展示了 continued self-certainty optimization 的失败案例：在 Qwen3-1.7B 和 Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct 的报告运行中，self-certainty 持续上升，但 MATH validation accuracy 最终降到 0。这是很强的反例，说明 internal reward 和 correctness 可以严重背离。第二，response 内部 certainty variance 在训练中上升，支持“均值不足以代表推理质量”的论点。第三，不同 certainty rank band 的训练行为差异明显：最高 certainty band 早期涨得快但后期掉得厉害，中间 band 更稳。这直接支撑了 SIF 采用 multi-window ranked comparison，而不是盯住单一 band。

The paper first diagnoses why prior self-certainty optimization fails. Self-certainty is defined from token-level predictive distributions using KL divergence from the uniform distribution, so it measures how peaked the model’s token distribution is. Methods such as Intuitor optimize the mean self-certainty over the whole response. The authors show that this average is too coarse: a response can have a few highly certain segments that raise the mean while crucial reasoning steps remain uncertain or wrong. They further examine within-response variance of self-certainty and find that it increases during training, which means the certainty profile becomes more dispersed and the mean becomes less representative. They then sort token-level certainty values and train variants using different ranked bands. High-certainty bands give fast early gains but severe late-stage deterioration; intermediate bands are more stable. This analysis motivates the SIF design.

The first component is comparative reward construction. Instead of using a response’s mean certainty as an absolute reward, SIF sorts token-level certainty scores to form a ranked certainty profile and compares responses across multiple certainty windows. These windows are defined over certainty ranks, not contiguous token spans in the original sequence. It also compares peer responses sampled for the same prompt. A response receives positive reward only if it is supported across multiple ranked certainty levels and also wins sufficiently in peer-level comparison. In effect, the reward becomes a multi-view relative preference signal rather than a single scalar confidence score.

The second component is selective policy updates. Even with a better reward, the authors argue that not every sampled response should contribute to optimization. They therefore apply filtering at two levels. At the sample level, only eligible responses with sufficient support participate in policy optimization. At the group level, if all responses for a prompt receive identical rewards, the whole group is skipped because it carries no useful preference information. This design aims to update the policy only when internal feedback is discriminative enough to be trusted.

Compared with TTRL-style methods, SIF does not require final-answer extraction. Compared with Intuitor, it does not treat self-certainty as an absolute objective. Compared with simple confidence-based reranking, it also controls whether learning should happen at all.c:T77f,The experiments train on MATH using three backbones and evaluate on 12 math and science reasoning benchmarks, with additional test-time adaptation experiments on unlabeled inputs. The headline result is that SIF’s final checkpoints outperform the best-validation checkpoints of Intuitor and Vigor on all three backbones. The paper also reports reduced late-stage deterioration and more persistent gains during test-time adaptation.

Several experimental observations from the provided text are especially important. First, the paper documents a strong failure case of continued self-certainty optimization: in reported runs on Qwen3-1.7B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, self-certainty keeps increasing while MATH validation accuracy falls to zero. That is a direct demonstration that internal reward and correctness can diverge badly. Second, the within-response variance of certainty rises during training, supporting the claim that the mean certainty is an unstable proxy. Third, different certainty bands behave very differently: the highest-certainty band improves quickly at first but degrades later, while intermediate bands are more stable. This directly motivates the multi-window ranked comparison used by SIF.

When reading the full paper, the most important results are not just final benchmark numbers but the training dynamics: batch accuracy, validation accuracy, internal reward, and policy entropy over time. The main claim is about sustained optimization under internal feedback, so those curves matter more than a small gain on a leaderboard. The key ablations should include removing sample/group filtering, collapsing multi-window comparison into a single window, and varying group size or support thresholds.
