a:T65c,The pipeline has three stages. First, video filtering and segmentation. The system identifies instructional videos and splits each long video into visually coherent shots. This is not just a preprocessing convenience. It creates local contexts where a single action or sub-action is more likely to dominate, which makes later grounding more reliable. The pipeline also filters segments with poor visual-text alignment to avoid forcing the model to explain mismatched evidence. Second, candidate step generation. Qwen2.5-VL-72B is used to jointly inspect frames and transcripts and produce candidate procedural steps for each shot. The target is not generic captioning. It is structured procedural abstraction: converting noisy narration plus visual evidence into concise, instruction-like steps tied to temporal spans. Third, refinement and merging. DeepSeek-R1 is then used to clean up semantics, resolve vague references, improve temporal alignment, and merge fragmented steps across neighboring shots when they belong to the same action. This division of labor is the key design choice. The VLM handles local perception and grounding; the reasoning LLM handles long-context cleanup, rewriting, and global consistency. Compared with earlier pipelines that relied on a single model over transcripts or coarse visual cues, this design explicitly separates local multimodal evidence extraction from global temporal-semantic consolidation. The paper also introduces DenseCaption100, a human-annotated benchmark of 100 instructional videos, to validate annotation quality directly rather than relying only on downstream task gains.b:T4a3,On the data side, the pipeline produces DenseStep2M with about 100K videos and 2M fine-grained procedural steps. On the validation side, the paper uses DenseCaption100 to compare automated annotations against human-written dense annotations. The abstract does not expose the full metric table, but the paper’s central claim is that the generated annotations align well with human standards in both semantics and temporal grounding. On the model side, fine-tuning VLMs on DenseStep2M improves both generative tasks, such as dense captioning and procedural grounding, and discriminative tasks, such as cross-modal retrieval. That pattern matters. If the pipeline only improved linguistic fluency, one would expect gains mostly on captioning. Gains on grounding and retrieval suggest that temporal alignment quality also improved. The most informative ablations are likely the ones removing shot segmentation, alignment filtering, or the DeepSeek-R1 refinement stage.
