18:T899,论文的技术路线分三步。

第一步是现象隔离。作者比较 released VLM 与对应 reference LLM 的文本能力差距，并引入 text-only further-training 对照，以及共享 base LLM 的 VL-versus-text matched trajectory，说明损失不是一般继续训练都会发生，而是和视觉模态适配强相关。损失最明显的不是开放式知识问答，而是那些对表面 token 格式、答案边界、最终输出规则很敏感的任务。

第二步是机制诊断。作者把问题归因到 attention-sink corruption。现代 LLM 往往会把大量注意力质量集中到少数早期位置，这些位置的 hidden state 又被少数高幅值固定特征维度主导，形成 sink。论文的假设是，这种 sink 有助于稳定每个位置对关键表面 token 的注意力分配，从而支撑格式敏感任务。VL fine-tuning 会扰动 query/key 投影 Wq、Wk；由于 logit shift 会随输入幅值放大，这种扰动在 sink 位置被特别放大，导致 per-head sink collapse。作者进一步分析不同 QK normalization 设计的差异：如果是 per-head QK-RMSNorm，可以直接去掉对原始输入幅值的显式依赖；如果只是 layerwise normalization，则每个 head 的尺度仍受整层能量分配影响，弱 head 会被进一步压缩，因此到 VL 训练开始前，某些 backbone 的 per-head sink 已经偏弱。

第三步是预测指标与干预验证。作者提出 Sink Strength，一个只在 base LLM 上通过少量 forward pass 计算的标量，用来量化 head-level sink concentration。这个指标不需要做任何 VL 训练，就能预测后续多模态适配后的文本能力损失。然后他们把 base-side sink weakness 与 post-VL gap perturbation 结合起来，区分“骨干先天脆弱”和“VL 更新额外破坏”两部分。最后做两个负对照：一是在预训练后给模型补装 QK-RMSNorm 再去做 VL 训练，发现不能复现原生架构自带的保护效果；二是对已经训练好的 VLM 做多种 weight merging，也无法把丢失的文本能力救回来。这说明问题更像训练过程中的 head-level 结构破坏，而不是事后线性修补能解决的。19:T843,The paper proceeds in three stages.

First, it isolates the phenomenon. The authors compare released VLMs against their reference LLM backbones on a text-only evaluation suite, and add text-only further-training controls plus a matched VL-versus-text training trajectory from the same base model. This is meant to show that the degradation is not just a generic consequence of continued training, but is tied to multimodal adaptation.

Second, it proposes a mechanism: attention-sink corruption. Many modern LLMs place a large fraction of attention mass on a few early positions. Those positions are dominated by a small set of high-magnitude feature dimensions and act as sinks. The paper argues that these sinks help stabilize attention allocation in ways that matter for tasks graded on exact output form. During VL fine-tuning, perturbations to the query and key projections, Wq and Wk, induce logit shifts that scale with input magnitude. Because sink positions have unusually large magnitude, the perturbation is amplified there, causing per-head sink collapse. The architecture analysis then distinguishes per-head QK-RMSNorm from layerwise normalization: per-head normalization removes explicit raw-magnitude dependence, while layerwise normalization can still compress weak heads because each head’s scale is tied to the layer’s total energy.

Third, the paper introduces a predictive metric and tests interventions. Sink Strength is a single scalar computed on the base LLM, before VL training, to quantify head-level sink concentration. It predicts how much text capability will be lost after VL adaptation. The authors then separate base-side vulnerability from update-induced corruption by combining pre-VL sink weakness with post-VL sink-gap perturbation. Finally, they run two negative controls: injecting QK-RMSNorm after pretraining and then doing VL training, and applying several post-VL weight-merging methods. Neither recovers the protection seen in native architectures, which suggests the issue is tied to training-time head-level dynamics rather than something easily repaired afterward.1a:T64f,The study covers six released VLM–LLM pairs from families including Qwen, InternVL, and LLaVA-OneVision, with headline examples such as Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, and LLaVA-OV, plus additional controls. The language backbone is extracted from each VLM and evaluated as a standalone causal LM on a nine-task text suite against a reference LLM.

Several results stand out. First, the text-capability gap reaches double-digit points on multiple format-sensitive tasks, while matched text-only further training causes much smaller degradation. That supports a modality-specific effect. Second, Sink Strength is a strong predictor of post-VL damage: the headline rank correlation is Spearman rho = 0.97, and correlations remain high across six pairs and four format-sensitive tasks, with rho >= 0.88. Third, the preserved-sink versus collapsed-sink split is sharp on instruction following: every preserved-sink backbone stays within 5.4 points of its reference LLM, while every collapsed-sink backbone loses at least 7.9 points. Fourth, native per-head QK-RMSNorm is associated with better sink preservation, but post-pretraining injection of the same module does not reproduce the benefit. Fifth, post-VL weight merging, including linear, spectral, and sparsified variants, fails to recover the lost text capability under the tested settings.

The strongest part of the empirical design is the control structure.
