9:T59f,MATCH augments a sparse-attention backbone with an external in-context retriever. The retriever scans or encodes the full prompt efficiently, identifies token positions that are likely useful for the current prediction, and passes those positions back to the Transformer. The attention layer then combines its usual sparse local context with a small set of retrieved distant tokens.

The design choice is motivated by a simple observation: fixed sparse patterns fail not because they see too few tokens in total, but because they cannot adaptively jump to the right distant evidence. MATCH therefore keeps the efficiency of sparse attention while adding a dynamic access path to arbitrary positions in the sequence.

From the full text, the method has two main components. First, a retrieval-enhancing encoder / retriever builds searchable representations over the whole context. Second, a retrieval-enhanced attention mechanism injects the retrieved positions into the sparse attention computation, effectively adding remote key/value access on top of the local sparse pattern.

Compared with standard sliding-window attention, MATCH is input-dependent rather than pattern-fixed. Compared with post-hoc KV eviction methods, it does not only prune memory; it actively restores missing recall through retrieval.
