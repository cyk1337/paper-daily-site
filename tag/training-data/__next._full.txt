18:T71d,The method has three layers: formalizing entity-level membership inference, designing prompt-based interrogation strategies, and turning model responses into a binary membership decision through semantic features.

The task definition is the first important step. Given a target entity and a small set of related clues, the attacker must decide whether information about that entity was used in training. The setting is label-only black-box: no logits, no gradients, no training data access, only textual outputs from queries. That makes the setup stricter and more realistic for auditing closed models.

The central intuition is related information leakage. If a model has been exposed to an entity during pretraining, then partial clues about that entity should make it more likely to reveal additional consistent information. Rather than using open-ended multi-turn dialogue, the authors distill six classic interviewing paradigms and instantiate five concrete interrogation strategies under a unified prompt template. The choice to avoid dynamic dialogue is deliberate: multi-turn interactions are harder to reproduce, harder to featurize, and more vulnerable to compounding errors.

At inference time, responses generated under each strategy are paired using two pairing schemes, converted into semantic similarity vectors, and fed into a binary classifier. So the method does not rely on exact fact matching. Instead, it uses the pattern of semantic consistency between clues and elicited responses as evidence of training exposure. This is more robust than keyword heuristics and more standardized than free-form judging. The paper also includes a theoretical analysis of feasibility under clue, input, and model constraints, aiming to characterize when entity-level inference is possible and where its limits lie.19:T4f4,The authors build dedicated datasets for entity-level membership inference and evaluate separately on person and organization entities. The main result is that all five entity-level strategies outperform strong sample-level label-only baselines. For person entities, the gains over the strongest baseline are 6.0%–17.5% in Balanced Accuracy and 0.072–0.17 in AUC. For organization entities, the proposed methods still maintain an advantage, but the task becomes harder for everyone.

That degradation on organizations is plausible and informative: organization names are often more ambiguous, their contexts are more diverse, and public evidence is noisier, which makes entity boundaries less clean. The paper also tests transfer across target LLMs with different scales and architectures. Reported differences across target models stay within 5.0% in Balanced Accuracy and 0.036 in AUC, suggesting the framework is not tightly coupled to one model family, although TPR@1%FPR remains model-dependent.
