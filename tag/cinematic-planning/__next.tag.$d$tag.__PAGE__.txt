9:T8bf,The method is built around a reverse-SFT pipeline followed by semantic-consistency RL. The system is formalized as a prompt enhancer πθ feeding a video generator Gϕ. The goal is to generate a textual condition y that is easier for Gϕ to realize faithfully. Instead of starting from a user request x and expanding it forward into a richer prompt, WanPE starts from a high-quality real video v, derives a hierarchical cinematic condition y from that video, and then reconstructs a compatible but information-sparser user request x. The pair (x, y) is used for supervised training. The motivation is straightforward: y is grounded in real video and therefore better aligned with the conditioning distribution seen by the generator, while x approximates realistic user input at inference time.

The hierarchical cinematic condition appears to be organized at the shot level rather than as a single long paragraph. It captures scene setup, entities, actions, camera language, lighting, audio or dialogue, and narrative progression across shots. This matters because cinematic planning is a coupled control problem: action, camera, pacing, editing, and atmosphere evolve together and cannot be handled well by local prompt embellishment.

After SFT, the model is optimized with SC-GRPO. The key contribution here is the reward design rather than GRPO itself. The paper defines a nine-dimensional semantic-consistency reward to check whether user requirements are preserved across the entire plan. The reward targets omissions, semantic drift, incorrect bindings between entities and attributes or actions, and temporal inconsistencies across shots. Compared with rewards based only on fluency or overall text quality, this is much closer to structured fidelity. In effect, the RL stage acts like a global verifier for long-form planning: the model is encouraged not only to sound cinematic, but to remain faithful to the original request throughout the generated plan.

Another notable point is transferability. The authors claim WanPE can transfer across video generators after format adaptation. That suggests the model is learning a more abstract planning representation rather than memorizing a single generator-specific prompt template.a:T806,Three parts of the experimental section matter most. First is scale and headline gain. The paper trains WanPE variants from 4B to 397B on 1.05M real-world videos. According to the reported main results, WanPE-397B improves over raw prompts by up to 18.84 points for 5–15 second generation and 50.86 points for 30-second generation. The larger gain at longer duration is important: it suggests the value of prompt planning grows with temporal horizon because longer videos depend more heavily on front-end structure.

Second is the ablation evidence. Reverse construction outperforms forward rewriting, which supports the paper’s central claim that distribution alignment to real video-grounded conditions matters more than simply making prompts more descriptive. SC-GRPO improves semantic consistency by 18.6 to 23.3 points across scales. That is a substantial effect size and indicates that the main bottleneck in long-form prompt planning is requirement preservation, not surface-level wording. If the full paper breaks down reward dimensions such as omission, binding, and temporal consistency, those tables are worth reading carefully because they reveal what kinds of failures RL is actually fixing.

Third is evaluation design. The authors build WanPEval, a human-annotated benchmark covering 5 to 30 seconds and varying intent granularity, and evaluate both at the text level and through roughly 11K blind pairwise video comparisons. That is stronger than text-only evaluation because the real utility of a prompt enhancer must show up in generated video. The paper also reports that WanPE leads evaluated commercial offerings at 5–15 seconds and remains competitive with Seedance 2.5 at 30 seconds. This suggests the method is already effective in medium-duration settings, while very long generation is still partly capped by the downstream generator.
