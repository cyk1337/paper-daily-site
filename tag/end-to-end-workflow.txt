The full collection contains 300 tasks; 97 are released and evaluated in this work, while 203 remain held out internally. The released tasks span six domains: quantum chemistry, molecular dynamics, materials characterization, analytical chemistry, life science, and electrochemistry/environment.

Each task is packaged as a self-contained unit with aligned components including a task description, fixed inputs, an execution environment, a deliverable contract, and a task-specific grader. The curation follows four principles: representativeness, complexity, diversity, and verifiability. In practice, that means tasks must resemble real professional workflows, require dependency-aware multi-step execution, avoid trivial template variation, and support automated, repeatable scoring through files, numerical checks, plots, or explicit acceptance criteria.

The evaluation protocol is built around two metrics. Pass Rate is the primary metric and requires satisfying the full task contract. Avg. This distinction is central to the paper: many agents can produce some correct intermediate outputs, but still fail the workflow because the final bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or not reproducible. The main empirical result is a large gap between partial progress and full completion. That pattern suggests the bottleneck is often not local scientific knowledge alone, but end-to-end execution reliability: file organization, consistency across outputs, formatting constraints, edge-case handling, and coherence among code, figures, tables, and prose.
