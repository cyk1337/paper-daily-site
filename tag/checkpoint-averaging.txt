18:T450,Terminal Shrinkage Averaging returns an interpolation between the last checkpoint and the mean of the last K checkpoints: (1-α)θ_T + α\bar{θ}_T. α=0 recovers the raw final iterate, and α=1 recovers uniform LAWA. Since the final checkpoint is included in the average, its total weight is 1-α+α/K, while each earlier checkpoint receives α/K. The analysis uses a local quadratic approximation to the loss over the short terminal region. Checkpoints are decomposed into their across-run mean and stochastic residuals, and risk is measured in the Hessian-induced seminorm. Under this model, the expected surrogate risk is quadratic in α. The expression exposes the tradeoff: averaging older checkpoints can move the mean away from the local optimum, but it can also reduce stochastic terminal variation. This gives conditions where the best α lies strictly between raw final iterate and full LAWA. The same framework explains why an averaged estimator can prefer a higher terminal learning-rate floor: the trajectory keeps making progress late in training, while the estimator damps endpoint noise.19:T432,The experiments are designed around the interaction rather than only the main effects. Depth-12 NanoChat runs first show that the best shrinkage coefficient is inside the interval, not at α=0 or α=1. A paired factorial experiment then crosses terminal learning-rate floors of 5%, 10%, and 15% of peak LR with two output estimators, raw final iterate and TSA, using five repetitions. Increasing the floor worsens validation BPB when deploying the raw endpoint, but improves validation BPB when deploying TSA; the effect grows as the floor is raised. A replication with pure AdamW preserves the direction of the interaction, so the result is not tied to the Muon+AdamW optimizer mix used in NanoChat. The higher-floor-plus-TSA recipe is also applied to a depth-22 NanoChat time-to-GPT-2 setting, where it improves over the public recipe at matched budget and yields a qualifying benchmark run.
