c:T783,The contribution has three layers. First, the collection itself: Reasoning Core contains 50 generators spanning math, logic, planning, state tracking, formal languages, structured data, games, causality, and code. The authors aim for semantic breadth rather than surface diversity. Many tasks vary expression trees, logical forms, program structure, or formal systems through recursive or grammar-based generation. Planning and game tasks also randomize the underlying domain, action schemas, and transition rules instead of repeatedly instantiating a fixed environment. Second, the SFT-oriented representation design. Problem metadata is separated from prompt rendering, and the training prompts use zero-shot instructions rather than few-shot demonstrations to preserve context budget for more independent samples. More importantly, targets are canonicalized whenever possible. For sets, plans, proofs, and programs, the training target is forced into a deterministic serialization, such as lexicographic ordering, so autoregressive supervision does not waste capacity on arbitrary but equivalent prefixes. The answers are also kept compact: numbers, labels, short expressions, proof-line indices, plans, or short programs are preferred over long explanations. Third, semantic scoring and auditing. Training uses one canonical target, but evaluation accepts any semantically valid answer: sets are order-invariant, lambda terms are compared up to alpha-renaming, plans are executed, proofs are compiled, and programs are tested. The paper also includes a repository-scale semantic audit using model-assisted review, API sampling, manual adjudication, and regression tests to expose subtle generator, target, and scorer failures in external collections. A useful conceptual distinction throughout the paper is between semantic validity and training utility: a problem can be well-defined yet still be poor supervised data.d:T4ef,The experimental emphasis is controlled comparison rather than isolated benchmark wins. Reasoning Core is compared against Procedural Warmup, Reasoning Gym, and SynLogic under a matched completion-supervised protocol, across four base-model settings and multiple training durations. The headline result in the abstract is that, in the main 3B setting, Reasoning Core achieves the best mean performance on DROP, LogiQA, and ARC-Challenge, outperforming both the no-procedural-data baseline and all three alternative procedural collections. The paper argues that the gain comes from data design rather than simply having more tasks: many external collections were built for RL, with more complex answer formats, weaker canonicalization, and lower token efficiency for SFT. Additional diagnostics include free-generation behavior, retention, and repository audits, which help separate immediate gains from forgetting and from latent semantic defects in the data source.
