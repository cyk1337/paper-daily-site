18:T509,The method starts from the sampled-token log-ratio r_t = log pi_student(y_t | c_t) - log pi_teacher(y_t | c_t). In token-level OPD, positive r_t means the student assigns higher probability than the teacher to the sampled token, and the local advantage A_t = -stop_gradient(r_t) pushes that token down. The paper notes that positive log-ratios dominate the training signal: in the analyzed vanilla OPD rollouts, tokens with r_t > 0 account for 83.5% of the total absolute advantage mass. To reduce token-level noise, responses are divided into contiguous blocks. Each block is summarized by the mean positive log-ratio D_i. A penalty jump is an abrupt increase in D_i between adjacent blocks. The diagnostic analysis finds that such jumps persist on held-out tokens and coincide with lower teacher confidence, measured by higher entropy and lower top-1 probability. AP-OPD responds by scaling down all signed supervision coefficients in the block after a detected jump. The preceding block serves as a local reference, and the scale is chosen so that the weighted average penalty of the post-jump block matches the previous block’s original mean. Selecting only one or two tokens with extreme teacher-student probability gaps often matches or outperforms dense OPD. The paper reports a non-monotonic sparsity curve: performance first degrades as supervision is reduced, but can recover under extreme sparsity and exceed dense supervision. Ablations show that the sign of the token signal matters. More expressive students benefit more from positive-reward tokens, while less expressive students in large-teacher/small-student settings benefit more from negative-reward tokens. Another important result is the mismatch between reasoning gains and reverse KL reduction: strong sparse OPD variants may increase the teacher-student reverse KL.
