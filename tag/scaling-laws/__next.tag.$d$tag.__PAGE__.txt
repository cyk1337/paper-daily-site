At budget k, the selected answer is the one with the highest verifier score among k samples, and the curve reports the average correctness over the fixed question list. The audit may adaptively choose questions, extend answer paths, query correctness labels, and stop when it can return a simultaneous confidence band with half-width ε and failure probability δ. The analysis counts three resources: generated answers, correctness queries, and question visits. For a fixed list, the main cost law for certifying all budgets up to K is, up to logarithmic factors, K/ε + min(M, ε^{-2}) + s/ε^2. The three terms correspond to rare high-scoring answers at large budgets, the identity of the benchmark questions, and within-question variance accumulated along the curve. Matching lower bounds show that these terms are intrinsic. A key technical device is a coupling of nested winners on one answer path, which lets a single path serve all budgets. The practical algorithm is a paired audit: each round draws two fresh paths per question, uses an exponential inequality for paired Bernoulli draws to build anytime-valid simultaneous bands, and then applies a corrected exact-binomial final look to stop at a fixed round. Combined with a multilevel audit, it attains near-minimax cost.a:T45b,The empirical study uses 185 held-out score pools.
