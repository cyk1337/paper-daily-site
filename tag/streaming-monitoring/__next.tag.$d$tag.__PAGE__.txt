b:T625,The paper frames hallucination onset as a quickest change detection (QCD) problem. A latent state z_t (faithful vs hallucinated) evolves as a first-order Markov chain; observed features x_t follow different pre-/post-change distributions. Main components:
1) Model validation: fit higher-order Markov models on labeled spans (RAGTruth) and show higher orders add negligible log-likelihood (<0.35%), so first-order captures ~99.7% of temporal structure, justifying Lorden’s minimax setting.
2) Lower bound: compute Lorden’s minimax delay lower bound at a target false-alarm rate (reported ~1.3 tokens at 1% false-alarm rate), giving a task-specific floor.
3) Practical detectors as stopping rules: compare (a) per-token linear scoring + thresholding, (b) parametric CUSUM with diagonal-Gaussian likelihoods over a 33-dim feature stream, and (c) a causal recurrent labeler. The key interpretation is that the recurrent labeler behaves like a neural/learned CUSUM: the hidden state accumulates evidence and the output log-odds act as a cumulative log-likelihood ratio with a learned increment, consistent with neural CUSUM theory (cross-entropy minimization recovers the LLR).
4) Gap analysis via information rate: detection delay is governed by the divergence rate realized by the score.
