RULER cells use the same 650 instances and cover retrieval, aggregation, and multi-hop axes. The central result is that query visibility changes rankings. Under query-agnostic compression on RULER, among the five backend-comparable methods, only the query-independent KeyDiff consistently beats the best-of-3 trivial baseline, winning 31 of 36 cells with an average +0.171 margin. SnapKV falls below that baseline on average in the agnostic protocol, winning 13 of 36 cells with a -0.066 margin, while its query-aware gain of +0.132 is reproduced. LongBench gives a less exclusive picture: the query-agnostic issue remains valid, but other methods can catch up or overtake. The audit also finds that eager versus SDPA attention backend changes pooled RULER accuracy by -0.221, larger than many method gaps, and that RULER-8192 can overflow gemma-2’s positional budget by up to about 30%, silently zeroing 7 of 13 subtasks even without compression.1a:T5fb,The method has three main components. First, Cascade Cache organizes the KV lifecycle into three tiers instead of treating the cache as a monolithic buffer. Tier 1 is a dense cache for recent tokens, preserving local dependencies and attention sinks with contiguous high-fidelity storage. Tier 2 is a sparse cache where older tokens are retained selectively using head-adaptive budgeting. Tier 3 is a condensed cache where even older blocks are further compressed through eviction or weighted merging to satisfy hard memory limits. The key idea is to turn 'recent dense, older sparse, oldest condensed' into a unified pipeline. Second, Logits Calibration maps heterogeneous importance scores into a shared probability space so that Top-p style budgeting can be applied consistently across heads and across different selection criteria. This is meant to make the retained-token pattern better match the raw attention distribution, rather than relying on fixed Top-k thresholds that are hard to compare across heads. Third, System-aware Index Regularization rewrites discontinuous logical selections into a more regular physical layout using memory rewriting and sparse loading. Together with a head-wise block table and metadata management, this makes dynamic head-adaptive selection compatible with CUDA Graph execution and block-based memory systems. The main distinction from prior compression work is that the paper does not stop at a better selection rule; it translates dynamic selection into a system-friendly access pattern.1b:T41f,The evaluation focuses on long-generation math reasoning, where decoding-time errors accumulate and KV compression quality matters most. This quantifies how much xi affects future generation, not just recent attention.

2) Entropy as informativeness: use the entropy of the next-token predictive distribution as a proxy for uncertainty/information content. High-entropy contexts are argued to carry richer semantic information and to have more persistent influence on distant future steps.

First, the paper reformulates attention approximation in terms of softmax KDE. This is natural because attention weights are normalized exponential kernels between queries and keys. Once written this way, the problem can be attacked using tools from coresets, polynomial approximation, high-dimensional geometry, and communication complexity.

Second, the analysis is split by regime. The table of contents makes clear that the authors treat a high-temperature regime and a low-temperature regime separately. Intuitively, when temperature is high, softmax is smoother and more amenable to low-degree approximation; when temperature is low, the kernel becomes sharper, local geometry matters more, and compression becomes harder. The paper therefore avoids a single coarse statement and instead derives regime-specific upper and lower bounds.

Third, the upper and lower bounds use different toolchains. On the upper-bound side, the paper employs multivariate polynomial methods, Hermite polynomials, KDE coresets, and a merge-and-reduce style conversion from offline compression to streaming compression. In the low-temperature regime, the contents indicate a more delicate pipeline: partition the dataset into pseudo-random and low-radius components, recenter and rescale them, build coresets for each part, then turn the offline compressor into a streaming algorithm. This suggests that the main technical novelty lies in decomposing the geometry of the instance before compressing it.

On the lower-bound side, the paper uses information-theoretic and communication-complexity arguments to show that some memory requirements are inherent rather than artifacts of loose analysis. The final section transfers the softmax-KDE results back to attention, yielding formal bounds for streaming attention itself.

If one reads the theorems as the equivalent of empirical results, the key quantities to inspect are the memory complexity as a function of approximation error epsilon, ambient dimension, and the temperature or radius parameter r, together with whether upper and lower bounds match up to logarithmic or lower-order factors.
