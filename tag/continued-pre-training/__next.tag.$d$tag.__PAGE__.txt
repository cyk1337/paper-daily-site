9:T5da,VLAct is best understood as a recipe, not a new backbone family. The first component is preserving the VLM prior. The model uses shallow-layer protection to reduce destructive drift in early representations, and caption-data mixing to keep generic semantic anchors alive during robot continued pre-training. This is a deliberate choice: robot trajectories provide action supervision but poor coverage of open-world semantics, so relying on them alone would adapt faster but forget more. The second component is multi-head continuous action co-supervision. During continued pre-training, the same backbone is trained against multiple continuous action heads rather than a single head. The point is not to deploy all heads later, but to prevent the backbone from collapsing onto one head’s output geometry and to encourage head-agnostic action representations. The third component is a partially unified cross-embodiment action layout. Instead of fully separate robot-specific action spaces or an overly rigid global unification, the method aligns semantically comparable action dimensions where possible and uses a wrap-aware loss for periodic joints so angular variables are trained with the correct topology. This middle ground matters because embodiments are related but not identical. A notable design choice throughout the paper is that these mechanisms are used to shape the backbone during continued pre-training; downstream users can still attach whatever action head fits their own setup.a:T4db,The evaluation spans simulation benchmarks, real-world robot settings, and unseen-embodiment transfer. Main numbers reported in the paper include 82.6% on LIBERO-Plus, 54.8% on VLA-Arena, and 92.5% on RoboTwin 2.0. On RoboDojo, VLAct reaches a score of 10.66 with a 7.60% success rate, ranking sixth by success and outperforming all explicitly designated WAM models according to the paper. A particularly strong data-efficiency result is that using only 20% of RoboCasa-GR1 data still surpasses the full-data GR00T-N1.6 baseline. The training setup is also intentionally modest: open-source data only and a 16-GPU run. The most important experimental control is that, across comparisons, only the VLM backbone weights differ; the action head, its fresh initialization, downstream data, optimizer, and fine-tuning budget are held fixed. That makes the attribution to continued-pretrained representations much cleaner. With the 2×2 pixel unshuffle in the Qwen2.5-VL backbone, these documents produce multimodal sequences from roughly 32K to 128K tokens. Potential evaluation contamination is filtered using SHA-256 hashes of PDF content. Five long-document training tasks are synthesized and grouped into long-document VQA and OCR transcription.
