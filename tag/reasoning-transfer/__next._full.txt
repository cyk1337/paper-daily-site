19:T673,The experimental design is simple and well controlled. The authors select seven open-source reasoning-tuned models and assign fixed provider and receiver roles, with the main focus on stronger-to-weaker transfer. A provider first generates a full reasoning trace for a problem. Instead of evaluating only the full trace, the trace is then exposed to the receiver through cumulative prefixes of increasing length. For each prefix, the receiver is tested in two modes. In force-answer mode, it must answer directly from the available prefix, which isolates what can be extracted from the provided text alone. In free-generation mode, it may continue reasoning before answering, which tests whether the prefix can scaffold the receiver’s own reasoning process. The protocol is run on three benchmarks chosen for complementary transfer dynamics: AIME for derivation-heavy math with single integer answers, MMLU-Pro for knowledge-intensive multiple-choice reasoning, and ZebraLogic for constraint reasoning with structured multi-field outputs. This benchmark choice is central to the paper’s logic: AIME makes answer appearance easy to track, MMLU-Pro highlights receiver-intrinsic competence, and ZebraLogic reveals whether partial structured information accumulates before the full answer is available. The paper also studies an inference-time policy based on receiver agreement: if multiple receivers converge to the same answer from a partial prefix, the system can stop early instead of consuming the full provider trace. The authors further test whether agreement that persists across consecutive prefixes is more reliable than one-off agreement.1a:T628,The main findings come in three layers. First, full traces often do transfer successfully across models, but that fact alone is not diagnostic of mechanism. Second, prefix trajectories reveal benchmark-specific patterns. On AIME, force-answer and free-generation diverge sharply: in force-answer mode, receivers usually become correct only after the final answer is already present in the prefix, while in free-generation mode they often succeed earlier by continuing from partial reasoning. This is the paper’s clearest result, because it shows that successful full-trace transfer does not necessarily mean the receiver has benefited from the provider’s reasoning process; sometimes it simply reads off the answer. On MMLU-Pro, transfer depends more strongly on the receiver’s own competence and prior knowledge, so shorter prefixes can already be sufficient. On ZebraLogic, the useful signal often comes from progressively accumulated partial structured answers rather than explicit final-answer leakage alone. Third, the agreement-based stopping experiments show that early stopping can retain much of the transfer benefit, and that agreement is substantially more reliable when the same answer remains stable across consecutive prefix lengths.
