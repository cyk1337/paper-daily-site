18:Ta8f,方法上，论文研究的是带动量的 normalised steepest descent。更新形式不是沿欧式梯度直接走，而是在给定范数下做单位步方向的最速下降；Muon 对应的几何是谱范数，因而其对偶平稳性指标是核范数。技术难点在于：在一般 Banach 空间里，重尾随机噪声的 martingale 控制不像 Hilbert 空间那样可以直接用内积工具，现有工作往往借助范数等价常数 ρ 把问题转回欧式空间，导致维度依赖。论文的关键推进有三点。

其一，直接在原生对偶范数下假设 heavy-tailed noise，即随机梯度误差满足 p 阶中心矩有界，而不是有界方差。这样做更贴近经验现象，也为 geometry-aware bound 留出空间。

其二，针对对偶范数 p-uniformly smooth 的情形，作者建立了带动量的非欧几里得归一化最速下降的最优样本复杂度：达到 ε-||·||_* 平稳点所需复杂度为 O(Δ_1 L / ε^2 · (σ/ε)^{p/(p-1)})。这里 Δ_1 是初始函数差距量级，L 是光滑常数，σ 描述重尾噪声强度。这个结果的重要之处不只是 rate 本身，而是它在更强的对偶范数平稳性下成立，并且不再通过 ρ 把噪声项粗暴地欧式化。论文还把分析扩展到较弱的 (L0, L1)-smoothness 假设，而不是只处理标准全局 L-smooth。

其三，Muon 的对偶核范数并不满足 uniformly smooth，因此不能直接套一般定理。作者为 Schatten-1 几何单独做了更细的分析，得到 Muon 在 m×n 矩阵上的样本复杂度大致为 O(min{m,n} · Δ_1 L / ε^2 · (σ/ε)^{p/(p-1)})，并指出这已经优于欧式算法在“校正到同样核范数平稳性指标”后的复杂度。直观上，欧式方法若想保证核范数意义下的 ε-stationarity，需要通过 Frobenius-to-nuclear 的范数转换支付更差的、且带 p 依赖的维度因子；Muon 则在原生几何里吸收了大部分重尾噪声代价。论文还给出针对 Schatten-1 平稳性的 first-order lower bound，量级与上界匹配到主要因子，说明 Muon 在这一问题类上是一阶最优，且不存在完全无维度代价的保证。

从方法区别看，和传统 SGD/Adam 理论相比，这篇工作不是再做一次 adaptive method 收敛证明，而是把“优化几何”“平稳性度量”“噪声尾部形状”三者绑在一起分析。和已有非欧式优化理论相比，它不再依赖 bounded variance + norm equivalence 的标准套路，而是引入函数分析工具直接控制一般范数下的噪声项。这部分工具本身也可能被后续用于分析其他矩阵几何优化器，如 Scion 或更一般的 Schatten-p 结构更新。19:T45a,For pretraining research, the contribution matters for three reasons. First, it gives a theoretical explanation for a class of optimizers that has already appeared in serious Transformer training setups. This is not just a generic convergence proof; it explains when and why matrix-valued non-Euclidean updates help under heavy-tailed gradient noise. Second, it argues that the noise model should be expressed in the optimizer’s native geometry instead of being projected back to Euclidean space. That is a useful lens for large-scale training, where parameters are naturally matrices and gradient noise is often empirically heavy-tailed. Third, the paper provides near-optimal sample complexity for Muon under nuclear-norm stationarity, together with a matching lower bound showing that a fully dimension-free guarantee is impossible in that metric. That changes how one should read the remaining dimension factor: it is not merely a proof artifact. In scale, this is more than a minor recipe refinement; it is a principled justification for why a new optimizer family can be well matched to modern pretraining.1a:Tb63,The algorithmic object is momentum-based normalized steepest descent in a general normed space. Instead of taking a Euclidean gradient step, the method moves along the steepest descent direction normalized in the chosen geometry. Muon corresponds to using the spectral norm on matrices, whose dual stationarity measure is the Schatten-1, or nuclear, norm.

The technical difficulty is the stochastic term. In Hilbert spaces, martingale noise can be controlled with inner-product tools. In general Banach spaces, that route is unavailable, so prior work often falls back to norm equivalence, introducing a factor rho that can scale badly with dimension. This paper avoids that standard reduction.

The first key move is to formulate the heavy-tailed assumption directly in the native dual norm: stochastic gradient errors have bounded p-th central moments, rather than bounded variance. This is both more realistic and more geometry-aware.

The second contribution is a general convergence theorem for momentum normalized steepest descent when the dual norm is p-uniformly smooth. Under this condition, the method reaches an epsilon-stationary point in the dual norm with sample complexity O(Delta_1 L / epsilon^2 * (sigma/epsilon)^{p/(p-1)}). The result is notable because it holds for a stronger stationarity notion than the usual Euclidean one, and because the noise is handled directly in the native geometry. The analysis also extends to weak (L0, L1)-smoothness, not only standard global L-smoothness.

The third contribution is a Muon-specific analysis. The nuclear norm is not uniformly smooth, so the general theorem does not apply directly. The authors therefore derive a dedicated bound for Schatten-1 stationarity on m by n matrices, obtaining a complexity of the form O(min{m,n} * Delta_1 L / epsilon^2 * (sigma/epsilon)^{p/(p-1)}). They then compare this with Euclidean methods after correcting for the stronger stationarity metric. The message is that Euclidean algorithms can only match the same nuclear-norm stationarity by paying a worse, p-dependent dimension penalty, whereas Muon absorbs heavy-tailed noise much more naturally in its own geometry. The paper also proves a first-order lower bound for Schatten-1 stationarity, showing that Muon is first-order optimal up to the main factors and that a dimension-free guarantee cannot exist in this setting.

Relative to standard SGD or Adam theory, the novelty is not another adaptive-optimizer convergence proof. The paper jointly analyzes optimization geometry, stationarity metric, and tail behavior of stochastic gradients. Relative to earlier non-Euclidean analyses, it replaces the bounded-variance plus norm-equivalence template with functional-analytic tools that control noise directly under general norms. Those tools may be reusable beyond Muon, for other matrix-geometry optimizers such as Scion or related Schatten-structured methods.1b:T5d8,This is primarily a theory paper, so the main results are theorems, upper bounds, and lower bounds rather than large-scale pretraining runs. The most important quantitative takeaways are the complexity expressions.

First, under p-uniformly smooth dual geometry, the sample complexity for reaching epsilon-stationarity in the dual norm is O(Delta_1 L / epsilon^2 * (sigma/epsilon)^{p/(p-1)}). When p=2, this recovers the more familiar bounded-variance style behavior. When p<2, the heavy-tailed regime appears through the factor (sigma/epsilon)^{p/(p-1)}.

Second, for Muon on m by n matrices, the paper derives a nuclear-norm stationarity bound whose leading dimension term is min{m,n}. The important comparison is not against Euclidean Frobenius stationarity, but against Euclidean methods forced to guarantee the same Schatten-1 stationarity.
