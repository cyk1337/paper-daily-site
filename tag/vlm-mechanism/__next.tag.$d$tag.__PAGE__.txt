5:T535,The analysis uses two attention-based probes. The answer-side probe measures how strongly answer tokens attend back to the question. The visual-side probe measures how strongly visual tokens attend to one another. Across depth, these probes reveal a recurring three-stage pattern. Early layers are dominated by question-conditioned organization, where the model frames the image under the query. Middle layers shift toward visual consolidation, where visual tokens interact more strongly and answer-relevant evidence is assembled. Late layers return to language-side answer formation. The authors call the middle interval the Visual Relay Window. Its geometry is task-dependent: counting-like tasks tend to require a broader and later relay, while tasks that rely more on downstream reasoning need an earlier handoff to the language side. TRACE builds on this observation. It uses lightweight prefill-time attention statistics to predict relay layers, applies task-aware scheduling to expand or contract visual relay allocation, and anchors selected visual support after handoff so that decoding remains grounded. Compared with contrastive decoding, fixed attention reallocation, or activation steering, TRACE is more temporally structured: it intervenes according to where the model is in the visual-to-language
