The central mechanism is the anytime-valid gate. Every self-modification must pass through a sequential statistical test that spends from a fixed error budget and emits an auditable certificate. Importantly, the authors are careful about epistemic status: they do not claim new proofs for endogenous closed loops. Instead, they package existing guarantees and state explicitly where survival of those guarantees remains conjectural once the environment becomes policy-dependent.

The paper then maps five endogenous-loop failure modes to five controllers: stability-plasticity, self-referential collapse, credit assignment, verifiable self-modification, and hypothesis-space expansion. The design choice that matters most is the restriction that gates can only select among behaviors already present in the frozen base model. That means a gate cannot create capability where the base model fails systematically. To address this, the verifier is moved into the loop as an active control signal. The mechanisms include best-of-N, micro-step search, process-level reward, search-layer control, self-authored reproduction oracles, and harness self-repair.

The self-authored oracle mechanism is one of the more interesting pieces. Given an issue, the model writes its own reproduction tests. An oracle suite is admitted if it fails on the unpatched base system. Search can then proceed using these in-loop tests, while the held-out grader is reserved for final measurement only. The paper also proposes a search-then-distill two-loop design: controlled exploration happens in the search layer, and successful behavior is later distilled into a more stable policy layer.
