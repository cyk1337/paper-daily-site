9:T66b,The authors introduce CoSee, an auditing framework for collaborative document VQA. It formalizes the interaction loop into a minimal action interface: read, write-to-board, and verify. The key object is the Board, a discrete and interpretable shared state. Each board entry contains a natural-language note, an optional grounding pointer such as page index or region coordinates, and the authoring agent ID. A lightweight controller enforces append-only updates, making the full reasoning trajectory observable and auditable.

Within this framework, the paper compares three inference protocols. The first is a direct single-turn baseline with no external state. The second is a single-agent board variant, where the model writes intermediate notes before answering. The third is a two-agent scanner-checker setup, where one agent scans and writes candidate evidence and another checks or answers based on the board. The authors keep prompts and decoding caps tightly matched across settings so that any difference is attributable to interaction dynamics rather than hidden compute advantages.

The main contribution is the diagnosis of failure mechanisms. The paper identifies two dominant modes. Noise Reinforcement happens when an ungrounded or incorrect note is written to the board and then repeatedly reused as evidence in later steps. Policy Collapse refers to drift in the model’s output behavior as context grows, for example toward overly short or under-covered answers. The proposed mitigation is lightweight verification, used as a minimal information bottleneck to prevent low-quality intermediate state from freely propagating.a:T587,Experiments are run on three document VQA distributions: SlideVQA, ChartQAPro, and VQAonline. The main backbone is Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, with scale and robustness checks on Qwen3-VL-8B, Phi-4, and Gemma-3-4B. The study compares single-turn answering, a single-agent board setup, and a two-agent collaborative setup, and evaluates them through cost-accuracy Pareto frontiers.

The empirical pattern is consistent. Naive shared memory gives at most marginal gains on retrieval-heavy tasks and often hurts reasoning-heavy tasks relative to the single-turn baseline. Multi-agent collaboration frequently underperforms because of coordination overhead and coverage failures. More tokens do not automatically buy more accuracy; under weak quality control, higher cost often correlates with worse performance. Trace-grounded analysis shows that chart reasoning is especially vulnerable to noise reinforcement, while open-ended QA is more prone to policy collapse, often visible as short-answer drift. Adding lightweight verification reduces error propagation, suggesting that the main issue is not just model size but the absence of a grounded information bottleneck.
