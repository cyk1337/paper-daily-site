b:T400,The paper addresses a central failure mode in asynchronous RL for LLM post-training. Asynchrony improves throughput by overlapping rollout generation with policy optimization, but the resulting data are stale and off-policy: updates are applied to trajectories not fully generated by the current policy, and long trajectories may even span multiple weight versions. Existing methods usually clip or mask tokens using a single threshold on the importance-ratio magnitude, implicitly assuming that a large ratio means an untrustworthy token. The paper argues that this assumption breaks in asynchronous agentic RL because the natural scale of the ratio depends strongly on token entropy. Large ratios at low entropy are often amplified sampling noise, while large ratios at high entropy can reflect legitimate exploration induced by asynchronous updates. The real problem is therefore how to suppress harmful off-policy noise without discarding useful exploration, and to do so without reconstructing an exact behavior policy.c:T7a7,The paper develops its argument in three steps. First, it establishes an entropy-ratio relation. Both empirically and theoretically, the authors show that the natural scale of the token-level log-importance ratio grows with token entropy. Intuitively, in low-entropy regions where the model is highly confident, a small train-inference discrepancy can be blown up into a large ratio because of near-zero probabilities. Such large ratios are often noise rather than meaningful policy drift. In high-entropy regions, by contrast, larger ratios are expected and can still be statistically normal. Second, the paper decomposes asynchronous staleness more carefully. Instead of treating each trajectory as if it came from one behavior policy, it separates inter-trajectory staleness from intra-trajectory version switching. The latter is the more novel observation: during a long rollout, a worker may sync to a newer model mid-generation, so different segments of the same trajectory come from different policy versions. Around the switch point, entropy and importance ratios rise together. The paper argues that this should not be interpreted as purely harmful off-policy drift; it is often valid exploration introduced by asynchronous training itself. Third, based on these observations, the authors propose ESTR, the Entropy-Scaled Trust Region. Rather than applying a fixed threshold to |δ_t|, ESTR uses a token-wise trust-region boundary that expands with entropy and contracts when entropy is low. Low-entropy tokens are constrained more tightly to block amplified noise; high-entropy tokens are given a wider admissible region so that useful exploration is preserved. A key practical point is that ESTR does not require explicit behavior-policy reconstruction and does not rely on hand-tuned magnitude bias. It is a token-level filtering or constraint mechanism that can replace conventional ratio clipping or hard masking inside asynchronous RL training.d:T52a,The main experiments focus on long-horizon agentic tasks and difficult reasoning settings. The headline result reported in the paper is on BrowseComp-Plus: ESTR reaches 37.3 avg@1, compared with 34.9 for the strongest prior asynchronous baseline, while matching synchronous GRPO in quality and training 2.6x faster. This is the right comparison to care about, because asynchronous RL usually faces a trade-off between throughput and optimization fidelity. The evidence is not just in the final score table. The paper first shows, on real rollouts, that |δ_t| increases systematically with token entropy, which undermines the statistical validity of a single global threshold. It then analyzes version-switch points inside trajectories and shows concurrent spikes in entropy and ratio, while the standardized deviation remains well-behaved, supporting the claim that many high-entropy large-ratio tokens are legitimate rather than pathological. Finally, against interval clipping, hard masking, and other asynchronous stabilization baselines, ESTR is more stable on long trajectories and reduces policy collapse. When reading the experiments, the most informative figures are the entropy-binned analyses and the training curves under different masking strategies; they explain why the method works, not just that it works.e:T4d2,This one is worth a careful read. Start with the introduction and motivation, focusing on the two assumptions the paper challenges: that ratio magnitude alone indicates token trustworthiness, and that each trajectory can be associated with a single behavior policy. Then study the analysis around Figures 1–3: first the coupling between entropy and |δ_t|, then the role of intra-trajectory version switching in creating joint spikes. In the method section, make sure to understand exactly how the ESTR boundary scales with entropy and how it replaces standard clipping or masking logic in training. In the experiments, prioritize the main results on BrowseComp-Plus and other long-horizon tasks, then the stability curves and entropy-bucket visualizations. The appendix is worth reading if time allows, because the theoretical derivation is central to the paper’s credibility. Good questions to keep in mind are: whether entropy is the best scaling variable or should be combined with reward/advantage uncertainty; how ESTR interacts with PPO/GRPO clipping; and whether a similar token-level trust region could be useful in asynchronous pretraining, speculative decoding mismatch, or multimodal RL with heterogeneous token types.f:T42b,The key idea is to constrain second-order statistics of the importance ratio ρ_t(θ)=π_θ(a_t|s_t)/π_old(a_t|s_t), rather than clipping ρ_t per token/step. The authors argue that, in a local regime, a KL trust-region constraint can be approximated by controlling the variance (and related second moment) of ρ.

They propose R2VPO (Ratio-Variance Regularized Policy Optimization): formulate policy optimization as maximizing an IS-style surrogate objective subject to a constraint on Var(ρ). The constraint is enforced via a Lagrangian primal–dual scheme:
- primal update improves the return surrogate (PPO-like objective without hard clipping);
- dual update adapts the penalty to keep ratio variance near a target.

This yields a “soft brake”: samples with extreme ratios are smoothly down-weighted (not zeroed), preserving gradient signal from rare but valuable trajectories. An off-policy variant (R2VPO-OFF) explicitly reuses stale trajectories; the same variance control acts as an automatic gate on how much those trajectories can influence updates.10:T46b,First, multi-agent team interactions are modeled as a discounted MDP, with shared context as states and agent messages as actions. Under the turn-taking protocol, team-level policy divergence is proven to reduce to single-agent divergence on visited states, solving the intractability of trust regions over the joint action space. Compounding occupancy shift is then formalized, with theoretical proof that stale-occupancy evaluation (using cached old-distribution rollouts) incurs performance penalty scaling quadratically with the number of agents n, while intermediate-occupancy evaluation (resampling trajectories after each update) reduces this penalty to linear scaling. Based on these findings, the TeamTR framework is proposed: trajectories are resampled after each individual agent update to ensure subsequent agents train on the latest context distribution, and per-agent trust region constraints monitorable via token-level KL divergence are enforced to bound distribution drift from each update.
