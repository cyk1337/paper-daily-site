18:T6ae,The method has two coupled pieces: ConsensusBench and ConsensusPR. The central hypothesis is that correct final answers are usually reached through a small set of stable intermediate outcomes, such as solved subproblems, validated constraints, case splits, or applied lemmas. Different correct trajectories may vary in wording, but they often converge on these checkpoints. The paper calls them consensus nodes. Construction proceeds by sampling N rollouts per prompt, keeping the correct ones, extracting intermediate conclusions from those trajectories, and clustering semantically equivalent conclusions into shared nodes. This is an important design choice: the target is not to imitate one canonical solution path, but to recover objective checkpoints that multiple successful paths pass through. Based on these nodes, the authors define ConsensusPR, a rule-based process reward that densifies the original terminal reward along the reasoning path. They also introduce three evaluation metrics: Final Answer Accuracy for endpoint correctness, Node Coverage Rate for how many consensus nodes a trajectory reaches, and Tokens per Node for reasoning efficiency. The metric design is tightly aligned with the failure modes they identify: NCR captures partial progress, while TPN distinguishes concise reasoning from verbose but uninformative chains. A practical advantage is that ConsensusPR plugs into existing GRPO and DAPO/RLVR pipelines without training a separate process reward model. Unlike PRMs, which try to score the quality of each step, ConsensusPR only checks whether the trajectory reaches verifiable intermediate states. That makes the signal more discrete, more objective, and easier to scale.19:T452,The experimental story has four main parts. First, the paper presents a case study of four reward-mismatch types under outcome-only supervision; this is not just motivation, but the basis for the benchmark and metric design. Second, ConsensusBench is used as an evaluation asset to show that models with similar final-answer accuracy can differ substantially in node coverage and reasoning efficiency. Third, ConsensusPR is integrated into GRPO and DAPO-style RL training, where it improves not only final performance but also process quality, measured by higher node coverage and lower tokens per node, while maintaining training stability. Fourth, the paper emphasizes the efficiency advantage over PRM pipelines: no human process annotation and no separate reward-model training. The provided text does not include the full result tables, so it is not safe to quote exact gains, rollout counts, or dataset totals here.
