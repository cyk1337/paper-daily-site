For text, BRAID uses an autoregressive policy-gradient objective in the GRPO style, with group-normalized advantages, clipping, and KL regularization. For images, it does not force a token-likelihood formulation. The image branch is modeled with flow matching, where the policy parameterizes a conditional velocity field along the denoising trajectory, and the shared advantage is injected through a likelihood-free objective native to the flow generator. The central design choice is to compute one trajectory-level advantage and then apply it through modality-compatible gradients. To improve credit assignment over long interleaved trajectories, BRAID adds a vision-thinking process reward: a VLM judge scores whether each intermediate image is useful for the reasoning path.
