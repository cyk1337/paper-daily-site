This is not a paper that changes the pretraining recipe tomorrow, but it is a solid step in measurement and mechanism analysis.1b:T793,The key move is to define commitment directly from the model’s conditional distribution rather than from an auxiliary classifier. For a frozen decoder-only LM at state ξ, each candidate answer a is associated with a set of verbalizers Σ(a). The model scores full verbalizer continuations with autoregressive log-probability, marginalizes over verbalizers, and obtains answer-level scores. In the binary case, this collapses to an exact log-odds code, δ(ξ)=Sθ(yes|ξ)-Sθ(no|ξ). All temporal notions are then defined from δt. Answer onset is the first output position where a parser can uniquely determine the answer. Commitment time is the earliest pre-onset position where the induced winner matches the eventual answer with sufficient margin and never flips afterward. Lead is the token distance between commitment and onset. Several design choices are important. First, they use total continuation log-probability rather than length-normalized scores because the object of interest is probability mass assigned to finite answer continuations; length normalization would define a different target. Second, they deliberately use delayed-verdict templates so that answer visibility is separated from template progress, making onset detection reliable and exact scoring feasible. Third, probes are introduced only after the target is defined. Compact hidden-state summaries are used to linearly recover δ, not to define commitment. Fourth, the paper attempts to separate commitment from realization progress via low-dimensional factors, and validates that separation with controls, transfer tests, bootstrap analyses, and multi-seed evaluation. Fifth, it adds quality-gated templates, online calibrated rules, and activation steering to test robustness, online predictability, and local causal sensitivity.
