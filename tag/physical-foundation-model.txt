18:T6ad,The core idea is to convert different outputs in the physical loop into discrete sequences. The model is built on the Qwen3-VL Instruct family and extends the original language vocabulary with action tokens and visual tokens. End-effector trajectories are discretized with ActionPiece, while future visual states are represented as tokens for RGB images, depth maps, and robot masks. Language, action, and future-state generation then share the same embedding table, LM backbone, and output head.

Training uses masked next-token prediction, with task formatting, modality delimiters, and loss masks specifying which target tokens are supervised for each task. A notable design choice is that the model does not introduce separate heads for action or future prediction. Instead, perceptual supervision, action supervision, and state-transition supervision all shape the same generative interface. The intended benefit is that semantic and spatial understanding can support action generation, while action and transition supervision inject physical priors back into perception.

On the data side, all embodied pretraining supervision comes from human interaction experience. The paper extends the earlier PhysBrain “understanding-first” setup by organizing egocentric video, synchronized ego-exo recordings, and panoramic video into task-centered episodes, paired with semantic and spatial annotations, recovered human motion, and subsequent visual states. Supervised fine-tuning then mixes human demonstrations, real-robot trajectories, and simulated interactions to bridge embodiment gaps and broaden task coverage.
