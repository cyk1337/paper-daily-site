18:T595,The authors use Monte Carlo continuations as a diagnostic estimate of intermediate state values. For a given prefix, they sample multiple independent continuations from the same policy and average the terminal rewards. These MC values reveal local changes in expected success along a trajectory. When compared with the PPO critic, the MC values often show sharp transitions, while critic predictions remain smooth and sometimes move in the wrong direction. A controlled stochastic FrozenLake experiment isolates the phenomenon further: as the maze size grows, the critic becomes smoother and less accurate. The analysis identifies two causes. First, in terminal-only reward settings, the standard token-level critic MSE contains an implicit penalty on variation among values within the same response, which encourages a flatter profile. Second, neighboring LLM states share almost all prefix tokens, so their representations and critic gradients are similar; dense supervision aggregates many near-duplicate updates and further pulls nearby predictions together. The proposed method, SP3O, sparsifies critic supervision by training the value function only on a small number of well-separated states. This reduces both the scope of the implicit variance penalty and the amount of redundant temporally correlated updating.
