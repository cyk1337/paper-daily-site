9:T5df,The proposed method is geometric iterative retrieval. It has three main ingredients. First, the model predicts per-layer continuous codebook vectors rather than discrete indices or a single full latent. This preserves the semantic hierarchy of RVQ while exposing the geometry of the codebook space. Second, training uses a CLIP-style symmetric contrastive objective instead of MSE. For each layer and time position, the predicted vector is trained to be closer in cosine similarity to the true codebook vector than to negatives from the batch. This addresses two failure modes at once: classification treats all wrong entries as equally wrong, while regression encourages mean-seeking behavior. Third, the method replaces the standard additive RVQ conditioning assumption with a self-attention aggregator over previously predicted lower layers, allowing non-additive cross-layer interactions. The backbone is a 12-layer bidirectional DeBERTa-v3 with hidden size 1536. During training, causal masking along the codebook axis lets the model learn all higher-layer predictions in one forward pass using ground-truth lower-layer conditioning. During inference, prediction proceeds autoregressively over RVQ layers: each continuous prediction is snapped to the nearest codebook entry and then used to condition the next layer. Compared with diffusion-based resynthesis, the key difference is that the iterative axis is aligned with the codec hierarchy itself rather than an externally imposed noise schedule.a:T401,Experiments are run on DAC codec restoration for both speech and music. The main empirical result is consistent: geometric iterative retrieval achieves the best LSD among learned baselines and is preferred in double-blind human listening tests.
