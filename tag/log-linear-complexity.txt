18:T4fb,The method, PISA, builds a coarse-to-fine pyramid over key blocks. The finest level contains fixed-size blocks, and each coarser level is formed by pooling adjacent lower-level representations. Instead of scoring every fine-grained block, routing starts at the coarsest level. At each level, the query computes LogSumExp scores over only a bounded candidate set, keeps the Top-K candidates, and expands those selected blocks into their children at the next finer level. This repeats until the leaf blocks are reached.

With a fixed branching factor, Top-K budget, and leaf block size, each query evaluates only a bounded number of candidates per level, and the number of levels is O(log N). That gives O(log N) routing per query and O(N log N) routing over the sequence. The final sparse attention is then computed over the selected original blocks.

The practical angle is important here. The authors implement hardware-aware Triton kernels that fuse multi-level routing and LSE scoring, so intermediate candidates do not need to be repeatedly materialized and moved through memory. They also avoid constructing a dense query-key score matrix.
