5:T439,The authors use the synthetic function-regression setup popularized by Garg et al., where small transformers are trained on serialized (x, f(x)) pairs and evaluated for ICL behavior. The main intervention is to split the context into two phases: misleading linear examples first, then recovery quadratic examples. By sweeping over the number of examples in each phase, they quantify how much prior context biases predictions after a task switch and how many post-switch examples are needed for recovery.

They also compare different training curricula, including sequential, mixed, and random exposure to tasks, to test whether training distribution changes the reversibility of in-context adaptation. This is the most interesting design choice: it asks whether transformers can learn a more flexible update rule if task variation is present during training, instead of overcommitting to early evidence. The paper does not introduce a new architecture.
