18:T40d,Multimodal Flow uses modality-specific encoders to map text blocks and images into continuous embeddings called ordered hyperchunks. Text hyperchunks preserve token order, while image hyperchunks preserve spatial-grid structure. These hyperchunks are arranged into task-defined multimodal sequences. A shared chunk-causal flow backbone predicts each target chunk conditioned on preceding chunks, while modeling positions inside the target chunk jointly. Training supports parallel target-chunk prediction, whereas inference generates chunks sequentially. The model uses joint attention for cross-modal interaction and modality-specific feed-forward networks to handle the different statistics of language and visual representations. Dedicated decoders map the generated continuous states back to text or images. Compared with fully discrete unified models, it avoids a visual quantization bottleneck; compared with hybrid text-AR plus image-diffusion or image-flow systems, it uses a common objective and a more unified sampling process.19:T414,The central object is the Discrete Average Generator. In a CTMC, the usual generator Q_t describes infinitesimal jump intensities. Few-step generation, however, needs the transition law over an interval [t, r]. The paper defines the average generator as the normalized increment of the transition kernel, so that one Euler-style step over length r - t reproduces the exact transition distribution for that interval. This mirrors the average-velocity field in continuous MeanFlow. The authors then derive a self-consistency identity for this average generator and use it as the basis of the training objective. A direct sequence-level implementation is infeasible because the generator matrix scales as |S|^D by |S|^D. The paper therefore moves from generator or rate prediction to posterior prediction, which is compatible with standard diffusion language model parameterizations, and projects the identity onto per-coordinate marginals. The resulting objective only involves D by |S| quantities and avoids full generator-matrix multiplication.1a:T613,The method is Prompt-Enhanced On-Policy Self-Distillation (PE-OPSD). Let p be the raw prompt and p+ the enhanced prompt produced by an external Prompt Enhancer. The student is conditioned only on p, while the teacher is conditioned on p+. Training proceeds on the student’s own on-policy flow trajectory: the student generates states under raw-prompt conditioning, and at those visited states the teacher provides target vector fields computed under the enhanced prompt. The student then matches those targets.

Two design choices matter. First, supervision is applied to the flow dynamics rather than only to final samples or to newly constructed text-image pairs. This is why the paper distinguishes itself from SFT-style alternatives: it does not require rebuilding the dataset around enhanced prompts. Second, the asymmetry is only in the conditioning signal, not in the task family. Teacher and student live in the same flow-matching framework, so the distilled object is: given the same latent state, how would a better-informed condition change the denoising or transport direction?

The paper positions this as on-policy self-distillation, where the teacher can be a stop-gradient or EMA view rather than a separately trained external model. The critical comparison is against three baselines: inference-time prompt enhancement, SFT with enhanced prompts, and off-policy distillation. The claimed advantage is that PE-OPSD removes inference-time overhead while aligning supervision with the states the raw-prompt student will actually encounter.1b:T64e,From the provided full-text excerpt, the experimental message is clear even though many exact numbers are not visible. The paper reports that PE-OPSD outperforms three baseline families on aggregate prompt fidelity across three main flow-matching backbones: 1) inference-time Prompt Enhancer, 2) SFT, and 3) off-policy distillation. It also preserves positive changes in visual appeal while keeping base-model inference latency, since both the Prompt Enhancer and the teacher are removed at deployment.

The evaluation setup is well chosen for the claim being made. Comparison to inference-time PE tests whether the external module can be internalized. Comparison to SFT tests whether simply training on expanded text conditions is enough. Comparison to off-policy distillation tests whether state-distribution alignment is the real driver.

The most important expected ablations are: on-policy versus off-policy supervision, raw versus enhanced teacher conditioning, EMA/shared-teacher variants, consistency across backbones, and stratification by prompt underspecification. The introduction already states the main findings: on-policy beats off-policy, suggesting state alignment matters; PE-OPSD beats SFT, suggesting static text replacement is not equivalent to dynamic trajectory supervision; and it beats inference-time PE on aggregate prompt fidelity, suggesting the model has indeed absorbed part of the enhanced-prompt behavior.
