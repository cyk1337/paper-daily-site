18:T41b,The analysis starts from a standard sparse MoE layer: the router scores all experts, selects the top-k experts, re-normalizes the selected scores, and forms a weighted sum of expert outputs. Under mild assumptions that activated experts have comparable output norms and weak pairwise correlations, the RMS of the MoE output is largely governed by the sum of squared normalized routing weights. Reducing k removes lower-scored experts and re-normalizes the remaining weights, increasing this squared-weight concentration. This explains why smaller k amplifies the RMS and variance of the MoE output. To correct the mismatch, the paper proposes Layer-wise Distribution Alignment (LDA). LDA applies a per-dimension moment correction after each SMoE layer, aligning representations produced under reduced expert activation with reference statistics collected from the default top-k0 routing configuration. It requires no retraining or architectural change and can be combined with static top-k reduction, adaptive top-p routing, or dynamic expert pruning.19:T7cc,方法上，作者构造了一个四类动作空间：tool_call、ask、refuse、confirm，并把模型每一轮输出映射到其中一类；若输出没有明确决策信号，则落到 residual 的 other 类，但 GAR 不计入 other。分类规则采用固定优先级 tool_call > refuse > ask > confirm > other，tool_call 由语法判断，其余三类由文本 cue 识别。关键设计是 case-level、any-turn 的 Gold Action Recall（GAR）：对某个 benchmark category，若模型在任意一轮曾发出该场景所需的 gold action class，就算命中。它不关心轨迹最终是否通过，只问模型有没有在合适场景下做出过正确动作类型。然后把 GAR 与 benchmark 原有的 conversation accuracy（Acc）配对分析。若 grader 真正要求先做出 gold action 才能通过，则应有 Acc ≤ GAR；一旦出现 Acc > GAR，说明 grader 掩盖了动作类型错误，属于 bound violation。反过来，如果 GAR 远高于 Acc，则说明模型动作类型选对了，但后续执行、参数、状态推进出了问题。这个分解把“校准错”和“执行错”变成两个近似正交的失败模式。

作者还设计了两个推理时干预来验证机制。SRI（State-Reconciliation Intervention）针对漏掉 tool_call 的情况，通过上下文扰动去促使模型补做工具调用；CRI（Call-Reconciliation Intervention）针对不该调用却调用的情况，分成 retry 和 bypass 两种：retry 让模型重新选择动作，测试 emission 是否向 gold class 回归；bypass 则不改 emission，而是绕过不必要调用，观察轨迹分数是否恢复。这个设计的好处是，它不只是报告“模型错了”，而是在问：错误来自动作分布本身，还是来自执行链条；以及这种错误在 inference-time 是否具有可塑性。相比普通 benchmark slicing，这里更像是把 tool-use 评测从 end-to-end pass/fail 变成了 decision layer + execution layer 的分层诊断。1a:T77f,The method defines a four-way action space: tool_call, ask, refuse, and confirm. Each model emission is mapped into one class using a fixed priority order: tool_call > refuse > ask > confirm > other. Tool calls are identified syntactically, while the other classes are detected through textual cues. The key metric is Gold Action Recall (GAR), computed at the case level with any-turn aggregation: if the model emits the gold action class at any turn in the case, the case counts as recalled. GAR ignores whether the full trajectory eventually passes. It only asks whether the model ever made the contextually appropriate action decision.

GAR is then paired with the benchmark’s conversation accuracy (Acc). If passing the benchmark truly requires taking the gold action, one should have Acc <= GAR. A violation, Acc > GAR, indicates grader masking: the benchmark passes trajectories that never emitted the required action class. Conversely, large slack, GAR >> Acc, indicates that the model selected the right action class but failed later in tool choice, arguments, or state progression. This decomposition turns a single end-to-end score into two approximately orthogonal failure modes.

To probe causality, the paper introduces two inference-time interventions. State-Reconciliation Intervention (SRI) targets missed tool calls by perturbing context when the gold action is tool_call but the model emitted none. Call-Reconciliation Intervention (CRI) targets unwarranted tool calls when the gold action is non-call. CRI has two variants: retry, which asks the model to re-decide and tests whether emissions move toward the gold class, and bypass, which avoids the unnecessary call without changing the emission itself. This is a strong design choice because it tests not only whether calibration is poor, but also whether it is plastic and whether fixing the decision layer actually improves trajectory outcomes.1b:T5dd,The main evaluation is on BFCL v3 multi-turn, with replication on tau-squared-bench retail and airline settings. The headline result is that some models with high aggregate accuracy have very low GAR on categories that require asking or refusing. The clearest example in the paper is xLAM: 71.8% aggregate accuracy, but only 19.5% GAR on miss_param, with a 64.5-point gap relative to size-matched peers, while BFCL’s state grader still marks many such cases as PASS. That directly supports the claim that aggregate accuracy hides action miscalibration.

The authors also run a 200-case manual audit and report inter-annotator agreement of kappa = 0.92, which strengthens confidence that the diagnostic is not an artifact of noisy labeling. The same pattern appears on a second benchmark, suggesting the issue is not specific to one grader implementation.

The intervention results are especially informative. First, calibration is plastic, but heterogeneously so: the same SRI perturbation can improve Acc for one model family and hurt another. Second, CRI cleanly separates two mechanisms. Retry often shifts emissions toward the gold action class, but tends to reduce final trajectory accuracy across nearly all families. Bypass, in contrast, can preserve or improve trajectory accuracy without changing the emission. That implies that re-deciding the action is not always the right fix; in some systems, isolating or bypassing the bad call is more effective than forcing a second generation step.1c:T6cb,The method is deliberately split into two layers. First is the belief model. Let Y∈{0,1} denote whether the junior agent would ultimately be correct if it completed the response on its own. At each generation step, the model emits a competence-evidence signal, such as normalized token entropy or next-token margin. These signals are not treated as calibrated probabilities. Instead, they are mapped, from labeled trajectories, into a competence posterior B_t=P(Y=1|e_1,...,e_t). Under an idealized conditional i.i.d. signal model, the paper derives a simple log-odds recursion and proves that the posterior process is a martingale. Second is the stopping decision. Continuing incurs junior-model token cost but may reveal more evidence; escalating incurs senior-model cost plus handoff overhead. This yields a finite-horizon optimal-stopping problem. The paper derives a closed-form myopic threshold and then characterizes the optimal policy via dynamic programming, proving that the optimal rule is a time-varying threshold in posterior space. A notable technical point is that the threshold result is obtained through monotonicity arguments, without relying on concavity or monotone-likelihood assumptions on the raw signal.
