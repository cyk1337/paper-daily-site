17:T73c,The method is simple, but the supervision-path argument is the core insight. The paper first formalizes standard agent SFT over a k-turn trajectory: for the first k-1 turns, the loss is applied only to reasoning and action tokens, while tool responses o_t are masked. This means o_t mainly receives gradients through nearby action prediction, whereas answer-relevant gradients must travel through many intermediate turns and become weak. In effect, the model is optimized for local tool-use decisions, not for preserving answer-critical features from earlier observations.

ACC rewrites the trajectory into a long-context QA training pair. It compiles the original question together with tool responses and environment observations gathered across turns, then uses the verified final answer as the target. This turns previously masked observations into explicit conditioning evidence for answer prediction. The model is therefore trained on a direct mapping from question plus distributed evidence to answer, instead of relying on indirect supervision through intermediate actions.

A few implementation choices matter. The paper uses answer-verified trajectories, which reduces noise from failed or misleading agent runs. It evaluates the idea on three agent families—search, SWE, and SQL—to show the recipe is not tied to one environment. ACC is also positioned as complementary to existing long-context extension methods rather than a replacement for them.

The mechanism analysis is one of the more interesting parts. After ACC training, the model exhibits task-adaptive attention restructuring and clearer expert specialization in the MoE backbone. That suggests the gain is not merely memorizing a new data format; the internal routing and retrieval behavior changes in a task-dependent way when the model needs to integrate distant evidence.18:T4fb,The main results are on two long-range dependency benchmarks, MRCR and GraphWalks. ACC-trained Qwen3-30B-A3B reaches 68.3 on MRCR, a gain of +18.1, and 77.5 on GraphWalks, a gain of +7.6. The paper highlights that these results are comparable to Qwen3-235B-A22B on those tasks, which is the strongest practical message: targeted supervision can let a much smaller model approach a much larger one on specific long-context integration abilities.

Equally important, the gains do not appear to come with a major collapse in general capability. The paper reports preserved performance on GPQA, MMLU-Pro, AIME, and IFEval, which matters because long-context fine-tuning often causes capability drift.

The most informative ablations are the ones that separate ACC from standard agent SFT, showing the benefit comes from supervision restructuring rather than simply exposing the model to more tokens. The paper also compares different agent sources and includes mechanism-level analyses of attention and expert behavior.
