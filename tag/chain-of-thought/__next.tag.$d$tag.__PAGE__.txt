9:T825,The key move is to formulate the gap between reverse CoT and verified forward CoT as a translation problem rather than a prompting problem.

Let q be the question and a the correct answer. Forward CoT (FCG) samples reasoning from the teacher conditioned only on q. Rejection sampling (RS) keeps only those forward samples whose extracted final answer matches a. Reverse CoT (RCG) instead conditions the teacher on both q and a. The authors argue that the student should ideally learn from the RS distribution, not from raw RCG outputs.

To do this, they build a small paired dataset. For each question, they sample one reverse CoT response x, then sample forward CoTs until they obtain the first accepted response z with the correct final answer. This yields paired examples (x, z). A translator model G_phi is then fine-tuned with teacher forcing to predict z from x.

A crucial design choice is that the translator only receives the reverse CoT text x, not the original question q or answer a. This serves two purposes. First, it matches the paper’s distributional derivation, where the goal is to map source responses to target responses. Second, it blocks a direct question-answer shortcut and forces the model to learn how to rewrite answer-conditioned rationales into outputs closer to accepted forward reasoning.

Once trained, the translator is used at scale: each new question requires one reverse teacher call to produce x and one translator call to produce z-hat, and the student is then trained on (q, z-hat). Compared with RS, which may need many forward teacher calls per accepted sample, this pipeline compresses the expensive part into one cheap teacher sample plus one translation step.

Relative to prior semantic suppression or structural-guidance methods, the difference is conceptual. Those methods still generate under answer access and try to reduce leakage. CoTranslator instead learns an explicit distributional correction from RCG-style outputs toward RS-style outputs. The appendix further provides a distribution-recovery analysis to justify this framing.a:T609,The experiments cover six student models and six math benchmarks, with transfer tests to a new math dataset, a different teacher model, and STEM problems.

The first headline result is quality. For students such as Qwen3-4B-Base, CoTranslator improves average accuracy over semantic suppression by up to 5.82 percentage points. That indicates that merely hiding answer cues is not enough; explicit translation toward the verified forward distribution works better.

The second result is the cost-performance trade-off. The two 4B students retain 95.0% of the average accuracy of rejection sampling while using only 35.9% of its estimated compute. This is the central empirical claim of the paper. RS remains the cleaner target, but it is expensive; CoTranslator uses a small paired set to train a translator and then amortizes that cost over large-scale data generation.

The third result is transfer. Without retraining, the translator generalizes to a new math dataset, a different teacher, and STEM tasks. Average STEM gains are 4.88 points for 4B students and 4.31 points for 8B students. This suggests the translator is learning a reusable transformation from answer-conditioned rationales to more question-grounded ones, rather than memorizing a dataset-specific template.

The paper also includes analysis showing that translated outputs are closer to RS responses and exhibit fewer answer-dependent shortcuts than raw reverse CoTs. 1. 逻辑任务上，COCONUT 经常在链开始前就已经把答案编码出来。作者发现，在 ProsQA 和 PrOntoQA 这类逻辑任务里，最终答案在 question 的最后 hidden state 上就已经可被读出；latent slots 的状态变化也很快收敛，后续 slot 只做很小更新。更关键的是，删除整条链通常几乎不改变预测，论文摘要里给出的量级是：在逻辑任务上，删除无监督 recipe 的链，最多只改变 3 个答案。这说明这些 latent steps 在很多配置里并没有承担形成答案的因果角色。

For RQ1, the authors combine deletion with multiple replacement interventions. Deletion only tests whether the model depends on the chain at all. Replacement distinguishes dependence on position from dependence on content. They use three replacements: noise, dataset-mean vectors, and latent vectors from another problem. This is a careful design. If deletion hurts but all replacements leave the answer unchanged, the chain behaves more like a placeholder. If replacing with another problem’s slots hurts more than deletion, that is stronger evidence that the model actually reads chain content.

For RQ2, the probing setup is broader than prior work. Instead of probing only the final answer, the paper probes every slot and every layer for the final answer, candidate answers, and each reasoning step, with permuted-label null baselines. This allows the authors to distinguish early answer readability, distributed path information, ordered step layouts, and candidate superposition. The key point is not probing alone, but probing interpreted jointly with the causal verdict from RQ1.

For RQ3, the paper studies trajectory evolution through step size, the last substantial change position L, and mid-chain editing. In the editing test, the chain prefix is replaced partway through with a twin prefix from another example, and the final answer is checked for whether it follows the edited content or remains anchored to the original computation. This goes beyond geometric description and directly tests whether a seemingly settled latent state can still be revised. 1. On logical tasks, COCONUT often has the answer available before the latent chain begins. In ProsQA and PrOntoQA, the final answer is frequently readable from the last hidden state of the question prefix itself. Latent states also settle early, with only small subsequent updates. More importantly, deleting the entire chain usually leaves predictions almost unchanged. The abstract gives the key scale: for the unsupervised recipe on logical tasks, deletion changes at most three answers. This is strong evidence that many latent steps in these settings are not causally responsible for answer formation.

2. On logical tasks, per-step supervision changes the organization of the chain, but not necessarily its content-level causal role. SIM-COT-COCONUT lays out reasoning steps in a cleaner one-step-per-slot manner, and deletion now hurts performance, indicating chain dependence. But in some configurations, replacing the chain content still leaves answers unchanged. That means the model may depend on the existence, order, or structural pattern of slots rather than the semantic information stored in them. This is one of the paper’s most important findings.

3. On arithmetic tasks, all tested recipes show stronger content dependence. Deleting the chain hurts, and replacing content also changes answers, which makes the latent chain look more like a genuine intermediate computation trace on GSM8k-style problems. The provided text does not include every table entry, but the paper clearly uses arithmetic cells as the main contrast against the logical-task results.

4.
