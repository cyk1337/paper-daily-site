19:T6eb,The work has two components. The first is a routing analysis built on OLMoE-Base, a decoder-only MoE model with 64 experts per MoE layer and top-8 routing, originally pretrained on roughly 5T mostly English tokens. The authors continue pretraining it on a balanced 35B-token multilingual corpus covering seven languages. They then collect post-softmax routing probabilities on held-out documents and analyze how routing mass is distributed across experts, layers, and languages. The focus is not merely which expert wins top-1 routing for a token, but how language-level routing distributions evolve, how similar those distributions are across languages, and where specialization concentrates in depth. The main empirical finding is that multilingual continual pretraining makes routing more diffuse and shared in early and middle layers, while language-specific structure emerges primarily in the final layers. They further relate routing similarity to token-level vocabulary overlap, suggesting that shared subword structure materially shapes expert sharing.

The second component is a parameter-efficient adaptation method, Selective and Shared Expert Finetuning (SSFT). The key design choice is to update both language-specific experts and shared experts in the final layers, rather than only the experts most associated with the target language. The rationale is straightforward: if late layers encode both language-specific behavior and cross-lingual abstractions, adapting only specialized experts leaves useful shared capacity untouched. SSFT is therefore a routing-informed adaptation strategy.
