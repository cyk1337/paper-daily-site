19:T82f,The method has three main components.

First, DeltaV introduces update-centric visual modeling. Instead of generating a full intermediate image at each reasoning step, the model conditions on historical visual states and predicts only the visual update tokens needed to represent the change from the previous state. The motivation is straightforward: allocate modeling capacity to changed regions and changed semantics, rather than repeatedly encoding static background or already established structure. In this formulation, interleaved multimodal reasoning becomes visual state transition modeling rather than image-by-image generation.

Second, the paper proposes the TSIM Router. Different reasoning steps involve very different amounts of visual change, so a fixed number of update tokens per step is inefficient. The router uses temporal similarity to determine how many tokens should be allocated to each update. The procedure groups samples into TSIM intervals based on change magnitude, fits a reconstruction-quality-versus-token curve for each interval, and stops allocating tokens once the marginal reconstruction gain falls below a threshold. As a result, highly similar states receive few tokens, while larger transitions receive more. The key idea is not a complicated routing network, but an explicit coupling between token budget and state-change magnitude.

Third, the authors build StructCoT, a 1.05M-sample dataset spanning 44 task domains. Their argument is that existing interleaved multimodal reasoning datasets are too narrow, often centered on mazes, puzzles, or visual search, and therefore insufficient for learning general update-centric reasoning behavior. In this setup, the data asset is tightly coupled to the modeling choice: without enough diverse state transitions, the model cannot learn a robust update policy.

At a higher level, DeltaV is not just a token compression trick. It changes the supervision target inside a unified model from reconstructing the full current image to predicting the incremental visual evidence that causes the next reasoning state.1a:T5e6,The provided excerpt does not include the full result tables, so the exact gains cannot be quoted reliably. Still, several important facts are clear. The training asset is substantial: StructCoT contains 1.05M samples across 44 task domains, which suggests the evaluation is not limited to a few synthetic tasks. The main empirical claim is that the visual-update paradigm is more effective than the full-image generation paradigm for interleaved multimodal reasoning, especially on tasks that require multi-step evolution of visual states.

The paper emphasizes two kinds of gains: reduced visual-token redundancy and denser supervision on reasoning-critical transitions. Based on the method description, the experiments likely include at least the following:
1. A direct comparison between DeltaV and full-image-generation ULMM baselines on multimodal reasoning and understanding tasks.
2. An ablation on the TSIM Router to test whether adaptive token allocation is better than a fixed token budget.
3. A data ablation showing the contribution of StructCoT and whether large-scale, multi-domain transition data improves generalization.
4. Efficiency analyses, likely including token usage, generation cost, and performance under different magnitudes of visual change.

What matters most is not merely lower token count, but whether DeltaV achieves better reasoning quality at equal or lower visual-generation cost. If the main tables support that claim, then the paper has a meaningful modeling contribution.1b:T5fb,This is a skim paper. Focus on the modeling setup rather than every benchmark.
