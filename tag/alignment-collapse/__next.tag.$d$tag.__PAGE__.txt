9:T423,The authors formulate iterative RLHF as a Stackelberg / bilevel problem with the policy as the leader and the reward model as the follower. The lower-level RM update depends on the data distribution induced by the current policy. By analytically unrolling this update, they decompose the true policy gradient into the usual policy-gradient term and an additional parameter-steering term. The latter measures how current policy samples change future reward-model parameters.

They then propose Foresighted Policy Optimization, or FPO. Rather than replacing the whole RLHF pipeline, FPO adds a targeted penalty during policy optimization to account for the missing steering effect. The analysis is first developed in a pointwise setting to expose the geometry, then extended to pairwise preference comparisons. The exact bilevel term would normally require inverse-Hessian computations. To make it usable for large models, the implementation uses a first-order TracIn-style relaxation, estimating training-example influence without explicit second-order solves.a:T400,The empirical section covers controlled continuous environments and a more realistic LLM alignment setup. In the controlled settings, standard iterative RLHF moves toward regions where the reward model overestimates true utility and then reinforces the same bias through retraining. With the FPO penalty, the policy is less attracted to those overestimated regions. In the LLM experiment, the method is tested in a Llama-3.2-1B alignment pipeline and is reported to reduce reward hacking relative to myopic iterative RLHF.

The confirmed experimental scale is Llama-3.2-1B. The confirmed comparison is standard iterative RLHF versus policy optimization augmented with the FPO penalty. The key empirical question is whether the penalty closes the gap between proxy reward and true utility, rather than merely making policy updates more conservative.b:T40f,Read the derivation of the effective reward carefully.
