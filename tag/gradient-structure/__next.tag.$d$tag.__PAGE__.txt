5:T98b,作者研究的是 Kulumba 等人训练的一组对比式 authorship attribution 模型。它们共享同一个预训练 encoder、同样的数据和 InfoNCE 训练目标，差别只在 scoring 机制：

1. Mean pooling：先把 passage 的 token 表示取平均，再做 cosine similarity。它要求 encoder 在单个向量里压缩整段文本的信息。
2. Late interaction, LI：保留 token-level 表示，用 MaxSim 之类的 token-to-token 匹配来打分。它不要求所有信息都提前压进一个全局向量。
3. Patch pooling：介于两者之间，把相邻 token 组成 patch 后先局部平均，再做 late interaction。

分析工具主要有三类。

第一类是线性 probe，用来测“风格特征是否存在于各层表示中”。作者探测的特征包括词长、标点密度、功能词频率、大小写等。结果显示，这些特征在所有模型、几乎所有层、甚至一个未针对该任务微调的 control encoder 中都能被线性读出。也就是说，fine-tuning 并没有创造这些特征。

第二类是 causal intervention，具体是 residual stream patching。做法是：给定 anchor、positive、negative 三元组，用 clean positive 和 corrupt negative 分别前向；然后在某一层把 negative 的隐藏状态替换成 positive 的对应状态，再看最终 same-author 评分恢复多少。这个实验测的是“某层表示对正确作者判断的因果贡献”。结果表明，mean pooling 模型在较早到中间层就已经把 authorship signal 整合到足以影响最终判断；LI 模型则把这种整合推迟到更后层，因为它可以依赖 token-level 匹配，不必过早压缩。

第三类是从梯度结构解释上述差异。mean pooling 的梯度会把所有 token 通过平均操作耦合起来，迫使中早层就形成可全局汇聚的作者风格表示；LI 的梯度更局部，允许不同 token 保留细粒度风格线索，到后层或 scorer 端再完成利用。patch pooling 则处在中间，形成一个连续谱，而不是二元对立。

论文还结合 alignment–uniformity 分析训练动态。作者观察到，不同 scorer 不只是最终 readout 不同，而是沿着不同的优化路径塑造 encoder：mean pooling 更早追求全局对齐，LI 则允许表示空间在更长阶段内保留局部结构。这解释了为什么两者即便共享 backbone，最终有效深度和可利用信息也不同。6:Ta44,The study analyzes a family of contrastive authorship-attribution models from prior work. They share the same pretrained encoder, the same training data, and the same InfoNCE objective. The only difference is the scoring mechanism:

1. Mean pooling: token representations are averaged into a single passage vector, then compared with cosine similarity. This forces the encoder to compress passage-level information into one vector.
2. Late interaction (LI): token-level representations are preserved and compared with a MaxSim-style token-to-token scorer. This avoids early global compression.
3. Patch pooling: an intermediate design where contiguous token patches are mean-pooled first, then scored with late interaction.

The analysis uses three main tools.

First, linear probes test whether stylistic features are present in hidden states across layers. The probed features include word length, punctuation density, function-word frequency, capitalization, and related style cues. The result is that these features are linearly recoverable from nearly all layers in all models, including an off-the-shelf control encoder. Fine-tuning does not create them.

Second, the paper uses causal intervention via residual stream patching. Given an anchor, a same-author positive, and a different-author negative, the model runs a clean forward pass on the positive and a corrupt pass on the negative. At a chosen layer, the negative hidden state is replaced with the positive hidden state, and the final score recovery is measured. This identifies where in the encoder the relevant authorship signal becomes causally important. Mean-pooling models consolidate that signal by early-to-mid layers, whereas LI models defer consolidation to later layers because token-level matching remains available downstream.

Third, the paper explains the difference through gradient structure. Mean pooling couples all token representations through the averaging operation, so the encoder is pressured to form globally aggregatable authorship features relatively early. LI produces more local gradients and allows fine-grained stylistic cues to remain distributed across tokens until later layers or the scorer itself. Patch pooling behaves as an intermediate case, which supports the gradient-based explanation.

The paper also studies training dynamics through alignment and uniformity. The claim is that different scorers do not merely read out the same encoder differently at the end; they drive the encoder along different optimization trajectories. Mean pooling pushes earlier global alignment, while LI preserves local structure for longer.7:T723,The key empirical findings, based on the provided full-text excerpt, are as follows.

1. Performance gap: with the same backbone, data, and loss, changing only the scorer can produce an approximately four-fold difference in authorship-attribution performance. This is the motivating puzzle.

2. Feature availability: linear probes show that stylistic features such as word length, punctuation, and function-word usage are readable from all models at nearly all layers, and even from an off-the-shelf control encoder. So the gap cannot be explained simply by one scorer learning better style representations.

3. Causal localization across layers: residual stream patching shows that in mean-pooling models, authorship signal becomes causally necessary by early or middle layers, whereas in LI models the critical contribution is shifted later. This is more informative than probing because it distinguishes readable information from actually used information.

4. Patch pooling as an intermediate case: bigram patch pooling (n=2) behaves between mean pooling and LI, which strengthens the claim that the effect is driven by scorer-induced gradient structure rather than an arbitrary architectural quirk.

5. Training dynamics: alignment/uniformity analysis indicates that the scorers induce different optimization trajectories during fine-tuning. The excerpt does not expose all numerical details, but the paper’s argument is that the main difference lies in when the model starts compressing authorship signal and when the representation space loses local detail.

The experimental strength of the paper is the coherence of the evidence chain: probes show the features are already there, patching shows where they become causally used, gradient analysis explains why, and training dynamics shows how the divergence develops.8:T550,This is a good skim for researchers interested in mechanisms rather than the task itself.

Then read the background section carefully, especially the mathematical definitions of mean pooling, late interaction, and patch pooling. The important question is how each scorer constrains gradient flow and information compression.

2. The residual-stream patching analysis, which is the central causal evidence.
3. The gradient-structure and training-dynamics discussion, which explains why signal emergence shifts across layers and is the most transferable part of the paper.
