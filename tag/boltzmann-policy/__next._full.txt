The optimizer is a Boltzmann target policy, proportional to π_ref multiplied by exp(r/β), where β controls the strength of the KL regularizer. A weighted SFT objective, however, induces a policy proportional to q w, where q is the rollout sampler and w is the sample weight. Matching weighted SFT to the RLVR optimizer requires q w to equal the Boltzmann target, so w must be proportional to π*/q. In the reference-sampled case q = π_ref, this uniquely gives the prompt-normalized Boltzmann density ratio exp(r(x,y)/β) / Z(x). The proposed BOLT procedure precomputes reference rollouts, verifier scores, Monte Carlo normalizers, and Boltzmann weights, then trains with standard multi-epoch SFT. The finite-data analysis separates a support gap from ordinary learning errors. For a one-shot sampled set S_N, even the best support-restricted replacement pays β log(1 / π*(S_N | x)) if the sampled support misses target mass. Other terms include partition-function estimation error, effective-sample-size variance, empirical generalization, optimization error, and parametric approximation.
