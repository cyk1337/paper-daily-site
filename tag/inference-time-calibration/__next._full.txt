On four ordinal benchmarks and four MLLM backbones, the authors probe frozen hidden states across layers and prompt protocols to test whether ordered labels are linearly recoverable. They then go beyond probe scores and analyze how the unembedding matrix filters the ordinal direction, and how much of that direction is retained by the digit-token row subspace W_S. This turns the gap between probe-readability and generation into a concrete output-subspace issue. Second, they propose Ordinal Lens Alignment (OLA), a frozen-backbone inference-time correction method. OLA does not modify the vision encoder, LM backbone, or original unembedding. It trains lightweight lenses on K mid-to-deep decoder layers, with the lenses anchored to the digit-token row space, produces layer-wise ordinal distributions, fuses them with learned softmax weights into an instance-specific P_OLA, and then applies a discrepancy-driven correction to logits only at the answer position and only over the target digit-token set.
