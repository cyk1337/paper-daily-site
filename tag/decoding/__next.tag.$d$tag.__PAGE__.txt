接着，论文分析 sampler 为什么会放大多数选项。核心区分是 commit rule 是否 peeking。所谓 peeking，是指 commit score 依赖于该位置刚刚采样出来的 token 身份，例如“采样到的 token 概率越高，越优先提交”。这种规则会让高概率 token 不仅更容易被采到，还更容易被保留下来，于是多数选项的 odds 被再次乘大。相对地，identity-blind rule 在决定提交哪些位置时，不看 proposal token 是什么，只根据位置本身的状态、熵或外部随机性来决定。论文给出形式化定义，并用一个 lemma 说明：如果 commit rule peeks，且提交概率随 token score 单调增加，那么更高概率的选项会得到额外的 odds amplification；identity-blind rule 则不会产生这类 token-channel 放大。

论文进一步指出还有第二条放大通道：ordering channel。即便 commit rule 本身不 peek，如果系统按 confidence order 先提交高置信位置，模型就会更早写入某些带有性别倾向或立场倾向的上下文，这些早期上下文又会反过来影响后续 fork 位置的条件分布。作者在职业故事场景里观察到，male-leaning context 往往在第一个 pronoun 之前被更早写入；而且每步 commit 的 token 越多，这种顺序偏置越强。

On a fully masked canvas, one forward pass is enough to read the model’s conditional distribution at the fork position. This is treated as the model’s belief. The authors show empirically that this belief matches exact sampling from the model, while costing only 1/11,520 as much as a full sampling audit. This gives them a practical reference distribution that isolates the model from the sampler.

The main theoretical distinction is between peeking and identity-blind commit rules. A commit rule is peeking if the decision to commit a position depends on the identity of the token just sampled at that position, for example by favoring positions whose sampled token has higher probability. Such rules amplify majority options twice: they are more likely to be sampled and more likely to be kept. By contrast, identity-blind rules decide which positions to commit without looking at the sampled token identity. The paper formalizes this distinction and proves that under peeking rules, if commit probability increases with a score that increases with token probability, then the odds of the more probable option are further amplified. Identity-blind rules do not create this token-channel amplification.

The paper then identifies a second mechanism, the ordering channel. Even without peeking, confidence-based ordering can write gender-leaning or opinion-leaning context before the fork is resolved. That early context then shifts the conditional distribution at the fork position. In occupational stories, the authors show that male-leaning context tends to be written before the first pronoun, and the effect becomes stronger when more tokens are committed per step.

As a mitigation, the paper proposes Fork-First Sampling (FFS). Before any other token is written, the fork is sampled either from the model belief (faithful mode) or from a target distribution (fair mode), and the partially fixed canvas is then handed to any downstream sampler. FFS costs one extra forward pass, works even with greedy decoding, and gives exact control over the fork distribution without the awkwardness of prompt-level attribute forcing.c:T7c5,The experiments cover LLaDA-8B, LLaDA-1.5, and Dream-7B on occupational stories, GlobalOpinionQA, TANGO, and a synthetic model with known ground-truth belief. The paper validates both amplification channels. For the token channel, it compares peeking and identity-blind commit rules and shows that minority proposals are selectively delayed or discarded under peeking. For the ordering channel, the paper shows that confidence-based ordering writes skewed context before the fork, and that committing more tokens per step strengthens this effect.

FFS performs well in both faithful and fair settings. In fair mode with a 50:50 target, the final fork distribution lands within 1.4 percentage points of the target on both model families, while also improving fluency. In faithful mode, generation tracks the model belief much more closely than the default sampler. For measurement, the authors build a step-wise and position-wise analysis framework for masked diffusion language models. At each denoising step, the model predicts clean-token distributions for all positions, but only a subset of still-masked positions is committed. Using this structure, the paper tracks refusal-related token mass across denoising steps and response positions under harmful prompts. This leads to the paper’s main conceptual finding: shallow-step alignment. The claim is that early denoising steps themselves form a safety-sensitive axis in dLLMs. Tokens committed early can strongly influence the final safety outcome even when they are not all at the leading response positions. Compared with shallow alignment in autoregressive models, diffusion models introduce an additional temporal dimension tied to denoising rather than surface order.

Based on that analysis, the paper proposes Refusal-Aware Early Commitment (RAEC), a training-free decoding method. RAEC changes only the commitment decision during decoding. If a refusal signal appears early and remains sufficiently strong and persistent, the decoder commits it early before later denoising dynamics can wash it out. Importantly, RAEC does not inject an external refusal template; it acts on refusal probability mass already produced by the model. This design is well matched to the diagnosed failure mode.
