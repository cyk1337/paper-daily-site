9:T423,LoopLM reuses a stack of L transformer layers for t recurrent steps (shared weights), effectively deepening the computation graph without increasing parameter count. A prediction head is attached at each step, enabling multi-step supervision.

Adaptive compute is implemented via a learned early-exit (or equivalently, a learned weighting over step-wise losses). The key training issue is collapse: without constraints, the model either exits too early (cheap but weak) or always runs the maximum steps (strong but no adaptivity). The proposed fix is an entropy-regularized objective with a uniform prior over exit steps to force unbiased exploration of depths early in training, followed by a focused stage that tunes the compute–quality trade-off and lets the model allocate steps based on input difficulty.

For interpretation, the paper positions recurrence as iterative refinement in latent space, and backs it with controlled studies that aim to disentangle “knowledge storage” from “knowledge manipulation” (composition, multi-hop reasoning).a:T52e,Scale: pretraining up to 7.7T tokens; open-sourced Ouro models at 1.4B and 2.6B parameters.

Efficiency claim: 1.4B/2.6B LoopLMs reportedly match roughly 4B/8B standard (non-looped) transformers on many benchmarks, implying ~2–3× parameter efficiency under their evaluation protocol.

Ablations: varying loop steps shows generally increasing capability with more steps, but training can be unstable due to exit-policy collapse; entropy regularization plus the two-stage schedule stabilizes depth usage and preserves peak performance while enabling early exit on easier inputs.

Mechanistic results: the paper reports similar “knowledge storage density” between looped and non-looped models (on the order of ~2 bits/parameter), while showing larger gains on tasks requiring fact composition and multi-hop reasoning—supporting the “manipulation over storage” explanation.

Safety/faithfulness: lower harmfulness on HEx-PHI, improving with more recurrent steps (including extrapolated steps). It also argues latent iterative traces align better with final answers than explicit CoT, suggesting less post-hoc rationalization.
