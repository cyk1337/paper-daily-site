9:T6c8,The method, CES, treats CoT generation as a sequence of chunks. At each step t, the model has a running prediction f_t(x) and a scalar signal s_t, such as confidence, answer stability, or another trajectory statistic. The paper is intentionally signal-agnostic: it does not propose a new confidence signal, but a new stopping policy that consumes existing ones.

The key tool is the e-process, a sequential evidence accumulator with anytime-valid guarantees. Informally, if a null hypothesis about lack of sufficient evidence holds, the e-process should not grow too large at any stopping time; if it does, one can reject the null while controlling false alarms via Ville’s inequality. This removes the need for held-out calibration.

CES runs two e-processes on the same signal:
1. a commit stream, which tracks the position of the signal relative to its slow trend and decides when there is enough evidence to return the current answer;
2. an abstain stream, which tracks signed velocity and decides when progress is weak, stalled, or moving in the wrong direction, so the system should stop and defer to a fallback.

A notable design choice is that the two streams are anti-correlated by construction. Rising signal supports commit and suppresses abstain; deteriorating signal does the opposite. This avoids relying on a single absolute mapping from signal value to correctness. The decision is instead based on trajectory dynamics.

The main limitation is also clear: the guarantee is at the signal level, not a direct correctness guarantee on the task. If the underlying signal is poorly aligned with answer correctness, CES cannot fix that. Its contribution is to consume a given signal more robustly than threshold-based rules.a:T548,The main empirical claim is that CES matches or exceeds the token-accuracy Pareto of Conformal Thinking across multiple model-dataset combinations while using zero calibration data. It also transfers more cleanly across deployment cells, where a cell refers to a model, dataset, prompt format, or token-budget configuration. This matters because calibration-based methods need per-cell validation data, and their guarantees do not survive distribution or horizon shifts.

The evaluation explicitly accounts for probing cost, which is a good design choice. Many early-exit methods understate compute by ignoring the cost of monitoring intermediate states. The paper focuses on the tradeoff curve between token usage and accuracy rather than raw accuracy alone.

The most informative experimental points are:
1. with the same underlying signal, CES is more stable than thresholded or calibrated stopping rules;
2. alpha keeps a consistent interpretation across models and datasets, unlike empirical thresholds;
3. the dual commit/abstain construction reduces both premature wrong commits and wasted reasoning on hopeless instances.

But the paper’s empirical target is clear: competitive token-efficiency without per-cell calibration, especially under transfer.b:T4da,For a skim-level read, start with the motivation rather than the math. The introduction clearly explains why threshold-based early stopping is brittle: thresholds do not transfer, calibration needs held-out data, and guarantees break when the deployment horizon or signal dynamics shift.

In the method section, focus on three points:
1. how the scalar signal s_t is defined and why the framework is signal-agnostic;
2. the intuition behind the commit and abstain streams;
3. what Ville’s inequality actually guarantees, and what it does not.

In the experiments, prioritize:
- fairness of the comparison with Conformal Thinking, especially whether probing cost is included;
- cross-cell transfer results;
- token-accuracy curves under different alpha values;
- any ablations comparing single-stream vs dual-stream designs or different primary signals.

If you are thinking about deployment, keep one practical question in mind while reading: where does the underlying signal come from, and how noisy is it? CES is an upper-layer policy, so its ceiling is largely determined by the quality of that signal.
