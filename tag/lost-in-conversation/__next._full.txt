最终 answer turn 单独处理。这里不再只做 history cleaning，而是构造 paired full-view reference：保留完整 user-side sharded dialogue，再附加与之配对的 canonical FULL specification，让 teacher 在任务已完整显式给出的条件下产生参考分布。这样 final answer 被拉向 FULL-view 下更稳定的解，而不是被前面可能已经偏掉的 assistant 历史牵着走。

此外还有一个 FULL-view branch 作为正则，防止模型为了适应 SHARDED 训练而破坏原有单轮 FULL 能力。论文还引入 reliability weight：如果某个中间轮 student sample 与 clean reference 偏差很大，则降低该样本权重，避免 teacher-student 条件错位时产生过强、可能有害的更新。这一点很像在 on-policy distillation 里控制 off-support supervision 的强度。

从方法设计上看，作者刻意没有引入 reward model、verifier 或额外 latent state annotation。原因很明确：LiC 的关键不是只在最终答案判对错，而是中间状态写入历史后会改变后续条件分布，所以需要 dense、turn-level 的监督，而不是稀疏标量反馈。19:T724,MAIGO is an on-policy self-distillation method with turn-role-specific reference construction. The student policy first rolls out on the SHARDED dialogue, so training remains on-policy with respect to the model’s own self-conditioned histories. The supervision, however, is produced by an EMA teacher evaluated under cleaner reference contexts.

For middle turns, the reference context removes previous assistant replies while keeping the user-side sharded prefix intact. This is the central design choice. The teacher still sees the incremental task specification already revealed by the user, but it is prevented from inheriting the student’s earlier textual mistakes. Distillation is then applied token-wise on the student-sampled response under this cleaned reference distribution.

For the final answer turn, MAIGO switches to a paired full-view reference. The teacher conditions on the completed user-side dialogue together with a canonical reserialization of the paired FULL specification. This anchors the final answer to the fully specified task rather than to a potentially corrupted assistant history.

A separate FULL-view branch regularizes the model toward its original single-prompt behavior, which is important because the goal is to improve SHARDED performance without damaging FULL competence. The method also introduces a reliability weight that downweights middle-turn updates when the sampled response diverges too much from the clean reference, reducing harmful supervision under severe teacher-student mismatch.

The design is notable for what it avoids: no verifier rewards, no extra state annotations, and no inference-time scaffolding. The paper’s argument is that answer-level scalar feedback is too sparse for a failure mode that originates in intermediate turns and propagates through context.1a:T620,The evaluation covers Math, Actions, Database, and Code under a FULL-vs-SHARDED paired-view protocol with deterministic verifiers, using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the base model.

The headline result is a substantial gain on SHARDED tasks: average SHARDED accuracy improves from 52.8 to 66.1, a +13.3 absolute increase. The SHARDED/FULL ratio rises from 66.5% to 84.1%, which is important because it indicates genuine gap reduction rather than a uniform lift in both settings. At the same time, no FULL task changes by more than 2.3 points relative to the base model, suggesting that the method largely preserves single-turn fully specified behavior.

The most informative ablations are likely the removal of history-cleaned middle-turn supervision, the removal of paired full-view answer references, and the removal of reliability weighting. Given the paper’s mechanism, the middle-turn component should carry most of the gain if the self-contamination hypothesis is correct. The answer-turn branch should mainly improve final alignment to the complete task, while the reliability term should affect stability and update quality.

Another important experimental choice is on-policy rollout. The method is not distilled from idealized clean transcripts; it learns on the actual history distribution induced by the student.
