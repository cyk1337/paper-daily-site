18:T48e,SenWorld is a physically grounded, deterministic, event-sourced digital twin. Personas, smartphones, external services, and physical context evolve together in one simulated world. Several design choices matter. Real-world constraints are injected through maps, weather, holidays, and network data, so movement, communication, and device state changes are not purely fictional. The system is deterministic, which supports reproducibility. It is also event-sourced, meaning state transitions are recorded as events and full-system snapshots can be exported at each simulated frame. This allows evaluation cases to be tied to exact moments in a longitudinal trajectory. Ground truth is represented as a pointer into the snapshot, such as a source table plus a unique record ID, rather than a natural-language answer. The paper also introduces a snapshot-centered projection mechanism: one world snapshot can be deterministically transformed into multiple downstream testing artifacts. Compared with prior state-grounded evaluation setups, the key difference is that SenWorld targets a richer smartphone ecosystem and avoids LLM-based judging in the final scoring loop.19:T41f,The empirical section focuses on three questions. The reported conclusion is that the synthetic data tracks the real benchmark reasonably well at the distribution level. Second, emergent behavior: instead of hand-authoring interaction scripts for each persona, the framework lets behavior arise from persona settings, schedules, environment, and device dynamics, producing nontrivial links across contacts, locations, communication logs, and app state. Third, practical utility: the generated evaluation cases are used to diagnose defects in a production smartphone assistant, and the paper shows that these cases can surface concrete failures and help localize them.
