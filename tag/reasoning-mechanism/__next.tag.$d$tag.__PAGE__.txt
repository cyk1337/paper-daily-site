a:T499,The paradox is traced to two distinct mismatches. First, coverage mismatch: Power Sampling applies exponentiation per-trajectory, which amplifies the weight of a single high-probability wrong trajectory while suppressing the collective support of multiple moderate-probability trajectories leading to the same correct answer, flipping the final majority decision. Second, dose mismatch: a fixed sharpening exponent α induces highly variable distribution shifts across different prompts, ranging from mild adjustment to near-complete distribution collapse on hard problems. The proposed Relative-Rank SoftSat fix addresses both issues: it replaces raw likelihood with per-prompt relative rank normalized to [0,1] to equalize sharpening strength across problems, and uses a saturating weighting function that boosts moderately ranked trajectories while capping gains for top-ranked trajectories, preventing a small number of dominant paths from absorbing excessive probability mass. An importance-weighted aggregation implementation is also provided that reuses the same N candidate trajectories from base model sampling, matching the compute budget of vanilla Self-Consistency.b:T4db,Probing experiments first establish three findings. (1) Truth is not encoded at the token level but emerges in sentence-level representations, and is entangled with latent reasoning patterns; disentanglement via clustering substantially improves discrimination. (2) Intervention follows a Decay Effect — editing is strongest early and becomes ineffective once the chain is committed; and an Uncertainty Principle — editing only helps at high-entropy forks and harms correct trajectories at low-entropy positions. (3) Naive Mean-Difference steering vectors carry semantic noise that damages originally correct reasoning paths; Fisher Linear Discriminant Analysis is therefore used to project activations onto a purified subspace that maximizes truth separability while minimizing distortion of the semantic manifold.

On top of these observations, the paper proposes DynaSteer: it first detects critical forks during generation via sentence-level lookahead entropy, computes a purified truth steering vector using Fisher-LDA within disentangled reasoning clusters, and injects the signal with a temporally decaying schedule, paired with a rollback mechanism that corrects errors as soon as they originate to prevent hallucination snowballs.c:T406,The analysis decomposes deliberation into difficulty registration and deliberation allocation. The intuition is that genuine reasoning should not look like a smooth, uniform hidden-state drift. It should produce stronger temporal transitions during decoding, and those transitions should be concentrated in a subset of layers rather than spread evenly across the network. The authors therefore define two local variation measures. The first, Δ_time, measures hidden-state change between adjacent decoding steps at a fixed layer. The second, Δ_layer, measures hidden-state change between adjacent layers at a fixed generation step. Together they form a token-by-layer grid of local dynamics. The empirical observation is that successful reasoning trajectories tend to show broader temporal fluctuations and sharper layer-local concentration. StALT operationalizes this by combining token-wise temporal amplitude with dynamic layer-local saliency into a single score. The method is training-free: it does not require fitting an auxiliary classifier, and it avoids collapsing all layers into a single averaged trajectory.
