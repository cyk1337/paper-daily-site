9:T573,The method has two parts. First, the paper analyzes update directions. For a parameter update ΔW at a training step, the authors compute its SVD and focus on the top-K dominant singular directions, which are treated as the main modes of change. To compare directions across checkpoints, they do not rely on a single vector angle; instead they compare the subspaces spanned by the top-k singular vectors using CCA. This gives a geometry-aware measure of whether the dominant update subspace remains aligned over time. Empirically, non-hacking runs show smoother evolution and higher subspace similarity, while hacking runs exhibit stronger rotations. Second, the paper turns this observation into a constraint. It proposes trusted-direction gradient alignment, or trusted-direction projection: run a small number of clean supervised updates, collect the induced parameter changes, extract a trusted subspace via SVD, and during RL project gradients onto that subspace. The idea is to allow learning only along directions that resemble clean progress. A notable design choice is that the method constrains the dominant output-side directions rather than freezing all updates, which makes it less restrictive than strong KL anchoring to a reference policy.
