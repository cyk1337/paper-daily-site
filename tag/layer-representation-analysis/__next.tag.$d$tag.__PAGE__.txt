a:Ta3b,方法分两部分：现象刻画和解码策略。

现象刻画上，作者不是只看 layer-wise accuracy，而是结合 residual stream 的层间位移、各层对最终 logits 的贡献范数、以及 token 预测轨迹的演化，去识别哪些 token 在中间层已经进入稳定语义轨道，哪些 token 在最后几层发生了明显偏转。全文把这种偏转解释为 late-stage perturbation，并把它和 planning–pragmatics tradeoff 联系起来：模型内部已经形成了偏推理的 token 倾向，但末层更偏向输出通用、安全、礼貌、对齐友好的 continuation。这个 framing 比单纯说“中间层有时更好”更有信息量，因为它把问题限定在 near-final layers 的局部选择，而不是鼓吹浅层替代深层。

具体算法是 Confident Decoding。它不做 early exit，也不改 transformer 前向。每个生成步仍然完整跑到最后一层，但额外从一个近末层窗口中取出若干层 logits，通常是最后几层到倒数十来层的一个小窗口。然后用 token entropy 作为置信度指标，从最后一层向前做 conservative backward search，寻找第一个局部 entropy trough，也就是在末层扰动出现前，分布已经足够尖锐、但还没有被后续层重新拉偏的位置。选中的那一层 logits 直接用于 greedy decoding 或后续采样。

这里的关键设计不是“选最低熵层”，而是“保守地向后回溯到第一个局部低谷”。如果直接全局选最低熵，容易落到过早层，分布虽然尖锐但语义尚未成熟；如果固定选某个中间层，又无法适应不同 token、不同任务、不同上下文的层深差异。作者因此把 layer selection 写成 optimal stopping 问题：在 bounded projection noise 和 late-stage perturbation 占主导的条件下，最优策略是停在扰动前的高置信点，而不是机械地走到最后一层。这个理论部分不是严格刻画真实 transformer 的完整动力学，但足以解释为什么“第一个局部低谷”比“最终层”或“固定早退层”更合理。

和已有方法相比，区别主要有三点。第一，它不是 contrastive decoding 那种用层间差分去抑制某些 token，而是直接替换输出层。第二，它不以省算为目标，因此保留完整前向，避免 early exit 破坏后续层对其他位置的计算。第三，它的选择是 token-level、step-level 动态的，而不是为整个模型预设一个静态 exit layer。全文也强调，这种做法更像是在 near-final layers 中寻找“语义最清楚的读出点”。b:Ta9d,The paper has two technical components: diagnosing the layer dynamics and designing a decoding rule.

On diagnosis, the authors do more than compare layer-wise accuracies. They analyze residual-stream shifts, contribution norms to logits, and token prediction trajectories across depth. This is used to identify tokens whose semantics stabilize in intermediate layers and tokens whose predictions are noticeably redirected in the final layers. The paper interprets this as late-stage perturbation and frames it as a planning–pragmatics tradeoff: the model internally forms a reasoning-oriented token preference, but the final layers may redirect it toward generic, safe, polite, or alignment-preferred continuations. That framing is more precise than simply saying intermediate layers can be better, because it localizes the issue to a near-final readout problem rather than a broad claim that shallow layers should replace deep ones.

The proposed method, Confident Decoding, keeps the full forward pass intact. It is not an early-exit method. At each generation step, the model still computes all layers, but the decoder additionally reads logits from a small near-final layer window. Entropy is used as a confidence proxy. Starting from the final layer and moving backward conservatively, the algorithm selects the first local entropy trough—intuitively, the point where the distribution has already become sharp, but before a possible late-layer perturbation has redirected it. The selected layer’s logits are then used for greedy decoding or downstream sampling.

A key design choice is that the method does not simply pick the globally lowest-entropy layer. That would often favor layers that are too early: sharp but semantically immature. Nor does it use a fixed intermediate layer, because the best readout depth varies across tokens, tasks, and contexts. The paper formalizes layer selection as an optimal stopping problem. Under bounded projection noise and dominant late-stage perturbation, the preferred policy is to stop at a high-confidence point before the perturbation, not to mechanically decode from the final layer. The theory is stylized rather than a full model of transformer dynamics, but it gives a coherent rationale for the “first local trough” rule.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear. It is not contrastive decoding that subtracts or contrasts layers while keeping the final layer as the semantic anchor. It is not an efficiency-oriented early-exit method. And it is dynamic at the token and step level, rather than assigning one static exit layer to the whole model. Static exits help only on subsets of tasks because the best readout depth is not fixed. The gains of Confident Decoding come from token-wise dynamic layer choice rather than merely exposing intermediate-layer information.

That is an important observation because it argues against the effect being just small-model noise or undertraining.
