9:T72d,The method has four stages. First, it defines circuit units at the output-dimension level of Transformer MLP projections, treating each dimension in the gate, up, and down projections as an editable unit. This is finer than layer-level or module-level masking and makes sparse selection feasible. Second, it performs supervised candidate-mask optimization. For prompts where the model fails, the authors construct teacher-corrected responses and freeze the model weights, optimizing only masks over internal units so that the masked computation better supports the corrected answer. The goal here is not to repair the model yet, but to identify which internal units matter for moving from failure toward success. The authors explicitly note that this stage suffers from a mismatch between offline references and free generation. Third, they introduce rollout-guided mask refinement, which is the central contribution. The masked model generates multiple rollouts, and the mask is updated with a group-relative policy-gradient objective using response-level task feedback. Intuitively, units that consistently matter for success or failure under actual generation are reinforced, while units that only look important under teacher forcing are filtered out. Unlike PPO- or GRPO-style updates that directly optimize model parameters, RL here is used to optimize circuit selection. Fourth, the refined mask is pruned into a final sparse circuit, and only the corresponding parameters are fine-tuned for targeted repair. Compared with prior work, the main differences are clear: the target is error localization rather than capability preservation, the localization stage is guided by generation-time RL rather than supervision alone, and the final update is circuit-restricted tuning rather than broad fine-tuning or LoRA over large modules.a:T531,The evaluation uses two 8B instruction-tuned models: Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The main repair results are reported on math and medical tasks. In math, targeted repair raises GSM8K accuracy from 80% to 91% and improves MATH-500 from 75% to 78%. On GSM8K, RESCUE outperforms a supervised-only interpretable baseline by 4 points and vanilla fine-tuning by 3 points, which is important because it isolates the value of rollout-based mask refinement beyond simple mask discovery. In the medical domain, it also beats vanilla fine-tuning on MedMCQA repair sets, suggesting the method is not restricted to arithmetic. The preservation result is equally important: across eight non-target benchmarks, the repaired models retain 99.4% of baseline aggregate performance on average. That number directly supports the paper’s main claim that local repair can preserve general capabilities. The paper also reports mechanism-oriented evidence: the discovered circuits are sparse, and their causal effects transfer across input rephrasings, which argues against mere template memorization. When reading the experiments, the most informative ablations are the comparison between supervised-only masks and rollout-refined masks, the sparsity or pruning trade-off, and the contrast between local parameter tuning and full fine-tuning.b:T40e,A good skim starts with the framing in the introduction: this is not generic interpretability, but error attribution tied to targeted repair.
