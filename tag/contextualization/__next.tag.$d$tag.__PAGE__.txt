5:T421,The model contains a D-layer Transformer block unit plus a dedicated correction FFN. During sequential generation, the current embedding e_t is combined with the cached block output from the previous position, z_{t-1}. The correction FFN produces a modified input for the block, so the token enters the block with information from the past already injected. Two design choices make the scheme trainable. First, the correction is non-cumulative: x^(k)=e+F_theta(x^(k-1), e), rather than a residual sum of K separate corrections. Iterations are used to approach a fixed point, not to accumulate depth. Second, the correction is past-only: position t depends on z_{t-1} and e_t, so at inference time the needed state is already cached. For training, the paper avoids T-step BPTT over the sequence. It unrolls the correction process K times over all positions in parallel, with K fixed and much smaller than the sequence length. The authors also describe a conversion path for pretrained Transformers by adding a zero-initialized correction FFN and fine-tuning.6:T409,The experiments cover OpenWebText, Wikipedia, and a synthetic pointer-chasing task, with widths from C=50 to 2048, depths from D=1 to 23, and block sizes of 64, 256, 512, and 1024. The reported headline comparisons are depth-efficiency results. A D=5, C=1120 context-ready model outperforms a 12-layer, C=768 Transformer and generates about 1.7x faster on an A100. With K=10, a D=1, C=2048 context-ready model outperforms a 6-layer, C=1088 Transformer and gives about a 2.6x inference speedup. Sequential inference matches K=10 training within 0.01 perplexity, suggesting that the fixed-point approximation used in training transfers cleanly to streaming inference in the tested regimes. On pointer chasing, a D=1 variant trained with BPTT solves all 10 composition levels, while standard Transformers show a depth-dependent staircase pattern. Ablations indicate that K=5 is usually enough for convergence when D>=5, that the architecture benefits from width and longer contexts, and that a dedicated correction network is
