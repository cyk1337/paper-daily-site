9:T4ed,The method is simple and well motivated. The authors adapt a classic compiler-testing idea: automatically generate programs and compare execution behavior instead of comparing strings. They use Csmith to generate random C programs with built-in checksum logic. These programs are compiled into binaries, then passed through the decompiler or binary lifter under evaluation to recover a higher-level representation. The recovered code is compiled and executed, and its checksum is compared with the reference checksum from the original program. Matching checksums are treated as evidence of semantic preservation for that sample.

Based on this pipeline, the paper defines a semantic correctness score: the proportion of translated programs that execute successfully and produce the correct checksum. This differs from BLEU in a fundamental way. It ignores variable names, formatting, and structural rewrites caused by optimization, and focuses on whether the translated program behaves the same.
