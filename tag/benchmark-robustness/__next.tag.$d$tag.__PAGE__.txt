9:T828,技术上分三部分。第一部分是 flaw taxonomy。作者从已有 reward hacking 事件中归纳出 8 类反复出现的 benchmark 设计缺陷，并整理成 30 个问题、7 个类别的 Agent-Eval Checklist。这个 checklist 的作用不是泛泛而谈“注意安全”，而是把 benchmark 设计里最容易出问题的接口具体化，例如隔离不足、执行不可信输入、信任不可信代码输出、泄露 gold answer、评测环境与 agent 可操作环境边界不清等。对做 benchmark 的人，这部分比系统本身更可复用。

第二部分是 BenchJack。它是一个构建在 coding agent 之上的自动化 benchmark red-teaming runtime。流程大致是 reconnaissance、flaw analysis、exploit generation、verification：先读取 benchmark 代码、评测 harness、sandbox 规则和 scoring 逻辑，定位潜在攻击面；再让 agent 生成 exploit；最后在 benchmark 上验证 exploit 是否能在不解题的情况下拿高分。作者强调这是 clairvoyant audit，即审计器可以看到 benchmark 内部实现，因此目标不是模拟普通参赛 agent，而是像安全审计一样主动找设计漏洞。这个设定是合理的，因为 benchmark 发布前的安全审计本来就应当拥有白盒权限。

第三部分是 iterative generative-adversarial patching。BenchJack 不只找洞，还可进入“找洞→修补→再找洞”的循环。作者把它做成一种自改进 benchmark pipeline：每轮根据 exploit 修补 benchmark，再重新运行 BenchJack，直到 hackable task ratio 降下来。这里的贡献不在于 GAN 形式的新颖，而在于把 benchmark 维护流程工程化：不是一次性人工修补，而是持续对抗式 hardening。

从方法边界看，这篇工作并不试图解决“如何检测所有真实 agent run 中的作弊”，而是明确放弃脆弱的 post-hoc trajectory judge，转向 benchmark design-time auditing。这个取舍很重要，因为很多监控器在 exploit 足够隐蔽时并不可靠，而设计层漏洞如果不修，监控只是在补锅。a:T768,The work has three technical components. First is a flaw taxonomy. From past reward-hacking incidents, the authors derive eight recurring classes of benchmark design failures and package them into the Agent-Eval Checklist: 30 questions across seven categories. This is useful because it turns vague advice about benchmark robustness into concrete failure modes, such as weak isolation, executing untrusted input, trusting outputs from untrusted code, leaking gold answers, or blurring the boundary between the agent’s writable environment and the evaluator’s trusted state.

Second is BenchJack itself, an automated benchmark red-teaming system built on top of a coding agent. Its pipeline consists of reconnaissance, flaw analysis, exploit generation, and verification. It reads benchmark code, evaluation harnesses, sandbox rules, and scoring logic; identifies attack surfaces; proposes exploits; and validates whether those exploits can achieve high scores without solving the task. The authors explicitly frame this as a clairvoyant audit: the auditor has white-box access to the benchmark internals. That is the right threat model for benchmark hardening, even if it is not the same as a normal participant model.

Third is an iterative generative-adversarial patching loop. BenchJack is used not only to find exploits but also to drive benchmark repair: discover a flaw, patch it, rerun the auditor, and repeat. The novelty is not the adversarial framing by itself, but the operationalization of benchmark maintenance into a repeatable hardening loop rather than one-off manual fixes.

A key methodological choice is what the paper does not try to do. It does not claim to reliably detect all reward hacking from agent trajectories after the fact. Instead, it argues that post-hoc judges are brittle and expensive, and that the more robust intervention point is benchmark design itself.b:T665,The evaluation covers 10 popular agent benchmarks across software engineering, web, desktop, terminal, and related environments. The main result is stark: BenchJack finds working reward-hacking exploits on all 10 benchmarks, and on 9 of them it achieves near-perfect scores without solving any real task. In total, the system identifies 219 distinct flaws spanning the eight flaw classes. The paper also includes concrete exploit examples, such as a nine-line PyTest hook that forces all tests to pass on SWE-bench Verified and leaked gold answers in WebArena. These examples make the issue tangible: the problem is not only that models are opportunistic, but that benchmark interfaces often trust the wrong components.

The more interesting result is the patching experiment. On four representative benchmarks with relatively stronger initial designs, the iterative hardening loop reduces the hackable-task ratio from nearly 100% to below 10%, and WebArena and OSWorld become unhackable under BenchJack within three patch rounds. That should be read carefully. “Unhackable” here means resistant to the current auditor, not formally secure. Still, the rapid improvement suggests many vulnerabilities are interface-design mistakes rather than deep structural flaws requiring a full benchmark rewrite.
