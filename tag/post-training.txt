19:T7ca,The setup is built around Qwen3-8B as a hybrid model. The same base model can be decoded in think mode, where it generates reasoning before deciding, or in no-think mode, where it emits a direct action token. This allows a relatively controlled comparison between a reasoning policy and a direct classifier without changing the model family. The task is binary intervention timing: given a prefix of a multi-party conversation, decide whether an assistant should speak now or remain silent. The dataset contains about 16,000 synthetic conversations expanded into roughly 173,000 token-level decision points, with only about 13% positive intervention labels, so the class imbalance is severe. The paper compares several training routes. One is direct decision-token SFT, which tends to produce a stronger classifier but suppresses reasoning. Another is a masked objective that preserves the model’s native reasoning, but only on cases the model already solves correctly; this means it receives little useful signal on failure cases and therefore struggles to improve beyond the base reasoning policy. A third route is think-mode RL, optimizing either accuracy or a behavioral faithfulness reward. The authors point out a key limitation of group-relative RL objectives: when all sampled rollouts agree, there is no learning signal, even if they all agree on the wrong answer. That leaves many confidently wrong prompts untouched. For auditing, the paper combines activation probes with behavioral interventions. Probes test when the decision becomes decodable in the hidden states, while interventions remove or replace parts of the generated reasoning to see whether the final action changes, which is a more causal test of whether reasoning matters. The paper also argues that several common metrics are misleading in this setting, including probability-based contribution scores, probe accuracy that ignores class imbalance, probing only at the end of reasoning, and uncontrolled flip tests.1a:T613,The main empirical result is that the strongest action policy is the direct classifier that does not explicitly generate reasoning, while the reasoning policy is more auditable but less accurate, with the largest cost coming from missed interventions. That is the central capability–auditability tradeoff. In training experiments, decision-token SFT further strengthens the direct policy but pushes reasoning toward irrelevance. The masked objective cannot substantially beat the base reasoning policy because it only preserves reasoning on examples the model already gets right. Think-mode RL also fails to clearly surpass the base reasoning policy, not because RL is useless in general, but because the group-relative objective gives no gradient on prompts where all sampled rollouts agree, including confidently wrong ones. The audit results are also informative. For the reasoning policy, the eventual decision becomes decodable during the chain of thought, whereas for the direct classifier the decision is already decodable at the prompt stage. That supports the interpretation that the direct policy commits early, while the reasoning policy at least partially forms the decision during reasoning. The authors also stress that single-run comparisons can confuse seed variance with reward-objective effects, so multiple seeds or replicates are necessary when comparing RL variants. The excerpt provided here does not include the full numeric tables, but the paper is clearly organized around mechanism evidence rather than a single headline score.1b:T858,方法叫 R²-OPD，关键设计有三步。第一步，用 teacher-independent 的 process reward 估计 reasoning progress。做法不是只看最终答对与否，而是对连续推理状态采样后续 continuation，比较从不同中间状态继续生成时的 solve probability 变化，把某段推理对最终解题概率的边际贡献当作 progress signal。作者强调 raw process reward 噪声很大，原因一是 Monte Carlo continuation 采样方差，二是中间 reasoning span 的边界本身不稳定。

第二步，做 sign-consistent span merging。原始轨迹先被切成 reasoning spans，然后把 process reward 符号一致、相邻的 span 合并成更大的 segment。这样做有两个目的：一是减少对细粒度边界估计的依赖，二是让后续比较发生在更稳定的语义单元上。文中给了理论说明：如果内部边界估计有误差，合并后这些误差在段内会相互抵消；同时对 token-level divergence 做段内平均，也能在弱相关假设下降低局部方差。

第三步，用 ranking conflict 做 reward filtering，而不是 reward replacement。对每个合并后的 segment，作者聚合 token-level reverse-KL distillation loss，得到该段“偏离 teacher 的惩罚强度”；同时用 process reward 给出该段“推进解题的贡献”。如果两个排序冲突，例如 progress 更高的段反而被蒸馏损失惩罚得更重，就说明 teacher guidance 在这一局部可能不可靠。R²-OPD 不把 process reward 加到 loss 里，也不重写 OPD objective，而是把这些冲突段的 distillation reward mask 掉，只保留 progress 与 distillation 一致的部分继续监督。这个选择很关键：它把 process reward 用作校验器而不是训练主信号，降低了高噪声 reward 直接驱动优化的风险。

从实现上，论文基于 reverse-KL OPD，并采用 support-restricted 近似来计算 token-level KL，只在学生或师生联合的 top-H support 上做分布匹配，避免全词表计算。R²-OPD 叠加在这个标准 OPD 框架之上，因此兼容现有 on-policy 蒸馏流水线。1c:T866,The method, R²-OPD, has three main components. First, it estimates reasoning progress using teacher-independent process rewards. Instead of relying only on final correctness, it samples continuations from successive intermediate reasoning states and measures how the probability of solving the task changes. This gives a marginal contribution estimate for each reasoning span. The paper emphasizes that raw process rewards are noisy because continuation-based estimation has Monte Carlo variance and because fine-grained reasoning boundaries are themselves unstable.

Second, it performs sign-consistent span merging. The trajectory is segmented into reasoning spans, and adjacent spans with process rewards of the same sign are merged into larger segments. This reduces sensitivity to noisy internal boundaries and creates more stable units for calibration. The theoretical discussion argues that internal boundary estimation errors can cancel within merged segments, and that averaging token-level divergence over a segment reduces local variance under weak dependence assumptions.

Third, it detects local ranking conflicts between progress and distillation penalty, and uses those conflicts for filtering. For each merged segment, token-level reverse-KL distillation losses are aggregated into a segment-level penalty for deviating from the teacher. Process reward gives a segment-level estimate of reasoning advancement. If a higher-progress segment is penalized more heavily than a lower-progress one, the two signals disagree in ranking, indicating that teacher supervision may be unreliable there. Instead of adding process reward into the objective or replacing OPD, R²-OPD masks distillation rewards on the most inconsistent segments and keeps teacher guidance elsewhere.

This design choice is the core idea. Process reward is treated as a reliability probe rather than a primary training signal, which avoids directly optimizing against a high-variance reward. The implementation is built on reverse-KL OPD with support-restricted approximation over a top-H token set, so the method can be layered onto standard practical OPD pipelines.1d:T776,The experimental section is aimed at validating the signal-calibration hypothesis rather than demonstrating a large-scale model jump. The tasks are reasoning-heavy, and the comparisons include vanilla OPD, variants that directly incorporate outcome or process rewards, and alternative filtering or weighting strategies.

Three findings stand out. First, the mismatch is real: the paper provides qualitative examples and aggregate analysis showing that some intermediate spans increase downstream solve probability while receiving stronger distillation penalties because they diverge from the teacher distribution. This is especially visible on harder reasoning problems.

Second, the gains come from removing bad supervision rather than injecting another reward. Compared with directly mixing process rewards into optimization, the filtering-based approach is more stable and more consistently helpful. The explanation is straightforward: raw process rewards are noisy, so using them as direct optimization targets can distort learning, whereas using them only to suppress clearly inconsistent teacher signals is safer.

Third, the ablations support the two main design choices: sign-consistent merging and segment-level averaging. Without merging, conflict detection becomes more brittle because boundary noise leaks into the comparison. For each original prompt x, the method constructs an offline matched triplet: the original prompt x, a meaning-preserving paraphrase x_para, and a counterfactual prompt x_cf that changes one task-relevant condition. The student first generates a rollout y from its current policy on the original prompt. CROP then keeps that realized rollout fixed and rescoring is performed under all three prompts on the same response prefixes. This is an important design choice: the comparison is over conditional next-token distributions along the same trajectory, rather than over independently sampled responses, which reduces variance from trajectory mismatch.

At each token position, CROP measures how much the model’s next-token distribution changes under the counterfactual prompt and subtracts how much it changes under the paraphrase prompt. Intuitively, sensitivity to the counterfactual captures dependence on task semantics, while sensitivity to the paraphrase captures dependence on superficial wording. The resulting score is a calibrated relevance proxy rather than a generic input-sensitivity measure. Tokens are then selected globally under a fixed batch-level budget, and only those selected sampled tokens receive the teacher distillation loss. The teacher target and the OPD objective remain unchanged; only the allocation of supervision changes.

Distribution shift is computed with a top-K JSD plus a residual bucket. This is a practical compromise: full-vocabulary divergence is expensive, but top-K alone can ignore tail mass.
