18:T66b,Ask-to-Distill (A2D) treats a prompt as a distribution elicitor: it determines which prefixes the student visits, hence where teacher token distributions become available for OPD.

Two-stage pipeline:
1) Train a prompt synthesizer before OPD.
- Initialize the synthesizer from the student.
- Optimize it with RL while keeping the scoring student and the teacher frozen.
- Crucially, the method avoids an expensive inner loop: it does not update the student per candidate prompt to estimate value.

2) A no-answer, distributional surrogate reward for prompt value.
- The reward aggregates token-level signals along student rollouts.
- Instead of rewarding pure disagreement (which can be dominated by support-mismatch), it uses a support-aware notion of teachability: combine teacher–student discrepancy with the teacher’s probability mass on the student’s candidate tokens, aiming to capture “corrective preference within student support” rather than “divergence outside support”.

Anti-gaming terms:
- Length control via sublinear length normalization plus a soft response-budget penalty to prevent inflating reward by longer outputs.
- A batch-level diversity term to prevent mode collapse into a narrow prompt family.

Curation:
- After training, prompts are format-filtered and lexically/semantically deduplicated; a frozen Qwen3-Embedding-4B is used only for semantic deduplication, not for reward computation.

Compared to synthetic SFT data, A2D does not need reference answers; compared to task-generation via success rewards, it optimizes the usability of teacher distributional supervision rather than task correctness.19:T4a4,RACE has two core operations. Referential Abstraction removes identity-revealing text and replaces it with image-grounded references. These can be specific visual references, such as position, appearance, occlusion, or spatial relation, or ambiguous relational references that require searching over multiple depicted candidates and selecting the one whose retrieved evidence satisfies a condition. The construction searches all candidates, requires a unique match, rules out alternatives, and uses a question-only probe to detect identity or answer leakage. Compositional Evidence then builds questions whose answer depends on every selected target’s evidence. The targets can come from one image or from several images, and the construction requires that removing any designated contribution leaves the answer underdetermined. The data starts from real images, including social media photos, Bilibili video frames, and MovieNet imagery. For unannotated images, filtering, visual analysis, and retrieval establish candidate identities; MovieNet provides identity-image provenance. A teacher solves the resulting tasks, and only trajectories with correct final answers are kept for SFT.1a:T479,The authors build content-matched user requests and reply pairs. The dataset contains 287 first-person requests across six task types, rendered by local native speakers into several English varieties: Standard American English, British English, Australian English, African American English, Colloquial Singapore English, Nigerian Pidgin, Appalachian English, plus informal and formal Standard English controls. They evaluate ten open model lineages with public checkpoints across post-training stages, including OLMo-2 from 1B to 32B, OLMo-3, Tülu-2/3, and Zephyr. For outputs, they define a copy-controlled convergence ratio that scores only novel reply spans, excluding shared 4-grams and quoted text, so copying the user does not count as accommodation. For updates, they define a matching bonus using the length-normalized log-ratio between a stage and its preceding checkpoint. This measures whether the update assigns more gain to a matched reply for a user of variety v than for a Standard-English user. The paper also constructs calibrated accommodation pairs for DPO, penalizing under-accommodation, over-accommodation, and imposition.1b:T65a,DataPraxis is built around verified workspace evolution. The starting point is a seed workspace containing data assets and an execution environment. Instead of generating a task immediately from that initial directory, the framework lets an agent analyze the workspace, produce scripts, intermediate datasets, findings, and artifacts, and then writes verified results back into the workspace. This creates an evolved state that can support deeper follow-up analysis. Different analytical branches can also be inherited or combined, which allows later questions to depend on earlier discoveries.

Once a chain of findings and dependencies has been accumulated, the framework constructs a task backward from those dependencies. It removes the intermediate support that existed during workspace evolution, so the final solver must reconstruct the analysis from the raw materials. In this way, the hidden dependency structure discovered during evolution becomes a challenging long-horizon task.

To address reliability, the paper introduces dual-evidence verification. One source of evidence comes from validated findings produced during workspace evolution. The second comes from independent solution trajectories used to build and validate rubrics. The verifier therefore checks not only final answers but also deliverables, supporting evidence, and overall analytical quality. Compared with direct task generation from data descriptions, this approach is better at surfacing questions that only emerge after prior analysis. Compared with exploration-only task mining, it puts more emphasis on dependency accumulation and reuse.1c:T53c,The paper builds DataPraxis-5K, a dataset of 5,000 long-horizon professional analytics training examples across 15 domains, including business, finance, sports, and entertainment. It trains two models: DataPraxis-27B and DataPraxis-35B-A3B, the latter being a 35B MoE with 3B active parameters.

A useful motivation experiment studies whether deeper questions really depend on prior analysis. In 50 workspaces, an agent proposes three successive questions, and Qwen3.6-27B is used to measure the perplexity of each question under different context conditions. When only the initial workspace is provided, mean perplexity rises from 16.87 to 31.07 for later rounds. When prior findings are also included, perplexity drops by 46.1% and 53.0% in rounds 2 and 3. This supports the paper’s main premise: deeper analytical questions are often not obvious from the raw workspace alone and become much more predictable once earlier findings are available.

The abstract also claims that DataPraxis-27B achieves leading performance among the evaluated models on data analysis and long-horizon professional tasks. From the provided full-content excerpt, however, the exact benchmarks, absolute scores, and strongest baselines are not yet visible, so those should be checked directly in the experimental section before drawing a stronger conclusion.1d:T6eb,The method, SpaRLax, has two main ingredients.

First, it builds stochastic but differentiable soft reasoning states. At each reasoning step, the model produces vocabulary logits, applies Gumbel reparameterization, and obtains a relaxed token distribution that can still support backpropagation. The key distinction from prior Gumbel-based soft-CoT methods is that the authors explicitly use the differentiability of the relaxation to restore gradient flow across reasoning steps. A soft token is therefore not treated as an opaque RL action; it becomes an internal node in the computation graph, allowing later losses and rewards to shape earlier soft reasoning choices through cross-step credit assignment.
