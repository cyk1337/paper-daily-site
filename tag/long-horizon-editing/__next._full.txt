1b:T6e9,The method has two central ideas. First, a round-trip editing primitive. Starting from a seed document s, the benchmark defines a forward instruction x→ and an inverse instruction x←. The model applies the forward edit to obtain t, then applies the inverse edit to reconstruct ŝ. If the model both follows instructions and avoids unintended changes, ŝ should match s. Because exact string match is too crude across heterogeneous document types, the benchmark uses domain-specific similarity functions tailored to formats such as code, configuration files, subtitles, crystallography files, and music notation.

Second, the paper composes these reversible edits into a relay. Since each round-trip should ideally return to the original seed, multiple round-trips can be chained to simulate long delegated workflows. This turns long-horizon reliability into a measurable degradation curve: reconstruction quality after 2, 4, ..., 20 interactions. That is the key move that makes cumulative corruption visible.

The benchmark covers 52 professional domains and 310 work environments across five broad categories. Each environment contains a real seed document, 5–10 complex edit tasks, and distractor context to mimic realistic working conditions. Each edit is executed in an independent single-turn session. This choice isolates the model’s native document-editing reliability rather than mixing in persistent agent state or external verification. The paper also validates that the backtranslation-style setup does not reward trivial copying: models must actually perform the forward edit, otherwise they fail the task. The appendices discuss assumptions behind reversibility and the limits of similarity metrics, which are important for trusting the benchmark.1c:T61e,The large-scale study evaluates 19 LLMs on DELEGATE-52. The headline result is that current models steadily corrupt documents under long delegated workflows. Even frontier systems such as Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude 4.6 Opus, and GPT 5.4 lose about 25% of document content on average after 20 interactions. Averaged across all tested models, degradation is around 50%.

The paper defines a model as ready for delegated work in a domain if it retains at least a 98% reconstruction score after 20 interactions. Under that criterion, Python is the only domain, among 52, where most models are close to being usable. This domain pattern is informative: strongly structured and easily verifiable formats like Python and databases are much more robust, while natural-language-heavy or niche symbolic formats such as earning statements and music notation are much more fragile.

Several targeted analyses are especially useful. Longer documents, longer workflows, and distractor context all worsen degradation, and the effects compound over time rather than staying local to a single step. Performance after only two interactions is not predictive of performance after twenty, which shows why short-horizon benchmarks are misleading for delegated work. The paper also tests a basic agentic harness and finds no meaningful improvement, suggesting that the bottleneck is not simply prompting or orchestration but the model’s underlying editing fidelity. The benchmark instances themselves are substantial: each environment uses roughly 15k-token documents and 5–10 edit tasks.1d:T44a,For a quick but useful read, start with the introduction and the figure that defines the round-trip and relay setup. After that, go straight to the main result figures showing degradation over interaction steps and the domain breakdown.
