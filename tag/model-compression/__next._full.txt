19:T4bf,All models are GPT-2 scale: 12 layers, 12 heads, hidden size 768, trained for 3 epochs on a 1B-token OpenWebText split, for roughly 3B token exposures. As dense language models, softmax variants remain better: sigmoid is worse by about 0.36 PPL with RoPE and about 1.21 PPL without RoPE. The picture changes once learned hard eviction is introduced. Relative to their own no-eviction references, the sigmoid-gated models achieve useful deletion rates with almost no perplexity cost. Sig+G RoPE removes 19.8% of KV entries with essentially unchanged PPL (22.424 vs 22.440), and Sig+G NoPE removes 32.2% with only a small increase (24.637 vs 24.603). Under a matched live-cache protocol, the learned sigmoid-gated models also outperform the paper’s H2O and KeyDiff implementations, whereas softmax-gated models do not. A random support-removal probe is important here: sigmoid attention is not generically more robust to arbitrary KV deletion, and can in fact be more sensitive than softmax at larger random removal rates. That strengthens the paper’s interpretation that the gain comes from better compatibility between learned deletion patterns and the attention substrate, not from generic deletion robustness.1a:T806,The method has two main components. The first is DVBP. The authors estimate the input activation covariance of each layer in an online manner, avoiding the need to store all activations. They then eigendecompose the covariance matrix and use random matrix theory, specifically the Marchenko-Pastur law, to identify the noise-dominated part of the spectrum. Those spectral components are filtered out, producing a denoised covariance matrix. Variance scores are then computed from this denoised covariance and used to rank neurons. The key point is that the original VBP relies on diagonal variance terms, but those diagonals are themselves corrupted by global spectral noise; denoising at the spectrum level leads to more stable rankings, especially under aggressive pruning.

The second component is OB²C. Starting from the Optimal Brain Compression reconstruction objective, the authors incorporate the mean-shift bias compensation directly into the objective instead of treating it as a separate post-hoc fix. With this formulation, the layer-wise Hessian reduces exactly to the input activation covariance matrix. This matters because it turns a generic second-order reconstruction problem into one that can be solved from calibration activations alone. Given a pruning mask, the method then solves for the optimal update of both the remaining weights and the bias so that the output reconstruction error is minimized after low-variance neurons are removed. Compared with original VBP, the difference is not just a better bias correction; the remaining weights are also allowed to absorb information from the pruned features. Compared with standard OBC, the explicit bias term makes the formulation better suited to the case where low-variance features behave like near-constants.

Operationally, the full pipeline is: use denoised covariance for more reliable neuron ranking, then use the same covariance for second-order recovery. The design is fairly clean, and the reuse of one statistical object across both stages is the main technical appeal.1b:T635,The experiments cover DeiT, Swin, and ConvNeXt backbones across Tiny, Small, and Base scales. The main comparison is against the original VBP under different structured pruning ratios. The headline result is that the gain widens as pruning becomes more aggressive. At 50% structural reduction, Tiny models retain roughly 80% Top-1 accuracy, while Small and Base models stay above 90%; relative to VBP, the improvement reaches up to 7.33% on Swin-S and 29.46% on ConvNeXt-T. These numbers suggest that the method mainly helps in the high-compression regime, which is consistent with the paper’s diagnosis that VBP fails because of noisy ranking and insufficient compensation.

The most informative ablations are of two kinds. First, whether spectral denoising alone improves neuron ranking. The paper shows that denoised variance by itself already outperforms raw variance at high pruning ratios, indicating that covariance noise is not a minor issue but directly affects pruning decisions. Second, whether OB²C improves over mean-shift-only compensation. The results show that bias correction alone is insufficient; jointly updating the remaining weights reduces reconstruction error and improves final accuracy. If the paper includes layer-wise or module-wise analyses, the gains are likely more visible in MLP/channel pruning settings, where the low-variance-as-near-constant assumption is most plausible.

A clear limitation is that all experiments are on vision models rather than LLMs. So the transferable value is mostly in the method, not in the empirical conclusions themselves.1c:T44d,Sniper is a two-stage structured pruning framework (depth then width).
- Mixed-granularity decomposition: each layer is represented by attention block A_l and MLP block M_l, plus a composite item T_l meaning “keep the whole layer”. A mutual-exclusion constraint prevents selecting T_l together with its sub-blocks.
- Depth pruning as 0/1 knapsack: each component x has weight w(x) (parameter count) and value v(x) (importance). Given a parameter budget C, maximize total value under the capacity constraint. The solver uses dynamic programming; to make it tractable for large C, parameter counts are discretized by a factor α, reducing complexity to O(N·⌊C/α⌋).
- Importance estimation: an iterative marginal-contribution scheme where v(x) is derived from the divergence in output logits when removing x on a calibration batch.
- Width pruning stage: applied after the depth-selected backbone to fine-tune the final model to match the target compression ratio closely.
- Metric: CRAFT (Compression Ratio Adherence Factor) quantifies how well a method meets the requested compression budget.1d:T52f,ReQuant is framed as fixed-grid discrete refinement. The quantization format is frozen (bit-width, scale, zero-point, grid layout, inference kernels). The only variables are the integer codes assigned to weights.

The objective follows reconstruction-based PTQ, with an explicit focus on activation mismatch: at inference, a layer sees activations produced by a quantized prefix. Similar to GPTAQ, it uses activation-aware reconstruction by collecting activations from (i) the full-precision model and (ii) the partially-quantized model to obtain \tilde{X}, then optimizes reconstruction under the more realistic activation distribution.

Optimization is backprop-free: instead of STE/gradient descent, it performs iterative discrete updates over integer codes (a coordinate-style local search). Updates are accepted only if they reduce the overall reconstruction loss, yielding monotonic loss decrease and finite termination (a finite number of accepted updates). The offline budget is controlled by the number of sweeps T, and the per-sweep cost is argued to be comparable to a GPTAQ pass.

A key design trade-off is deliberate: by freezing the grid and kernels, deployment complexity stays unchanged, but the search is constrained to the existing grid, so gains depend on the upstream choice of scales/grid being reasonable.1e:Ta19,方法部分分四步，逻辑很完整。

第一步是定位跨尺寸关系。作者选 Pythia 1.4B→410M 作为主分析对，二者层数和 head 数相同，只缩宽度，因此可以把问题集中在 width transfer。用相同输入序列抽取各层激活后，作者用 ridge regression 拟合大模型表示到小模型表示的线性映射，发现 held-out R^2 达到 0.84，说明表示层面高度线性可对齐。相对地，参数层面对齐很弱，最干净的 embedding 情况也只有 R^2≈0.25–0.39。这一组结果奠定全文主线：函数相近，不代表参数坐标系相近。

第二步是解释为什么稠密权重投影会坏。作者先做了一个很关键的 bit-exact control：从 donor 模型提取权重，再按 head-aware 方式重建模型，logits 完全一致，排除了“权重导出/重组装配错误”的可能。随后分析 dense linear map 的结构性问题。Transformer 里很多子结构不是任意线性基都等价：RoPE 依赖成对维度，attention 依赖 per-head 分块，GELU 是逐元素非线性，LayerNorm 依赖逐特征均值方差。稠密投影把这些坐标混在一起，等于把原本有语义的轴打散。作者还做了分模块破坏分析，指出 LayerNorm 相关结构被破坏带来的影响最大。

In contrast, parameter alignment is weak, with even the clean embedding case only around R^2 = 0.25 to 0.39. This gap drives the rest of the paper.

Second, it diagnoses why dense weight projection is destructive. A key control is bit-exact reconstruction: extracting donor weights and rebuilding the donor reproduces logits exactly, ruling out implementation or assembly artifacts. The failure is therefore in the projection mathematics. A dense linear map mixes bases that are not freely interchangeable in a Transformer. RoPE depends on paired dimensions, attention depends on headwise partitioning, GELU is elementwise, and LayerNorm depends on featurewise statistics. Once these axes are mixed, the projected model no longer respects the computation the architecture expects. The paper further decomposes the damage and finds LayerNorm-related breakage to be the single largest contributor.

Third, it tests whether the residual after the best-fit linear operator contains recoverable structure. The authors analyze residuals with spectral tests, consistency checks, patch-level learnability, and cross-pair transfer, all against shuffle controls. The result is negative: residuals are statistically indistinguishable from noise, with bootstrap comparisons giving p >= 0.99. That rules out a simple zero-shot correction layer at this granularity.

Fourth, it reframes conversion as an initialization problem and proposes Compensated Selection. The method has two independent levers. Least-squares compensation is the function lever: after structure-preserving weight selection, it applies closed-form least-squares corrections so the retained subnetwork better matches the donor’s computation. Variance-preserving rescale is the optimization lever: it restores parameter scale so the optimizer sees a healthy initialization during continued pretraining.
