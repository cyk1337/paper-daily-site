The paper reframes post-training as concept selection over data-induced evidence, not merely reward maximization under KL regularization. That matters for foundation-model research because many failures at the pretrain–posttrain interface come from signal quality rather than model scale or optimizer choice. The contribution is substantial in three ways. First, it turns interpretability from a retrospective diagnostic into a training-time data curation and signal-shaping tool. Second, it gives a concrete account of why scaling alone does not reliably fix post-training pathologies: if the reward is confounded, larger models may simply exploit the confound more effectively. Third, the framework is portable. Although instantiated on preference optimization, the same logic applies to SFT data auditing, synthetic data acceptance, reward-model dataset construction, and continual post-training where concept drift is a real issue.19:T7b0,The technical pipeline has three stages: discover concepts, validate them, and explain them away. For discovery, the authors use SAE-derived sparse features as an interpretable basis over model activations. Given a contrastive preference dataset, they compare feature activations between preferred and dispreferred responses and identify statistically significant features that separate the two groups. The point is not that SAE features are perfect semantic atoms; rather, they serve as scalable hypothesis generators for latent variables that may be carrying the preference signal. For validation, the paper treats these features as candidate concepts and examines whether they correspond to meaningful behavioral patterns—such as stylization, sycophancy, or other undesirable correlates—rather than the intended target behavior. The central conceptual move is the “explain away concepts” framework. Preferred-vs-dispreferred evidence is viewed as a mixture of latent concepts. If some concept is merely spuriously correlated with preference labels, or if optimizing it induces off-target behavior, then the training signal should be adjusted so that this concept no longer provides useful evidence for the update. The paper connects this to probabilistic-inference views of post-training: shaping learning means controlling which evidence is admissible when distinguishing preferred from dispreferred generations. The authors then instantiate this idea with concrete protocols that modify the learning signal using feature-level distinctions, and they show that several recent methods can be understood as special cases of concept removal or concept control in either representation space or input space. A notable strength is that the paper does not overclaim causality for SAE features. If time is limited, read the method figure, the concept-discovery pipeline, the main case-study figures, and the ablation tables.1c:T720,The framework has two phases. Phase 1 is Edit-SFT. From existing rollouts, the model extracts two step-level internal signals offline. The first is posterior belief over the final mark. At each reasoning-step boundary, the partial chain is appended with a final scoring scaffold, and the model’s next-token logits over valid mark tokens are read out. This gives a posterior distribution over the final integer score, from which the expected mark and its change relative to the gold mark can be computed. A step is therefore labeled as moving the model’s belief closer to or farther from the correct score.

The second signal is grounding. Using mask-based scoring, the method estimates whether a reasoning step depends on the student answer, the rubric, and prior reasoning context. This is meant to detect steps that are weakly anchored in external evidence or rules. Using these two signals together, Edit-SFT does not rewrite the whole chain of thought. Instead, it identifies the first atomic step where the reasoning either drifts away from the correct final mark or becomes weakly grounded, and revises only that local segment. A rubric checklist is supplied as privileged context to guide the edit, but the output is not forced into a rigid checklist format.

Phase 2 is Edit-RL. Rather than using plain outcome RL, the method adds belief-guided reward shaping on top of a group-relative outcome reward. In addition to rewarding closeness of the final mark to the gold score, it penalizes excessive mid-trajectory belief drift. Small fluctuations are treated as benign exploration, but large deviations away from the gold mark incur a penalty proportional to the excess drift. This encodes the task structure that grading should progressively converge rather than wander arbitrarily and only be judged at the end.1d:T46a,Proposes EAPO (Efficient Agentic Policy Optimization) with three key components: 1) Efficiency-aware rollout: for each query, the rollout set is forced to include a proportion of tool-free trajectories (all external tools disabled), providing direct evidence about the model's intrinsic reasoning capacity; 2) Difficulty-aware reward shaping: when successful tool-free trajectories exist in the rollout group, tool-using trajectories are penalized, with penalty strength inversely proportional to query difficulty—stronger penalties for easy queries (high tool-free success rate), weak or no penalties for hard queries (near-zero tool-free success rate); 3) Confidence-aware token-level reweighting: gradients are modulated by the log-probability of each generated token, giving higher weight to high-confidence tokens to stabilize policy learning. Key differences from existing methods: OTC (Wang et al. 2025) applies uniform tool-use penalties regardless of query difficulty; R1-Searcher++ similarly does not account for difficulty.
