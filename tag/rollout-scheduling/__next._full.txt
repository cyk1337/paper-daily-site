1. bulk pool 处理大多数普通请求，保持较高并发；
2. tail pool 专门处理疑似长尾请求，使用较低并发，避免超长样本把大 batch decode 拖慢。

作者还设计了一个 hierarchical controller 联合调节两个量：tail isolation size 和 replica split。内层控制器根据观测到的完成时间平衡两个池子的结束时刻；外层控制器根据 response-work history 以及实测的 concurrency-throughput model，调节边际容量分配。这里的关键不是静态规则，而是随着 workload 演化在线跟踪最合适的隔离规模和资源占比。

论文特别强调两点边界。第一，它不同于 predictor-based skewness-aware dispatch，因为它不依赖额外长度模型。第二，它不同于异步或 partial-rollout reuse 系统，因为未完成轨迹不会跨 policy update 复用；下一轮只是重新生成这些 tail candidates。因此它保持 on-policy generation，不引入 routing-induced length bias，也不需要处理 policy lag。19:T990,The method has two layers. First, the authors analyze an offline oracle problem: if all completion lengths were known in advance, how should requests be assigned to replicas to minimize rollout-step makespan? Their main result is that, in the long-tail regime, the optimum has a tail-isolation-with-balancing structure. A small number of extreme-tail requests should be isolated onto a tail replica or tail pool, while the bulk of requests goes to a separate bulk pool. The goal is not to equalize request counts, but to balance completion times across pools. They further show that a simple top-k tail-isolation rule recovers most of the oracle gain, so the useful structure is much simpler than a full combinatorial assignment.

Second, they turn this structure into an online system called TailSieve. The challenge is that response lengths are unknown before generation, and the best tail size and replica split vary over time with policy updates, workload shifts, and concurrency-dependent decoding throughput. The key empirical observation is that prompt groups that are relatively long in one rollout round tend to remain relatively long in the next. TailSieve therefore runs a partial rollout, stops at a cutoff, and treats the unfinished prompt groups as high-recall tail candidates for the next round. This gives a training-free tail signal, avoiding an auxiliary length predictor.

The runtime then uses two-pool routing:
1. a bulk pool for most requests at higher concurrency;
2. a tail pool for likely long requests at lower concurrency, so a few very long generations do not stall large decoding batches.

A hierarchical controller jointly adapts two quantities: the tail isolation size and the replica split. The inner loop balances observed pool completion times, while the outer loop adjusts marginal capacity using response-work history and a measured concurrency-throughput model. The important point is that both workload assignment and hardware allocation are adapted online rather than fixed.

The paper is careful about what it does not do. It is not a predictor-based skewness-aware dispatcher, because it does not train a separate length model. It is also not an asynchronous or partial-rollout reuse system, because unfinished trajectories are not carried across policy updates. Tail candidates are regenerated from scratch under the current policy.
