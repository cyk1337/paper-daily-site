9:T555,ZGCM-1 is a 7.39B decoder-only Transformer with 32 layers, hidden size 4096, SwiGLU intermediate size 11008, 32 query heads, 8 KV heads, GQA, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, and a 256K maximum context length. Its central architectural choice is a hybrid causal-attention backbone. Twenty-seven layers use gated sliding-window attention with a 128-token window, while five layers use global causal attention at layers 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30. This 5:1 local-to-global pattern trades full attention for much lower KV-cache and bandwidth cost, while still allowing periodic long-range information mixing. The model also uses QK normalization and partial RoPE with a rotary fraction of 0.33 to improve long-context stability.

On the training side, the recipe combines the Muon optimizer, hybrid FP8 precision, and TWEO outlier regularization. The goal is lower time-to-loss rather than only faster per-step execution. Mid-training uses a 600B-token curriculum that increases context length from 16K to 64K to 256K. Multi-turn interaction traces are reformulated as Markov decision process state-action transitions, which gives the model dense supervision at intermediate steps instead of supervising only the final answer. Post-training uses execution-verified trajectories and mixed think/no-think SFT, so the model learns both extended reasoning and efficient direct responses.a:T49e,The paper reports results on 20 benchmarks. At the systems level, the hybrid attention design reduces per-token KV-cache footprint by 6.4x and improves throughput by 3.94x at 256K context compared with standard full attention. The combination of Muon, hybrid FP8, and TWEO gives about a 4.2x pre-training time-to-loss speedup over an AdamW/BF16 baseline.

On reasoning tasks, ZGCM-1-7B is reported to lead the 7B–8B comparison group on the average of 14 reasoning benchmarks. Specific scores include 75.0% on AIME 2026, 97.1% on MATH-500, and 70.4% on HMMT 2025. On agentic search and system-agency tasks, it reaches 63.1% on WebWalkerQA, 19.4% on BrowseComp, and 62.0% on Binary Function Search. These results support the paper’s practical claim: for compact models, long-context reasoning plus supervised tool interaction can be a more effective route than relying on parametric memorization alone.

The paper also reports how AI agents were used in the model-development workflow.
