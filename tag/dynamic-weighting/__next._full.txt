18:T671,OSW-FT keeps the standard expert trajectory as the optimization target, but rescales its loss online. For each query q, the current model runs K inference-only rollouts to estimate a pass rate p-hat(q). The usual SFT loss on the expert output o* is then multiplied by (1 - p-hat(q)). Queries the model already solves reliably get downweighted; unresolved frontier queries get larger updates.

The design choice is important. The method does not optimize on sampled trajectories, does not apply token-level reward shaping, and does not alter the training distribution. The rollout is used only to estimate a scalar weight. This sharply separates it from PPO/GRPO-style methods, where the sampled trajectories both define the learning signal and receive the update.

The theoretical framing is built for binary-verifiable reasoning. The authors start from expected correctness, apply the score-function trick, and introduce a query-dependent control variate. This yields a variance-reduced estimator whose optimal baseline depends on the model’s success probability on that query. That connection motivates why scaling the expert log-likelihood gradient by a function of success rate is sensible. They further argue that with finite K, the Monte Carlo estimate remains unbiased for the exact OSW-FT surrogate update, so rollout noise affects variance but not the target update itself.

Conceptually, this is best read as online difficulty-aware SFT rather than offline RL or reward-weighted regression. The online component estimates whether an example is still informative; the offline expert trajectory still specifies what the model should learn.19:T5a9,The experiments use Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 4B parameters on binary-verifiable reasoning benchmarks including AIME, AMC, MATH-500, and GPQA-Diamond. The empirical claim is deliberately modest: OSW-FT consistently improves over standard SFT at small-to-medium scales, but it is not presented as a universal replacement for GRPO.

The results support four main points. First, adaptive online weighting beats uniform SFT across multiple model sizes, which suggests that competence-aware reweighting is useful even when the supervision target stays fixed. Second, under limited rollout budgets, OSW-FT often offers a better compute-performance trade-off because it only needs a few inference-only samples per query and avoids the full RL optimization loop. Third, the comparison with GRPO is mixed in the expected way: OSW-FT is favorable in several low-budget settings, while GRPO remains stronger in some configurations. Fourth, the ablations matter: continuous success-rate weighting outperforms hard-thresholding and random weighting, indicating that the gain comes from a smooth online estimate of current competence rather than generic sample reweighting.
