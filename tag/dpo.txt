5) Regularization: analyzes quadratic regularization variants, quantifying the trade-off between increased curvature (stability) and additional bias relative to clean unregularized DPO.1a:T6b4,The method starts with diagnosis. A behavioral checker replays successful trajectories, tracks repository state and required checks, and labels actions that go beyond what the task requires. The first such action in a successful run is treated as the onset of over-defense. By resampling from the same prefix, the paper shows that these points are silent forks: the next action can vary in proportionality while preserving success.

The paper then explains why a straightforward RL fix is weak. One can add checker-based penalties to the trajectory reward, similar in spirit to length-controlled RL. But that signal reaches a given fork only through trajectories that remain proportionate at all relevant forks. As trajectories get longer, such fully proportionate rollouts become rare. Under GRPO, which updates from within-group reward differences, many rollout groups become reward-identical, so the policy receives little or no learning signal.

The proposed solution is ForkDPO, a fork-level preference optimization scheme. Instead of inferring credit from outcome, it directly constructs preference pairs at the behaviorally meaningful fork. There are two pair types. One targets over-defense: the checker identifies the first disproportionate action in a successful trajectory, and the model is resampled from the same prefix to obtain a more proportionate alternative. The other targets under-checking: when immediate submission would skip a required check, the method also creates pairs on that side, so the model does not simply learn to check less. DPO is then applied to these fork-level pairs. The key difference from random-turn DPO is precise signal placement at the actual behavioral branching point.1b:T618,The empirical story has three parts. First, the diagnosis: on a 4B model and four frontier models, silent forks are shown to be where over-defense begins. Under implicit budgets, when models are prompted from these fork prefixes, they choose the disproportionate branch in up to 91% of decisive actions such as testing, checking, or submitting. The paper also notes that both branches usually leave the repository unchanged and that over-defensive runs succeed as often as other runs, which directly supports the claim that outcome does not distinguish behavior quality here.

Second, the failure of reward-based fixes: when the checker is folded into the trajectory reward and training is done with GRPO, 71% to 74% of rollout groups under implicit-budget settings receive identical rewards, so relative updates largely vanish. After 12 rounds, the disproportion rate does not meaningfully improve. Random-turn DPO also helps little, indicating that the issue is not merely the absence of preference data, but the location of that data.

Third, ForkDPO improves behavior on 48 test tasks from unseen repositories. For the 4B model, the implicit-budget disproportion rate drops from 70.3% to 58.9%, an 11.4-point reduction. Median runtime falls from 143 seconds to 90 seconds, roughly a 37% decrease. Random-turn DPO only reaches 67.7%.
