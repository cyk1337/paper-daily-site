第二层是监督构造。HiSentinel 的关键不是直接拿 pre-action context 做分类，而是先训练一个 privileged teacher。teacher 在训练时能看到 proposed action 以及该动作的 recorded execution result，因此它的判断带有 hindsight evidence。然后把 teacher 的干预判断蒸馏给一个 causal student sentinel，student 在部署时只能看到轨迹前缀和待执行动作。这样做的原因很明确：如果直接用人工或启发式在 pre-action 空间打标签，很多“看起来合理但会把轨迹带偏”的动作很难标；而 teacher 可以借助执行后果把 supervision 变得更接近最终任务完成。

第三层是反馈生成。作者把“是否干预”和“如何反馈”拆开优化。sentinel 先做三分类决策；若需要介入，再由单独的 feedback adapter 生成简洁、证据约束的指导语。Redirect 对应最小必要修正，Hard-Pause 对应明确指出需要的人类信息、权限或授权。这个拆分是合理的，因为决策头追求校准和低误报，生成头追求可执行性和最小干预，两者目标不同。

从问题设定上看，这篇工作和常见 process critic 的区别在于时机。process critic 往往在动作执行后根据最近几步给反馈，trajectory diagnosis 更偏事后定位 decisive error；HiSentinel 把边界前移到 action proposal 与 environment execution 之间，试图在环境状态被污染前阻断错误。和简单的 prefix anomaly detection 也不同，它不是检测“看起来奇怪的动作”，而是预测该动作是否会降低 eventual task completion，并区分 agent 自主可修复与必须人工介入两类情况。19:T94e,The method has three main pieces. First, the dataset. SWE-Intervene is built from natural software-engineering trajectories drawn from Open-SWE-Traces, SWE-Hero, and SWE-chat. Each instance preserves the task, full pre-action context, and exact proposed action, and is labeled as Allow, Redirect, or Hard-Pause. Natural AskUserQuestion interactions from SWE-chat provide Hard-Pause examples. Labels are produced by GPT-5.5 under human-written guidelines, then filtered through a two-stage human audit that rejects 25.3% of candidates. The final dataset contains 5,680 training instances and 1,243 held-out test instances, with explicit de-duplication against downstream benchmarks.

Second, hindsight-distilled supervision. A privileged teacher is trained or prompted with access to the proposed action and its recorded execution result. This gives the teacher post-action evidence when judging whether intervention would have improved the trajectory. Those judgments are then distilled into a lightweight causal sentinel that only sees the trajectory prefix and proposed action. This is the key design choice: instead of asking annotators or heuristics to guess from incomplete pre-action context, the training signal is informed by what actually happened after execution.

Third, feedback generation is optimized separately from intervention classification. The sentinel first decides whether to intervene. If intervention is needed, a dedicated feedback adapter generates concise, actionable guidance: minimal correction for Redirect, or a specific request for missing human information, authorization, or access for Hard-Pause. This separation makes sense because the classifier needs calibration and low false positives, while the generator needs usefulness and brevity.

Relative to prior work, HiSentinel differs from process critics and trajectory diagnosis in timing. Process critics usually react after execution; diagnosis methods localize decisive errors retrospectively. HiSentinel moves the decision boundary to the gap between action proposal and environment execution, aiming to prevent state corruption before recovery becomes expensive or impossible. It also differs from generic anomaly detection: the target is not whether an action looks odd, but whether executing it is likely to hurt eventual task completion, and whether the agent can self-correct afterward.1a:T683,The motivating statistics are strong. On 1,184 failed CLI-agent trajectories from Terminal-Bench, half of the decisive errors occurred by around step 7, trajectories became unrecoverable around step 12, and the first observable failure signal appeared only around step 16. Prefix monitoring detected only 3.7%–8.7% of failures before lock-in. That directly motivates pre-execution intervention.

The dataset is modest but curated: 5,680 train and 1,243 test instances, including 1,122 natural test cases and 121 AUQ-derived cases. The explicit benchmark de-duplication is important, since trajectory overlap can easily inflate apparent intervention quality.

The deployed sentinels are lightweight, at 0.6B and 1.7B parameters. Evaluation is done at two levels: offline intervention and feedback quality on in-domain and external diagnosis benchmarks, and end-to-end coding-agent performance on SWE-bench Verified mini and Ask-or-Assume. The reported metrics include task completion, unnecessary interventions, and inference overhead. In the provided full-text excerpt, the main result tables are not visible, so it would be unsafe to invent exact gains. What is clear from the setup is that the paper evaluates the right objective: not just classification accuracy, but whether the sentinel improves completion while avoiding excessive interruption and keeping latency acceptable.

The most important ablations to verify in the full paper are likely: with vs. without hindsight teacher, with vs. without AUQ-derived Hard-Pause supervision, and joint vs. decoupled decision/feedback training. Those are the places where the paper’s central claims should cash out.1b:T616,The central move is to treat interpretability as a measurement-design problem. The unknown \(x\) can be an effect vector, a local gradient map, or an interaction matrix, depending on the chosen basis and intervention family. Importantly, the framework does not force all methods to estimate the same target; instead, it provides a shared language for how measurements are obtained and how their errors should be analyzed.
