b:T5bd,The work has three technical layers. First, it introduces a claim-level audit. For each salient claim in an explanation, the authors create a minimal counterfactual edit that flips only that claim and measure the drop in reconstruction quality. If flipping a claim barely changes reconstruction, that claim was not actually grounded in the activation. They add edit-validity controls to avoid artifacts from malformed edits, and a transform control to show that the score mostly tracks gist rather than specific factual wording. Second, they build an exact-ground-truth synthetic sandbox to study training dynamics. In this setting, standard co-training of verbalizer and reconstructor consistently produces private codes: wording that is false relative to ground truth but still causally used by the reconstructor. They detect this using grounded-vs-true comparisons and evaluator swaps, which separate dependence on the paired evaluator from genuine semantic truth. Third, they propose RECAP, which shifts the intervention from the reader to the target model. Linear heads on external targets are co-trained with the model so that designated content becomes probe-decodable in the hidden state. Fresh NLAs are then trained on top of those activations.
