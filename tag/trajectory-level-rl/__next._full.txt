19:T832,方法上分三层。第一层是定义。作者把多轮 MDP 中的状态分成 critical state 和 routine state。critical turn 的动作会改变后续 return distribution；routine turn 虽然是完成任务所必需的执行步骤，但在给定状态下，不同可行动作对最终回报分布近似不变。基于这个划分，decision density ρ 被定义为轨迹中 critical turn 的比例。

第二层是估计器比较。论文比较两类 policy gradient：一类是 turn-level estimator，用每步 advantage A_t 做信用分配，对应 PPO 这一族；另一类是 trajectory-level estimator，用整条轨迹共享的标量优势，对应 GRPO 这一族。关键观察是：在 perfect critic 下，routine turn 的真实 advantage 为 0，因此 turn-level estimator 会逐点把 routine contribution 消掉；而 trajectory-level estimator 只能在期望意义上抵消 routine turn，因为它仍把同一个轨迹级回报乘到这些 turn 的 score function 上，所以会保留额外方差。

第三层是理论推导。作者用 SNR 作为训练步效率代理，分析 turn-level 与 trajectory-level 的信噪比差距。在非退化条件下，perfect critic 情况下 turn-level 相对 trajectory-level 的 SNR 优势按 Θ(ρ^{-1/2}) 增长。也就是说，critical turn 越稀疏，trajectory-level 的浪费越严重。论文还把分析扩展到 imperfect critic：critic 不是免费午餐，若 critic error variance 足够小，turn-level 仍保留优势；但当 critic 误差方差上升到与 decision density 同量级甚至更大时，这种优势会饱和甚至被吃掉。这个扩展很重要，因为它回答了“为什么不是永远上 critic”——因为 critic 的收益要和其误差、成本、稳定性一起看。

实验环境 Diluted Doors 也是技术方案的一部分。它是一个可控 MDP，能精确调节 ρ，同时保持其它因素尽量稳定，用来隔离 signal dilution 机制，而不是在复杂 agent benchmark 上混入太多 confounder。这个设计比直接在真实代码 agent 上做 ablation 更干净。1a:T727,The paper proceeds in three steps. First, it defines critical versus routine turns in a finite-horizon MDP. Critical turns are those where the chosen action changes the future return distribution. Routine turns are necessary execution steps, but alternative admissible actions leave the return distribution effectively invariant. Decision density ρ is then defined as the fraction of critical turns in a trajectory.

Second, it compares two policy-gradient families. A turn-level estimator uses per-step advantages A_t, as in PPO-style methods with a value function. Under a perfect critic, routine turns have zero true advantage and are canceled pointwise. A trajectory-level estimator, as in GRPO-style methods, attaches one scalar advantage to the entire trajectory. In that case, routine turns only cancel in expectation, not per sample, so they retain variance contributions.

Third, it derives an SNR comparison. Using signal-to-noise ratio as a proxy for training-step efficiency, the paper shows that under non-degeneracy assumptions, the SNR advantage of turn-level over trajectory-level estimation scales as Θ(ρ^{-1/2}) in the perfect-critic regime. As decision density decreases, the penalty of trajectory-level credit assignment grows. The analysis is then extended to imperfect critics: the turn-level advantage survives only while critic-error variance remains below the scale set by ρ, and saturates once critic noise becomes too large. This extension is important because it explains why adding a critic is not automatically beneficial.

The controlled environment, Diluted Doors, is also part of the technical contribution. It allows the authors to vary ρ directly while keeping other factors as stable as possible, making it easier to isolate the signal-dilution mechanism than in a full LLM-agent benchmark.1b:T5ea,The experiments are designed to validate the mechanism rather than to maximize downstream scores. The central testbed is Diluted Doors, a controlled MDP with tunable decision density.

The first key result is an SNR study. Over roughly a 50x range of ρ, the empirical SNR ratio between turn-level and trajectory-level estimators follows the predicted ρ^{-1/2} law extremely closely. The paper reports that the fit explains 99.9% of the variation, which is unusually clean evidence for a theory paper.

The second key result is a training-efficiency study. As ρ decreases, trajectory-level learning becomes increasingly inefficient, while the gap in favor of turn-level estimation widens. This supports the paper’s main claim that the cost of critic-free multi-turn RL is governed by decision density rather than horizon alone.

The paper also discusses the imperfect-critic regime. When critic-error variance is controlled, turn-level estimation retains its advantage; when critic noise grows too large, the gain saturates. If the appendix includes explicit critic-noise sweeps, that part is especially relevant for real LLM RL where value estimation is often unstable.

A limitation is that the empirical evidence is primarily from a controlled MDP, not a full-scale LLM agent training run. So the paper is strong on causal identification and weak on external validity. The next practical question is how to estimate ρ in real agent traces and whether routine versus critical turns can be detected reliably.1c:T6b2,Start with the introduction. The two terms to anchor on are signal dilution and decision density. The paper’s main claim is already visible there: the problem in multi-turn RL is not just long horizon, but sparse consequential decisions.

Then read the formal setup and theory sections carefully. Pay attention to how routine states are defined through return-distribution invariance, what exact estimators are being compared, and what assumptions are needed for the SNR scaling result. The usefulness of the conclusion depends on how restrictive those assumptions are.

Next, inspect the construction of Diluted Doors. The important question is whether the environment really varies ρ without simultaneously changing horizon, reward scale, or transition stochasticity. If that control is clean, the empirical validation is much more convincing.

In the experiments, prioritize the figure showing SNR ratio versus ρ and the figure showing training efficiency as ρ changes. One validates the theoretical slope; the other shows that the effect matters for actual learning speed, not just for a derived statistic.

Three follow-up questions are worth carrying into a second pass. First, how could ρ be estimated in real agent tasks from rollouts, tool traces, or verifier signals? Second, can the critic-error-versus-ρ comparison be turned into a practical criterion for whether a critic is worth training? Third, are there cheaper middle-ground solutions—process rewards, step-level verifiers, segment-level return decomposition—that recover some of the benefit without a full critic?
