17:T950,The core innovation is a reframing identity that rederives softmax attention as maximum-entropy Boltzmann-weighted aggregation over fixed Euclidean distances. The derivation rests on four assumptions, of which only one — "keys are hyperspherical vectors with constant norm" — has empirical weight; the other three are merely a geometric lens to standardize the metric form.
Expanding the squared Euclidean distance shows the ||q||² term cancels during softmax normalization because it is identical across all keys; the ||k||² term only cancels if all keys have equal norm. Standard dot-product attention is thus by construction a distance metric omitting the ||k||² term, making it blind to key magnitude.
When this norm-blind routing metric mismatches learned representations (where key norms do vary), the model produces three invariant compensation signatures: concentrated routing weights, collapse of routed representations, and norm stratification of routing-relevant tensors.
Validation proceeds in four steps: First, quantify the three signatures across 9 pretrained Transformers and compare them against matched null baselines (controls for random routing, norm distribution, and other confounding factors). Second, replicate validation across four non-token-level routers: graph attention on heterophilic graphs (routes over nodes), selective state-space models without explicit attention (routes over timesteps), temporal recurrent mixers, and depth-wise attention residuals (routes over layers). Third, perform causal ablations: where routing weights can be reconstructed from fixed quantities, remove the effect of the norm-blind metric to verify that routing concentration arises from the mechanism rather than incidental training dynamics. Fourth, formalize a "fixed mechanism, variable form" hypothesis: the invariant mechanism is metric mismatch inducing three compensations, while variable phenomenon form is set by positional brake strength. This hypothesis is validated by sweeping positional brake strength across two architectures. Finally, a router with enforced key normalization (satisfying the constant norm assumption) confirms norm stratification is a compensation effect rather than a root cause.
Key difference from prior work: prior studies only attribute individual phenomena to individual architectures, with no cross-router unifying mechanism.18:T4db,All three signatures in 9 pretrained Transformers (spanning multiple parameter scales and training recipes) deviate significantly from matched null baselines (statistical significance p < 0.001): routing concentration — top 1% of tokens account for 45–62% of attention weight, compared to 8–12% in null baselines; representation collapse — final-layer hidden state rank is 22–38% of first-layer rank, compared to 85–92% in null baselines; norm stratification — coefficient of variation of key norms is 0.28–0.41, compared to 0.06–0.09 in null baselines.
All four non-token-level routers exhibit identical signature patterns with comparable magnitude: for example, in selective state-space models, the top 2% of historical timesteps account for 51% of gating weight, with a 31% rank decay rate across temporal processing depth.
Positional brake sweep: doubling Transformer positional encoding strength shifts attention sink onset from layer 12 to layer 4, and increases rank decay rate by 2.1x.

Fourth, investigate whether the low-rank collapse subspace overlaps with the task semantic subspace for in-context learning.1a:T6cd,The work has three layers. First is the theoretical analysis. Starting from a standard sparse top-k MoE layer, the authors derive that for a token routed to expert i, the router weight vector associated with expert i and the expert weights processing that token receive gradients along the same input hidden-state direction, differing mainly by scalar coefficients. This is stronger than the generic statement that both modules are in the same computation graph. It means matched router–expert pairs accumulate updates from the same routed-token history and therefore evolve as coupled geometric directions. Second is empirical validation. In a 1B-scale SMoE trained from scratch, the paper compares router scores with the internal activations of experts on the same token and finds that experts ranked higher by the router also respond more strongly internally. So routing is not merely an external assignment layer; it is mirrored in expert computation. Third is a reinterpretation of the auxiliary load-balancing loss. Because this loss sends gradients to every router direction on every token, not only to selected experts, it mixes expert-specific directions and weakens the natural coupling induced by routed-token history. The paper supports this both theoretically and empirically. Motivated by that view, the authors introduce a parameter-free centroid router: each expert maintains an online K-Means or EMA centroid of the hidden states assigned to it, and routing is done by cosine similarity to these centroids, with sign-updated biases for load control. The point is not to beat learned routers outright, but to test whether a large part of routing can be explained as geometric summarization of expert-specific token regions.1b:T4b1,The experiments address three questions. First, does geometric coupling appear in practice? On a roughly 1B-parameter SMoE trained from scratch on about 50B tokens, the paper shows that experts receiving higher router scores also exhibit stronger internal activations for the same token, which supports the coupling hypothesis. Second, how does auxiliary balancing alter geometry? The authors train two otherwise matched 1B SMoEs differing only in the balancing rule and measure similarity among router directions. With the auxiliary loss, distinct router vectors become nearly three times more similar, indicating stronger directional homogenization. Load balance improves, but specialization weakens. Third, can a centroid router recover much of the learned router’s function? The answer is largely yes: the centroid router achieves the lowest load imbalance among the tested variants while keeping perplexity close to the learned router trained without the auxiliary loss, with only a modest degradation.
