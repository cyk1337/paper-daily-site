9:T493,AutoNorm replaces a fixed normalization (typically LN) with a learnable mixture/selection between LN and DyT at each Transformer layer. A lightweight NormSelector predicts mixture weights, trained end-to-end via a Gumbel–Softmax reparameterization to approximate discrete routing.

The paper’s technical core is the diagnosis of when this routing is learnable: on stationary datasets, the selector sees high-variance gradients early on while representations are still unstable, so the gating distribution does not collapse into meaningful preferences; on non-stationary language modeling/classification, sustained gating diversity persists long enough for the selector to discover useful layer-wise policies after representations become informative.

AutoNorm-S addresses this with a gate-freezing schedule (and related control of gating noise/temperature): reduce selector degrees of freedom early to let the backbone settle, then re-enable routing to allow specialization.
