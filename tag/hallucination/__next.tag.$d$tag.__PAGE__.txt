9:T467,Key results quantify how metrics change under different readouts while keeping generations fixed.
- Setup: LLaVA-1.5-7B, POPE (3 splits × 3,000 = 9,000 questions), greedy decoding; reproduction done with 4-bit NF4 quantization on an RTX 3070 Ti.
- Token audit: the two vocab IDs used by the two-token readout appear 0 times as the generated first token across all 9,000 runs; the model instead emits the space-prefixed ' Yes' (ID 3869) and ' No' (ID 1939).
- Metric impact (adversarial): among 1,644 cases where the model generated 'No', the two-token rule would flip 924 to “yes” based on excluded-logit comparison; this inflates yes-rate to 0.760 and recall to 0.9587, and drops F1 to 0.7608—about a 6.13-point shift relative to the reproduced 82.21 F1.
- Literature check: the readout-induced shift exceeds the reported adversarial gains in 5 of 6 surveyed papers, many of which do not specify their readout protocol.
- Secondary: nine inference-time correction attempts (grounding/contrastive) do not surpass the reproduced baseline under the corrected readout; detailed per-question diagnostics are in the appendix.a:T83a,The method has two stages: diagnosis and training-time credit gating.

The diagnostic tool is a fixed-rollout counterfactual test. Instead of resampling a new response under a modified image, the method teacher-forces the exact same generated response under both the original image I and an intervened image I^cf. This removes confounding from trajectory changes. Evidence-Function Sensitivity (EFS) measures how much the predictive distribution at claim tokens changes under the intervention. If the model is visually responsive, EFS should increase. But EFS alone is not enough, because changing the image can shift the likelihood of the whole response. To isolate claim-level support, the paper defines claim persistence and calibrates it using residual tokens from the same rollout, so that trajectory-wide likelihood drift is discounted. This separation between sensitivity and retractability is the conceptual core of the paper.

Based on that diagnosis, the authors propose Persistence-Aware Credit Gating (PACG). During RL training, they identify routed direct visual claims, meaning token spans that are treated as direct image-grounded assertions. For tokens with positive advantage, claims that retract sufficiently under the counterfactual keep their full positive credit, while unusually persistent claims receive attenuated positive credit. Negative-advantage tokens and non-routed tokens are left unchanged. The design is intentionally conservative: it does not punish all visual tokens, and it does not require supervised labels for claim support. It only reduces positive reinforcement where the model appears to preserve unsupported visual assertions. PACG is designed to plug into existing RLVR optimizers such as DAPO and VPPO, and it adds no inference-time cost.

Compared with prior perception rewards, token reweighting, or multimodal process reward models, the novelty here is the target of supervision. The paper is less concerned with whether the model looked at the image, and more concerned with whether the specific visual statement should survive when the evidence is removed.b:T664,The paper begins with a strong empirical observation. On four multimodal reasoning benchmarks, among 8,521 answer-correct trajectories produced by Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 2,370 trajectories, or 27.81%, contain at least one direct visual claim that is not supported by the image. This shows that the issue is not rare noise; outcome-level RL can systematically reinforce unsupported claims.

The diagnostic experiments reveal what the authors call Sensitivity–Persistence Decoupling. Under DAPO and VPPO, training increases EFS and improves overall retractability, yet unsupported claims become more persistent. GRPO also increases EFS, but without the same deterioration in unsupported-claim persistence. This is an important result because it ties the problem to the optimizer and its credit-broadcasting behavior, rather than treating it as an unavoidable property of multimodal RL.

The main method is evaluated on 9 multimodal reasoning benchmarks. PACG improves DAPO on all 9 benchmarks and further improves VPPO, yielding the best aggregate performance among matched 7B runs, with gains reported as significant across three random seeds. Beyond final-answer accuracy, PACG significantly reduces unsupported-claim persistence while preserving or improving EFS. It also improves HallusionBench, suggesting the gains are related to grounding rather than narrow benchmark fitting.
