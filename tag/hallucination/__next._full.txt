18:T7e0,The contribution is a benchmark plus a cascading evaluation protocol. The dataset is built from two expert-grounded sources.

The first source is ICLR 2023–2026 OpenReview forums. The authors run a two-stage LLM pipeline to extract novelty-overlap claims from official reviews and author rebuttals, then have three PhD-level annotators verify prior-work identity, overlap dimensions, and reviewer evidence. The released high-confidence subset contains 236 pairs. This source is anchored in real review disputes and tends to emphasize method and problem overlap.

The second source is survey co-citation grouping. Papers grouped together by survey authors as similar are used to construct positives after annotation, while negatives are created by cross-group sampling from distinct similarity groups mentioned within the same sentence. This is a better negative construction than random pairing because it keeps topical proximity while preserving dimension-specific non-overlap. It also contributes more task-level similarity cases.

Each instance is labeled independently along three dimensions: Task, Problem, and Method. These correspond to application goal, core technical challenge, and solution approach. A key design choice is that these are fixed ground-truth labels rather than post hoc interpretations of free-form model outputs. That makes error localization explicit.

The evaluation protocol is stricter than standard benchmark scoring. Models must not only predict overlap per dimension, but also provide verbatim evidence spans. Outputs then go through a three-stage cascade: per-dimension correctness, evidence hallucination check, and reasoning mismatch check. Only predictions that pass all three stages count toward Verified F1. In other words, the benchmark measures “correct answer with faithful support,” not just label coincidence. The benchmark also includes 50 multi-paper grouping sets to test whether models can organize related work more structurally than pairwise comparison alone.19:T486,The benchmark is evaluated on 18 LLMs. The main empirical message is not that one model reaches a particularly high score, but that failure modes are systematic: even when the final overlap judgment is correct, models often support it with fabricated evidence or with evidence that is only weakly connected to the stated conclusion. These failures vary substantially across Task, Problem, and Method dimensions. The paper also notes that the two data sources induce different distributions—ICLR-derived pairs skew toward method/problem overlap, while survey-derived pairs skew toward task similarity—which helps expose dimension-specific weaknesses rather than hiding them under a single distribution.

In terms of scale, the benchmark contains 619 paper pairs and 50 multi-paper sets. The ICLR source starts from 42,682 OpenReview forums, indicating substantial upstream mining before strong human filtering at release time. For readers, the most important quantitative pattern is the gap between ordinary correctness metrics and Verified F1. That gap shows that getting the label right and grounding it faithfully are materially different capabilities.1a:T40b,HEAL first applies causal noise intervention to multi-head attention outputs. Instead of inferring head importance from attention weights or output projection weights, it perturbs head outputs directly and measures the resulting change in the layer representation. This is a better intervention point because a head with small projection weights can still matter if its activation is large, and a head with large weights can be inactive in a given context. After filtering causally redundant heads, the method uses counterfactual Difference-in-Differences to disentangle visual and language information at the head level. The remaining heads are categorized as redundant, visual, language, or synergy heads. The central observation is that hallucination is associated with distributional drift inside synergy heads, not simply with weak visual heads or strong language heads. HEAL then introduces a dynamic calibration rule with an equilibrium factor α to adjust the visual-language information balance during autoregressive decoding.1b:Ta85,方法上，这篇工作核心不是提出新模型，而是搭了一套面向 dLVLM 的系统评测与机制分析框架。评测对象包括 6 个 diffusion LVLM：LLaDA-V、LaViDa-LLaDA、LaViDa-Dream、MMaDA-MixCoT、Dream-VL、Dimple，并与 7 个有竞争力的 AR LVLM 做对比。四类评测分别对应不同失效模式。

在 object hallucination 上，作者用 POPE，在 Random、Popular、Adversarial 三种采样设置下报告 Accuracy、Precision、Recall、F1 和 Yes%。这里 Yes% 很关键，因为它直接暴露回答倾向。结果显示 AR 模型常见的 affirmative bias 在 dLVLM 上被明显改写，很多 diffusion 模型更偏向否定回答，形成一种“yes-bias reversal”。这不是简单的阈值问题，因为它在多个模型家族上重复出现。

在 open-ended hallucination 上，作者用 CHAIR 评估自由 caption，并把 hallucination rate 与语言质量拆开分析。结论不是“dLVLM 更会幻觉”，而是“它们的 hallucination rate 可以接近 AR，但语言质量更差”，说明 diffusion 生成中的迭代去噪和并行提交机制，可能在 grounding 与表面语言组织之间引入了新的张力。

在 demographic bias 上，作者用 FairFace 做 gender / race 识别，并在两种 padding 条件下测试。这里的发现比较尖锐：对 underrepresented racial groups，部分 dLVLM 准确率接近塌缩到零；同时不同 diffusion 家族出现相反方向的 gender bias，而不是统一偏向某一类。这说明偏置不只是数据分布问题，也和具体 backbone、视觉接入方式、后训练策略有关。

在 selection bias 上，作者构造了长度受控的视觉多选题，专门测选项长度是否成为混杂线索。结果显示 dLVLM 的长度偏置显著强于 AR，而且作者进一步把这种偏置追到 first denoising step：模型在最早一轮去噪时就对较长选项表现出系统性偏好，后续步骤更多是在强化而不是纠正这个早期决定。

机制分析部分是全文最有信息量的地方。作者利用 diffusion 推断天然可见的两个过程信号：per-token confidence trajectory，以及 token 的 commit step。commit step 指 token 第一次被赋予最终非 mask 值的时刻。作者发现 hallucinated content 往往伴随特定的置信度轨迹和提交模式，这意味着 dLVLM 比 AR 多了一类可观测的内部诊断信号。AR 模型通常只能看最终 token probability，而 diffusion 模型可以看“这个 token 在第几步被定下来、之后是否稳定、置信度如何演化”，这为后续做 hallucination detection、early stopping、selective regeneration 提供了接口。1c:T9ac,This is an evaluation and analysis paper rather than a new model paper, but the setup is carefully designed for diffusion LVLMs. The authors benchmark six diffusion models—LLaDA-V, LaViDa-LLaDA, LaViDa-Dream, MMaDA-MixCoT, Dream-VL, and Dimple—against seven competitive autoregressive LVLM baselines.

For object hallucination, they use POPE under Random, Popular, and Adversarial settings, reporting Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, and Yes%. Yes% is central because it exposes response tendency directly. A consistent pattern is that many diffusion models reverse the familiar AR yes-bias in binary visual questions, often becoming overly negative instead of overly affirmative.

For open-ended hallucination, they use CHAIR on free-form captioning and explicitly separate hallucination rate from linguistic quality. The key result is not that diffusion models hallucinate more across the board, but that they can achieve competitive hallucination rates while producing weaker language. This suggests that iterative denoising and parallel token commitment alter the trade-off between grounding and surface fluency.

For demographic bias, they evaluate gender and race recognition on FairFace under two padding conditions. The striking observation is near-collapse on underrepresented racial groups for some diffusion models, together with opposite-polarity gender bias across model families. That points to an interaction between the diffusion paradigm and implementation choices such as the base LM, vision integration, and post-training recipe.

The mechanistic part is the most informative section. Diffusion inference exposes process signals that AR models do not naturally provide: per-token confidence trajectories and token commit steps. A commit step is the denoising step at which a token first receives its final non-mask value. The paper shows that hallucinated content correlates with characteristic confidence and commitment patterns. That creates a practical interface for future work on hallucination detection, selective regeneration, and inference-time calibration in diffusion LMs.1d:T6b8,The experimental contribution is more about stable patterns than about one headline number. The study covers 6 diffusion LVLMs, 7 AR baselines, and four evaluation suites: POPE, CHAIR, FairFace, and length-controlled MCQA.

The first major result is the reversal of yes-bias on POPE. AR LVLMs often over-answer “yes” under uncertainty, while many diffusion models lean toward “no.” This changes the precision-recall balance: some diffusion models maintain reasonable precision but lose recall because they become too conservative.

The second result is on CHAIR. Diffusion models can be competitive with strong AR baselines in hallucination rate, yet their linguistic quality is noticeably worse. The paper argues that hallucination and language quality are separable failure dimensions, which is an important evaluation lesson.

The third result comes from FairFace. Some diffusion models show near-zero accuracy on underrepresented racial groups, and gender bias is not even consistent in direction across model families. This indicates that reliability behavior is highly implementation-dependent rather than a single generic property of diffusion.

The fourth result is the strong length bias in visual MCQA. Diffusion models are much more sensitive to answer-option length than AR baselines, and the analysis traces this effect to the first denoising step. That early-step commitment is one of the clearest mechanistic findings in the paper.

The paper also includes analyses under different padding conditions and studies the correlation between confidence trajectories and hallucinated tokens. The exact numbers matter less than the fact that these patterns recur across multiple model families and tasks.1e:T816,The method has two stages. First is AHAF, Adversarial Hallucination Attribute Flipping. Instead of using adversarial perturbations for standard classification attacks, the paper uses PGD to flip the model’s object-existence judgment on the same image, for example from present to absent or vice versa. This produces paired images that are semantically almost identical, with differences concentrated around the visual cues that move the model across a hallucination boundary. That is the key distinction from ordinary contrastive fine-tuning with random negatives: random negatives change too many factors at once, such as scene, object identity, and background, so the model can still solve the task through coarse spatial shortcuts.

Second is ACFT, Adversarial Contrastive Fine-Tuning. The model is fine-tuned with a contrastive objective built on AHAF-generated pairs, pulling correct image-text pairs together and pushing apart representations associated with hallucination-inducing perturbations. The important design choice is not merely the use of contrastive loss, but the controlled negative construction. Positive and negative samples share nearly all image content; the only systematic difference is the perturbation that flips the hallucination attribute. That makes the learning signal much more focused on the visual decision boundary.

AHAF also serves as a probe. If tiny pixel-level perturbations can flip the answer, then the visual representation is already sitting close to a fragile decision boundary even on clean inputs. That observation is more general than the specific method: many hallucination mitigation techniques may improve outputs without actually moving the underlying visual representation into a more stable region.

Compared with prior methods, VCD, OPERA, and VTI are more inference-time or post-hoc control methods; RLHF and DPO variants mainly reshape output preference; ordinary OCFT lacks targeted negatives. ACFT differs by directly training against the diagnosed visual misalignment mechanism, with no extra inference cost.1f:T7ee,The most informative evidence comes from three groups of experiments.

First, diagnostic evidence. On LLaVA v1.5, hallucinated samples show much worse image-text embedding alignment than correct samples: average cosine similarity is 0.158 for correct cases versus -0.122 for hallucinated ones. Grad-CAM analysis shows an inverted attention pattern. When the target object is present, hallucinated cases have more dispersed attention; when the object is absent, they show overly concentrated attention on irrelevant regions. Using normalized Shannon entropy over 500 hallucinated and 500 non-hallucinated samples, entropy is 5.1% higher for hallucinated cases when the object is present, and 6.2% lower when the object is absent. This supports a visual-perception failure rather than pure language-side fabrication.
