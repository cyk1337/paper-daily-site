18:T4f3,SCAS (Student-Centric Answer Sampling) operates in a candidate-answer setting: for each question q, there is a set of verified-correct teacher answers A(q). Instead of picking the strongest teacher, SCAS samples answers according to an estimated student-centric learning cost.
The cost is motivated by gradient mechanics in SFT:
- Consider the squared Frobenius norm of the gradient on a student weight matrix W induced by the concatenated sequence x=[q;a].
- Expand the gradient norm into pairwise token interaction terms, then partition token indices into question (Q) and answer (A) positions, yielding AA (answer–answer), AQ/QA (answer–conditioning interactions), and QQ (conditioning–conditioning) blocks.
- This decomposition explains why two correct answers can differ in how much parameter change they demand from the current student.
- To avoid per-candidate backprop, the paper derives a forward-only proxy to approximate the learning cost using signals available from forward passes (representations and error-related quantities), enabling efficient scoring over large candidate pools.
