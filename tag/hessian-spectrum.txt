17:T965,技术路线很扎实，核心在于把“局部近似是否有效”做成可直接检验的实验。模型是 150M 参数 Transformer，在 FineWeb 上训练 3B tokens，并每 10% 训练进度保存 checkpoint。围绕每个 checkpoint，作者构造两类代理目标。prox 是对网络输出做一阶线性化，但保留交叉熵，因此等价于 NTK 参数化下的多类 logistic regression；quad 则进一步把这个目标在 checkpoint 处做二阶展开，得到凸二次目标。然后从同一个 checkpoint 出发，在相同数据流和训练设置下继续训练这些代理模型，并与原始非线性模型的真实轨迹比较。这里关键不是只比较 loss 数值，而是比较在一个非平凡时间窗口内，代理模型是否能复现真实训练的下降趋势和超参敏感性。

在谱分析部分，作者估计 Hessian 和 Gauss-Newton 矩阵的谱，并分别考察原始参数空间和 Adam 预条件后的谱。方法上使用很深的 Lanczos quadrature probe，并给出误差带，分辨率覆盖约六个数量级。这一点很重要，因为很多过去的谱结论只看头部几个特征值，难以判断尾部是否真有稳定幂律。作者发现谱存在清晰的 head-tail 分裂：head 的大小与 vocab size 对齐，且主要来自 unembedding；tail 呈 power law，并且在不同 batch size 下几乎重合，Adam 预条件也不改变其形状，只改变某些尺度关系。基于这些谱，作者进一步估计 source exponent β 和 capacity exponent α，在可分辨尾部中得到 α≈1，而 β<1，不满足一些文献常用的可求和假设。这意味着若用经典 inverse-problem 或 kernel-regression 风格的谱假设来分析 LLM 训练，很多前提并不成立。

First, it shows that local Taylor proxies are not merely infinitesimal approximations: in a 150M-parameter transformer trained on 3B tokens, they can remain predictive over windows as long as roughly 10% of total training, with noticeably better agreement later in training. That gives empirical support to using quadratic analyses for practical pretraining questions. Second, the spectral measurements are unusually informative: the spectrum separates into a head whose size is set by vocabulary and dominated by the unembedding layer, and a power-law tail that is nearly invariant across batch sizes and largely unaffected by Adam preconditioning. Third, the stability analysis sharpens the edge-of-stability story: small and medium batches operate in a noise-dominated stochastic edge regime, while large batches move toward the deterministic edge predicted by linear theory. The broader implication is that LLM training is typically run close to stability limits regardless of batch size, but the mechanism changes with noise scale.19:T9e0,The methodology is strong because the paper turns a vague theoretical claim into a falsifiable training experiment. The base model is a 150M transformer trained on 3B FineWeb tokens, with checkpoints saved every 10% of training. Around each checkpoint, the authors build two local proxies. The first, prox, linearizes the network outputs while keeping the cross-entropy objective; this is equivalent to multiclass logistic regression in the NTK parameterization. The second, quad, further takes a second-order Taylor expansion of that objective, yielding a convex quadratic problem. Starting from the same checkpoint, they continue training these proxy systems and compare them against the original nonlinear trajectory under matched conditions. The key point is not just local loss fitting at a single step, but whether the proxies reproduce meaningful optimization behavior over a sizable future window.

For geometry, the paper estimates Hessian and Gauss-Newton spectra along training, both before and after Adam-style preconditioning. The estimation uses very deep Lanczos quadrature probes with error bands, resolving the spectrum over roughly six orders of magnitude. This is a major practical detail: many prior empirical studies only resolve the top few eigenvalues, which is insufficient for claims about tail behavior. The resulting picture is a clear head-tail split. The head is tied to vocabulary size and is dominated by the unembedding layer. The tail follows a power law and appears universal in the sense that it is nearly identical across batch sizes and not materially altered by Adam preconditioning. Using these curves, the authors estimate a capacity exponent around alpha ≈ 1 in the resolvable tail and a source exponent beta < 1, contradicting summability assumptions that appear in parts of the optimization and inverse-problems literature.

For stability, the paper combines theory with direct blow-up tests. From multiple checkpoints, it runs preconditioned SGD over a grid of learning-rate and batch-size multipliers, measuring which settings diverge and comparing the observed boundary to linear stability predictions. This is a good design choice because it tests the practical content of edge-of-stability theory rather than treating it as a metaphor. The results separate two regimes: stochastic edge of stability at small to medium batch sizes, where gradient noise is essential, and deterministic edge of stability at large batch sizes, where classical linear analysis becomes more accurate.1a:T7ee,The main experiments use a 150M-parameter transformer trained on 3B FineWeb tokens. Checkpoints are saved every 10% of training, and around each checkpoint the authors construct the prox and quad local surrogates. The central empirical result is that both surrogates can track the real optimization trajectory over windows up to about 10% of total training, with substantially better agreement later in training than early on. The paper does not claim that a single quadratic model explains the entire run; the claim is more precise and more useful: over a practically meaningful local horizon, the approximation is predictive enough to support analysis.

The spectral experiments estimate Hessian and Gauss-Newton spectra with deep Lanczos probes over six orders of magnitude. Three observations stand out. First, the spectral head has a size linked to vocabulary and is dominated by the unembedding layer, indicating that the output layer contributes a distinct curvature structure. Second, the tail follows a stable power law and is almost unchanged across batch sizes. Third, Adam preconditioning does not remove this tail structure, suggesting that preconditioning does not whiten the problem into something close to isotropic geometry. From the measured spectra, the paper estimates a capacity exponent alpha ≈ 1 and a source exponent beta < 1 in the resolvable tail, which runs against common summability assumptions.

The stability experiments scan learning-rate and batch-size multipliers to map divergence boundaries. The outcome is that training spends most of its time near the edge of stability. At small and medium batch sizes, the regime is noise-dominated and better described as a stochastic edge of stability. At large batch sizes, the observed boundary aligns more closely with deterministic linear stability theory.
