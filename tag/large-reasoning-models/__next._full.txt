18:T554,The method has two parts. First, difficulty probing: for each reasoning step, the authors extract a step-level embedding and fit a linear regressor on a small seen dataset to map that embedding to step-level difficulty. The important distinction is that this is not initial problem difficulty; it is local difficulty as the reasoning trajectory unfolds. The authors argue that this signal is already sufficiently linear in representation space, so a simple probe is enough. Second, inference-time control: instead of hard stopping generation or training a termination head, DyCon dynamically adjusts the logits of reflection-related keywords. If estimated difficulty is low, it downweights tokens associated with further reflection, encouraging convergence. If estimated difficulty is high, it upweights those tokens, encouraging deeper exploration. This is a soft intervention rather than a hard truncation. Compared with verifier-based methods, it avoids an extra model in the loop. Compared with entropy or certainty heuristics, it does not rely on manually chosen generic thresholds. Compared with SFT/RL stopping policies, it avoids specialized supervision and the associated data dependence. The real claim is not that a linear regressor is powerful, but that evolving difficulty is already encoded in step embeddings strongly enough to drive useful control.19:T573,The evaluation spans four reasoning models from 4B to 32B and 12 benchmarks covering math reasoning, general QA, and coding. The headline result is that DyCon reduces redundant reasoning without hurting accuracy, and generalizes across models and tasks. The most important evidence is likely in three buckets. First, representation analysis: Figure 2 reportedly shows both the temporal evolution of difficulty and an approximately linear relationship between step embeddings and ground-truth step difficulty. That is the empirical foundation of the method. Second, the efficiency–performance trade-off: DyCon should reduce reasoning tokens or steps relative to standard CoT while keeping pass@1 or accuracy close; gains on easier tasks would suggest that less overthinking also reduces derailment. Third, transfer and robustness: the regressor is fit on a small seen set but is claimed to transfer to unseen benchmarks and different model scales. If that holds numerically, it suggests the probe is reading a fairly general internal signal rather than a dataset-specific artifact. The provided full-text excerpt does not include the detailed result tables, so I would not state exact deltas without checking the paper directly.
