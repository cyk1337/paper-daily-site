19:T60c,The method, Implicit Compression Regularization (ICR), extracts compression targets from the model’s own successful rollouts. Instead of adding a handcrafted reward for shorter outputs, it identifies the shortest correct responses within each rollout group and uses them to induce a virtual shorter distribution. The current policy is then regularized toward these concise-but-correct trajectories.

The paper first analyzes the dynamics of explicit length penalties under different coefficients. Stronger penalties reduce length faster, but they also degrade accuracy earlier and worsen the accuracy-length Pareto frontier. More importantly, the group-wise length-correctness correlation starts negative and gradually increases during training. A negative correlation means shorter responses are, on average, more likely to be correct within the current rollout distribution; a positive correlation indicates compression is now cutting into necessary reasoning. ICR is designed to exploit the former regime.

Mechanistically, this is closer to policy regularization than reward shaping. The compression signal is not “be shorter”; it is “move probability mass toward shorter trajectories that are already verified as correct under the current policy.” That distinction matters. It reduces the shortcut where the model simply writes less rather than reasoning more compactly. The paper also argues that ICR is compatible with mild length penalties when stronger compression is needed, with ICR acting as a guardrail against underthinking.1a:T498,From the provided full text, the experiments span three reasoning backbones and multiple math and knowledge-intensive benchmarks. The main empirical claim is consistent: ICR reduces response length while preserving or even improving accuracy, and yields a better accuracy-length Pareto frontier than explicit compression baselines. The training-dynamics plots are central: compared with pure length penalties, ICR maintains a healthier length-accuracy correlation for longer, meaning concise responses remain aligned with correctness instead of quickly drifting into underthinking.

The available text does not include the full result tables, exact model sizes, rollout group sizes, training budgets, or per-benchmark gains, so those numbers should not be fabricated. What is clearly supported is the experimental structure: (1) dynamic comparisons across different length coefficients; (2) Pareto comparisons between ICR and explicit length-penalty or truncation-style methods; (3) generalization across multiple backbones; (4) compatibility with mild length penalties for stronger compression; and (5) correlation trajectories as a diagnostic for the onset of underthinking.1b:T4f1,Pipeline:

The paper argues reasoning tokens split into (a) structural tokens—recurring multi-token phrases whose continuation becomes near-deterministic once started (low entropy), and (b) organic tokens—problem-specific content (math expressions, bindings, intermediate results) with higher entropy. They derive a compression ceiling showing that if the mean entropy inside merge spans is much smaller than log|V|, the achievable compression is approximately the fraction of tokens covered by merges.

In outcome-reward GRPO, a verifier-failed rollout receives a negative group-relative advantage across the whole sequence, even when many intermediate reasoning steps are valid. Step-level Advantage Selection treats reasoning as a sequence of steps and selectively zeros out advantages. In correct rollouts, low-confidence steps receive zero advantage so they are not reinforced. In verifier-failed rollouts, high-confidence intermediate steps receive zero advantage so they are protected from negative updates that may be caused by truncation or verifier errors.
