The key result is an exact decomposition of the local weight-activation quantization error into an activation-guided weight compensation term and an orthogonal residual. The first term is the part that weight optimization can reduce along directions supported by the quantized activations. The second term is orthogonal to that compensatable subspace; once P is fixed, it cannot be removed by better weight compensation. This separates two roles that are often mixed together: weight optimization fits the output within a chosen coordinate system, while transformations change the residual structure before quantization.

The paper then analyzes the orthogonal residual to explain common design choices. Random orthogonal rotations reduce the impact of persistent channel-wise outliers on per-token quantization scales.
