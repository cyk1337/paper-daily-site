9:T804,The work has three main components. First, it formalizes Online Video Question Answering under Interruption. A user asks an initial question at time tq, the model starts autoregressive generation, and an interruption arrives at time ti. The observable context is the partial video V≤ti, the original question Q0, the partial answer A≤ti, and the interruption signal I. The model must jointly perform interruption-type recognition and response generation. The interruption taxonomy has three classes—Cancellation, False Trigger, and Correction—defined by whether the interruption changes the semantic context and whether it requires a change in generation strategy. That is a more meaningful decomposition than a simple stop/continue label.

Second, the paper builds OVIBench and an offline simulation protocol. The key practical challenge is reproducibility: true online interruption during generation is hard to benchmark at scale. The proposed protocol freezes a unified temporal state—what video has been seen, what has been generated, and when the interruption occurs—so models can be evaluated in a controlled and repeatable way. The benchmark supports both open-ended and multiple-choice settings. Open-ended evaluation is closer to real interaction, while multiple-choice reduces evaluation noise and makes model comparison more stable.

Third, the paper introduces a metric suite and a training set. Metrics cover interruption understanding and response generation, including interruption-intent recognition, post-interruption intent fulfillment, fluency and consistency before and after interruption, and alignment with video evidence. To test whether the benchmark can support improvement rather than only diagnosis, the authors construct OVI-Train, a multiple-choice training set for interruption-aware fine-tuning. The design choice is pragmatic: use a structured format to teach the model the chain from interruption recognition to response strategy, instead of relying only on unconstrained generation to absorb this behavior.a:T518,The main empirical result is straightforward: mainstream MLLMs that perform well on standard offline VideoQA degrade substantially under interruption. The weakest case is Correction, where the model must revise or override an ongoing answer rather than simply continue or stop. That pattern suggests the failure is not only in visual grounding, but in updating intent and rewriting generation policy mid-trajectory.

The most informative quantitative result comes from targeted fine-tuning. Starting from Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the interruption-aware fine-tuned 7B model improves multiple-choice accuracy by 16.88% and interruption-type accuracy by 13.94%, and even surpasses the 72B model in the same series. That is a strong indication that interruption handling is highly data- and objective-sensitive. Larger scale alone does not reliably induce the behavior if the model never sees the right supervision.

The paper also contrasts open-ended and multiple-choice evaluation. Open-ended testing is more realistic but noisier; multiple-choice is cleaner for controlled comparison and for closing the train-eval loop.
