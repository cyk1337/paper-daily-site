9:T437,QLPO is a resampling variant of GRPO. For each prompt, it first over-generates K candidate responses from the old policy, then constructs a training group of size M. Candidates are split by correctness, and the final group approximately preserves the empirical correct/incorrect ratio: N+(x)=round(M·N~+(x)/K), with N−(x)=M−N+(x). Within each correctness class, responses are divided by relative length, producing four quadrants. QLPO favors short correct responses and long incorrect responses. The rationale follows from GRPO gradients: correct responses usually receive positive advantages and are reinforced, so selecting short correct trajectories encourages concise successful reasoning; incorrect responses usually receive negative advantages, so selecting long incorrect trajectories suppresses verbose failure modes. The reward function, advantage estimator, and downstream GRPO update remain unchanged. Compared with shortest-only filtering, QLPO keeps the negative-gradient contribution of wrong samples and avoids distorting the group’s correctness statistics.a:T593,EvoThink has two stages. Stage one is Self-Pruning Training (SPT). The model first generates a full reasoning trace inside <think> tags plus a final answer. The method then identifies redundant self-verification patterns inside the trace, such as repeated checking of already established conclusions or repeated solution attempts that do not add new information. These parts are pruned, and the model is self-trained on the resulting concise trajectories. This process is iterative: a model at iteration i generates traces, those traces are pruned, and the pruned data is used to train the next model. The key point is that SPT is not simple length truncation; it aims at fine-grained removal of redundant reasoning segments. Stage two is Aha-Moment Preference Optimization (AMPO). Here the authors collect incorrect reasoning trajectories and treat them as a population of failures. A fitness function is used to select high-potential failures, with emphasis on diversity and exploratory value. From these, they construct aha-moment preference data that explicitly captures a transition from a wrong path to a correct resolution. DPO is then used to train the model to prefer such trajectories. Compared with prior work, the novelty is the combination of trajectory-level redundancy pruning and preference learning from curated wrong-to-right transitions, rather than only compressing outputs or training on correct solutions.b:T4e2,The evaluation covers mathematical reasoning and code generation. One motivating statistic from the paper is that more than 65% of tokens in baseline reasoning traces are spent on redundant computation or repeated verification. The main empirical claim is that EvoThink improves both efficiency and capability. SPT alone shortens reasoning traces substantially while largely preserving, and sometimes slightly improving, task performance. Adding AMPO further improves accuracy, suggesting that the wrong-to-right preference signal helps recover or exceed any capability lost during pruning. The paper also compares AMPO against optimization using only correct solutions and reports that aha-moment data is more effective, presumably because it better reflects the exploratory process of discovering a new reasoning path. The most important ablations are likely the number of SPT iterations, the pruning strategy, the fitness function for failure selection, and the effect of removing AMPO. Given two checkpoints with identical pretraining and instruction tuning, differing only by a reasoning-distillation stage—an instruct model M and a reasoning model R—define a reasoning task vector τ_reason = θ_R − θ_M. Construct an “overthinking” model by extrapolating past R:

θ_Oα = θ_M + α(θ_R − θ_M), with α=0 recovering M, α=1 recovering R, and α>1 amplifying the reasoning direction.

Hypothesis: amplifying the reasoning direction increases the probability of explicit deliberation and self-reflection, destabilizing learned suppression so that audit-relevant information is more likely to be verbalized.

A practical issue is coherence degradation at large α. The paper evaluates layer-wise coefficient schemes to attenuate perturbations in sensitive regions: uniform scaling, freezing subsets of layers, linear decay across depth, and Fisher-weighted scaling (down-weighting parameters/layers deemed loss-sensitive). The consistent takeaway is that reducing perturbation in sensitive regions yields better coherence–elicitation trade-offs.

They also test composability with input-side “prefill attacks” (seeding the assistant response with a leading prefix to bypass refusal). Since prefill acts on inputs and overthinking acts on weights, they can be stacked; synergy depends on prefix style, with metacognitive/self-reflective prefixes benefiting more than direct “leak the secret” prefixes.d:T467,They evaluate on four “model organism” secret settings:
1) in-context secrets: hidden metadata in the prompt influences answers but is not verbalized in CoT;
2) learned secret words: models give clues about a target word while never saying it;
3) learned implicit beliefs: models are trained to hold a belief (e.g., user gender) but adversarially trained to deny it;
4) learned secret behaviors: models execute encoded instructions without acknowledging them.

Experiments span 2B–32B models, and the paper reports that the elicitation gains from overthinking improve with scale. They also show partial complementarity with prefill attacks, achieving the highest audit success rates for some secret types when combined.

On stability, they demonstrate that naive high-α extrapolation harms coherence, motivating the layer-wise attenuation study; Fisher-weighted / sensitivity-aware scaling tends to dominate simpler schemes.

1) Make “answer emergence” observable: for each sampled trajectory, parse intermediate outputs to extract an answer candidate and use an external verifier to find the earliest position where a correct answer can be verified. This splits tokens into a necessary prefix vs. post-answer continuation.
2) Edit successful overthinking rollouts: when a successful rollout contains substantial continuation after emergence, construct an edited counterpart that preserves a conservative verified prefix up to a safe boundary around the emergence point, and replaces/truncates the remaining overthinking segment while keeping the correct answer.
3) Rewire the GRPO preference within the same group: place the original and edited trajectories in the same GRPO group and prefer the shorter edited one, so the positive learning signal is less coupled with post-answer verbosity.
Two training stabilizers are introduced:
- Prefix masking: avoid directly penalizing the shared verified prefix, preventing degradation of the reasoning needed to reach the answer.
- GClip: provide a controlled learning signal around the edit boundary to mitigate instability caused by hard edits near the cut point.
Key difference from alternatives: it is not a global length penalty (which can suppress useful reasoning), and not a post-hoc stopping controller (which does not change what RL reinforces).
