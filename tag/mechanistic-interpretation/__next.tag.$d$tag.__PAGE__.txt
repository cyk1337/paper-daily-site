9:T488,The core contribution is the mechanistic interpretation of "Muon as an implicit residual connection", which complements existing mathematical perspectives: the core function of residual connections is to provide downstream layers with both preserved original input and transformed representations via explicit orthogonal (identity mapping is a special case of orthogonal matrices) skip paths; the core operation of Muon is to replace the weight update ΔW with its nearest orthogonal matrix Orth(ΔW), which is equivalent to implicitly injecting orthogonal transformation updates into the optimization trajectory — the two follow the same logic, with the only difference being that residuals are explicit architectural operations while Muon’s effect is an implicit optimization-level operation. The work further analyzes the tradeoff in controlled linear optimization settings: orthogonal updates sacrifice a small amount of local target fitting speed (since updates do not strictly follow the gradient direction) but better preserve the structural properties of representations, improving the efficiency of downstream layer utilization of representations.a:T6fc,The technical contribution is an attention decomposition framework for augmented in-context learning. The prompt is written as x=[I;D;K;q], where I is instruction, D demonstrations, K injected knowledge, and q the query. Attention from query tokens to K is decomposed into a semantic component and a structural component. The structural term is written as λ·σ(K), where σ(K) measures structural intensity and λ is a model-intrinsic sensitivity coefficient that can vary by layer and head.

σ(K) is defined from observable prompt statistics: density of structural delimiters, frequency of relation/slot markers, and repetitiveness of local token patterns. The point is not to claim an exact generative theory of attention, but to provide a testable abstraction: even when semantic content is held fixed, more structured formatting should capture more attention.

The empirical design is built around format-content decoupling. The authors compare semantically equivalent content rendered in different forms, including natural-language passages, relevant triples, irrelevant triples, and noise triples. They also derive a compression-style bound showing that, under normalized attention, increased structural intensity in K must reduce the attention available to D.

The mitigation section proposes five structure-aware strategies, all aimed at reducing λ·σ(K). Two are tested empirically. S3, format flattening, rewrites triples into weaker or more natural language-like forms to reduce delimiter and slot salience. S1, structural dispersal, breaks up highly regular local patterns to reduce repetitive structural cues. Other ideas, such as logit suppression and training-time regularization, are presented more as mathematically motivated directions than fully validated methods.b:T6ab,The experiments use two model families, Mistral-7B and LLaMA-3-8B, across three QA tasks. Four results matter most.

First, KG triples capture substantially more attention than neutral natural-language text. The paper reports representative structural intensity estimates around 0.70 for KG-formatted content versus about 0.25 for neutral text, and triples absorb roughly 2x to 3x more attention per token.

Second, this attention capture is largely insensitive to semantic relevance. Relevant triples, irrelevant triples, and even noise triples show very similar attention patterns. That is the central empirical support for the paper’s claim: the tax is driven by format before usefulness is even considered.

Third, demonstration attention is compressed by as much as 42% in some settings. So the effect is not a small local preference; it changes the competitive allocation of context budget inside the prompt and can alter ICL behavior.

Fourth, source alignment dominates gating sophistication. On HotpotQA, task-matched BM25 retrieval outperforms mismatched ConceptNet retrieval by more than 30 percentage points, while more elaborate gating contributes no more than about 2 points. The intended message is that choosing the right knowledge source matters far more than adding control logic on top of a poorly matched source.

For mitigation, S3 format flattening is the strongest result: it improves accuracy and reduces attention concentration on structured regions. S1 structural dispersal is mixed and model-dependent, suggesting that reducing structural regularity does not automatically translate into better task performance, possibly because it also harms readability or local parseability.c:T4bf,For a skim, focus on three parts.
