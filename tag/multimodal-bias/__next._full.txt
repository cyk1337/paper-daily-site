18:T65b,Melange is built as a visual extension of BBQ, but the construction pipeline is more careful than simply generating images from prompts. First, the authors select seven categories with visually representable attributes: age, gender, appearance, socioeconomic status, race, religion, and disability. They exclude categories such as sexual orientation and nationality because these often lack reliable visual cues. Second, they manually filter BBQ contexts to keep only scenarios where the relevant demographic distinction can be meaningfully depicted in images.

Third, they generate images with diffusion models and apply two filtering stages. An automatic VQA-based filter checks prompt-image alignment, and a human validation stage removes artifacts, semantic mismatches, and cases where image generation may have introduced confounds. The paper explicitly tries to reduce the risk that the benchmark merely reflects the text-to-image model’s own bias. Fourth, for each unique prompt, they curate ten visually diverse but semantically equivalent images, which makes the benchmark less sensitive to a single image realization.

The evaluation protocol is one of the stronger parts of the paper. It uses three matched settings—text-only, image-only, and image-with-context—to isolate modality effects. It also includes a perception control to test whether models can correctly identify the depicted demographic attributes. Finally, the paper introduces polarity-aware stereotype alignment, arguing that earlier analyses can miss important structure if they ignore question polarity when measuring stereotypical responses.19:T469,The benchmark contains 12,640 bias scenarios across seven categories and 126,400 released evaluation images, with ten images per scenario. The evaluation covers 23 open-source MLLMs from nine model families, ranging from 1B to 72B parameters. Three findings stand out. First, switching from text to vision reduces fairness by an average of 0.291 in FaiR. Second, this modality gap is not explained by scale alone: within some families, such as Qwen2.5-VL, larger models are consistently fairer, but other families still show large visual fairness drops despite strong text-only performance; the abstract highlights InternVL3.5-38B as an example. Third, the perception control shows 90–95% accuracy in recognizing demographic attributes, which rules out simple visual comprehension failure as the main explanation.

A particularly useful result is the polarity-aware stereotype analysis: when models do produce a biased answer, they choose the human-stereotypical option roughly three-quarters of the time.
