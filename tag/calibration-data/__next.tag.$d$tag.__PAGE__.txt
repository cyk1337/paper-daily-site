9:T4ff,The method, DPQ (Doubt-Preserving Quantization), is a family of pre-quantization calibration-data recipes rather than a new quantizer. The pipeline is lightweight. First, run the full-precision model on candidate examples. Then select target-aware 'fragile' examples using FP uncertainty signals. Two signal families matter most: high-doubt examples, such as high entropy, low confidence, or small top-2 margin; and answerability-boundary examples, especially for answer/no-answer settings like SQuAD2. These are mixed with generic anchor examples and then passed to an unchanged quantizer such as GPTQ or AWQ. The theoretical argument is modest but useful: if the deployment target puts more mass near decision boundaries, while the calibration mixture is dominated by generic anchors, the optimal calibration set will differ from the one preferred by broad MCQA-style targets. The paper calls this mixture mismatch. The framing is built around two preservation risks: a distributional risk that measures deviation between quantized and FP predictive distributions, and a boundary risk that measures decision flips in low-margin regions, optionally reweighted toward answerability or abstention-heavy inputs. This is a better fit for real systems than accuracy-only calibration.a:T539,The evaluation spans 8 language models, 9 NLP benchmarks, and 22 comparison methods or configurations. The metric suite is broader than standard quantization papers: besides ECE, adaptive ECE, NLL, and Brier score, the authors report FP-behavior metrics such as FP agreement, JSD between quantized and FP predictive distributions, confidence shift, top-two margin drift, and margin correlation. On SQuAD2 they also evaluate answerability-boundary behavior directly. Three findings stand out. First, the best calibration recipe depends strongly on the target. For boundary-sensitive answerability preservation, a high-boundary mixture such as DPQ-s128-r75 works best. For broad MCQA, milder recipes such as DPQ-r50 or single-signal confidence-only / entropy-only selection are often better. Second, accuracy can hide substantial uncertainty drift: some settings preserve top-1 behavior while materially changing confidence and margin structure. Third, preservation signals transfer only partially across quantizers. The post-hoc calibration and AWQ analyses suggest that some useful signals are not tied to one quantizer, but the best score-preservation recipe still remains quantizer-specific. The paper is less about one headline average gain and more about establishing the robustness of this target dependence across models and tasks.b:T439,The authors replace aggregate post-pruning accuracy with a capability-retention view over four dimensions: General, Commonsense, Code, and Math. The strongest predictors are Distortion, operationalized through perplexity, and Aggregation, measured through repeated 4-gram patterns. Broader measures such as Scope, Variety, Granularity, and Mismatch are less reliable. Based on this analysis, the paper proposes two calibration constructions. The first is mix_balanced, which samples uniformly from capability-aligned pools under the same 128-sequence budget used by the baselines. The second is IGSP, a self-generation protocol that uses the base model and the evaluation taxonomy to create capability-balanced calibration data, while controlling perplexity bands and reducing repeated-pattern bias.
