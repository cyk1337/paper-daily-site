1) AraBehave benchmark: 1,623 culturally grounded Arabic prompts focused on open-ended behavior. Six models are answered and rated by native Arabic speakers from five regions on a 1–5 Likert scale, with mandatory written rationales. Total: 29,214 judgments; inter-rater reliability ICC=0.58. Importantly, the paper’s error taxonomy and conclusions are derived from annotator rationales rather than author impressions or LLM judges.
2) Automated scorer: a regression-based judge model trained on the human labels. Generalization is tested via leave-one-model-out (train on labels from 5 systems, predict on the 6th), achieving Pearson r=0.74 on unseen systems. The scorer is then used for controlled, higher-throughput analyses (system prompts, language switching, scaling, instruction-tuning variants).
Analytical framing: decompose failures into (a) normative stance/framing errors (secular/relativist “both-sides” answers for culturally settled issues) vs. (b) grounded cultural inaccuracies (notably scriptural errors). - Six-model evaluation (3 Arabic-centric + 3 frontier general models). - Language effect: asking in English instead of Arabic drops performance for 5/6 models (example: 3.86→3.34), suggesting language-conditioned alignment/behavior.
