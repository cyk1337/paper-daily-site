9:T720,The key idea is Anchored Stochastic Flow Map Composition, or ASFMC. The paper first makes a negative point: for velocity fields or finely discretized ODE samplers, one can introduce stochasticity through a path-preserving SDE reformulation. For long-range flow maps, however, the stochastic transition from source time t to target time r generally depends on the entire intermediate stochastic path. It cannot, in general, be reduced to a simple Gaussian perturbation around the deterministic flow-map output. Exact SDE stochasticization would therefore require stochastic integration and would largely destroy the efficiency advantage of few-step generators.

ASFMC avoids direct stochasticization of the long-range map. Instead, it introduces an anchor time τ and constructs a stochastic transition by composition. Starting from a deterministic transition t to r, the method first maps the sample to the deterministic endpoint at time r using the pretrained flow map. It then transports that endpoint to an anchor time τ and resamples back to time r through a conditional transition defined on the probability path. This yields a stochastic kernel for the t to r step while preserving the marginal path learned by the original model.

The framework is instantiated for both major few-step flow-map parameterizations: single-time endpoint maps such as sCM, and two-time maps such as MeanFlow. For the latter, the paper discusses local and endpoint anchors. Once ASFMC is in place, each sampling step becomes a stochastic policy transition, so the few-step generator can be cast as an MDP and optimized with GRPO-style likelihood ratios. A major practical point is that this is post hoc: the pretrained flow-map parameterization is kept intact, and the method does not fall back to expensive fine-step ODE or SDE sampling.a:T577,The experiments are conducted on few-step FLUX-based text-to-image generators, with pretrained MeanFlow and sCM checkpoints as the starting point. The paper reports improvements after RL post-training on reward-based, perceptual, and task-level metrics. That suggests ASFMC is not only a formal device for making the RL objective well-defined, but also useful in practice for aligning image generators to downstream rewards.

From the available text, the experimental agenda appears to have three parts. First, show that Flow-Map GRPO improves over the pretrained checkpoints under reward optimization. Second, compare ASFMC against simpler stochasticization baselines or unsuitable SDE approximations to demonstrate better stability and better preservation of the original probability path. Third, validate the method on both sCM and MeanFlow to support the claim of parameterization-agnostic applicability.
