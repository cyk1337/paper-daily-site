9:T7c9,The method, called Graft, is a training-free and lossless hybrid tree construction framework built around a prune-then-graft strategy.

Step one is pruning. Similar to prior dynamic-tree methods, Graft uses calibrated confidence checkpoints to decide when low-confidence branches should be cut early. The important shift is interpretive: the removed branches are not treated merely as deleted candidates, but as released candidate budget. The paper argues that pruning-only methods are fundamentally limited because they must remain subtrees of the original static tree. As a result, any pruning error directly lowers coverage and therefore lowers the achievable accepted length.

Step two is grafting. Instead of leaving the freed slots empty, or spending them on deeper uncertain autoregressive drafting, Graft fills them with retrieval-based continuations. The total verification budget remains identical to that of the original static tree, but the candidate pool now becomes a hybrid of a pruned parametric subtree and retrieval branches. This matters because retrieval branches are not constrained to be descendants of the retained subtree, so they can recover some of the valid continuations lost to pruning and thereby move beyond the usual latency-MAT tradeoff.

Implementation is designed to be GPU-friendly. Retrieval branches are rooted at the current token and prepared in parallel with autoregressive drafting. A GPU-resident adjacency matrix stores local transition information, is initialized during warm-up, and updated online using target-model verification signals. This avoids CPU-side lookup and synchronization, preserves the standard tree-attention verification path, and does not require extra target forward passes.
