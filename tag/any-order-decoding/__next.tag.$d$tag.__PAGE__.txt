9:T49f,PONDER frames sequential masked generation as an MDP.
- State: the current partially filled canvas x^(t) (visible patches as tokens, missing patches as MASK).
- Frozen generator: a backbone produces per-patch features from x^(t); a diffusion/continuous head fills selected patches.
- Policy actions: choose a set of patches to unmask and generate next; optionally choose previously generated patches to re-mask so they will be regenerated later (“rethinking”). This explicitly models revision rather than hoping later steps fix early mistakes.
- Training: keep the generator fixed; train only the policy with RL using a terminal reward computed on the final sample. Crucially, the method does not need token probabilities or exact log-likelihoods from the generator, which is what makes it applicable beyond discrete masked LMs.
- FIDgrad reward: approximate each sample’s marginal contribution to FID by linearizing the FID objective in Inception-feature mean/covariance statistics, yielding an image-level scalar reward usable by policy gradients.
At inference, a rethinking budget R can be adjusted to trade compute for quality while keeping both generator and policy fixed.a:T512,The framework is built on three core components: First, theoretical grounding: The order selection problem is formalized as minimizing the KL divergence between the data distribution and the distribution induced by the decoding procedure. A tractable upper bound on this intractable objective is derived, expressed in terms of the frozen denoiser's pathwise log-likelihood under teacher forcing. This bound yields a dense, model-self-aware reward signal that assigns credit to every unmasking decision: an optimal order selects the next token that maximizes the expected future log-likelihood of the remaining trajectory, rather than the token with highest local certainty. Second, Self-Aware Scheduling (SAS): A lightweight order policy is trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) while keeping the base denoiser frozen. At each decoding step, the policy outputs a distribution over currently masked positions to select the next token to unmask. The policy is plug-and-play, and works seamlessly for both token-level any-order decoding and blockwise semi-autoregressive decoding.
