9:T4ab,The method is called Declarative Attention (DA). The model is prompted to emit parseable control tags inside its chain-of-thought, declaring the attention scope it needs at each phase of generation. An inference-side state machine reads these tags online and constructs the attention mask dynamically. DA defines three modes. <global> allows access to the full context and is used when the model needs to survey or search broadly. <focus> commits the model to a specific region of the context, restricting attention to that region. <local> limits attention to recent generated tokens, avoiding long-context reads altogether. This is the key departure from prior sparse-attention methods: the selection signal comes from the model’s own generated reasoning trace, not from hidden-state proxy scorers, recency heuristics, historical attention statistics, or an auxiliary scan over the cache. The paper also includes a practical implementation in vLLM using block-aligned, in-place KV-cache masking compatible with FlashAttention. Another notable point is that the protocol works zero-shot on off-the-shelf models, without task-specific fine-tuning or hand-designed partitions for each benchmark.a:T4cc,The evaluation covers 15 long-context tasks, with main results reported on Gemma-4-31B and Qwen-3.6-27B. The central finding is a favorable cost-accuracy trade-off. DA reduces average decoding attention cost by 52.0% on Gemma-4-31B with only a 1.27 percentage-point accuracy drop, and by 31.1% on Qwen-3.6-27B with a 2.75-point drop. The paper also shows positive scaling behavior: as model size increases from 4B to 31B, the accuracy gap between DA and full attention narrows, suggesting that self-declared attention depends on model capability. Absolute savings grow with context length, reaching up to 21 million attended tokens saved per response. The ablations are important. Relative to a maskless ablation, the DA-induced dynamic mask cuts attended tokens by as much as 71.1%, indicating that the savings are driven by the masking mechanism itself rather than by prompt formatting alone. On the systems side, a roofline-style analysis estimates decode wall-clock cost reductions to 0.71× of vanilla for Gemma-4-31B and 0.77× for Qwen-3.6-27B on a well-optimized serving stack. The paper is careful not to equate token savings with end-to-end speedup directly; it grounds the estimate in memory-bandwidth considerations.b:T491,Begin with the introduction, especially the discussion of why long-context decoding is bandwidth-bound due to repeated KV-cache reads. In the experiments, start with the main 15-task results to get the overall cost-accuracy picture, then read the scaling section because it reveals whether the protocol improves with model capability. After that, focus on the ablations, especially the maskless comparison and any analyses of mode transitions, since those are the best tests of whether the gains come from dynamic masking rather than just extra CoT structure. Three questions are worth tracking while reading: how well declared focus regions align with actual useful context; whether the protocol can induce premature narrowing of attention; and how much additional gain might be unlocked by explicit post-training for DA.
