Trade-off vs suffix arrays: less coverage for arbitrary cross-boundary substrings, but far better scalability, shard invariance, and controllable retention behavior.1d:T788,The method has three stages. First, train a standard byte-level BPE tokenizer as usual and keep the full learned merge tree. There is no modification to the merge-learning objective itself. Second, after training, compute each learned token’s final exposure, meaning how often it actually appears in the fully encoded corpus after all merges are applied. Tokens with low final exposure are marked as internal-only: they remain in the merge tree and can still serve as construction nodes for longer tokens, but they are removed from the model-visible vocabulary. Third, to preserve the target visible vocabulary size, the tokenizer training is resumed beyond the original vocabulary budget, producing additional candidate merges. Better-exposed candidates are then used to fill the visible slots vacated by the pruned tokens.

The encoding design is the key implementation detail. Encoding first follows the full merge rules exactly as in standard BPE. If a resulting token is internal-only, it is recursively expanded into visible child tokens until the returned token sequence contains only visible vocabulary items. This preserves the learned merge structure while changing the model interface. The paper is careful to distinguish this from ordinary vocabulary pruning: the visible vocabulary size is preserved, and the merge tree is not discarded. What changes is which learned tokens are exposed to the LM.

This design is more conservative than simply deleting low-frequency tokens, because it does not break the construction path for longer merges. It is also more controlled than retraining a larger tokenizer and truncating it, because it explicitly separates internal merge nodes from model-visible units.
