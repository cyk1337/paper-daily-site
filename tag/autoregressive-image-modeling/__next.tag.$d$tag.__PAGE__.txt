5:T50f,Given a latent tensor Z∈R^{h×w×c}, conventional VQ treats each spatial vector z(i,j,:) as a codebook lookup target, producing h×w local patch tokens. CVQ instead slices the tensor along the channel dimension and quantizes each channel map z(:,:,k), producing a 1D sequence of c channel tokens. Each token therefore carries global spatial structure rather than a local patch. The motivation is that autoencoder channels often separate different levels of visual information: early channels can encode coarse structure, layout, or color tone, while later channels add finer attributes. This change also addresses codebook collapse. Patch embeddings are highly redundant across images and tend to cluster around a small subset of codes early in training. Channel-wise embeddings are more separable, so codebook updates are spread over more entries. On top of CVQ, the authors define a Channel-wise Autoregressive framework that performs next-channel prediction instead of next-patch prediction. Nested dropout is used to encourage an ordered channel sequence where prefixes reconstruct meaningful coarse content and later tokens refine details.
