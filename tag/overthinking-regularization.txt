1a:T60c,The method, Implicit Compression Regularization (ICR), extracts compression targets from the model’s own successful rollouts. Instead of adding a handcrafted reward for shorter outputs, it identifies the shortest correct responses within each rollout group and uses them to induce a virtual shorter distribution. The current policy is then regularized toward these concise-but-correct trajectories.

The paper first analyzes the dynamics of explicit length penalties under different coefficients. Stronger penalties reduce length faster, but they also degrade accuracy earlier and worsen the accuracy-length Pareto frontier. More importantly, the group-wise length-correctness correlation starts negative and gradually increases during training. A negative correlation means shorter responses are, on average, more likely to be correct within the current rollout distribution; a positive correlation indicates compression is now cutting into necessary reasoning. ICR is designed to exploit the former regime.

Mechanistically, this is closer to policy regularization than reward shaping. The compression signal is not “be shorter”; it is “move probability mass toward shorter trajectories that are already verified as correct under the current policy.” That distinction matters. It reduces the shortcut where the model simply writes less rather than reasoning more compactly.
