9:T613,The method is Prompt-Enhanced On-Policy Self-Distillation (PE-OPSD). Let p be the raw prompt and p+ the enhanced prompt produced by an external Prompt Enhancer. The student is conditioned only on p, while the teacher is conditioned on p+. Training proceeds on the student’s own on-policy flow trajectory: the student generates states under raw-prompt conditioning, and at those visited states the teacher provides target vector fields computed under the enhanced prompt. The student then matches those targets.

Two design choices matter. First, supervision is applied to the flow dynamics rather than only to final samples or to newly constructed text-image pairs. This is why the paper distinguishes itself from SFT-style alternatives: it does not require rebuilding the dataset around enhanced prompts. Second, the asymmetry is only in the conditioning signal, not in the task family. Teacher and student live in the same flow-matching framework, so the distilled object is: given the same latent state, how would a better-informed condition change the denoising or transport direction?

The paper positions this as on-policy self-distillation, where the teacher can be a stop-gradient or EMA view rather than a separately trained external model. The critical comparison is against three baselines: inference-time prompt enhancement, SFT with enhanced prompts, and off-policy distillation.
