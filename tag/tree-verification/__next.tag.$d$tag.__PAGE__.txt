9:T999,The central idea is to use the prefix acceptance probability of each node as the coupling variable across levels. Intuitively, the acceptance mass that can be assigned to a child does not depend only on the overlap between target and draft distributions at that node; it also depends on how likely the parent prefix is to be accepted in the first place. Single-step OT ignores this scaling because each depth is treated as a standalone probability space. RRSw handles prefix survival implicitly, but only through sequential renormalization after each rejection, which prevents globally coordinated allocation among siblings.

UniVer decomposes tree verification into two phases. The first is a top-down allocation phase. Starting from the root, the algorithm propagates prefix acceptance probabilities downward and formulates acceptance allocation over children as a conditional OT problem. The “conditional” part means that each local allocation is constrained by the acceptance mass inherited from the parent prefix, so the optimization is performed in a dynamically scaled conditional space rather than in an independent full space at every depth. The second is a post-order decision phase, where actual accept/reject decisions are made based on the precomputed acceptance plan while preserving exact sampling equivalence to the target model.

A key distinction from RRSw is that UniVer removes the need to process siblings one by one with repeated residual renormalization. Instead, all branches at the same depth can have their acceptance plan computed in parallel within a single coordinated probability space. The theory shows that, under the Greedy draft selection strategy, UniVer is conditionally optimal for any prefix acceptance probability p_v in (0,1], and that its upper bound is at least as good as the single-step Greedy method. When the tree collapses to one level, UniVer reduces naturally to the known single-step OT formulation. So the method is presented not as a heuristic, but as a principled extension of single-step OT optimality to tree-structured verification.

The design choice is also clear: the paper does not modify draft-model training or the target-model verification kernel. It focuses only on the verification rule. That keeps the lossless guarantee clean and makes the method easier to plug into existing speculative decoding systems, though the achievable gain still depends on draft quality and tree construction.a:T656,The empirical story is centered on acceptance length rather than end-to-end latency. Across multiple LLMs and different tree architectures, UniVer is compared against Vanilla RRSw, Traversal-style baselines, and single-step OT methods such as Greedy. The main result reported in the paper is that, at temperature 1.0, UniVer improves acceptance length over standard RRSw by about 4.2% to 8.5%, with the headline gain reaching 8.5%. The more informative experimental pattern is structural. The benefit becomes larger when the draft tree is deeper or has more branching, because horizontal and vertical dependencies are then more strongly coupled and local verification wastes more acceptance mass. The paper also argues that the single-pass allocation is implementation-friendly because it removes the sequential dependency inherent in RRSw. The theoretical and empirical stories are aligned: when the structure degenerates to a single-step setting, UniVer should not underperform the single-step OT baseline; when genuine tree dependencies exist, it can do better.
