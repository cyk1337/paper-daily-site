18:T72a,The method has two stages. The first builds the baseline Live-ProsodyJudge (LPJ). Gemini is used as the teacher to compare paired audio clips in both A/B orders, and only swap-consistent high-confidence outcomes are retained for distillation into Qwen3-Omni. The task protocol defines seven dimensions: four always-on core dimensions (C1–C4: fluency, intonation, emotion, and live-stream expressiveness) and three transcript-triggered dimensions (L1–L3: key-information emphasis, emotional-state switching, and interaction-mode switching). Training uses a curriculum from easier human-vs-TTS comparisons to harder cross-model and same-model comparisons, with rank-32 LoRA attached to Qwen3-Omni during SFT.

The second stage introduces Decoupled-Live-ProsodyJudge (D-LPJ) to address verdict coupling. There are three main changes. First, the overall-verdict target is removed. The reason is straightforward: if the model is trained to predict a global winner, that label becomes a shortcut and drags all dimension-level judgments toward the same answer. Second, the model uses dimension-wise SFT masking. Instead of discarding a whole pair when some dimensions are uncertain or sparsely supervised, only the affected dimensions are masked, preserving more data while reducing label contamination. Third, the paper applies span-local GRPO, where each dimension’s advantage is assigned only to the rationale span for that dimension. This is the most interesting part methodologically. A global RL signal would continue to reward shortcut behavior; local credit assignment forces the model to ground each dimension in separate evidence.

The key contribution is therefore not a new backbone, but a factorized supervision design. The paper decouples the problem at three levels: target space, sample utilization, and reward routing.19:T757,The main evaluation suite contains 1,043 internally human-annotated pairs, plus a 222-pair transfer set. T1–T4 cover Qwen3-TTS base versus an SFT variant, within-model comparisons for an internal GDPO variant, cross-model comparisons against BERT-CosyVoice, and human recordings versus Qwen3-TTS. T5 is a transfer setting with IndexTTS2 versus FireRedTTS, a model combination not seen during coupled-judge training. T1–T4 span 135 speakers and multiple live-stream styles, and each pair shares the same transcript to control lexical confounds. Three annotators label each pair independently; 45% are unanimous 3–0 decisions and 55% are 2-vote agreements with one abstention. Test partitions are disjoint from training and validation in audio, pair identity, normalized transcript, speaker, and source stream.

Empirically, LPJ works as a much cheaper distilled alternative to Gemini. Under 10-sample balanced-order inference, it shows negligible position bias and achieves higher point agreement than a single Gemini call on the four main test sets. D-LPJ reaches 86.10% pooled agreement while reducing verdict collapse, which is the more important result: the model produces genuinely varying dimension-level verdicts instead of mirroring the overall winner. In a practical Best-of-8 candidate-selection setting, LPJ’s top choice falls within the human top-3 preferences in 85.29% of cases, suggesting that the distilled judge is already useful for reranking and RL feedback.

The most informative parts of the experiments are not the headline agreement numbers, but the comparisons between coupled and decoupled training in terms of dimension diversity, inter-dimension correlation, and transfer behavior.
