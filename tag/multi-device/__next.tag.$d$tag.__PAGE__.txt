b:T5cc,The core idea is to benchmark agents under a much broader operational scope. The environment is expanded along three axes: long-horizon event histories, interdependent backend services, and multi-device interaction through both GUI and CLI. Unlike many prior benchmarks that isolate a single app or a single-turn task, Claw-Anything embeds each task inside an evolving, persona-grounded digital world.

The environment is built through an automated synthesis pipeline. Starting from a minimal persona seed, an LLM-based simulator performs multi-round event injection, incrementally adding everyday events and updating both persistent world state and dynamic service traces such as email, calendars, and social platforms. As the process continues, the event history grows, the persona becomes more specific, and the environment accumulates realistic noise, including irrelevant and contradictory events. The next event is then turned into a grounded task with an executable verifier, so evaluation becomes a question of whether the agent can perform the appropriate next action in an evolving digital life.

Two design choices are especially notable. First, tasks emerge from the simulated world rather than being written independently of it, which makes temporal dependencies and hidden assumptions more natural. Second, the benchmark includes proactive assistance, so agents are evaluated not only on responding to explicit requests but also on inferring useful actions from context.c:T4c5,The main empirical result is straightforward: GPT-5.5 reaches only 34.5% pass@1 on Claw-Anything, much lower than the performance many models report on earlier benchmarks. The intended conclusion is that prior evaluations underrepresent the difficulty of integrating long histories, coordinating across services, and acting across devices. For a benchmark paper, that is the key result.

In terms of scale, the paper builds 200 human-verified evaluation tasks and 2,000 training environments. More importantly, it shows that these environments are useful beyond evaluation. Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-27B on 1,500 successful trajectories derived from the training environments improves success by 23.7%. That suggests the synthetic pipeline is not merely generating harder tests; it can also produce supervision that helps close part of the capability gap.

From a research perspective, the most informative numbers are not leaderboard rankings but the size of the drop under broader context and the amount recovered through fine-tuning. The first indicates strong sensitivity to environment complexity.
