9:T77c,The paper applies a unified screen-and-ablate pipeline, inherited from earlier work, to a matched cross-model panel. Step 1 is a spectral signal: for each attention head, the authors compute a participation-ratio integral over the singular value spectrum of attention outputs across checkpoints. This is meant to surface heads engaged in sustained content-dependent computation rather than generic sink behavior. Step 2 is task-pattern screening: for each task, they define candidate attention patterns and keep heads whose selectivity exceeds fixed thresholds. For IOI, they explicitly test four candidate screens—prev-token, induction, name-mover, and S-Inhibition—rather than assuming the canonical IOI decomposition will transfer. Step 3 is causal verification: they group-ablate the selected heads via mean ablation and compare the drop in top-1 and logit-diff against matched-random controls drawn from the same layers with equal head count and no overlap.

Three methodological refinements matter. First, the paper does not equate selection with mechanism. It introduces a five-way taxonomy: primary cause, secondary cause, correlate, interferer, and null. A head class can be highly selective yet only correlated, or even harmful, unless causal tests confirm its role. Second, the matched-random control is treated carefully. When selected heads concentrate in very early layers, same-layer random controls have high variance and a weak null floor because L0-L1 already carry broad embedding and routing functions. The paper explicitly marks where that bound is informative and where it is not. Third, the authors formulate a falsifiable structural hypothesis from the observed MoE behavior: OLMoE at 1B active scale may build composed-task circuits on top of a foundational prev-token positional substrate, except when the task directly probes another pattern such as final-position name copying in IOI.a:T7a7,The model panel contains Pythia 1B trained on the Pile, OLMo 1B trained on DCLM, and OLMoE 1B-7B trained on DCLM with 64 experts and top-8 routing, all compared at roughly 1B active parameters. The scale choice is motivated in the paper: GPT-2 small does not reliably solve IOI, reaching only 13% top-1 and 57% IO-vs-subject, so the authors move to the smallest natural-text models where the task is actually solved.

The main empirical findings are strong. On IOI alone, the primary causal screen already differs by model: prev-token for Pythia, S-Inhibition for OLMo, and name-mover for OLMoE. Extending to greater-than, successor, and variable binding makes the pattern sharper: across all 12 task-model cells, there is no repeated primary causal screen at comparable effect size. This is stronger than saying there is some variation; it suggests systematic non-isomorphism in implementation. A second notable result is the OLMoE prev-token-primacy pattern. On three of the four tasks—greater-than, successor, and variable binding—the primary causal screen in OLMoE is prev-token rather than the task-specific pattern one might expect from the task structure. IOI is the exception, which the authors argue is consistent with IOI directly probing final-position name copying.

The control design is also worth noting. Each cell uses a matched-random null sampled across ten seeds, allowing the authors to report specificity differentials with uncertainty rather than relying on one random comparison. The paper also emphasizes that group ablation can mix supportive and interfering heads, so individual-head ablations are needed to disambiguate supporters from interferers.
