18:T867,The setup is simple and clean. Given a pretrained model of size N and a total annotation budget B, an allocation ratio r sends rB examples to SFT and (1-r)B to RL. Performance is written as P(N, r, B; tau). A standard formulation would optimize r* = argmax_r P, but the paper argues that this is the wrong target because post-training curves are often flat near the top. A tiny gain in measured score may not justify a full retraining run, and the exact argmax can be unstable under noise. The paper therefore defines the epsilon-near-optimal region: the set of ratios whose performance is at least (1-epsilon) times the best observed performance.

This shift enables three analyses. First, region characterization: the authors sweep allocation ratios across tasks, model families, and RL methods, then measure how wide the near-optimal region is under tolerances such as 2% to 10%. Second, scale transfer: instead of asking whether the exact optimum ratio matches across scales, they ask whether the near-optimal region found on a small proxy model covers high-performing ratios on a larger model. This is a much more stable transfer criterion. Third, asymmetric annotation cost: the default setup measures budget in number of labeled examples, but the paper extends the framework to cases where SFT and RL labels have different unit costs. Under that view, the effective optimum shifts, and the acceptable region can widen further when SFT data is relatively more expensive.

Relative to prior work, the novelty is in the problem formulation and the transfer criterion. Earlier work mostly reported broad trends, such as SFT being more important in low-data regimes and preference optimization becoming more useful at larger scales, but did not provide a principled near-optimal allocation framework or establish transfer from small to large models. Work on data mixture optimization and scaling-law extrapolation usually focuses on single-stage training, especially pretraining mixtures, not sequential SFT-then-RL coupling. The paper’s contribution is to turn a brittle tuning problem into a region-finding problem with practical transfer value.19:T6f4,The most important empirical findings are structural rather than benchmark-centric. First, the performance-versus-allocation curve typically has a broad plateau near its maximum. Even with small tolerances, such as a few percent from peak performance, the near-optimal region is already wide. Second, this region generally widens with model scale. Larger models are therefore more forgiving to imperfect SFT/RL splits. Third, transfer works better at the region level than at the exact-optimum level: a near-optimal region identified on a small proxy model tends to reliably contain high-performing allocations for a larger target model, even when the exact best ratio shifts. Fourth, the pattern is observed across tasks, model families, and both off-policy and on-policy RL settings, which argues against the effect being tied to one specific algorithm.

The paper also includes a useful cost sanity check. Using measured runtimes and L40S cloud pricing, per-example training compute cost is reported to range from about 2e-5 USD for the cheapest 1B SFT setting to about 3e-4 USD for 8B DPO. Compared with human annotation costs around 0.5 to 1.0 USD per example, compute is lower by roughly three to five orders of magnitude. Even compared with cheap synthetic annotation around 1e-3 USD per example, compute is still usually below annotation. This supports the paper’s decision to treat annotation as the dominant first-order budget axis.

The paper is less about headline absolute scores and more about curve geometry, region width, and transfer coverage. When reading the experiments, the key is not the single best number but how flat the top is, how the width changes with scale, and how much overlap exists between proxy-model and target-model high-performance regions.1a:T563,Start with the introduction and preliminaries. The central conceptual move is the replacement of exact optimality with near-optimality. If that framing makes sense, the rest of the paper becomes straightforward. Then focus on the sections that define and analyze the near-optimal region. The most informative figures are the allocation-performance curves, the region-width comparisons across scales, and the small-to-large transfer plots. In those figures, do not over-focus on the exact argmax. Look at plateau width and overlap.

The most useful sections to read carefully are the ones corresponding to region analysis, scale transfer, and asymmetric annotation cost. Those sections answer three practical questions: why a region is a better target than a point, why small proxy experiments can replace large-scale search, and how the framework changes when SFT and RL labels have different prices. It is also worth checking how tolerance is defined, how noisy training outcomes are handled, and what criterion is used to claim reliable transfer. The paper’s main value lies in recipe design logic, not in a new optimizer or RL objective. Anyone working on large-scale training pipelines can reuse the same lens for other multi-stage allocation problems, including continual pretraining versus SFT, SFT versus preference data, or allocation across multiple RL signal sources.1b:T897,方法叫 FactAgent，核心由两部分组成：fact-centric agent state，以及基于 Bayesian fact utility estimation 的 dense process supervision。

先看 agent 形式。与标准 ReAct 只维护原始对话和工具返回文本不同，FactAgent 在状态里显式维护一个 fact store。每次 search 动作得到 observation 后，agent 不只是继续推理，还可以执行 assert 动作，把 observation 里的信息抽取成结构化事实，写入 fact store。这样，状态从“越来越长的文本历史”变成“问题 Q、当前 observation、累计事实集合 Ft”。作者把这些事实视为离散语义单元，而不是普通字符串，这一步是后续 credit assignment 的基础。

再看奖励构造。论文的关键不是直接给 assert 打启发式分，而是从 group rollouts 里估计事实的后验效用。具体做法是先把语义等价的事实聚成 cluster，避免同一证据因表述不同被分散统计；然后基于一组 rollout 的最终 outcome，用 Bayesian estimation 推断每个 fact cluster 与成功结果的统计关联。直观上，如果某类事实经常出现在成功轨迹里、较少出现在失败轨迹里，它的 posterior utility 就更高。随后，这个 fact-level utility 被映射回 step-level dense rewards，用来训练 policy。

这比直接做 token-level process reward 更适合搜索任务，因为搜索任务的有效中间单位不是 token，也不一定是整句 reasoning，而是“找到并确认了哪条证据”。作者因此把 credit assignment 的对象从 action surface form 转成 evidence semantics。和纯 outcome-based GRPO 相比，FactAgent 仍然利用 group rollouts，但不再让整条轨迹共享同一个粗粒度 advantage，而是通过事实聚类和后验估计，把奖励分配到真正有贡献的中间步骤上。

从设计取舍看，这个方法避免了额外训练 reward model，也不依赖人工标注每一步是否正确，代价则是需要可靠的事实抽取与聚类。如果抽取质量差，后验 utility 估计会被噪声污染。全文也承认这一点，因此 fact store 既是方法优势，也是误差传播的主要入口。1c:T89d,FactAgent has two main components: a fact-centric state representation and a Bayesian utility estimation scheme for dense process supervision.

On the agent side, the paper departs from standard ReAct-style agents that keep only raw text history. The state explicitly includes a fact store. After a search action returns an observation, the agent can take an assert action that extracts structured facts from the observation and writes them into the store. The state is therefore represented as the question, the current observation, and the accumulated fact set. These facts are treated as discrete semantic evidence units rather than plain text snippets. This is important because the method assigns credit at the level of evidence semantics, not at the level of surface-form actions.

On the reward side, the paper estimates fact utility from grouped rollouts. Semantically equivalent facts are first clustered so that the same evidence is not split across multiple textual variants. Then, using final outcomes from a group of trajectories, the method performs Bayesian estimation of the posterior utility of each fact cluster. Intuitively, if a fact cluster appears disproportionately often in successful trajectories, its posterior utility increases. These fact-level utilities are then converted into dense step-level rewards for policy optimization.

This differs from standard outcome-based GRPO, where all steps in a trajectory inherit the same coarse trajectory-level signal. Here, group rollouts are still used, but the reward is redistributed through an explicit evidence layer.
