9:T568,The authors first run controlled ablations on a compact 13M-example dataset, then scale the resulting recipe to Pixel Linguist II trained on 280M examples. The method has four main components. First, it uses a native-resolution ViT-style encoder, following the NaViT direction, so inputs can have variable resolutions and aspect ratios. The key observation is that high-resolution document pretraining is not always necessary if the model sees suitable spatial proxies: variable natural-image resolutions and variable rendered font sizes. Second, the model uses layout-aware visual augmentation. Text is rendered on the fly with diverse fonts, backgrounds, spatial layouts, and perturbations, reducing the chance that the encoder solves the task through font or canvas artifacts. Third, training combines natural image-text pairs and rendered text-text pairs under a single contrastive objective. Natural images are not merely auxiliary data; they provide grounding and prevent a text-only collapse in the visual domain. Fourth, multilingual learning uses a two-stage curriculum: large-scale unsupervised multilingual visual-text pretraining followed by semantic mid-training for cross-lingual alignment. This differs from prior fixed-resolution, rendered-text-only, single-stage recipes by treating resolution, layout, grounding, and multilinguality as coupled pretraining variables.a:T4a2,The experimental evidence is organized around four research questions. Fixed fonts and plain canvases lead to pixel-level shortcuts and near-collapse on document retrieval.
