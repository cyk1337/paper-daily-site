The student samples continuations from its current policy, and teachers provide next-token distribution targets on those sampled trajectories. Each example is routed by a teacher tag to either a tool-call teacher or a response teacher. Both teachers start from the same base model and are specialized on structured tool-call targets or direct natural-language answers. A small supervised loss is also used as a format anchor for tool-call serialization.

The paper introduces the diagnosis of behavior leverage imbalance. The authors check several plausible aggregate explanations: whether tool-call samples receive more token exposure, whether full-sequence per-token JSD is larger for the tool teacher, and whether a gradient proxy is larger. These explanations do not match the observed behavior; in some runs, response tokens receive more exposure or larger aggregate divergence. The proposed explanation is that local signals near the decision boundary have higher behavioral leverage than average sequence-level signals.

The mitigation is Soft Clamp. For token divergence d_i, the method defines a batch-level threshold C = k * mean(d). Divergences below C are left unchanged. Divergences above C are transformed as d'_i = d_i * C / stopgrad(d_i). The forward contribution is capped around C, while the backward gradient is scaled down rather than removed.
