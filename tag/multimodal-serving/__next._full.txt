推理时，模型自己决定是否打开 side channel。如果预测出 <|asr_start|>，就贪心解码最多 64 个文本 token，直到 <|asr_end|>；若超长或遇到控制 token，则强制闭合。随后，当 resident KV cache 超过目标预算时，系统驱逐更早的 acoustic states，但保留这些 transcript token，并保留一个最近的 acoustic window 以覆盖转写延迟。于是，较老的语音内容以文本形式留在上下文里，最近一段仍保留原始声学细节，形成“远处文本化、近处声学化”的混合记忆。

训练上，作者没有单独接一个 ASR 模型，而是复用原有 LM backbone，只用 LoRA 适配 side channel。监督信号来自带词级强制对齐的转写数据：每个词根据其声学结束时间和额外延迟 δ 被分配到某个 1 秒 unit 中，每个词只出现在一个目标 transcription segment。仅用 transcription cross-entropy 会改变原模型的原生对话行为，所以作者再加入 token-level knowledge distillation。具体做法是：冻结原始 full-duplex 模型作为 teacher，让它在同一音频上、但不插入 transcription segment 地运行；收集 teacher 在所有 native prediction positions 的全词表分布；student 在插入 transcription segment 的序列上训练时，对这些 native 位置同时拟合 teacher 分布。这样 side channel 学会转写，同时尽量不扰动原有 listen/speak 决策、停顿处理、打断响应等行为。

这个设计和“单独训练一个流式 ASR，再把文本喂回模型”相比，有两个差异。其一，推理时不需要额外 ASR 模块，部署更简单；其二，蒸馏约束的是 full-duplex 原生行为，而不只是转写准确率，因此目标更贴近语音对话模型的系统需求。19:T828,实验围绕三件事展开：KV 峰值控制、长时语音理解、实时性与交互行为保持。主模型是 MiniCPM-o 4.5。作者指出，转写后的文本记忆大约每秒只占 3 个序列位置，而原始 acoustic history 约占 17 个位置，这给压缩上限提供了直观背景。

从方法设计推断，值得关注的消融应包括：只做 CE 转写监督、不做 KD 时 native behavior 的退化；不同 KV budget 下理解与实时性的折中；recent acoustic window 大小对性能的影响；以及 emission delay δ 对转写完整性和驱逐时机的影响。给出的全文片段没有完整表格数字，但主结论已经足够明确：在线文本化旧语音上下文，可以在不破坏 full-duplex 交互的前提下显著降低峰值 KV，并提升长时理解。1a:T95a,The core method is acoustic-to-text KV compression. The base model, MiniCPM-o 4.5, processes interaction in 1-second duplex units. Each unit contains audio tokens, a native <|listen|> or <|speak|> decision, and possibly response generation. After the first native decision in each unit, the authors insert an optional transcription segment delimited by two new tokens, <|asr_start|> and <|asr_end|>. This segment stays in the language-model context as textual memory, but it is excluded from the speech decoder input and from spoken output.

At inference time, the model decides whether to open the side channel. If it predicts <|asr_start|>, it greedily decodes up to 64 text tokens until <|asr_end|>; if the segment overruns or hits a control token, the segment is forcibly closed. When the resident KV cache exceeds a target budget, older acoustic states are evicted while transcript tokens are retained, together with a recent acoustic window that covers transcription delay. The resulting memory is hybrid: older context is preserved in text form, while recent context remains acoustically grounded.

Training avoids a separate ASR model. The side channel is adapted with LoRA on top of the existing LM backbone. Supervision comes from transcribed utterances with word-level forced alignment. Each word is assigned to a 1-second unit based on its acoustic endpoint plus an additional emission delay delta, and each word appears in exactly one target transcription segment. However, pure transcription cross-entropy would alter the model's native conversational behavior. To prevent that, the paper adds token-level knowledge distillation. A frozen teacher, namely the original full-duplex model without transcription segments, processes the same audio. Its full-vocabulary distributions at native prediction positions are collected, and the student is trained to match them at those positions while also learning the inserted transcription segments. This constrains changes to native listen/speak decisions, pause handling, interruption behavior, and response generation.

Compared with a pipeline that runs a separate streaming ASR and feeds text back into the model, this design has two practical differences. The paper argues that the key is identifying what must be bounded, not adding elaborate machinery.

The first component is bounded resident KV. Inside the engine, the authors enable a windowed-KV or sliding-window path so that each session retains only the most recent W tokens rather than the full conversation history on GPU. To avoid the quality drop that often comes from naively replacing full attention with a local window, they keep a small set of pinned attention-sink tokens that remain globally visible. The important shift is not KV compression in the abstract; it is changing per-session memory from unbounded growth to a bounded resident footprint.

The second component is a deadline-aware AIMD admission controller. Once resident state is bounded, per-frame latency becomes a monotone function of load, and the controller can search online for the schedulable concurrency N*. The paper demonstrates the dependency rather than assuming it: the same controller over-admits and runs into collapse on the unbounded-KV system, but converges cleanly on the bounded one.

The paper also introduces a first-order pool-fill model to explain when collapse occurs. This model frames the cliff as a memory event driven by cumulative KV growth, not a compute bottleneck. That distinction is central. In ordinary chatbot serving, requests are ephemeral and KV can be freed, swapped, or recomputed between turns. In interaction serving, there is no idle gap, so the session state must remain resident. Standard throughput-oriented serving tricks do not solve the unbounded-state problem.1f:T6cd,The evaluation is end-to-end on a real full-duplex stack rather than a synthetic microbenchmark. The main findings are clear.
