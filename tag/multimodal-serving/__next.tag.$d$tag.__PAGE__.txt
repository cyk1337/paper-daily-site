5:T4c3,The optimization targets the MiMo-V2.5 family, which combines Hybrid SWA, sparse MoE, and multimodal encoders. For MiMo-V2.5-Pro, the document describes a 70-layer model with 10 full-attention layers and 60 SWA layers, using a window size of 128. The central system change is a KV-cache refactor. Instead of a single KV pool that forces all layers to reserve O(N) sequence storage, the system separates full-attention KV from SWA KV. Full-attention layers retain full-sequence keys and values, while SWA layers keep only window-local entries, enforcing O(W) storage for the SWA part. A unified logical sequence view is still exposed to prefix trees, schedulers, and transport layers, with full-attention indices used as the authoritative reference and mappings maintained from full positions to SWA positions. Admission control checks both full and SWA cache constraints. The broader pipeline adds layerwise prefetching, SWA-aware prefix-cache trees, specialized placement policies, an RDMA-optimized distributed cache named GCache, and a KV-cache-affinity router to trade off cache reuse against load balance. Multimodal encoder throughput is treated as another bottleneck, especially for large images and long videos.6:T658,The method is intentionally minimal. The paper argues that the key is identifying what must be bounded, not adding elaborate machinery.

The first component is bounded resident KV. Inside the engine, the authors enable a windowed-KV or sliding-window path so that each session retains only the most recent W tokens rather than the full conversation history on GPU. To avoid the quality drop that often comes from naively replacing full attention with a local window, they keep a small set of pinned attention-sink tokens that remain globally visible. The important shift is not KV compression in the abstract; it is changing per-session memory from unbounded growth to a bounded resident footprint.

The second component is a deadline-aware AIMD admission controller. Once resident state is bounded, per-frame latency becomes a monotone function of load, and the controller can search online for the schedulable concurrency N*. The paper demonstrates the dependency rather than assuming it: the same controller over-admits and runs into collapse on the unbounded-KV system, but converges cleanly on the bounded one.

The paper also introduces a first-order pool-fill model to explain when collapse occurs. This model frames the cliff as a memory event driven by cumulative KV growth, not a compute bottleneck. That distinction is central. In ordinary chatbot serving, requests are ephemeral and KV can be freed, swapped, or recomputed between turns. In interaction serving, there is no idle gap, so the session state must remain resident. Standard throughput-oriented serving tricks do not solve the unbounded-state problem.7:T6cd,The evaluation is end-to-end on a real full-duplex stack rather than a synthetic microbenchmark. The system includes WebSocket clients, a Go gateway, and a GPU worker, and the paper tests four interaction models. Representative timing scales are also realistic: Moshi operates around 80 ms frames, while omni-style models use roughly 1–2 second frames, and sessions last for minutes, long enough to expose pool saturation.

The main findings are clear. First, the unmodified resumable-KV path exhibits a sharp cliff under sustained load: per-frame latency stays in the millisecond range and then abruptly pins at a ceiling while the engine stops producing meaningful tokens. Second, the cliff is memory-triggered rather than compute-triggered; per-frame compute remains cheap until the end, but the block pool fills because each session keeps accumulating KV. Third, the failure is metastable: nominally identical five-minute runs may collapse or survive due to small run-to-run variation in pool fill. Fourth, and most importantly, bounded KV turns the cliff into a slope. Latency rises smoothly and monotonically with load, making the system observable again and allowing the AIMD controller to converge to a schedulable concurrency while shedding excess sessions cleanly.

The paper also includes quality-oriented ablations. Sliding windows are needed to bound resident memory, while pinned attention sinks are needed to preserve generation quality under the bounded context. Another important ablation shows controller dependence: the same admission controller fails without bounded KV and succeeds with it, which supports the claim that the gain comes from restoring a truthful latency signal rather than from controller tuning alone.8:T4b6,A good skim starts with the introduction and the first few figures.
