18:T7e9,The method is built around three levels of unification.

First, representation unification. The paper introduces an Everything-as-RGB scheme: depth, embodiment segmentation, and first-frame-relative 2D flow are each mapped into a three-channel image-like sequence. These are then encoded by the same frozen video autoencoder used for RGB video. The motivation is straightforward: reuse the pretrained video latent space instead of introducing modality-specific encoders and decoders. The trade-off is that non-RGB modalities are forced through an RGB-like interface, so the burden is on experiments to show that important geometric structure is preserved.

Second, architectural unification. A shared Video DiT backbone handles all modalities, with lightweight task modulation providing task-specific conditioning. The transformer blocks and decoder remain shared, and the design explicitly avoids modality-specific output heads. Compared with standard multi-task systems, this is a stronger form of parameter sharing. The upside is tighter cross-task coupling and less architectural fragmentation. The downside is a higher risk of task interference, especially because generation and perception may prefer different internal representations.

Third, objective-space unification. All tasks are trained in a common velocity prediction space, following the native v-prediction formulation of diffusion models. Instead of attaching separate regression or classification heads for perception, the model learns all modalities through the same denoising-style prediction framework. At inference time, the model supports two modes: iterative future video generation with aligned dense outputs, and single-step dense perception from an observed video without full denoising rollout.

The important point is that the paper is not merely doing multi-task learning on top of a video generator. It is trying to show that generation and perception can coexist inside one shared latent dynamics model, with a common prediction formulation.19:T651,From the provided full-text excerpt, the evaluation spans diverse embodied settings, including multiple robot embodiments, human-hand interactions, and unseen tasks. The paper claims strong performance in both future video generation and dense perception, together with systematic design ablations. The excerpt does not include the full numerical tables, so the exact gains cannot be quoted here, but the experimental structure is still clear.

The first goal is to test whether joint modeling improves over a generate-then-estimate pipeline, especially in alignment and temporal consistency of dense outputs. The second is to verify that the three-way unification matters: shared backbone versus modality-specific heads, unified velocity prediction versus separate task objectives, and lightweight modulation versus more fragmented architectures. The third is to confirm that the same model is useful in both inference regimes: multi-step future imagination and single-step perception on observed videos.

The main empirical claim is reciprocal transfer. Video generation contributes strong spatiotemporal priors that help dense perception, while supervision from geometry, semantics, and motion pushes the shared backbone toward more physically meaningful representations. When reading the full paper, the most informative ablations to inspect are the ones that remove task modulation, reintroduce separate heads, or replace the unified velocity objective with task-specific losses. Those are the experiments that reveal whether the gains truly come from unification rather than from simply adding more supervision.1a:T5c6,Ψ-0.6 is an 8B-parameter autoregressive transformer with a single unified vocabulary. It serializes multiple modalities into one sequence: RGB images, text, camera pose, depth, optical flow, and point tracks.
Key mechanisms:
- Streams of Thought: explicit multi-step sequences interleaving text/images with geometric and motion abstractions. These intermediates function as writable working memory produced by the model itself.
- Any-to-any prompting: tasks are expressed by choosing what token blocks are given vs predicted and in what order. Different orderings correspond to different inference pathways (e.g., image pair→pose; image+pose→novel view).
- Counterfactual world modeling: given a specified motion/displacement, the model predicts flow/geometry, generates the resulting scene, and then answers questions by interpreting its own predictions—reasoner and imaginer are the same model.
- RL for representation-adaptive reasoning: intermediate generations (which modalities to emit and their content) are treated as policy actions optimized end-to-end from task rewards, unlike VLM agents that call fixed external tools.

It constructs an executable personal-computer world containing websites, desktop applications, MCP services, and local files, with stateful dependencies among them. On top of that world, it synthesizes XAI tasks that require agents to combine GUI interaction, command-line operations, and MCP tool use.

The main methodological contribution is the quality-control layer. Instead of relying on static LLM scoring, the pipeline uses execution-based multi-agent quality gates. Heterogeneous agents, built from different models and harnesses, actually execute the synthesized tasks. Their trajectories provide evidence for three properties: solvability, difficulty, and judge consistency. This is important because synthetic tasks often fail in two ways: they are superficially plausible but impossible under the generated world state, or they come with weak verifiers that produce noisy rewards.

A second notable idea is verifier stress testing via sycophancy probes. The authors inject deliberately incorrect outcome feedback and check whether the judge follows the misleading signal. This is a practical way to test reward robustness and is closely related to reward hacking concerns in RLHF-style pipelines.

For training, the paper runs online RL on two Qwen models, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.5-4B, using an OpenCode CLI harness augmented with GUI and MCP tools. The key empirical claim is that training exclusively in these synthetic worlds improves performance on all eight out-of-distribution benchmarks spanning web, claw, and computer-use agent settings. The paper’s emphasis is less on squeezing benchmark maxima and more on demonstrating transfer from synthetic interactive worlds to external environments.

The most important experimental signal is the role of the quality gates and verifier checks. Without execution-based filtering, synthetic tasks are likely to be either unsolvable or weakly verified, both of which would poison RL. 为了解决真实奖励昂贵的问题，作者引入 Research World Model, RWM。它不是单独训练的回归器，而是一个冻结的大语言模型，输入包括任务描述、reference 配置、历史实验记录以及候选 proposal，输出该 proposal 相对 baseline 的预期 gain。也就是说，RWM 通过 in-context experimental records 来做 outcome prediction，再把预测 gain 变成 RL reward。这样避免了为每个 proposal 都跑一次完整训练。

但作者的重点不只是“用 LLM 当 reward model”，而是处理分布漂移。随着 proposer 经 RL 更新，它会越来越倾向于生成 RWM 评分高、但历史记录覆盖不足的 proposal。固定记录下的 RWM 会在这些区域系统性高估，RL 随后放大这种偏差。为此，LM2LM 引入 on-policy calibration：周期性地从当前策略生成的 proposal 中，挑选 RWM 排名靠前的一部分做真实执行，把测得的真实 gain 追加进 RWM 的上下文知识，再继续训练。这个设计有两个关键点。其一，不是随机补数据，而是专门校准当前策略最可能 exploit 的高分区域；其二，不重新训练 reward model，而是通过更新 in-context records 让冻结 LLM 的判断重新锚定到真实结果。

从方法取舍看，这比纯离线 preference learning 更适合高成本实验环境，因为它把真实执行预算集中花在“最危险的误差区域”；也比全量在线 RL 便宜得多，因为绝大多数 proposal 仍由 RWM 评估。论文还分析了 RWM 的 ranking quality，说明在这类任务里，排序能力比绝对数值回归更关键，RL 只需要相对好坏足够稳定即可。1e:T84a,LM2LM formulates automated LM development as a sequential decision problem. Each environment specifies a target model family, size, training budget, and a reference configuration. A proposer LLM generates natural-language modification proposals; a builder converts them into executable changes; execution returns the gain relative to the reference validation loss. The proposer is trained with RL to generate proposals with higher expected gains.

The key scalability device is a Research World Model (RWM). Instead of executing every proposal, LM2LM uses a frozen LLM to predict the gain of a candidate proposal conditioned on the task description, the reference setup, and a set of prior experimental records provided in context. These predicted gains become RL rewards. This is not just a generic reward model. The design relies on the frozen LLM’s ability to reason over structured experimental history and produce useful relative rankings among proposals.

The more important contribution is how the paper handles reward drift. As the proposer improves, it shifts toward proposals that score highly under the RWM but may lie outside the support of the initial experimental records. A fixed surrogate then becomes vulnerable to systematic overestimation, and RL amplifies that error. LM2LM addresses this with on-policy calibration: at intervals during training, it takes a subset of high-scoring proposals from the current policy, executes them for real, and appends the measured outcomes to the RWM context. This keeps the surrogate grounded on exactly the region of proposal space that matters for the current policy.

That choice is deliberate. Randomly collecting more experiments would be wasteful. Re-training a separate reward model after each batch would also be heavier. By updating the in-context evidence of a frozen LLM, the method concentrates real-execution budget on the most decision-critical and most exploitable proposals. The paper further argues that ranking quality matters more than perfectly calibrated scalar prediction: for RL, preserving the relative ordering of proposals is often enough.1f:T56e,The experiments evaluate proposal quality in held-out LM-development environments using real execution. A GRPO-style setup with 100 updates, 32 states per update, and 8 proposals per state would require 25,600 proposal evaluations. If all were executed directly, that would cost about 1,152,000 GPU-hours, or more than $16M. This establishes why surrogate-based training is necessary.

Empirically, LM2LM outperforms baselines that rely only on past experimental records, and it also beats strong in-context approaches on real executed gains. The paper additionally claims that a Qwen3.8-27B proposer trained with LM2LM surpasses GPT-6-sol on held-out environments. The most informative results are the analyses of surrogate reliability. Early in training, the RWM provides useful proposal rankings. Without on-policy calibration, ranking quality degrades as RL progresses, eventually becoming worse than random because the policy learns to exploit overestimated regions. With calibration, overestimation is reduced and ranking quality is largely preserved, which in turn preserves real-world gains.
