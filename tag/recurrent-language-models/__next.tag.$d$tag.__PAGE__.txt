9:T639,The paper has two technical layers. First is the experimental design. The authors train four prelude-recur-coda variants with recurrence counts r in {1, 2, 4, 8}, where r=1 is the non-looped baseline. All models have 20 effective layers. Prelude and coda are fixed at 2 layers each, while the recurrent block has 16/r unique layers and is executed r times. This keeps effective depth and, at matched width, per-token training and inference FLOPs nearly constant across architectures. As a result, the main changing factor is parameter sharing, not depth or token-wise compute. That is a cleaner setup than fixed-parameter recurrence sweeps, which entangle sharing, depth, and inference cost. Second is the joint scaling law: L = E + A (N_once + r^φ N_rec)^(-α) + B D^(-β). Here N_once denotes parameters used once in the prelude and coda, N_rec denotes the unique parameters in the shared recurrent block, and φ measures how much recurrence contributes in parameter-equivalent terms. φ=1 would mean each recurrence is fully equivalent to adding the same amount of unique parameters; φ=0 would mean recurrence adds no effective capacity at all. This formulation lets the authors fit all 116 runs jointly instead of fitting separate Chinchilla laws and comparing them indirectly. They also fit per-architecture Chinchilla laws to study how compute-optimal width and token allocation shift with recurrence. Higher-r models prefer wider widths and fewer tokens per parameter, but because wider models are more expensive per token, they end up training on fewer total tokens at fixed compute.a:T4c4,The study includes 116 pretraining runs over six compute budgets from 4.64×10^17 to 2.15×10^19 FLOPs, about a 50× span, with r in {1, 2, 4, 8}. The central result is a joint fit with φ=0.46 and R²=0.997. That number is easy to interpret: recurrence helps, but much less than linearly. One concrete example from the paper is that at r=4 and matched training FLOPs, the looped model reaches only the validation loss of a non-looped model with 42% fewer parameters. A 410M r=4 model performs roughly like a 580M non-looped model, while costing about as much to train as a 1B non-looped model under matched token-wise FLOPs. So looped models save parameter memory, but not enough effective capacity to match the compute they consume. The downstream suite spans five axes. Parametric-knowledge tasks track the validation-loss gap most clearly. Open-book tasks narrow as validation loss improves. Reasoning-related axes remain near measurement noise at these development-scale budgets, so the paper does not find strong evidence that looping yields a measurable reasoning advantage in this regime.
