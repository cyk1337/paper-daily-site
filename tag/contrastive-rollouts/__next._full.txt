19:T4bb,CANTANTE uses contrastive in-group attribution. For the same query, it runs multiple rollouts under different joint configurations (different combinations of per-node prompts/parameters) and obtains system-level scores for each rollout. An attribution model (typically a prompted LLM) then compares these rollouts and assigns per-node credit by explaining which node-level differences account for outcome differences.

Notable design choices:
- The contrast is over full trajectories and final outputs for the same query, echoing counterfactual credit assignment in cooperative MARL, but without enforcing additive decomposability of the global reward (unlike value-factorization methods such as QMIX).
- Attribution is decoupled from the local optimizer: once per-node credits are produced, any prompt optimizer can be used to update each node’s prompt.
- The paper isolates attribution as the main driver via an identity-attribution ablation (using the global score as each node’s signal), plus robustness checks over attributer prompts/models, local optimizers, and dataset size.
