9:T5cb,The authors first isolate a simplified Muon update by removing momentum and Newton-Schulz implementation details. The remaining operation replaces each matrix gradient by its polar factor, keeping singular directions while removing singular values. This isolates the effect of gradient orthogonalization. The theoretical testbed is Burer-Monteiro factorized matrix sensing, which is connected to training two-layer quadratic neural networks and allows the gradient and Hessian geometry to be controlled through the sensing operator. The key model is mixed-spiked matrix sensing. Its sensing operator decomposes into signal, spike, and bulk components. The signal component is task-relevant anisotropy aligned with the ground truth. The spike component is strong but task-irrelevant anisotropy. The bulk component captures the remaining more isotropic directions. This construction mirrors a common feature of LLM training: gradients are highly anisotropic, but not every dominant direction is useful. The authors then analyze the landscape using a river-valley decomposition. The river direction corresponds to meaningful progress toward the ground truth, while hill directions capture sharp transverse deviations. In this geometry, GD quickly descends from the hillside into the river but then makes slow progress along the river.
