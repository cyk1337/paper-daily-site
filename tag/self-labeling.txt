18:T601,The training setup is GRPO. A step samples B prompts and n responses per prompt. Rewards are binary and converted to within-prompt advantages. The gold reward checks the final answer against a reference answer. The majority-vote reward marks responses correct if their final answer matches the most frequent answer among the n samples for that prompt.

Near the sampling policy, the GRPO update is a sum of prompt gradients. If the majority answer for a prompt is correct, the MV and gold prompt gradients are identical. The entire difference between the two batch updates comes from prompts where the majority answer is wrong. The paper decomposes the MV update relative to the gold update into shrinkage and misdirection. Shrinkage is the component along the gold direction and mainly changes the effective step size. Misdirection is the orthogonal component and is the part that changes the optimization direction.

The key claim is that misdirection is controlled by cross-prompt coherence of label errors. Independent wrong labels can be frequent and still average out at realistic batch sizes. Coherent wrong labels, such as a reward that systematically prefers confident outputs, frequent outputs, or outputs favored by a learned reward model, leave a directional bias that batch averaging cannot remove. To test this directly, the authors use per-response gradient sketches, allowing multiple reward functions to be evaluated on the same model samples and compared as update directions rather than as final training curves alone.19:T5fa,The empirical study uses five models from two model families. The main measurement is directional agreement between batch updates. MV updates align with gold updates about as well as two independent gold updates align with each other. After correcting for prompt-sampling noise, the cosine between MV and gold is statistically consistent with 1. The paper also reports that, at practical batch sizes, a prompt’s own gradient accounts for less than 1% of the change in that prompt’s output, supporting the batch-level interpretation.

Controls match the proposed decomposition. Randomly flipping up to 30% of gold labels leaves the update direction essentially unchanged, because the induced errors are incoherent. In contrast, rewards that consistently favor frequent answers, confident answers, or responses preferred by a learned reward model fall short of the gold direction, because their errors share structure. Training runs show the limits of the equivalence. The local gradient match holds at examined checkpoints, but MV and gold training do not become identical. Gold training remains ahead on held-out majority accuracy, while MV mainly sharpens single-sample answers. On held-out problems whose initial majority answer is wrong, MV training turns them into correct-majority problems at roughly three times the rate expected from resampling alone.
