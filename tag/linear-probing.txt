17:T642,The work has two parts. The first is budget forcing. Instead of modifying the model, the authors implement a custom LogitsProcessor that counts tokens inside the <think> ... </think> region and, once a budget B is reached, forces emission of </think> by masking all other logits to negative infinity. A subtle but important implementation detail is that the chat template already inserts <think>\n in the prompt, so the processor must start with thinking mode enabled; otherwise the token counter is misaligned. They evaluate budgets B in {256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096} plus an uncapped condition with a practical 10k token ceiling, and define the minimum sufficient budget B* as the smallest budget that reaches at least 95% of uncapped accuracy and stays there for all larger budgets.

The second part is mechanistic early detection. Using forward hooks, the authors capture hidden states at early checkpoints in the reasoning trajectory and train linear probes to predict whether the generation will eventually converge. The choice of linear probes is deliberate: it tests whether convergence information is already linearly accessible in the representation, without adding a powerful classifier that could obscure interpretation. They compare these probes against behavioral baselines built from surface-level signals such as logit entropy and repetition statistics. They sweep all 28 layers at token 150, then sweep nine token positions from 50 to 300. They also run a contamination check on post-cutoff AIME 2025 problems to argue that the convergence-correctness relation is not just memorization.18:T50b,There are four results worth retaining. First, token-budget saturation happens early. On both GSM8K and MATH-500, 256 thinking tokens are enough to recover roughly 95% of uncapped accuracy, so the marginal value of longer reasoning is small beyond that point. Second, AIME shows a strongly bimodal pattern: about 56.5% of generations converge naturally and achieve 96.5% accuracy, while the remaining 43.5% fail to terminate even under large budgets. The authors estimate that roughly 40% of inference compute is wasted on such doomed generations. Third, problem difficulty explains only a small part of the non-convergence phenomenon, with reported r^2 around 0.19, so this is not simply “harder problems need more thinking.” Fourth, activation probes beat behavioral baselines. In the layer sweep, layer 20 at token 150 is best with AUC 0.608 ± 0.080, and the strongest signal sits in upper-middle layers rather than near the output. In the checkpoint sweep, probes outperform behavioral baselines at 8 of 9 positions, with the largest early advantage at token 50, where ΔAUC = +0.074. The contamination analysis on AIME 2025 is also telling: converged generations are 100% correct while non-converged ones are 0% correct, reinforcing the link between convergence and answer quality.19:T5bd,The work has three technical layers. First, it introduces a claim-level audit. For each salient claim in an explanation, the authors create a minimal counterfactual edit that flips only that claim and measure the drop in reconstruction quality. If flipping a claim barely changes reconstruction, that claim was not actually grounded in the activation. They add edit-validity controls to avoid artifacts from malformed edits, and a transform control to show that the score mostly tracks gist rather than specific factual wording. Second, they build an exact-ground-truth synthetic sandbox to study training dynamics. In this setting, standard co-training of verbalizer and reconstructor consistently produces private codes: wording that is false relative to ground truth but still causally used by the reconstructor. They detect this using grounded-vs-true comparisons and evaluator swaps, which separate dependence on the paired evaluator from genuine semantic truth. Third, they propose RECAP, which shifts the intervention from the reader to the target model. Linear heads on external targets are co-trained with the model so that designated content becomes probe-decodable in the hidden state. Fresh NLAs are then trained on top of those activations. The paper also analyzes two intuitive but unsuccessful fixes: reconstruction-targeted objectives collapse previously decodable content, and frozen-probe supervision fails under representation drift unless realigned.1a:T5d6,There are four key empirical results. First, on a released Qwen-2.5-7B layer-20 verbalizer/reconstructor pair, explanations achieve high reconstruction quality, with floor-normalized centered cosine around r~=0.84, yet the flip audit finds that only about 2% of salient specific claims are reconstruction-dependent. The score is therefore carried by coarse gist, not factual detail. Second, in the synthetic sandbox, the standard recipe develops private codes in 5/5 runs across two domains, with grounded-minus-true gaps of 0.30 to 0.80. This cleanly demonstrates that reconstruction dependence and truth can diverge. Third, natural fixes aimed at improving the reconstruction test do not solve the problem: directly optimizing hidden-state reconstruction removes content that was previously probe-decodable, and rewarding grounding mostly changes which sentences the metric counts rather than increasing true claim-level grounding. Fourth, RECAP restores faithful reporting of designated content in the sandbox in 5/5 runs and transfers to Pythia-160M. Free-form verbalization remains incomplete, but designated content becomes decodable. The adversarial evaluation is especially telling: a report-space adversary that optimizes the deployed reconstruction score suppresses about 87% of its lie penalty, nearly fooling the score on both RECAP and controls, while the RECAP activation probe still detects lies with AUC 0.95 and recall 0.86; the control probe falls to 0.51, essentially chance.1b:T42f,The authors treat sender intent as a first-class interpretable feature. Their main binary contrast is recognized versus evaluated: a user shares the same artifact, but in one case wants acknowledgment and in the other wants critique. The dataset is designed so that the final message suffix is identical across the pair; only a preceding clause changes the intent. Multiple lexical phrasings are used, and evaluation is done with leave-one-phrasing-out cross-validation so the probe cannot simply memorize wording. The core method is a linear probe on the hidden state at the generation-prompt position, where the model is about to answer. They then analyze depth: at which layers intent becomes decodable, and at which layers it actually starts to influence the output. Finally, they apply activation steering along a direction tied to the probe to test whether behavior can be shifted toward honoring the inferred intent without adding an explicit instruction. The central claim is not just that intent is decodable, but that representation and readout are dissociable.1c:T4ff,The experiments span six models and four task families, with the paper emphasizing that the representation is already present in base checkpoints rather than being purely a product of instruction tuning. In the main setup on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, the authors use n=120 messages, extract the hidden state at the generation-prompt position, standardize it, reduce dimensionality with PCA to at most 40 components, and train an L2-regularized logistic probe. Generalization is tested with GroupKFold over phrasing templates, and chance is estimated with a shuffled-label permutation baseline rather than assumed from nominal class balance. The probe cleanly decodes intent and generalizes to cases where the intent is only pragmatically inferred rather than explicitly stated. A valence control suggests the probe is not merely reading warmth or emotional tone. The depth analysis is the most informative result: intent becomes decodable several layers before it reliably drives the output, and across models the difference appears as a model-specific readout stratification. In models where the represent-but-do-not-act gap is behaviorally visible, steering along the relevant direction recovers intent-honoring behavior, with an effect comparable to giving an explicit instruction.1d:T7f5,The setup is deliberately narrow and controlled. The authors use the camera-relative direction subset of ViewSpatial-Bench and decompose items into pure horizontal, vertical, and depth axes, which avoids mixing different spatial factors. They evaluate both the native 18-way task accuracy and a within-axis binary accuracy. On top of this task decomposition, they run a standard probe-and-steer pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. A five-fold cross-validated logistic probe is trained on PCA-50 residual-stream features at the decision token, layer by layer, to decode each axis. Steering directions are then extracted either from class mean differences or from back-projected probe weights, and injected as a training-free activation edit in the L20-L24 band, scaled by residual-stream standard deviations and concentrated on top-k neurons with a single global coefficient. Up to this point, the setup mirrors the usual latent-knowledge recovery recipe. The key addition is the vision-ablation arbiter. The image is replaced by a gray blank while keeping the prompt, answer options, and decoding unchanged. The signed gap between real-image and blank-image performance is then used to classify each axis into one of three regimes: grounded-correct if real-image performance is higher and blank performance collapses toward chance; grounded-inverted if real-image performance is below chance but blank performance returns toward chance; prior-driven if real and blank conditions are statistically indistinguishable, including under steering. The paper also includes robustness checks against the objection that a gray blank is an unnatural input, using multiple ablations and mismatched but in-distribution real images. In later sections, the authors analyze the geometry of depth inversion and test minimal corrective edits. Some models can be fixed by a training-free rotation in representation space, while others require a trained low-rank edit, suggesting a spectrum from localized geometric misalignment to more distributed errors.1e:T80a,The headline result comes from the three-axis analysis on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Behaviorally, horizontal is strong, vertical is near chance, and depth is below chance. Probing tells a very different story: all three axes are decodable from the residual stream, with vertical being the most decodable. The reported within-axis probe accuracies span roughly 73% to 97%, with vertical around 94% and horizontal around 85%. The training-free projection result looks even more convincing under the standard interpretation: vertical task accuracy rises from about 59% to 79%, while horizontal remains largely unchanged, exactly the pattern one would describe as unlocking latent knowledge. The gray-image arbiter overturns that interpretation. Horizontal drops from about 82% with the real image to 49% with the blank, so it is genuinely vision-grounded. Depth rises from 31% with the real image to 45% with the blank, showing that the error requires looking at the image and is therefore grounded but inverted. Vertical stays essentially unchanged, about 59% versus 60%, and the projection gain is almost identical with and without vision, roughly +21 versus +19, indicating that the decoded and amplified signal is a prior rather than visual grounding. The broader sweep covers 14 VLMs from 6 LM families, ranging from 2B to 27B parameters. The recurring pattern is that horizontal tends to be grounded-correct in capable models, vertical is often prior-driven, and depth inversion emerges with scale within families. Another important result is the task boundary: the same models that invert camera-egocentric depth can still be grounded-correct on near-field and metric depth, which argues against a generic lack of depth understanding and points instead to a specific coordinate-mapping failure. In the correction study, depth inversion replicates in 7 of 8 tested models across 5 families. The cleanest model can be corrected by a training-free rotation with performance close to a trained edit, whereas more distributed models need a low-rank trained intervention.1f:T471,The paper introduces a dual diagnostic framework: layer-wise linear probes measure when the answer is externally recoverable (availability), while Context-Stripped Decoding (CSD) tests whether the representation at a given layer is already sufficient for the model itself to decode the answer after stripping downstream contextual leakage (readiness). Using this lens, the authors describe a brewing-to-resolution lifecycle and partition samples into four outcomes: Resolved (answer is brewed and then correctly read out), Overprocessed (a usable answer forms early but is degraded in later layers), Misresolved (the model brews into a wrong answer and stabilizes it), and Unresolved (no usable answer forms by the final layer). They design six synthetic code task families—Value Tracking, Computing, Conditional, Function Call, Loop, Loop-unrolled—with single-digit outputs, ensuring a one-token target across tokenizers and a unified 11-way diagnostic space. Value Tracking reaches 70.8% Resolved while semantically related Loop-unrolled reaches only 28.0%. For Function Call, Resolved rate drops from 61.1% at depth 1 to 2.5% at depth 3. Across models, the brewing scaffold is relatively stable in normalized duration (about 24–42% of total layers), whereas resolution success varies substantially with capability, scale, and training. Causal interventions validate the taxonomy: Overprocessed samples benefit from layer skipping, while Unresolved samples can be improved by re-injecting activations from the correct layer, indicating intervention-sensitive computational states rather than post hoc labels.21:T47f,The work instantiates the Bayesian ‘hypothesis space’ as a metric conceptual space and lifts it to a belief space of distributions.
1) Conceptual space: following Gärdenfors, concepts live in a low-dimensional attribute space with a distance metric encoding similarity; concepts correspond to regions.
2) Conceptual belief space: beliefs are probability distributions over the conceptual space; belief updating becomes motion (a trajectory) in this lifted space.
3) Setting: story understanding is used as a natural sequential evidence-accumulation environment, where new narrative information forces continual belief revision.
4) Evidence stack:
- Behavioral: model outputs over story progression reflect systematic belief changes.
- Representational: linear probes on hidden states predict positions/updates in the proposed space.
- Interventional: manipulating hidden representations can steer the trajectory and alter subsequent ICL behavior.
A key design choice is to avoid fully specifying the Bayesian update rule for arbitrary text; instead, the paper tests whether a stable low-dimensional geometry exists that can support such dynamics.22:T44c,H-probes are a family of linear probes designed to recover hierarchical structure from hidden states. The controlled task is binary-tree traversal: nodes are randomly labeled, and the model must output the shortest path between two nodes. During generation, the authors collect activations at the tokens corresponding to nodes in the model’s final path answer. To reduce overfitting, they first project hidden states onto the top 10 PCA directions, which usually preserve about 90% of variance in the analyzed layers. The probes then predict two structural quantities: the depth of individual nodes and pairwise tree distance between nodes. This separates absolute position in the hierarchy from relational structure. The same identified subspaces are then used for interventions: removing or perturbing these directions tests whether task performance depends on them. Compared with classical syntactic structural probes, the target is not dependency syntax but hierarchy in the reasoning process. Compared with pure representation visualization, the paper adds transfer tests and causal ablations.23:T440,The experiments use both reasoning and non-reasoning Qwen-family models: R1-distilled Qwen at 1.5B, 7B, and 14B parameters, and Qwen 1.5 Chat at 1.8B, 7B, and 14B. Generation is greedy with a 2000-token limit, including CoT for reasoning models. The main synthetic dataset contains 1000 traversal examples from depth-1 and depth-2 binary trees, balanced between one-step and two-step paths. Transfer uses 1000 examples from depth-3 and depth-4 trees, with sparsity sampled between 0.5 and 1.0 while preserving ancestor-closed structure and target maximum depth. The reported findings are that H-probes generalize to held-out samples, converge to similar subspaces across training splits, appear across model sizes, and transfer from shallow synthetic trees to deeper and sparser trees.
