9:T558,The proposed framework is Token-level Bregman Preference Optimization, or TBPO. The starting point is a token-level Bradley–Terry preference model defined over next-token actions conditioned on a prefix state. Instead of treating a completion as an indivisible object, the method assigns a token-level score to each state-action pair and learns the corresponding preference density ratio. The estimation objective is built from a Bregman divergence family, which makes standard logistic-style DPO only one special case rather than the default formulation.

The paper introduces two concrete parameterizations. TBPO-Q defines the token-level score through a state-action value function Q. TBPO-A defines it through an advantage function A, emphasizing relative improvement over the state baseline. The derivation is grounded in a KL-regularized sequential decision formulation of autoregressive generation. Using closed-form expressions for optimal Q or A under a reference policy, the authors derive a token-level ratio-matching loss that can be trained from ordinary sequence-level preference pairs. The claimed theoretical payoff is stronger than prior token-level DPO variants: under the token-level preference model, minimizing the TBPO objective recovers the desired token-wise optimal policy, rather than merely improving a decomposed sequence-level objective.a:T40f,The paper claims three empirical benefits: better alignment than strong baselines across benchmarks and backbones, improved training stability, and better preservation of response diversity.
