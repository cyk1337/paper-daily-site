18:T740,The method, ARIA, stands for Autoencoder-gated Inference-time Unlearning. It has three main components. First, the authors pretrain sparse autoencoders on general corpora to decompose hidden activations from the frozen base model into sparse latent features. SAE latents are used instead of raw hidden states because they are more interpretable and sparse, which makes downstream detection easier to audit and less entangled. Second, they train a lightweight sparse linear detector on top of these frozen SAE latents to recognize forget-related generation states. Importantly, the detector operates over generation trajectories rather than only classifying prompts, so it can catch leakage that appears through paraphrases, intermediate reasoning, or indirect continuation patterns. Third, at inference time, the detector gates the next-token policy. If the detector is not triggered, decoding follows the original frozen model exactly. If it is triggered, ARIA applies a suppressive intervention to the token distribution to block exposure of the target knowledge. This is the key difference from weight-editing approaches: the intervention is local, conditional, and state-dependent rather than global and permanent. The theoretical section provides a useful decomposition: forget leakage is bounded by detector false negatives, while retain degradation is bounded by detector false positives. That turns the problem into learning a high-quality state detector rather than searching for a single ideal weight update. The feature-level analysis is also notable: because the detector is sparse and linear over SAE features, individual triggers can be attributed to a small set of layer-specific latents. The paper uses this to show that some residual retain loss may come from stylistic artifacts in unlearning data rather than the target knowledge itself.19:T57d,The evaluation covers three established unlearning benchmarks, TOFU, R-TOFU, and WMDP, on two model families: DeepSeek-R1-1.5B as a thinking model and Gemma-3-1B-it as an instruction model. Against weight-based baselines, ARIA consistently shows a better forget–retain trade-off. The headline result reported in the introduction is on WMDP-cyber: ARIA pushes forget-set accuracy below all competing methods while keeping MMLU within about a 1% drop from the original model. That matters because it indicates the method is not simply degrading the model broadly to appear safer. The robustness section is especially important. The authors introduce three post-unlearning adversarial attacks spanning both weight-space and decoding-space recovery attempts, including lightweight post-unlearning fine-tuning and decoding-time recovery strategies. ARIA remains more robust under these attacks, which is plausible given that the base weights are left untouched and the control mechanism lives at inference time.
