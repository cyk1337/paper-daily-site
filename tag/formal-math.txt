19:T874,Expected Value Alignment (EVA) uses a “surface discrete, latent continuous” design. Instead of asking the model to generate arbitrary floating-point scores, the model is constrained to emit an integer score from a predefined anchor set, such as {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}, inside a fixed JSON schema. This avoids the tokenization instability of floating-point strings, where a score like 3.8 may be split into multiple tokens and the first-token probability becomes a poor proxy for the intended value.

The key move is that the sampled integer is not treated as the actual reward. At the score position, EVA reads the logits or probabilities over the anchor tokens and computes an expected value over the anchor set. That expectation becomes the continuous reward signal. So if the target is 3.8, the model can place probability mass across 3 and 4 and recover a continuous estimate close to 3.8, even though the visible output remains an integer.

Training combines two losses. The standard language-modeling loss teaches the model to produce the critique, the JSON structure, and the discrete score token. An additional EVA loss applies MSE to the expected score derived from the anchor distribution, aligning that expectation with the continuous supervision target. This is the central distinction: the continuous signal is imposed on the distribution over discrete anchors, not on a generated floating-point string.

A practical detail the paper handles carefully is token indexing. Because token boundaries may not align cleanly with character positions in the JSON output, the method uses character-to-token mapping to identify the exact score-token location. That matters a lot in practice; if the wrong token position is supervised, the EVA objective becomes noisy or even harmful.

Relative to value-head reward models, EVA preserves the base LM interface and keeps rationale generation. Relative to direct numeric generation, it avoids fragmented supervision over multi-token numbers. The trade-off is that score resolution depends on the anchor design, since continuity comes from the expectation over anchors rather than explicit free-form number generation.1a:T654,The experiments are built around Leibniz, a process reward model for Lean 4 tactic-state evaluation, with dimensions such as Logic, Alignment, and Clarity. The paper’s goal is not to show a broad capability jump in theorem proving, but to show that EVA is a better way to train and extract rewards in this setting than common alternatives.

1. Comparisons against value-head models and direct generative numeric scoring, measuring regression quality, ranking consistency, or downstream usefulness in search/policy optimization. 2. Ablations on anchor design, such as different integer scales, argmax-only scoring, and removing the EVA loss. The key expected observation is that integer-only generation suffers from quantization, while EVA recovers part of the continuous ordering information through the anchor distribution.
3. Engineering validation of token extraction, showing that using only the first digit token is unreliable, especially under tokenizer-dependent digit grouping.
