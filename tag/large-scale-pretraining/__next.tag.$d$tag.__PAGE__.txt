The point is not sparsity for its own sake, but larger total capacity under fixed active FLOPs while keeping the architecture consistent across scales. MTP is added to improve efficiency and reasoning-related performance, and load balancing is handled without an auxiliary loss to avoid interference with the main objective. 2) Scaling laws: the Ling Scaling Laws are built from more than a thousand experiments and used as a low-cost “wind tunnel” for architecture and hyperparameter selection. The claimed contribution is not a new closed-form law, but an empirical framework that jointly tracks sparsity, active compute, stability, and final capability. 3) Pretraining data: the corpus is explicitly reasoning-oriented, with Ling Math and Ling Code emphasized and the reasoning-data ratio increased from 32% to 46% over 20T tokens. This treats reasoning data as a shaping signal for the base model, not merely a late-stage booster. 4) Mid-training: the paper inserts a reasoning pre-activation stage between pretraining and alignment, extending effective context length and introducing CoT data. The implicit claim is that RL works better when the base model has already moved into a representation regime suitable for multi-step reasoning. 5) Post-training: DFT with differentiated system prompts is used to create a diverse reasoning-focused initialization, followed by Evo-CoT to progressively deepen reasoning trajectories. In RL, LPO defines sentences rather than tokens or full sequences as the action unit, aiming for better credit assignment and stability. Preference alignment uses GAR, a group-based reward mechanism for finer-grained intra-group comparisons in open-ended outputs. 6) Systems: the full training stack runs in FP8, with activation/gradient quantization at [1,128] and weight quantization at [128,128]. This is paired with heterogeneous fine-grained pipeline scheduling, interleaved 1F1B, and partial recomputation to reduce bubbles caused by modules such as MTP and First-K-Dense. The model family spans 16B through intermediate scales up to 1T total parameters under a unified sparse MoE design. With roughly 3.5% parameter activation per token, the authors claim up to 7x active-compute efficiency relative to dense counterparts. Pretraining uses 20T tokens, and increasing the reasoning-oriented data ratio from 32% to 46% yields average gains of 5% to 8% on reasoning benchmarks. The WSM scheduler improves average benchmark performance by 1% to 2% over conventional learning-rate decay, and the gain survives later post-training. DFT plus Evo-CoT reportedly surpasses comparison models on competition-level math reasoning while requiring 25% fewer training tokens to reach comparable or better performance. LPO improves complex reasoning benchmarks by around 10% over token-level and sequence-level RL baselines. GAR improves consistency scores in open-ended evaluation by 2% to 10%. On the systems side, full FP8 training stays within 0.25% of BF16 after 900B tokens while reducing memory usage by more than 15%, and the heterogeneous pipeline design improves throughput by about 40%.
