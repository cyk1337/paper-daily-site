18:T610,FeaTok has two main components. First, it replaces pixel reconstruction as the training signal for the discrete representation with feature reconstruction. Instead of the conventional encoder-quantizer-pixel-decoder pipeline, the tokenizer is trained with an encoder, a quantizer, and a feature decoder that predicts patch-level features extracted from a frozen pretrained vision model. The paper studies several feature sources, including SigLIP, SigLIP2, DINOv2, and DINOv3. The important point is not that feature losses are added on top of pixel losses, but that feature-space supervision becomes the primary and exclusive signal for shaping the encoder and codebook. Second, image rendering is explicitly decoupled from representation learning. A separate image decoder is still trained to reconstruct pixels from the discrete tokens, but the tokens are detached before entering this renderer. Pixel losses therefore improve visual reconstruction without pulling the tokenizer back toward pixel-friendly but generator-unfriendly representations. The paper also introduces global-augmented generative modeling. A global image representation, such as a CLS token from the encoder, is discretized and generated before the patch tokens. This gives the autoregressive generator image-level context and improves spatial coherence and layout. Compared with earlier feature-target tokenizers, the distinctive move here is the full removal of pixel loss from the representation path, plus the detached renderer that preserves usability for image synthesis.19:T619,The experimental story has three parts. First, tokenizer ablations show that feature-only training consistently produces better downstream generation than pixel-only or mixed pixel-plus-feature objectives across all four pretrained feature backbones tested: SigLIP, SigLIP2, DINOv2, and DINOv3. The paper also reports better spatial structure in the learned tokens, suggesting that the resulting discrete sequences are easier to model at object and layout level rather than only at local texture level. Second, on class-conditional ImageNet-1k generation, the paper evaluates with gFDr6 instead of a single Inception-space FID. gFDr6 averages Fréchet-distance ratios across six feature spaces, which makes the comparison less tied to one evaluator. At 256×256, FeaTok improves over the strongest discrete baselines by 29% without guidance and 25% with guidance. At 512×512, the gains become larger: relative to BAR, gFDr6 is reduced by 43% without guidance and 39% with guidance, compared with 32% and 25% at 256×256. The paper also reports that FeaTok outperforms recent continuous generators, including RAE-style approaches, at matched sampling cost. Third, the tokenizer transfers well. Reusing the ImageNet-trained tokenizer, the method surpasses discrete and continuous baselines on three text-to-image benchmarks after only 75K pretraining updates and 500 fine-tuning updates. It also achieves higher rewards on three human-preference models without any reward-based optimization, which suggests that the gains are not limited to one automatic metric.1a:T50c,Start with the method figure and trace the gradient flow carefully. The key idea is easy to miss if FeaTok is read as just another tokenizer with an extra perceptual objective. The important distinction is which losses are allowed to shape the encoder and quantizer, and which are restricted to the renderer. Then read the ablations over feature targets to judge whether the method depends on one specific pretrained vision space. After that, focus on the global-token experiments. They are useful for understanding how much of the gain comes from better local patch tokens and how much comes from adding image-level context to the autoregressive sequence. In the main results, the most informative comparisons are the matched-compute plots against strong discrete baselines and recent continuous generators at both 256 and 512 resolution. For readers interested in multimodal pretraining, three questions are worth keeping in mind: whether feature-shaped image tokens are easier to integrate into a unified AR model with text, whether the global token behaves like a latent planning token, and whether the same decoupling recipe could be applied to speech or video tokenization.
