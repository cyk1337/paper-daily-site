18:T560,The paper defines internalization as a transition from implementing a computation through many explicit steps to implementing it in fewer steps through modified weights. The main empirical setting is semiautomaton simulation. A transformer is trained to generate CoT tokens corresponding to intermediate automaton states over T steps, and then a curriculum progressively removes these CoT tokens so that the model must predict the final state with less explicit computation. This separates learning the step-by-step state transition from compressing the computation. The paper studies task structure through modular counter semiautomata and relates difficulty to the arithmetic structure of the counter order. Counters with small prime-power factors appear easier to represent compactly, while prime-order counters are harder. The theoretical part analyzes sparse parity with a stylized one-layer transformer using linear attention and a fixed nonlinear MLP. The model first learns the hidden-support parity with explicit CoT supervision, then internalizes the autoregressive computation as CoT tokens are removed one at a time, eventually computing the parity directly. The paper also connects the mechanism to Positive Distribution Shift, where the training distribution is deliberately changed in a way that allows earlier learned representations to support later learning.19:T41f,The empirical findings are mainly mechanistic. On semiautomaton tasks, wider transformers internalize CoT more reliably than deeper transformers under a fixed parameter budget. This weakens the simple picture that implicit reasoning is just explicit reasoning unfolded vertically across layers. Task structure matters: among modular counters, prime-order cases are the most difficult to internalize, consistent with the compact-representation argument. Internalization also has a cost. Models that succeed in-distribution often show degraded out-of-distribution performance, suggesting that the compressed computation can become a shortcut rather than a faithful implementation of the original stepwise algorithm. The theoretical sparse-parity result provides the cleanest positive result: explicit CoT makes the hidden computation learnable, and progressive token removal transfers that computation into the direct predictor.
