5:T603,The proposed method, dGRPO, combines Group Relative Policy Optimization with on-policy distillation. The key idea is not merely adding two losses together, but aligning them on the same on-policy trajectories. First, the student samples its own long-context rollouts, so training happens on the model-induced state distribution rather than on fixed expert traces. Second, each rollout receives two learning signals: a GRPO-style relative reward signal for direct task optimization, and a token-level teacher distillation signal that provides dense next-token guidance under the same prefixes. Third, the implementation removes the usual reference-policy KL term by setting beta_ref to zero, following recent reasoning-model practice, which reduces memory/compute overhead and allows the policy to move farther from initialization when that helps reasoning.

The paper positions the method against three alternatives. SFT/KD gives dense supervision but is off-policy and does not optimize arbitrary rewards. GRPO is on-policy and reward-driven but sparse. On-policy distillation is on-policy and dense but not reward-optimizing. dGRPO is meant to inherit all three desirable properties at once: on-policy sampling, dense token guidance, and direct reward optimization. The accompanying LONG-BLOCKS synthetic dataset is also part of the recipe. It covers multi-hop reasoning, contextual grounding, and long-form generation, so the method is evaluated and trained on a broader long-context task mix rather than a narrow retrieval-style setup.6:T45e,The experimental contribution is mainly comparative and recipe-oriented. The paper contrasts SFT/KD, OPD, GRPO, and dGRPO in a Qwen3-based setup for long-context reasoning. The qualitative conclusion is consistent throughout the text: off-policy methods help with structure and imitation but struggle to recover from self-generated errors over long horizons; pure GRPO can improve reward alignment but is less stable and less sample-efficient; dGRPO improves long-context performance more consistently while better preserving the base model’s short-context capability.
