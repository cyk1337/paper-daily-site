18:T828,方法上最核心的设计是 NLI 化。对一条推理轨迹 Q, s1...sn，在第 i 步构造输入 xi=(Q ⊕ s1...s{i-1}, si)，也就是“问题+前缀”对“当前步骤”。标签 yi 表示该步在当前上下文下是否被支持。若一条轨迹的首个标注错误出现在第 k 步，则只保留前 k 个实例；k 之后的步骤被丢弃，因为它们的 premise 已经污染，继续标注会把“当前步错”与“前文已错导致的后续不成立”混在一起。这个数据构造很关键，它保证标签语义是局部且条件化的。

在模型比较上，作者刻意把三种范式放到同一任务定义下。Encoder classification：标准双句分类，encoder 输出 pooled representation，再接二分类头。Decoder verdict generation：把输入 prompt 给 decoder，让它在最后生成单 token verdict，通常是 Yes/No，并用这两个 token 的归一化概率作为 non-entailment 分数。Decoder classification：仍用同一个 decoder backbone，但不走全词表生成，而是取最后一个非 padding token 的 hidden state，接一个两类线性头；头部初始化还借用了 Yes/No 的输出 embedding。这里的关键区别不是“都在做二元决策所以差不多”，而是优化目标不同：生成式 verifier 仍在全词表 softmax 上做 next-token cross-entropy，分类式 verifier 则直接在两个标签上优化。作者的论点是，验证本身是判别任务，若还强迫模型走生成接口，会把学习信号稀释到不必要的词表竞争里。

实验设计也比较扎实。Encoder 侧覆盖 BERT、RoBERTa、DeBERTa、ModernBERT 的 base/large；decoder 侧用 Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B、Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B/7B，以及 Llama 系列等。数据覆盖数学、形式推理和多领域 reasoning 五个数据集。作者不仅比较最终性能，还比较数据 scaling：同样的验证质量，分类器需要多少标注问题，生成式 verifier 需要多少。这个维度很重要，因为 verifier 的瓶颈常常不是模型，而是高质量 step annotation 的成本。19:T866,The technical core is the NLI formulation. For a reasoning trajectory with problem Q and steps s1...sn, the i-th verification example is built from the pair xi = (Q plus the prefix s1...s{i-1}, current step si). The label indicates whether the current step is supported by the preceding context. If the first annotated error in a trajectory occurs at step k, only the first k instances are kept; later steps are discarded because their premises are already contaminated by an earlier mistake. This is an important dataset-construction choice, since it preserves the semantics of local, conditional validity.

The paper then compares three verifier families under the same instance definition. Encoder classification uses a standard sentence-pair encoder with a binary head. Decoder verdict generation prompts a causal LM to emit a single verdict token such as Yes or No, and uses the normalized probability over those two tokens as the error score. Decoder classification uses the same decoder backbone, but instead of generating over the full vocabulary, it takes the hidden state at the final non-padding token and applies a two-way linear head. The head is initialized from the LM output embeddings of the verdict tokens. The important distinction is the training objective: generative verification still optimizes next-token cross-entropy over the full vocabulary, while classification optimizes directly over the two verification labels. The paper’s argument is that verification is fundamentally discriminative, and forcing it through a generation interface dilutes the learning signal.

The experimental setup is broad enough to make the comparison meaningful. On the encoder side, it includes BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, and ModernBERT at base and large scales. On the decoder side, it uses Qwen2.5 at 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, and 7B, Qwen2.5-Math at 1.5B and 7B, and Llama-family models. Evaluation spans five datasets covering mathematical, formal, and multi-domain reasoning. A particularly useful part is the data-scaling analysis: how many annotated problems are needed for classification versus generation to reach comparable verification quality.1a:T746,The main empirical pattern is consistent. First, encoder classifiers outperform much larger generative verifiers on multiple datasets. The paper highlights that encoder models with only hundreds of millions of parameters can beat generative decoder verifiers with several billion parameters. Second, on identical decoder backbones, classification fine-tuning outperforms generative fine-tuning from 0.5B through 4B, and roughly matches it at 7B. That suggests the gap narrows as the backbone gets stronger, but generation does not show a reliable advantage. Third, the data-efficiency result is especially important: classification often reaches comparable performance with roughly one tenth as many annotated problems as generative verification. For step-level supervision, where annotation is expensive, this is a practical result rather than a cosmetic one.

One of the strongest comparisons is the same-backbone ablation: same decoder, same inputs, different heads and losses. That isolates the effect of the objective and shows that much of the gain comes from training verification as classification rather than generation. Another useful observation is that math-specialized backbones such as Qwen2.5-Math raise the overall ceiling but do not change the overall ordering between classification and generation. 具体流程是：给定当前上下文，小模型在每一步采样 k 个长度为 L 的候选 chunk；大模型不生成文本，只对这 k 个候选 continuation 计算分数；选出最高分 chunk 拼接回上下文，再进入下一轮采样。这样大模型只承担判别式打分，计算可以并行，最终答案仍由小模型逐块生成。

作者提出两种打分规则。

1. Likelihood-Guided Selection, LGS：直接用大模型对候选 chunk 的长度归一化 log-prob 评分，选分数最高者。
2. Contrastive-Guided Selection, CGS：用大模型分数减去小模型分数，即 ℓ_large - ℓ_small。这个设计的动机很直接：如果某个 chunk 小模型自己就很偏好，那么大模型的额外信息量有限；真正值得保留的是那些“大模型明显更认可、而小模型没那么认可”的 continuation。它有点像把大模型当作 residual teacher，而不是单纯的 reranker。

为什么要用 fixed-length chunk，而不是自然语言里的“一个推理步骤”？这是全文最关键的技术判断之一。作者发现，大模型 likelihood 对 variable-length reasoning step 有系统性长度偏置，哪怕做了长度归一化，仍然偏向更长的 step。原因不是简单的 token 数除法能消掉的，因为不同长度 step 的语义完成度、局部确定性和格式模式都不同。固定长度 chunk 让同一决策点上的候选具有相同 token 长度，分数才真正可比。

与 PRM guided search 的区别主要有三点。第一，不需要 step-level 标注和 reward model 训练。第二，评分信号来自通用大模型的 next-token likelihood，而不是专门学出来的 process reward。第三，搜索粒度是固定长度 chunk，不依赖人工定义的 reasoning step 边界。与 Best-of-N 的区别则在于选择时机前移：不是整条轨迹生成完再选，而是在中间不断纠偏。1d:T886,实验覆盖 5 个数学推理 benchmark：GSM8K、MATH、Minerva Math、AMC23、AIME24。模型组合有三组：Qwen2.5-1.5B 由 Qwen2.5-32B 引导，Llama-3.2-1B 由 Llama-3.1-70B 引导，以及更大的 Qwen2.5-7B 由 Qwen2.5-72B 引导。

It follows a generate-score-select loop similar in spirit to PRM-guided search, but replaces the trained PRM with an off-the-shelf large LM used purely as a likelihood scorer. The scoring unit is not a natural reasoning step but a fixed-length chunk.

At each decision point, the small model samples k candidate chunks of length L from the current context. The large model does not generate any text; it only scores the k candidate continuations. The highest-scoring chunk is appended to the context, and generation continues from there. This makes the large model a parallelizable discriminative guide rather than the generator of the final solution.

Two scoring rules are proposed.

1. Likelihood-Guided Selection (LGS): select the candidate with the highest length-normalized log-probability under the large model.
2. Contrastive-Guided Selection (CGS): score each chunk by large-model log-probability minus small-model log-probability, i.e. ℓ_large - ℓ_small. The intuition is that chunks already strongly preferred by the small model provide less incremental value from the larger model. CGS emphasizes continuations where the larger model contributes information beyond the generator’s own preference.

The fixed-length chunk design is one of the paper’s most important technical choices. The authors show that large-model likelihoods are systematically biased toward longer reasoning steps even after length normalization. Natural step boundaries are therefore a poor unit for direct likelihood comparison. Fixed-length chunks remove this confound by ensuring that all candidates at a given decision point have the same token length, making scores genuinely comparable.

Relative to PRM-guided search, the method differs in three ways: it requires no step-level labels or reward-model training; it uses native next-token likelihoods from a general LM rather than a learned process reward; and it operates on fixed-length chunks rather than human- or model-defined reasoning steps.
