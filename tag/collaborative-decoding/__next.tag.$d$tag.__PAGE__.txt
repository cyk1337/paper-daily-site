9:T781,The framework is sparse collaborative decoding. A draft model generates most tokens, and a stronger target model is invoked only at selected intervention points to generate a bounded corrective segment. That segment is appended to the visible context, and the draft resumes generation. The intervention is purely textual: no logits, hidden states, or parameters are shared. This makes the method compatible with black-box targets and clearly different from speculative decoding, which keeps target computation tightly coupled to generation.

The key technical question is the trigger policy. The paper first tests the most obvious signals: current-token entropy, negative log-likelihood, logit margin, top-k mass, and short-window velocity features. These turn out to be weak predictors of final correctness. The reason is intuitive: a model can be confidently wrong on a bad reasoning path, or briefly uncertain on a path that is still recoverable.

The authors then move to trajectory-level failure prediction. Instead of using a single token state, they summarize the confidence trajectory observed so far. Features are built from time series of entropy, NLL, margin, probability concentration, and temporal variation, then aggregated into means, extrema, percentiles, slopes, variances, and position-dependent summaries. A lightweight logistic predictor is trained using only draft correctness labels. At inference time, the system updates these trajectory features online and triggers the target when predicted failure risk crosses a threshold.

This creates an explicit trigger-timing tradeoff. Early calls have more leverage to redirect the trajectory but weaker evidence; late calls have stronger evidence but less room to help. The paper evaluates this learned online policy against fixed-position open-loop schedules, whole-example cascade routing, posthoc rewriting, self-consistency, and speculative decoding.a:T56d,Three results matter most. First, local uncertainty is nearly useless as a trigger. On GSM8K with Qwen-1.5B as the draft model, entropy and NLL achieve only AUC 0.531 and 0.528 for predicting final failure, and velocity features are similarly weak. This is an important negative result: common token-level confidence signals do not reliably indicate when a reasoning trajectory is going off track.

Second, trajectory-level summaries are much stronger. A lightweight logistic predictor over aggregate confidence-path features reaches AUC 0.86. That gap is large enough to support the paper’s main claim that trajectory shape carries information that local uncertainty misses.

Third, the learned trigger improves actual collaborative decoding. Across five GSM8K draft-target pairs, learned triggering improves final accuracy by 5.3 to 20.0 points over the best open-loop sparse-collaboration schedule. The method also transfers to StrategyQA and shows gains on MATH-500, although the paper is careful to note that benefits depend on alignment between early failure prediction and the target model’s ability to repair the specific failure mode.

The evaluation also reports target calls, target-generated tokens, wall-clock time when available, rescue rate, and break rate. These metrics are useful because they separate genuine efficiency gains from simple overuse of the target model.b:T43c,For a skim, focus on two parts: the table showing that local uncertainty features have near-chance AUC, and the table showing the much stronger trajectory-feature predictor plus the online triggering results.
