For a prompt x and a candidate first token v, the evaluator forces Y1=v, then repeatedly samples the rest of the response from the second token onward under a specified decoding protocol. A jailbreak judge J labels each completed trajectory, and the branch score h_x(v) is the estimated probability of a harmful continuation given that first-token branch. Measuring h_x(v) over the top-K first-token candidates gives a continuous branch-risk landscape. DHS weights branch scores by the evaluated decoder’s first-token distribution, making it closer to operational exposure. AHS weights measured branches uniformly, describing the average risk in the inspected neighborhood. CHBR@K measures hidden basins: among prompts whose top-1 branch appears safe, it asks how often a high-risk branch appears when the neighborhood is expanded. The paper defines an access path as any transformation or recovery mechanism that changes how the same targeted information is queried or extracted while keeping the underlying information approximately fixed. Cross-lingual querying is chosen because it is a clean instance of this idea: semantics are largely preserved while surface form changes substantially.

The experiments use cTOFU, a multilingual extension of TOFU, with parallel synthetic factual associations in English, Chinese, Hindi, Italian, and Spanish, plus romanized Hindi and Chinese. The original forget/retain partitions are preserved. The study focuses on one-to-one unlearning: unlearn in one language, evaluate in another. The contribution is therefore not a new training recipe but a reframing of what unlearning evaluation should claim. Standard metrics such as Truth Ratio, KS-test-based forget quality, and utility preservation can show distribution-specific behavioral change, but they do not by themselves support a privacy claim. A model family may look successfully unlearned under one language-specific evaluation and still reveal the same information under another language or romanized script. That means monolingual evaluation can materially overestimate forgetting strength.

The paper also makes three broader empirical points. First, this is observed across multiple multilingual model families rather than a single isolated model. Safety is defined by a predicate over the state space. The central quantity is k-turn certified robustness: the worst-case probability of staying safe for k turns under any adaptive adversary. A naive certificate gives a lower bound of p^k, where p is the worst-case single-turn certified safety probability. MTCR tightens this through embedding-space mode decomposition, separately certifying intra-mode safety and inter-mode transitions.
