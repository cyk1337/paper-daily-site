9:T6e7,The method has three connected pieces. First, the authors run a layer-wise degradation study. In the partial-RoPE stage, they convert only one layer from GQA to MLA at a time while keeping all other layers unchanged, then measure perplexity degradation. This isolates each layer’s sensitivity to different FreqFold settings. The main finding is clear: shallow layers often prefer larger FreqFold values, suggesting that principal-component concentration helps more than frequency approximation hurts; middle layers often prefer FreqFold=1, meaning accurate RoPE frequency preservation matters more there.

Second, they build a layer-adaptive GQA-to-MLA transition algorithm. The per-layer FreqFold assignment is formulated as a combinatorial optimization problem, then reframed as a multi-round sequential decision process to avoid brute-force search over all layer configurations. The central idea is simple but important: replace a uniform conversion hyperparameter with a per-layer allocation strategy guided by measured sensitivity. That is the main methodological difference from one-shot, globally uniform MLA conversion.

Third, they add a post-training pipeline to recover capability and inject financial expertise. This includes stratified data compression, financial-domain augmentation, refusal-data construction for compliance scenarios, and instruction-following reinforcement. The logic is coherent: structural conversion solves the serving bottleneck, but it weakens representation quality; post-training is then used to restore general and domain-specific competence.
