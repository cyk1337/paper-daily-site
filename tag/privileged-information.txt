The core design is a token-level routing mechanism that dynamically adjusts weights for the two signals based on the privilege advantage gap, measured as the relative prediction probability difference between the privileged teacher and the student on each token. Tokens where the teacher shows credible, transferable capability advantage receive higher teacher distillation weight; tokens where the teacher's advantage likely comes from inaccessible privileged information, or is weakly tied to core capabilities, receive lower teacher supervision weight and rely more on lightweight self-distillation to preserve training stability and encourage exploration. This design enables token-level adaptive adjustment of supervision source, strength, and granularity, avoiding indiscriminate fitting of non-transferable privilege signals.1c:T7bb,The core idea is to decompose the token-level signal from multiple privileged teacher views into two parts: a consensus signal and a residual signal. The intuition is simple. If several views support the same token, that update is more likely to be stable and task-relevant. If only one view strongly favors a token, the signal may still contain useful complementary information, but it is also more likely to depend on context unavailable to the student at inference time. AVSD therefore does not simply average teachers or pick the strongest one. Instead, it constructs two pooled targets. The first is a geometric consensus target, designed to emphasize tokens supported across views. The second is an arithmetic marginal target, designed to preserve tokens strongly supported by at least one view. Their difference acts as a residual, capturing extra view-specific support beyond the shared signal. Training does not add this residual back unconditionally. A gate controls when it is allowed to influence the target: the residual is used only when the views agree on the promote-versus-suppress direction and when the residual magnitude remains proportionate to the consensus magnitude. This design is motivated by a clear trade-off. Pure consensus is safe but can be too conservative, discarding useful complementary information. Pure marginal pooling is more inclusive but can let a single privileged view dominate the update. AVSD aims for a constrained middle ground. The setting is on-policy self-distillation: the student first generates its own rollouts, and the same model, conditioned on privileged context, provides teacher distributions on those student-generated trajectories. This avoids the off-policy mismatch of standard offline distillation. Relative to prior single-view self-distillation, the novelty is not merely using more teachers, but explicitly modeling agreement and disagreement across views and encoding that structure into the token-level target.1d:T5f0,The experiments cover math and code reasoning, with the strongest emphasis on math. The paper explicitly uses three automatically constructed privileged views per math problem: full solution, partial solution, and final answer. The model set includes at least Qwen3-4B. Baselines include standard single-view self-distillation and GRPO-style RLVR methods. The reported outcome is that AVSD outperforms both the single-view self-distillation baseline and GRPO on competition-style math benchmarks including AIME24, AIME25, and HMMT25. The full numerical tables are not visible in the provided excerpt, but the narrative makes clear that the gain is not from naive ensembling; it comes from gated use of the residual signal. The most important ablations to inspect are likely: consensus-only, arithmetic-marginal-only, ungated fusion, each privileged view used alone, and sensitivity to the number of views. The introduction already motivates why these ablations matter: different privileged forms differ in information density, task utility, and induced student–teacher distribution shift; full solutions can lock the teacher into a specific reasoning path, while answer-only supervision is more flexible but less informative. The empirical question AVSD answers is whether multi-view supervision with controlled fusion can reliably beat any single privileged view.
