9:T8c1,方法设计很克制，核心是“保留标准 student update，只在严格筛选的条件下做 student-to-teacher adaptation”。训练仍然从 student on-policy rollout 开始：student 只看题目 x 采样解答 y，teacher 在同一条 partial rollout 上、但额外看到参考解 y*，输出逐 token 分布。标准 OPSD 部分不变，student 继续用 teacher 的 token-level target 做蒸馏更新。VISTA 新增的是 teacher 更新，而且只在两层筛选后发生。

第一层是 rollout-level gating。只有当 student rollout 通过 outcome verifier 时，这条 rollout 才被认为包含了值得 teacher 学习的 problem-only reasoning 轨迹。这里的判断不是“student 比 teacher 强”，而是“这条 rollout 至少在结果上成立，因此其中某些 student token 偏好可能比 reference-conditioned teacher 更贴近部署时可行的推理分布”。这样做避免把 teacher 拉向明显错误的 student 行为。

第二层是 token-level selection。即便 rollout 最终正确，也不意味着整条序列的每个位置都该拿来更新 teacher。论文用 teacher-student disagreement 的 KL 信号做位置选择，只在 top-k 个分歧最大的 token 位置上，把 teacher 向 student 适度拉近。这里的直觉很重要：真正需要修正的是 teacher 最不贴近 problem-only reasoning 的位置，而不是所有位置。若全序列都更新，teacher 很快会塌缩到 student，失去 privileged knowledge，后续也就无法继续提供互补监督。

从损失形式看，VISTA 复用了标准 OPSD 的同类蒸馏损失，没有再造一个复杂目标。它的创新不在 loss 公式本身，而在“哪些 rollout 能作为 teacher 反馈”“哪些 token 位置值得反馈”这两个选择机制。论文还把这一设计与已有方法区分得比较清楚：RLSD 是在 student 侧用 verifier reward 约束更新方向；TRACE 是限制 distillation 的 token span；DemoPSD 是在 teacher-student disagreement 时重加权 student target。它们都在减少 student 受坏 teacher 影响，但 teacher 本身仍是静态的。VISTA 则让 teacher 在受控条件下被 student 的成功行为校准，因此改变的是监督源本身。a:T4a5,The main significance is conceptual and methodological. Conceptually, the paper reframes privileged distillation as a target-alignment problem rather than a simple stronger-model-supervises-weaker-model setup. That matters beyond reasoning post-training. Any setting with a conditioning gap between teacher and student—reference-conditioned teachers, tool-augmented teachers, retrieval-conditioned teachers, multimodal teachers supervising text-only students—faces the same issue. Methodologically, VISTA introduces a controlled feedback loop from student to teacher without discarding the standard OPSD student update. This is a meaningful contribution because it changes the supervision source itself, not just the student's robustness to bad supervision. Empirically, the gains are consistent across three Qwen3 scales, and the method stays lightweight: no extra sampling and no separate RL objective. For researchers working on pretrain/post-train interfaces, the useful lesson is that supervision quality depends not only on teacher capability, but also on whether the teacher distribution is calibrated to the student's observable information and feasible reasoning trajectories.b:T9b5,VISTA keeps the standard OPSD pipeline intact and adds a tightly controlled student-to-teacher adaptation step. Training still begins with an on-policy student rollout: the student policy sees only the problem x and samples a solution y, while the teacher sees both x and the reference solution y*. Both models are evaluated on the same partial student rollout. The student update remains unchanged: it is still distilled toward the teacher's token-level distribution, exactly as in standard OPSD.

The new part is the teacher update, and it is gated twice.

First, VISTA applies rollout-level selection using an outcome verifier. Only student rollouts that pass verification are allowed to provide feedback to the teacher. The logic is not that the student is globally better than the teacher, but that a verified rollout contains evidence that some of the student's token-level preferences are compatible with successful problem-only reasoning. This prevents the teacher from being pulled toward clearly bad student behavior.

Second, VISTA applies token-level selection within each eligible rollout. Even a correct rollout should not be used wholesale. The paper computes teacher-student disagreement and selects the top-k positions with the largest KL discrepancy. Teacher adaptation is applied only at those sparse positions. This is the central design choice. The goal is to correct the teacher exactly where its distribution is most misaligned with successful problem-only reasoning, while preserving the rest of the teacher's privileged knowledge. If the teacher were updated densely across all positions, the teacher-student gap would collapse too quickly and the teacher would lose its complementary value as a privileged supervisor.

An important practical point is that VISTA reuses the same rollouts and the same family of distillation losses as standard OPSD. The novelty is not a new reward function or a complicated optimization objective. It lies in deciding which rollouts are trustworthy for teacher feedback and which token positions are worth adapting. This also distinguishes VISTA from prior methods. RLSD constrains student updates with verifier rewards; TRACE restricts distillation to selected spans; DemoPSD reweights the target toward the student when teacher and student disagree. All of them reduce student exposure to mismatched teacher targets, but the teacher remains static. The analysis section appears to target the three failure modes identified in the introduction.
