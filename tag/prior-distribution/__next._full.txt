18:T5a0,The setup is simple but well controlled. The dataset is a credit card transaction table with 7 numerical features, 4 categorical features, and a highly imbalanced fraud label. The key stress-test variable is job, which has 494 categories with a very long tail; even the most frequent class is only 0.75% of the data. That makes the task hard to solve through semantic generalization alone and forces the model to deal with distributional adaptation. The authors evaluate Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 under a unified schema prompt, comparing zero-shot, 1/5/10-shot ICL, and LoRA finetuning. Records are generated one by one as JSON with strict validation and regeneration until 10,000 valid samples are collected. Evaluation has three parts: structural reliability via invalid generation rate, statistical fidelity via per-feature TVD between real and synthetic marginals, and memorization risk via DCR ratio plus exact duplication rate. They also track whether the rare fraud base rate is reproduced and inspect how marginal failures propagate into joint structure. The main empirical pattern is that more in-context examples steadily improve numerical fidelity, while categorical fidelity quickly plateaus, especially on high-cardinality long-tail variables. Prompting changes do not fix it. LoRA does, which supports the interpretation that parameter updates can perform the global prior recalibration that ICL cannot.19:T48b,The experimental signals are consistent across both 7B instruction-tuned models. Rare and minority categories are effectively absent from generation. The paper argues that this is not an isolated marginal issue: once categorical marginals are wrong, cross-feature dependencies also degrade. LoRA trained on only 10% or 50% of the real data substantially improves both marginal and joint fidelity, showing that lightweight parameter updates are enough to induce the needed distribution shift. But every LoRA configuration yields a DCR ratio below 1.0, which the authors treat as a warning sign for memorization risk, and exact duplication must be checked alongside it. One of the stronger takeaways is that the ICL failure does not look like a sample-efficiency problem that would vanish with a few more demonstrations.
