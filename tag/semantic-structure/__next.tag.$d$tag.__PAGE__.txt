9:T9c8,The paper avoids uncontrolled analysis on natural corpora and instead builds three synthetic toy languages where latent semantic structure is known by construction while supervision remains strictly one-hot. Each example is framed as a single next-word prediction rather than a full next-token loss over every position in a sequence. That design choice is important: it makes the one-hot nature of supervision explicit and removes the usual soft-label explanation based on repeated contexts with multiple continuations.

To measure what geometry the model learns, the authors use Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA). For each toy language, they define a target similarity structure induced by latent semantic categories, expressed as a Gram matrix. They then compare the similarity matrix of learned context embeddings against both this semantic target and the ETF geometry predicted by NC. This lets them track, throughout training, whether representations are organized by latent shared factors or by label-only collapse.

They train standard decoder-only Transformers while sweeping depth and width, and they analyze trajectories rather than only endpoints. The central empirical finding is that gradient descent first discovers a semantically structured geometry organized by shared latent factors, despite purely one-hot supervision. With sufficient capacity and enough optimization time, however, this semantic geometry is eventually replaced by the symmetric ETF geometry predicted by NC. In other words, semantic structure is not evidence against NC; it is a transient phase before the NC-like terminal state.

The paper also introduces a simplified mathematical model that reproduces this “semantic alignment first, collapse later” behavior. Its value is not that it fully models real language training, but that it provides a minimal analyzable mechanism: early optimization can exploit directional signals induced by shared latent factors across examples, while prolonged optimization under one-hot labels pushes representations toward a more symmetric label-driven endpoint.

Relative to prior work, the novelty is not another proof of NC and not another argument that soft label overlap induces semantics. The distinctive contribution is to study the regime where explicit label bridges are absent and still show how semantic geometry can emerge transiently. That makes the paper especially relevant to long-context language modeling, where repeated contexts are vanishingly rare.a:T5fe,From the provided text, four experimental conclusions are clear.

First, the same qualitative pattern appears in all three toy languages: RSA alignment with latent semantic categories rises early in training, then declines later, while alignment with ETF geometry increases. So the phenomenon is not tied to a single synthetic setup.

Second, model capacity and training duration matter. Larger models and longer training make the terminal ETF regime more visible; smaller or undertrained models can remain in an intermediate semantically organized phase. This is important because practical pretraining runs may never fully reach the asymptotic regime assumed by theory.

Third, the experiments are deliberately set up with strict one-hot supervision through single-position prediction, not full next-token training. That makes the result cleaner: the emergence of semantic structure cannot be explained away by multi-label or repeated-context effects.

Fourth, the simplified mathematical model reproduces the transient semantic alignment trajectory both empirically and theoretically, which gives the phenomenon more than anecdotal status.
