5:T4c5,A quantifiable Memorization Window is defined from two complementary perspectives: 1) Retention window, measured by the loss retention gap between the first and subsequent encounters of a sample, which marks the number of additional training tokens after which the model forgets the sample; 2) Generalization window, measured by downstream performance, which marks the training horizon after which continued training on the sample leads to overfitting. The effective memorization window is the minimum of these two values, representing the safe interval after which a sample can be reintroduced to the training stream. Based on this signal, Memorization-guided Data Reuse (MDR) continuously tracks per-sample loss retention dynamics during training, adaptively scheduling repeat exposures only when the interval since last exposure exceeds the memorization window, rather than using fixed epoch schedules. A core empirical finding is that when repeat intervals exceed the memorization window, the slope of validation loss decrease from repeated tokens is indistinguishable from that of fresh tokens, with no diminishing marginal returns; the only hard performance ceiling is set by the intrinsic diversity of the dataset.6:T4ba,Experiments are conducted on LLM pre-training setups ranging from 1B to 7B parameters.
