9:T767,The technical core is a structure-preserving textbook conversion pipeline. From 2,118 university-level mathematics textbooks, the authors extract and organize textbook hierarchy, propositions and their proofs, and textbook-authored exercises. The resulting supervision is built around two main record types. Concept QA focuses on definitions, propositions, and theorem-level mathematical statements. Gap QA decomposes proofs into intermediate proof obligations, so the model learns to fill local reasoning gaps rather than only imitate full end-to-end proofs.

The dataset preserves structure at three levels. First, hierarchical structure keeps the native textbook organization: book, chapter, proposition, proof, and exercise order. Second, dependency structure links theorem-level proof questions to intermediate gaps and preserves references among propositions. Third, curriculum structure keeps textbook order and proof granularity, enabling controlled comparisons between forward, reverse, and shuffled training schedules. Compared with NaturalProofs-style resources, this work puts more emphasis on textbook-native pedagogical order and broad multi-subject coverage. Compared with synthetic math instruction corpora such as MetaMathQA or OpenMathInstruct, the goal is not to maximize the number of QA pairs but to preserve evidential and curricular structure.

On the training side, the paper uses supervised fine-tuning on a 30B base model rather than proposing a new optimizer or RL objective. It also introduces structure-guided retrieval at inference time: instead of generic retrieval, the model is given context selected using textbook structure and proposition links under the same context budget. Another important design is the source-breadth study, where the authors fix the training-token budget and compare broader textbook coverage against deeper sampling from fewer sources.a:T6a9,The headline result is a large gain from full-corpus fine-tuning: the unweighted mean over six evaluations rises from 29.17% to 45.83% on a 30B base model, a gain of 16.66 points. The paper also reports the best result on five of the six evaluations and aggregate gains over three public instruction-data baselines. The evaluation setup matters: it includes both book-held-out textbook exercises and established public math benchmarks, so the paper measures both transfer within the textbook domain and generalization beyond the training books.

The more informative results are the controlled studies. First, structure-guided retrieval improves proof-generation pass@1 from 13.25% to 17.38% under a matched context budget. That suggests the retained structure is useful not only during training but also at inference time when the model needs supporting evidence. Second, across six subjects and three data scales, forward textbook curricula consistently outperform shuffled and reverse schedules. This is a strong indication that textbook order carries usable weak supervision rather than being incidental metadata. Third, under a fixed training-token budget, broader source coverage yields further gains, implying that mathematical supervision benefits from source diversity and not just more samples from the same stylistic distribution.

The experimental design is a strength because it separates structure, order, and source breadth instead of conflating them into a single final score. A limitation is that the validation is still centered on SFT; the paper does not yet show how far these gains transfer to larger-scale continual pretraining, RL-based reasoning training, or formal proof settings.b:T4db,Start with the introduction and the dataset construction section. Drawing from sensemaking theory, the authors decompose research ideation into eight ordered cognitive stages and use citation neighborhoods as conditioning context, producing full research trajectories rather than short proposals. The dataset, SciSense-Traj, has two modes. In Target mode, given a target paper and its cited works, the model reconstructs a plausible ideation trajectory that could have led from those citations to the known paper. In Infer mode, given the same citation context, the model proposes novel directions without anchoring to a known outcome. These trajectories are then distilled into SciSense-LM models ranging from 3B to 70B.

The most interesting part is not the eight-stage framework by itself, but the supervision design. Intuitively, open-ended Infer supervision might seem more creative. The paper finds the opposite: outcome-anchored Target supervision produces trajectories that are both higher quality and more diverse. The proposed mechanism is that Target trajectories spend more effort on constructive stages such as Schema and Elaboration, which organize prior work into a coherent explanatory structure, whereas Infer trajectories overemphasize early Hypothesis and Questioning stages, leading to broader but less grounded plans. In that sense, the constraint is not merely narrowing the search space; it provides a stabilizing structural prior for a difficult planning problem.

From a training perspective, this is best understood as trajectory distillation: generate structured research plans with a stronger process, then transfer that planning behavior into smaller or more standard LLMs.d:T50b,The main empirical result is consistent and simple: Target supervision beats Infer on both diversity and quality across model families and scales, without the usual quality–diversity tradeoff. The paper reports a 2.0% gain in aggregate plan quality for Target over Infer, while also showing stronger diversity under multiple automatic metrics. It also includes stage-level analysis showing that Target plans emphasize Schema and Elaboration, whereas Infer plans front-load Hypothesis and Questioning.

The downstream transfer result is more important than the planning metrics alone. When these trajectories are used to condition coding agents, Target trajectories improve the quality of generated scientific artifacts, especially scientific grounding, and also help executability and downstream utility. That suggests the planning text is not decorative; it changes downstream agent behavior in measurable ways.

In terms of scale, the dataset is around 100K trajectories and the distilled models span 3B to 70B.
