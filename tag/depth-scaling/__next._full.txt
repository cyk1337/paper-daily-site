1a:T643,The technical core is to make the extra amplification from repeated visits to shared layers explicit in a first-order perturbation analysis. The starting point is DeepNorm: in Post-LN Transformers, one chooses a skip scale alpha and a residual-branch initialization gain beta so that the cumulative effect of parameter updates on the forward signal stays bounded. For untied depth, the condition is roughly M(beta/alpha)^2 = O(1), where M = 2N counts residual sublayer applications. In a looped Transformer, however, the same physical sublayer is visited R times, so both gradient writing and parameter reading are shared across rounds. The authors introduce a visit-alignment coefficient kappa_R to measure how strongly gradients and sensitivities align across visits. If visits decorrelate, kappa_R stays small and the analysis reduces to the DeepNorm case up to constants. If visits are coherent, kappa_R grows with R, and the shared update is effectively amplified. This yields a loop-aware stability condition kappa_R M(beta/alpha)^2 = O(1). Under the scaling family alpha = (cN)^p and beta = (dN)^(-p), the usual DeepNorm threshold p = 1/4 is recovered in the decorrelated regime, but in the conservative aligned regime with fixed physical depth K and growing loop count R, the threshold shifts to p = 1/2. Based on this, the paper proposes DeepLoop: keep the DeepNorm Post-LN architecture, but use the more conservative scaling alpha = (2N)^(1/2) and beta = (8N)^(-1/2).
