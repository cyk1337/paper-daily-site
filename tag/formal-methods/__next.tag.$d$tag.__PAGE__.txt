5:T64f,ANCORA uses a single shared policy that alternates between two roles: a Proposer that generates new specifications and a Solver that attempts verified solutions. The method has three main components.

The first is a two-level group-relative update. On the Solver side, the update is close to GRPO, but replaces standard-deviation normalization with mean normalization so that the gradient better matches the MLRL weighted-pass@k objective. This gives rare successful trajectories larger weight. On the Proposer side, advantages are defined over candidate specifications based on the distribution of Solver outcomes, with the aim of generating tasks near the Solver’s competence frontier rather than trivially easy or impossible ones.

The second is iterative self-distilled SFT. The authors argue that sparse-reward exploration alone causes the Proposer to drift away from the valid-output manifold. They therefore repeatedly project the model back onto a region that reliably produces valid specifications and solutions before full RL. This is presented as a stabilizing requirement, not a minor warm-start trick.

The third is a UCB-guided Curriculum DAG. Newly proposed tasks are admitted only if they pass strict filtering, novelty checks, and Solver verification. UCB is used to select seeds from the existing DAG, balancing exploration and exploitation while preventing curriculum poisoning.

Compared with standard self-improvement setups, the novelty is not the verifier itself but the closed-loop coupling of proposer learning, solver learning, manifold preservation, and controlled curriculum expansion.6:T51d,FM-Agent is proposed as an LLM-based framework to (i) synthesize function-level specifications and (ii) perform Hoare-style compositional reasoning at scale. It is driven by three key insights:
1) Top-down spec synthesis from callers: instead of deriving specs from a callee’s implementation (which may be buggy or misaligned with intent), FM-Agent asks an LLM to infer the callee’s preconditions from the call site arguments/context and postconditions from how the caller uses the results/state after the call. Expectations from multiple callers are merged to form a more comprehensive spec.
2) Natural-language Hoare-style reasoning: since developer intent and design docs/prompts are in natural language while verifiers expect formulas, FM-Agent generalizes Hoare inference to allow NL pre/postconditions. Using an LLM’s strong ability to predict the semantics of small code blocks, it iteratively propagates postconditions along statements/paths and checks whether the derived end-of-path postcondition implies the spec’s postcondition; mismatches are flagged as potential bugs.
