The local component is a standard token-level uncertainty measure, with the paper comparing Shannon entropy and evidential epistemic entropy variants under length normalization. The global component is built from the hidden-state trajectory during generation. For a response of length T, the model collects hidden states into a T by d matrix H, forms the Gram matrix K = H H^T, and computes a matrix Rényi entropy from the eigenvalue distribution of K. The intuition is straightforward: when the model is well grounded, hidden states evolve along a small number of coherent semantic directions, making the spectrum concentrated and the geometric entropy low; when the model is uncertain, the trajectory spreads across more directions, producing a flatter spectrum and higher entropy. The authors argue that this signal lives in a different representational layer from token entropy: one is defined in the unembedding space of next-token probabilities, the other in the hidden-state space of semantic directions. They test additive and multiplicative fusion and find multiplicative fusion consistently stronger, which becomes the final GLU score. The method does not require extra sampling, labels, or architectural changes.1b:T512,The evaluation covers three model families and six benchmarks. A central empirical claim is that global geometric uncertainty and local token entropy are nearly orthogonal statistically, which supports the idea that they capture different failure modes rather than redundant views of the same one. In particular, the paper shows that a nontrivial set of wrong answers lies in the low-token-entropy but high-geometric-entropy region, exactly where logit-only confidence methods tend to fail. The main results indicate that multiplicative GLU outperforms either component alone and also beats additive fusion for reliability prediction.
