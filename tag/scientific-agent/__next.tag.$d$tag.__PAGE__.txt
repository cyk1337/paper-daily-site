The key idea is not a new foundation model, but adaptive orchestration across domains with different verification costs and safety constraints. In materials science, the system interfaces with a semi-automated CVD setup, proposes candidate recipes under lab constraints, and relies on experts to select and execute promising options. In another materials setting, Gemini 3 Deep Think is used for fast inference and direct hardware control to drive single-attempt growth of 2D semiconductors. Each task is packaged as a self-contained unit with aligned components including a task description, fixed inputs, an execution environment, a deliverable contract, and a task-specific grader. The curation follows four principles: representativeness, complexity, diversity, and verifiability. In practice, that means tasks must resemble real professional workflows, require dependency-aware multi-step execution, avoid trivial template variation, and support automated, repeatable scoring through files, numerical checks, plots, or explicit acceptance criteria.

The evaluation protocol is built around two metrics. Pass Rate is the primary metric and requires satisfying the full task contract. Avg. This distinction is central to the paper: many agents can produce some correct intermediate outputs, but still fail the workflow because the final bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or not reproducible.
