17:T445,TaskPress uses a task guide as a meta-query. During prefill, the task description is appended after the context. The model computes attention from task-guide tokens to context tokens, and the KV entries for the task guide are immediately discarded after prefill. The retention score has two factors. The key-side score is derived from the attention map between the task guide and the context, with aggregation over guide tokens and one-dimensional average pooling to capture local spans. The value-side score uses the per-token quantization scale factor γ, which is proportional to the maximum magnitude of the value vector under per-token quantization and acts as a proxy for activation outliers. The final score is the elementwise product of key importance and value-outlier importance, so a retained token must be both task-retrievable and representation-rich. For known tasks, the guide can be generated zero-shot by an off-the-shelf LLM. For unknown or changing tasks, the authors use a small calibration set of queries and evolutionary prompt optimization to infer and refine the guide.18:T475,1) Aggregate final scores show no statistically significant quantization gap after multiple-comparison correction; the largest 2.0-point gap is only 1.3× run-to-run noise, creating a false lossless appearance. 2) Per-step decomposition reveals quantization does not alter failure mode sets (0.97 correlation between BF16 and INT4 hallucinated tool names, only 0.18% novel errors) but amplifies frequency: 1× to 4.6× across cells, with Gemma-4 dense in telecom showing 2.5× more out-of-list calls (649 → 1,646). 3) Error budget masking is verified: tightening allowed failed calls from 10 to 2 per episode widens the INT4 gap from 1.3 to 16.7 points, with the full 15.4-point significant gap localized exclusively to the high-amplification cell (max 0.2 points elsewhere); a targeted repair prompt only improves scores in that cell. 4) Damage is gated by full-precision failure propensity, not size or architecture: Qwen-3.6 with rare BF16 hallucinations shows no amplification, while Gemma-4 with inherent hallucination tendency is significantly amplified, consistent with quantization noise only flipping thin-margin argmax decisions.19:T5b9,GaugeQuant learns orthogonal rotations at transformer boundaries so that activation mass is spread more evenly across dimensions. The paper identifies usable symmetries in attention and MLP blocks. Value vectors in attention admit an SO(d_k) rotation, with corresponding transformations of W_V and W_O preserving the exact output; under GQA, each KV head group shares its rotation. Query/key rotations are constrained by RoPE: they must commute with the RoPE matrix, reducing the available symmetry to U(1)^{d_k/2}, which the paper does not exploit. In the SwiGLU MLP, the down-projection boundary also admits an invariance, but the rotation cannot be fully absorbed upstream because SiLU and the element-wise product do not commute with a general dense rotation. As a result, H·R remains a runtime operation while R^T can be fused into W_down. Instead of simulating quantization, the method adds a LogSumExp penalty on rotated boundary activations. LogSumExp acts as a smooth dense proxy for the infinity norm, which matters because the uniform quantization step size is driven by the largest outlier. A stop-gradient operator ensures that this auxiliary term updates only the rotation matrices, while the base model parameters are still trained only by cross-entropy. Compared with SpinQuant-style post-training rotation search, GaugeQuant does not require calibration data, a frozen model, or a non-differentiable quantization operator in the optimization loop.1a:T5c5,The method has three layers. First, the paper analyzes non-orthogonality from both tensor approximation and dot-product error perspectives. The key point is that the error of low-rank approximation composed with quantization is not just the sum of two independent errors; cross terms appear, so the composition cannot be treated as harmless stacking. Second, it studies ordering and derives that low-rank decomposition should be applied before quantization. The intuition is sensible: if quantization is applied first, the subsequent low-rank fit is performed on an already distorted matrix, so the principal subspace estimate itself is biased by quantization noise. If low-rank decomposition comes first, the dominant structure is extracted before discretization, and the later quantization acts on a more compact and regularized representation. Third, the paper introduces DAM, a Diagonal Adhesive Method, motivated by activation outliers. After low-rank decomposition, representation statistics can shift so that a few large-magnitude channels dominate block-wise quantization scales, reducing effective resolution for the majority of values. DAM keeps a diagonal-form corrective component to preserve scale information that is poorly captured by the joint low-rank-plus-low-bit approximation. The diagonal structure is important: it adds little compute and memory overhead and can be inserted into existing pipelines without redesigning the main matrix multiplication path.1b:T492,From the provided full-text content, the experiments are organized around validating three claims rather than chasing a single best number: combined low-rank plus quantization is worse than an independence assumption would predict; low-rank-then-quantize is better than the reverse order; and DAM recovers a meaningful portion of the loss in the high-compression regime. The central idea is to treat trainer-side and rollout-side errors differently instead of forcing a single quantization scheme over both. The diagnostic step is crucial: the authors separately quantize weights and activations during rollout and compare W4A16 against W16A4. W16A4 closely reproduces the failure mode of full W4A4, while W4A16 is much more stable, showing that activation quantization dominates the instability and that weight and activation errors are roughly additive. Based on this, QUADS has two components. First, trainer-side asymmetric QAT: weights are fake-quantized to NVFP4, but activations remain in BF16. The rationale is that weights are synchronized across rollout and trainer engines, so their quantization–dequantization path can be aligned; activations are recomputed online during rollout and cannot be faithfully matched by symmetric W4A4 fake quantization on the learner side. Second, rollout-side residual activation compensation: since activation error is the main problem, the rollout engine performs a second-pass quantization on high-residual channels to reduce activation-induced log-prob bias while preserving native W4A4 GEMMs. The implementation uses fused kernels so that most of the throughput benefit of FP4 Tensor Cores is retained. This differs from prior work in an important way. Weight-only NVFP4 methods such as QeRL never enter the true W4A4 regime because activations stay in BF16, and FP8/INT8 RL systems operate on a much denser numerical grid where mismatch can often be controlled by clipping or truncated importance sampling. QUADS is designed specifically for full-parameter MoE GRPO with native FP4 activation quantization.1e:T480,The empirical story is straightforward.
