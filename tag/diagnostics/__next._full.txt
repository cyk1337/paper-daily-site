19:T5ef,DiffScore reframes evaluation as masked reconstruction. A candidate text is corrupted under varying masking rates, and a bidirectional model is asked to recover masked tokens; higher-quality text should be more confidently recoverable under diverse masking patterns.
- Backbone: Masked Large Diffusion Language Models (MDLLMs) with an absorbing mask token [M]. The forward process masks each token independently with probability t; the reverse model is a non-causal (bidirectional) Transformer predicting clean tokens at masked positions.
- DiffScore-Zero: zero-shot scoring using the MDLLM ELBO. A key detail is the 1/t reweighting term, compensating for the expected number of masked tokens (~tL) so that contributions across timesteps are comparable.
- DiffScore-FT: task-adaptive fine-tuning without gold references, intended to isolate the gain from the evaluation paradigm rather than reference-based supervision.
- Diagnostics: (1) multi-timestep quality profiles that decompose the score across masking rates (low t ≈ local fluency; high t ≈ global coherence), and (2) a bidirectional PMI-style decomposition to separate fluency vs faithfulness/coverage dimensions—argued to be structurally difficult for AR scoring due to directional coupling.
Core difference from BARTScore/GPTScore is not merely swapping models, but changing the scoring factorization: from a single left-to-right likelihood to a recoverability-based score aggregated over masking severities, reducing positional/directional artifacts.1a:T6fc,The central object is the information processing gap, Δ(q). For a hypothesis variable θ and evidence sequence X1:n, the model is prompted to produce: (1) a prior π(θ|X1:n), (2) a likelihood for the next evidence item, ℓ(Xn+1|θ,X1:n), and (3) a post-data belief q(θ|X1:n+1). From π and ℓ one can also construct the Bayes posterior p. The authors define input and output information as expectations of log-probability terms under q, and use an information-theoretic optimality result to show that Δ(q)=0 if and only if q equals the Bayes posterior induced by π and ℓ. Positive gap means the model’s stated prior, stated likelihood, and stated updated belief are not mutually consistent.

This setup is attractive because it does not require π, ℓ, and q to come from a true joint distribution, and it does not require access to a gold posterior. It only asks whether the model is internally coherent given its own probabilistic claims. The experiments instantiate this on sequential multiple-choice QA, where evidence is revealed piece by piece. The paper compares at least three elicitation regimes: belief propagation mode, where the model explicitly updates after each evidence step; batch mode, where multiple pieces of evidence are integrated together; and explicit-Bayes mode, where the model is asked to combine its own prior and likelihood according to Bayes’ rule. The analysis also decomposes the gap into interpretable components, helping separate errors in the model’s stated likelihood from failures to faithfully use that likelihood in the final update.

Relative to prior work, the novelty is not another benchmark for Bayesian reasoning. It is a self-consistency framework for probabilistic belief updating inside the model’s own reported semantics.1b:T5ed,The main empirical findings are qualitative but important. First, belief propagation mode is consistently closer to Bayesian updating, in the sense of lower information processing gap, than batch mode. Second, despite being less Bayesian, batch mode often yields better downstream task performance. This is the paper’s key tension: probabilistic consistency and task utility are not aligned. Third, explicitly forcing Bayes-rule computation does not consistently improve performance over the model’s direct update behavior. That suggests the elicited likelihood is often not the same object the model implicitly uses when making its final judgment. Fourth, when the model’s updated belief disagrees with the posterior implied by its own likelihood, the updated belief is often better aligned with an oracle likelihood from a stronger LLM. In other words, the model’s explicit probability report can be weaker than the heuristic actually driving its answer.

Another useful result is the strong Spearman correlation between batch-mode information processing gap and belief-propagation task performance. That gives a practical diagnostic: if the batch-mode gap is large, one should be cautious about trusting the model’s sequential evidence integration behavior.
