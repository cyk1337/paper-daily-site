18:T408,The paper analyzes eigenspectra of FFN representations. FFNs are a natural target because they contain a large fraction of Transformer parameters and expose an expanded latent space whose utilization can be measured spectrally. The authors compute soft and hard spectral ranks from FFN representation spectra. Soft rank captures entropy-weighted spectral spread, while hard rank is more sensitive to concentration into dominant eigenmodes. They then fit power laws between spectral rank and FFN width under a fixed architecture and width schedule, comparing AdamW, Muon, NorMuon, and rank-constrained Dion variants. The key distinction from loss-scaling work is that the measured object is representation geometry, not only validation perplexity. The paper also stratifies representations by token frequency, which tests whether optimizer-induced capacity changes are uniform or concentrated in difficult frequency regimes. Rényi-entropy spectral analysis is used as a robustness check across different concentration sensitivities.19:T47f,The strongest result is the separation between soft-rank and hard-rank scaling. Soft-rank exponents are relatively clustered, with β_soft in roughly the 0.66 to 1.01 range. Hard-rank exponents are much more optimizer-dependent, spanning about β_hard=0.29 to 0.82. AdamW shows weak hard-rank scaling, around β=0.29 in the aggregate setting, while Muon reaches near-linear scaling, around β=0.82. On rare-token or TAIL representations, AdamW also scales poorly; the abstract reports β=0.44 for AdamW in this regime, while Muon substantially improves utilization. The hard-soft asymmetry is itself optimizer-dependent: AdamW has a larger gap, about Δ_1,2=0.37, whereas Muon and Dion(1/2) reduce it to around 0.14. The paper further shows that learning-rate tuning, longer training, and matched perplexity do not remove the spectral gap. Additional comparisons with attention-rank changes, RoPE removal, and partial PostLN configurations indicate that optimizer-induced spectral shifts can dominate or reshape architecture-induced effects.
