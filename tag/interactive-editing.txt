18:T5bd,The method is ActionSplice, built around Counterfactual State Transport (CST). Consider a frozen action-conditioned world model that generates an active chunk of T latent frames with a K-step sampler. If a new action arrives after solver step r, the current internal state s_r^- lies on the trajectory induced by the old action a^-. The target is a matched counterfactual state s_r^*, defined at the same solver step under the revised control schedule c^(m), while keeping the initial state, committed history, solver schedule, and stochastic inputs fixed. During training, rollback is used to construct these matched state pairs, but rollback is not used at inference time. Instead, a lightweight corrector predicts a masked residual in the backbone-native transport representation, moving s_r^- toward s_r^*, after which sampling resumes for the remaining K-r steps. There are two variants. CST_R retargets the entire active chunk when the action changes asynchronously. CST_T preserves the prefix and edits only the suffix starting at temporal boundary m, enabling finer-than-chunk action changes. An important design choice is that the method does not force a shared latent space across backbones. It defines transport in each model’s resumable internal state space: clean-prediction transport for one backbone and Euler solver-state transport for another. That makes the method more practical, though it requires separate correctors per backbone and per variant.19:T419,The reported gains are concrete. Relative to direct condition swapping, CST_R reduces rollback-relative LPIPS by 61.5% on minWM–Wan Action2V and by 75.9% on HY-WM1.5. CST_T reduces suffix LPIPS by 56.1% and 77.5% on the two backbones, while delivering 2.73x and 1.69x pixel-ready speedups over waiting. This supports the main claim that the method improves both trajectory correctness and responsiveness. On the HY-WorldPlay benchmark, CST_R reaches 25.66 PSNR, 0.6902 SSIM, and 0.1337 LPIPS against the original rollout, outperforming the reported comparison values in the paper. The evaluation protocol is one of the stronger parts of the work: it covers interruption step, directed action transitions, temporal boundaries within a chunk, and repeated interruptions, and it uses rollback as a matched counterfactual reference rather than relying only on visual plausibility.
