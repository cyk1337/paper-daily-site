18:T4e6,SESSE is a five-stage pipeline. Stage 0 runs the judge holistically on a development set and collects examples where the judge disagrees with the reference label. Stage 1 prompts the same judge to derive conditional evaluation sub-questions from those errors, using a format such as: if a condition applies, which response better satisfies a given property, A, B, or NA. These criteria require neither human-written rubrics nor oracle responses. Stage 2 embeds the candidate questions and clusters them into k thematic groups. Stage 3 ranks questions within each cluster using greedy farthest-first traversal, producing a diversity-ordered bank that can be truncated at different depths. Stage 4 asks an LLM to summarize each cluster into an abstract protasis and merges near-duplicate clusters through binary equivalence checks. Stage 5 first selects relevant clusters for a new example, then answers selected concrete sub-questions with A/B/NA votes. NA votes are ignored; A/B majority gives the final preference; ties are abstentions and are excluded from the accuracy denominator. The key distinction from prior rubric or specialist-judge methods is that the criteria are induced from the judge’s own failure distribution without parameter updates.19:T4d5,The study is organized around three components. First, it covers 21 general-purpose LLM judges across nine providers and three tiers, ranging from 8B-class systems to models above 100B parameters, including April 2026 frontier-generation systems. Second, it evaluates them on MT-Bench, JudgeBench, and RewardBench. MT-Bench contributes 2,391 pairwise comparisons with expert human labels; JudgeBench contributes 350 items across math, coding, creative writing, and analysis with objective correctness labels; RewardBench contributes 2,981 chosen-versus-rejected pairs with per-item position randomization. Third, it uses three protocols. The agreement protocol reports Cohen’s κ, Krippendorff’s α, and tie-excluded exact match. The consistency protocol runs each item independently 3–5 times with response caching disabled and with both AB and BA orderings, measuring test-retest reliability, self-consistency, and position flip rate. The bias-audit protocol uses paired AB/BA presentations and response-length analysis to estimate position and verbosity bias. The important design choice is to separate accuracy-like agreement from repeatability and bias, rather than collapsing judge quality into a single raw agreement number.1a:T43c,The experiments were run over five weeks in March and April 2026, with temperature set to zero. The full procedure produced 118 evaluation runs and roughly 541,000 judgments. The main empirical result is universal kappa deflation on MT-Bench: across all 21 judges, exact match exceeds Cohen’s κ by 33.8 to 41.3 percentage points, with a mean gap of 38.6 points. Even the best chance-corrected judge, Gemini 3.1 Pro, has exact match of 0.849 but κ of 0.511, a 33.8-point gap. All Tier 3 models show Δκ of at least 30 points. Rankings are not stable across benchmarks, with models moving by as many as 14 positions. The paper also reports a consistency-bias paradox: some judges can be highly reliable under repeated testing while still showing strong position bias. Verbosity bias appears much lower than in earlier judge studies, with all 21 models below 0.011, contrasting with 2023 reports where length explained 20–40% of variance. JudgeBench separates models more sharply than MT-Bench, with a 60.4-point κ spread versus 13.5 points on MT-Bench, about a 4.5× difference.1b:T492,The paper evaluates five non-finetuning interventions on RB2’s 4-candidate, 1–10 integer scoring setup, using a conservative decision rule (the correct response must be the unique top by mean score; ties count as wrong) and bootstrap CIs.
Key techniques:
- Task-specific criteria injection: add a one-sentence, category-aware scoring criterion (Factuality/Focus/Math/Precise-IF/Safety) to the generic judge prompt.
- Ensemble scoring: sample k independent judge completions and average per-candidate scores; use temperature=1.0 to increase diversity and leverage the API’s n parameter so input tokens are paid once while output scales with k.
- Calibration context: prepend a previously scored reference example to anchor the 1–10 scale.
- Adaptive escalation: run a cheaper model first and escalate to a stronger model when score variance indicates uncertainty.
- Soft blending/combination: weighted fusion of mini/full scores; tuned variants are evaluated on a held-out split to avoid in-sample optimism.
