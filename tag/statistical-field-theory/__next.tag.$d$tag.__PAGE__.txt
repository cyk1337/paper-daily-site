9:T51f,The authors map generated token embeddings to a high-dimensional vector chain and borrow language from O(N) statistical models to define measurable quantities. First, susceptibility is derived from a connected two-point correlator over embeddings and is used to quantify fluctuation strength across samples and token positions. A sharp increase near a characteristic temperature is interpreted as critical fluctuation. Second, the order parameter is defined from the ensemble-averaged embedding field. At high temperature, diverse generations cancel out and the average tends toward zero; at low temperature, generation becomes more deterministic and the average remains aligned, indicating a more ordered regime. Third, the paper applies the TwoNN estimator of intrinsic dimension to the cloud of generated representations. This serves as an independent geometric probe: if the system changes regime near a critical temperature, the intrinsic dimension should show a non-monotonic feature and typically decrease in the more ordered phase. The analysis is done directly in embedding space rather than through token entropy or lexical diversity. The authors also use PCA on the temperature-dependent order-parameter vectors and report that low-temperature generations collapse onto a dominant semantic direction.a:T4c1,Experiments use Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 32B parameters, with generation length fixed at 300 tokens and 1,000 sampled sequences per temperature. Prompts come from English Wikipedia, with controls including Chinese Wikipedia, jokes, poems, novels, and nonsensical text. The main findings are: a pronounced peak in susceptibility near a characteristic temperature Tc; a rapid change in the order parameter in the same region; and corroborating non-monotonic behavior or a minimum in the TwoNN intrinsic dimension near that temperature. The paper reports approximate power-law fits around the susceptibility peak with an exponent around gamma ≈ 0.1. These patterns appear across model scales and prompt categories, although the curves do not collapse to a single universal form, which the authors interpret as evidence that model size and prompt type act like different effective fields.
