9:T647,The system starts from frozen document embeddings with dimension 1024 over about 5×10^7 documents. HERMES first applies a Learned Semantic Transform, h_i = normalize(W e_i + b), with W initialized as the identity. The transform is trained to make the embedding space more quantization-friendly while preserving semantic structure. The losses include pairwise structure preservation, quantization-aware reconstruction, and an orthogonality constraint to avoid degenerate rotations. A three-stage residual vector quantizer then emits a code (c1, c2, c3), with codebook size K=256 at every stage. The first stage gives L1 buckets, the first two stages give L12 buckets, and all three stages give L123 buckets. In the observed corpus this corresponds to 256 buckets, roughly 65k buckets, and roughly 130k cells. Since all resolutions come from the same trained codebook stack, changes in downstream behavior can be attributed to granularity and sampling rules rather than to different reclustering runs. For training mixtures, the outer weights are kept at the L1 level using Uniform or DoReMi, which avoids fitting a high-dimensional mixer over tens of thousands of cells. The Stage-2 sampler controls which documents are drawn within the coarse buckets. The key comparison is between max-entropy coverage, which spreads mass across sub-buckets and keeps all documents eligible, and quality top-30%, which uses a corrected FineWeb-Edu reader to select the top 30% within the relevant candidate sets. The proposed explanation is candidate competition: the median candidate pool shrinks from 2,271 documents at L12 to 429 at L123, a 5.3× reduction.
