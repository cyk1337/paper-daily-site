9:T502,The method, HeadCast, starts from an empirical observation: attention heads in pretrained AR video models show stable and heterogeneous temporal/spatial dependency patterns. The authors group heads into four archetypes. Sink heads anchor strongly to the first frame or first block. Dummy heads attend almost entirely to the current denoising block. Spatial heads do not need the full history, only a local spatial neighborhood across historical frames. Global heads genuinely require broad historical context. After a short warm-up, HeadCast performs a one-time head classification at the maximum-noise step, t=1000, where attention is argued to reflect structural preference more than content-specific details. Each head is then routed to a dedicated computation path: Sink and Dummy heads keep only tiny buffers, Spatial heads attend within a fixed spatial grid, and Global heads retain the full sliding-window context. The monolithic KV cache is split into compact head-specific buffers. The key design choice is not merely pruning harder, but explicitly preserving the small subset of heads that carry long-range temporal dependencies, avoiding the temporal instability seen in coarser cache-eviction schemes such as Dummy Forcing.
