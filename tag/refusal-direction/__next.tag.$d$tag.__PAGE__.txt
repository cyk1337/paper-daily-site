b:T537,The paper introduces Minionese, a multilingual jailbreak benchmark paired with a geometric mechanistic analysis. It starts from 385 harmful prompts from AdvBench and constructs a matched harmless counterpart for each one, preserving sentence structure and length as much as possible. The benchmark covers 18 languages grouped into four resource tiers: Tier 1 has English, Spanish, Chinese, German, and French; Tier 2 has Arabic, Russian, Korean, and Japanese; Tier 3 has Turkish, Indonesian, Hindi, and Swahili; Tier 4 has Yoruba, Zulu, Scottish Gaelic, Guaraní, and Javanese. Each harmful-harmless pair is transformed with four perturbation types: standard translation, code-switching, transliteration, and translationese. This gives 385 × 2 × 4 × 18 = 55,440 prompts. The mechanistic part builds on refusal-direction analysis but decomposes the failure into harmfulness detection and refusal activation. It studies residual-stream separability, principal angles between non-English and English harmfulness subspaces, and projection strength onto the refusal direction. Two failure modes are highlighted: subthreshold activation, where harmfulness is internally represented but does not cross the refusal threshold, and semantic recovery failure, where the harmful representation collapses before the refusal mechanism can use it.c:T46b,The method, SafeVec, extracts a refusal direction from a safety-aligned reference model within the same architecture family, then scores a target model by how strongly its hidden states align with that direction under unsafe and jailbreak prompts. The pipeline has five stages:
1. collect last-token residual activations for safe and unsafe prompts at each layer;
2. define the layer-wise refusal direction as the normalized difference between mean unsafe and mean safe activations;
3. select a continuous layer window, avoiding early lexical layers and very late layers that may be dominated by readout effects;
4. use a small set of calibration models with different safety levels to map raw cosine similarities into a 0–100 Refusal Alignment Score (RAS);
5. score the target model using its alignment to the refusal direction on unsafe and jailbreak inputs.

This is a white-box method by design. It requires access to hidden states and benefits from having a family-matched reference model plus calibration models.
