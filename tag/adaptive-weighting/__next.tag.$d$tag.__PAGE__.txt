9:T524,The method is Adaptive Multi-Reward Projection, or AMRP. Instead of using a fixed weighted sum of reward dimensions throughout training, AMRP updates the projection weights online based on training dynamics. It uses three signals.

The first is relative shortfall, which measures whether a reward dimension is lagging behind the others. If a dimension remains under-optimized, its weight is increased. The second is reward volatility, which tracks instability in a dimension. High volatility suggests that the policy has not stabilized there, so AMRP keeps attention on it. The third is recent progress, which measures whether a dimension is improving, stagnating, or regressing. Saturated and stable dimensions get down-weighted; stagnant or regressing dimensions get up-weighted.

This design addresses the two identified failure modes in a practical way. It does not eliminate the information loss of scalarization entirely, but it reduces persistent aliasing by making the projection time-varying rather than static. More importantly, it counters shortcut lock-in by reallocating learning pressure toward neglected dimensions. Compared with manual reward tuning or simple normalization, the key difference is that AMRP is driven by observed reward dynamics during training rather than by fixed prior assumptions.a:T4a0,The evaluation covers three multi-reward settings: rule-based mathematical reasoning, citation-grounded generation with automatic evaluators, and open-ended alignment using learned reward-model scores. The main empirical claim is that AMRP consistently improves downstream performance across all three, with especially strong gains in math reasoning and citation-grounded generation, and smaller but still consistent gains in the more correlated open-ended alignment setting.

The paper also provides mechanism-level evidence. It shows that under static aggregation, training is often dominated early by dense rewards such as format or length, while harder dimensions such as correctness remain under-optimized. AMRP changes the reward-profile dynamics by shifting weight toward lagging or unstable dimensions, which leads to more balanced profiles over time. Another useful result is compatibility: the method composes with PPO, GRPO, and GDPO, suggesting that it is best viewed as a reward-aggregation module rather than a replacement for the RL optimizer itself. The emphasis is less on one dramatic benchmark number and more on consistent gains plus healthier reward trajectories.b:T5f5,SCOPE (Signal-Calibrated On-Policy Distillation Enhancement) is a dual-path adaptive framework that routes on-policy rollouts by correctness and applies complementary weighting schemes:
1) Dual-path routing: split rollouts into correct vs. incorrect groups using outcome correctness.
2) Incorrect-path (teacher-calibrated OPD): apply KL distillation on incorrect trajectories, but weight each instance by teacher perplexity. Lower teacher perplexity indicates higher teacher confidence and stronger empirical correlation with genuine post-error corrective capability; such instances are up-weighted, while high-perplexity (unreliable) teacher signals are down-weighted.
3) Correct-path (student-calibrated self-reinforcement): avoid teacher KL on correct trajectories to prevent suppressing the student’s valid alternative reasoning. Instead, use weighted MLE on the student’s own correct sequences, with weights based on student perplexity: higher student perplexity marks capability-boundary samples with higher learning value, while low-perplexity (already-mastered) samples are down-weighted to reduce over-reinforcement and mode collapse.
4) Within-group normalization: each path uses a normalization mechanism to calibrate the weight distribution within the group, stabilizing training and keeping weight scales comparable across batches/stages.
