9:T4bf,ArenaFlow starts with tournament-based relative ranking. For each query, the policy samples a group of trajectories. A seeded single-elimination tournament compares candidates, with a greedy decoding trajectory used as an anchor for the initial ranking prior. The judge does more than choose the better trajectory. It also marks pivotal success steps, extracts strategy-like skills, and attributes whether retrieved historical skills contributed to decisive reasoning. The framework then propagates credit at two levels. For step-level optimization, tournament survival depth is used as a confidence signal: pivotal steps that support wins in later rounds receive higher weight because they have survived comparison against stronger candidates. Depth-aware weighting and intra-trajectory normalization convert trajectory-level advantages into more targeted step-level training signals. For skill-level propagation, a global memory stores candidate skills. Their utility is estimated from group-level usage attribution; skills repeatedly used in pivotal success steps are reinforced, while stale or ineffective ones are pruned.
