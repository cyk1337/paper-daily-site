18:T6a7,The main contribution is the controlled experimental setup. The task is SECDED ECC memory-wrapper generation, chosen because it is finite, parameterized, and formally verifiable. The authors define four output languages for the same design: fully expanded flat Verilog (L_raw), parameterized Verilog with generate loops (L_param), a parity-check matrix plus wrapper options (L_hmatrix), and an 8-field configuration expanded by a deterministic backend (L_cfg). For the widest design, the target length drops from about 44,237 tokens to 69 tokens, a roughly 640x compression.

They then compare seven training regimes: zero-shot, few-shot ICL, retrieval-augmented generation, LoRA SFT, full SFT, continued pretraining followed by SFT, and reinforcement learning from a formal verifier. This design is important because it separates representation choice from training recipe rather than conflating them.

A second technical strength is the leakage analysis. The encoder and decoder depend only on data width, so across 3,584 valid configurations there are only 8 distinct encoders and 8 distinct decoders. Random config-level splits therefore leak byte-identical substructures between train and test. The paper quantifies this and switches to width-disjoint splits, which is the right unit of generalization here.

Evaluation is also unusually rigorous. The oracle harness has five gates: lint, elaboration, self-checking simulation with exhaustive single-bit fault injection and random double-bit injection, formal equivalence against the reference, synthesis/PPA checks, and rejection of illegal configurations. That makes correctness decidable rather than estimated from a small handwritten testbench.19:T597,The empirical message is straightforward. First, output representation dominates training regime. As the target moves from long-form RTL toward compact configuration-level specifications, generation becomes much shorter, the error surface shrinks, and formal-equivalence rates rise sharply. On long representations, stronger training recipes help only modestly because the model still has to emit a large brittle artifact.

Second, naive data splits substantially overstate generalization. With width overlap, the train-test near-duplicate rate reaches 0.65–1.00 depending on the split, and the leakage worsens as corpus size grows. This is one of the most useful parts of the paper because it shows exactly how structural reuse can invalidate code-generation evaluation.

Third, once deployment cost is measured properly, compact-interface learning is far more efficient. A sub-50M model paired with the verified generator reaches 0.89 formal equivalence, while a 32B fine-tuned model reaches 1.00, but the smaller interface-learning setup uses about 1600x less inference energy per correct design. The point is not that small models beat large ones; it is that if a deterministic backend absorbs most of the implementation burden, model scale and training sophistication stop being the main bottlenecks.
