19:Ta2d,The paper starts by defining the object of study carefully. It does not claim to access the model’s internal belief state. Instead, it defines a protocol-conditioned surrogate belief: for a fixed observation set D, prompt p, and query protocol π, the LLM induces a predictive distribution over values at queried locations. This framing is useful because it shifts the discussion from hidden cognition to observable optimizer-facing behavior.

The experiments compare two query protocols. Under POINTWISE, each query location is asked and answered independently. Under JOINT, the full list of query locations is answered in a single autoregressive completion. Even if the two protocols have similar marginal uncertainty at each point, they can imply very different cross-location dependence and therefore different curve-level uncertainty. The paper also varies three prompt dimensions: prompt style, explicit underdetermination warnings, and structural information ranging from no structure to function-family cues, structural hints, parameter hints, and intentionally incorrect cues. The key claim is that prompt text acts as an effective prior in a behavioral sense: it systematically shifts the surrogate belief toward certain function classes.

A central methodological contribution is the uncertainty-alignment criterion. Rather than checking whether confidence matches realized error, it compares model-side uncertainty against the ambiguity that remains among sample-consistent functions. In an underdetermined setting, a good surrogate is not just accurate where it can be; it should also remain uncertain where the data do not identify a unique continuation. The paper further distinguishes token-level confidence proxies from output-level uncertainty. Token-level confidence is derived from the NLL of the generated numeric answer, while output-level uncertainty is estimated by repeated sampling under fixed conditions. This distinction matters because token confidence in a language model is not the same thing as epistemic uncertainty over functions.

Another strong design choice is the sequential-evidence analysis. Instead of evaluating only the final state after all observations are shown, the paper adds evidence incrementally in different orders and tracks how the elicited surrogate belief changes. The resulting confidence updates are often non-monotonic, with a dip-and-recovery pattern whose location depends on evidence order. That is a clear sign that the exposed ICL behavior does not behave like a stable order-invariant Bayesian posterior, at least at the behavioral level.1a:T7de,The experiments cover controlled sparse-function inference tasks and downstream Bayesian optimization studies. The function families include Gaussian, quadratic, linear, sinusoidal, and logistic objectives, chosen for interpretability and control rather than benchmark complexity. The model suite spans Llama, GPT, and Qwen families, including open- and closed-weight models as well as uni- and multimodal variants. The setup intentionally avoids chain-of-thought and probes more immediate numeric judgments, which is closer to how LLMs are often used as surrogates in practice.

There are four main empirical findings. First, structural prompts behave like priors: correct structural cues improve the induced belief, while incorrect cues systematically mislead it, and that shift propagates into acquisition choices and regret in Bayesian optimization. Second, POINTWISE and JOINT querying expose different surrogate beliefs. POINTWISE is reported to be more ambiguity-sensitive and more faithful to the observation constraints, while JOINT is more globally coherent but less faithful to local constraints. Third, under sequential evidence, confidence does not simply shrink as more data arrive. Instead, the paper observes a dip-and-recovery pattern reminiscent of a Dunning–Kruger curve, and the position of the confidence valley depends on the order of evidence. Fourth, realized error and standard calibration are insufficient diagnostics in underdetermined regimes: a model can look numerically reasonable while its uncertainty is badly misaligned with the remaining sample-consistent ambiguity.

The paper, at least from the provided full text excerpt, emphasizes robust behavioral patterns and downstream decision consequences more than a single headline metric. During a skim, the most important pieces are the definition of uncertainty alignment, the quantified divergence between beliefs induced by different prompts/protocols, and the changes in acquisition behavior and regret in the BO experiments.1b:T51e,This paper is best read through its framing rather than through benchmark tables. Start with the introduction and problem setting to understand what the authors mean by a protocol-conditioned surrogate belief and why ordinary calibration is not enough in underdetermined regimes. Then read the formal definition of the uncertainty-alignment criterion, which is the most methodologically interesting part. After that, inspect the POINTWISE-versus-JOINT comparisons and the sequential-evidence confidence curves. Those sections make the main claim concrete: the language interface changes the surrogate that the optimizer actually sees.

For a skim, a good order is: problem formulation → prompt and protocol design → uncertainty metric → controlled function experiments → Bayesian optimization results. Around 25–35 minutes should be enough to capture the main ideas. Three follow-up questions are worth deeper attention: how the reference distribution over sample-consistent functions q_D is constructed and how sensitive the conclusions are to it; whether the gap between token-level confidence and output-level uncertainty is consistent across model families; and whether the observed effects come mainly from pretraining priors, numeric formatting constraints, or autoregressive sequence coupling.1c:T5db,They introduce Pando, a model-organism benchmark designed to break the elicitation confounder via an explicit “explanation axis” that controls black-box information quality.
1) Ground-truth computation: Inputs are tables with p=10 named fields; outputs are binary. For each model instance, a depth d=1–4 decision-tree rule T is sampled and then internalized into a language model fθ via finetuning (so the rule is in parameters, not readable from context). This yields exact mechanistic ground truth (which fields/thresholds determine y).
2) Fixed interaction budget: At evaluation, an agent gets k=10 labeled query–response pairs (xi, yi=fθ(xi)) and must predict the model’s decisions on held-out inputs (behavior recovery).

5) Mechanistic diagnosis: A variance decomposition separates signals due to task representation (field identity/value) from decision computation (decision relevance).
