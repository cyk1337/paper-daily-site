18:T674,The method has two layers. First, it evaluates rationale quality as certificate quality. A generated rationale is parsed into a directed acyclic graph of atomic claims and dependencies. Each claim is associated with a claim type, an evaluation criterion, polarity, and support relations. The certificate is then audited along three dimensions. Soundness checks whether claims are supported by the candidate responses and whether the rationale introduces false evidence or invalid comparisons. Coverage checks whether the rationale addresses the decision-relevant criteria specified by the evaluation policy rather than cherry-picking a few convenient points. Decision Support checks whether the local assessments made in the rationale are consistent with the final verdict, penalizing cases where the rationale argues for A but concludes B. Second, DeCert converts these audits into preference supervision. If two sampled judgments differ in verdict correctness, correctness dominates. If they share the same correct verdict, the model constructs rationale preference pairs based on certificate quality. This is the key design choice: it isolates rationale quality while holding outcome correctness fixed. Training then combines outcome-level and rationale-level preference optimization. Relative to RationaleRM, R-Align, and RM-NLHF, the main difference is that supervision is reference-free and based on validity of support rather than similarity to a target explanation. Another useful distinction is that DeCert exploits within-verdict variation among sampled judgments, which gives finer supervision than assigning a single scalar rationale score.19:T491,The empirical story has four main parts. First, on standard pairwise judgment benchmarks, DeCert consistently improves over strong generative judge baselines on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings, and the gains are not merely due to extra sampling because the paper compares under matched sampling budgets. Second, rationale-quality analyses show fewer unsupported claims, fewer omitted decision-relevant issues, and fewer verdict-rationale inconsistencies. Third, the positional-bias result is notable: without swap-based augmentation or explicit position-consistency supervision, DeCert improves position-consistent accuracy by up to 14.8 points over the corresponding base judges. This suggests that certificate auditing pushes the model toward content-grounded comparison rather than superficial A/B position cues. Fourth, ablations support the full design: all three certificate dimensions matter, verdict-only supervision is insufficient, and removing the same-verdict rationale preference construction weakens the gains substantially. The paper appears stronger on consistency of trends and analysis depth than on one dramatic headline number.1a:T415,For a quick but useful read, focus on two questions: why reference rationales are the wrong supervision target, and why the paper treats a rationale as a certificate rather than just an explanation. Start with the introduction, especially the critique of prior rationale supervision. Then read the method overview and the sections defining Soundness, Coverage, and Decision Support. Three parts deserve close attention. First, the claim-graph construction and claim taxonomy, because that determines whether the method is a structured auditing framework or mostly prompt scaffolding. Second, the preference-pair construction, especially the same-correct-verdict comparisons; this is where the method gets its extra signal. Third, the OOD and positional-bias results, since they best test whether the model learned better reasoning rather than better benchmark mimicry.
