18:T62b,The method has three main components. First, a physical mechanism interpreter maps each action-outcome branch to a distribution over latent mechanisms rather than a point estimate. This matters because different interventions constrain the underlying physics to different degrees: a strong push may sharply identify friction, while a weak interaction may leave many mechanisms plausible. Second, the paper introduces a shared-world evidence score. Instead of forcing branch posteriors to be identical, it measures whether all branches retain common support for a shared mechanism relative to an initial-scene prior p(z|I0). The prior correction is important: it prevents uninformative branches from appearing falsely consistent simply because they remain broad and uncertain. Third, the learned compatibility signal is injected into both training and sampling. The interpreter is trained using same-world groups versus mismatched-world groups, then frozen. Its evidence score is used during flow training and also as sampling-time guidance, so the generated rollouts are nudged toward sets of futures that admit one common physical explanation. A key design choice is weak coupling: the model does not force videos or latent posteriors to collapse to the same representation, only to remain jointly explainable by one mechanism.
