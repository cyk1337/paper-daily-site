18:T655,SparKV has three main components. The first is a KV chunk scheduler. The cloud stores precomputed KV caches and partitions them into indexed chunks along token, attention-head, and Transformer-layer dimensions. For each chunk, the scheduler decides whether it should be streamed from the cloud or recomputed on the device, subject to causal and layer-wise dependencies, with the objective of minimizing end-to-end TTFT.

The second component is an overhead model. The paper uses a lightweight MLP to predict per-chunk local computation latency from features such as attention sparsity. This matters because recomputation cost is highly non-uniform across chunks: some are cheap to recompute locally, while others are better streamed. Compared with coarse layer-level or segment-level scheduling, this gives the system a finer cost model.

The third component is a runtime controller. Offline schedules are fragile under real wireless conditions and changing edge workloads. The controller monitors wireless throughput and available compute headroom over sliding windows, then dynamically migrates chunks between the streaming path and the local-compute path. The key point is that SparKV is not just cloud-edge cooperation in a generic sense; it is dependency-aware, cost-aware, and runtime-adaptive.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear: it is not only KV compression or priority-based streaming; it is not only local prefill acceleration through kernels or sparse attention; and it is not a coarse bidirectional overlap scheme. The novelty is chunk-level routing plus overlap under dependency constraints.19:T4fe,The experiments span multiple LLMs, datasets, and edge devices, with TTFT as the main metric, plus energy and quality preservation. The headline result stated in the paper is a 1.3x to 5.1x TTFT reduction over prior efficient KV-loading schemes, together with 1.5x to 3.3x lower per-request energy. The paper also claims robustness under varying compute availability and real wireless conditions. The motivation numbers are also useful: on a Redmi K80 Pro with 16 GB memory, sequential prefill for long reused contexts can exceed 15 seconds; even with sparse attention on a Jetson Orin, a 15K-token context still takes more than 5 seconds. So the target regime is genuinely challenging.

The most informative ablations are likely to be the value of overhead-aware scheduling, the effect of the runtime controller, the impact of chunk granularity, and degradation under bandwidth fluctuation. What matters most is not the average speedup alone, but whether the method remains effective when network throughput and device load shift during execution.
