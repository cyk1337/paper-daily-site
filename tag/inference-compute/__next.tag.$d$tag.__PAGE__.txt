a:T49f,The central formulation treats test-time scaling as budgeted inference over the implicit prefix tree of an autoregressive model. Nodes are partial prefixes, complete paths are traces, and an inference algorithm specifies how to expand, score, stop, and aggregate under a budget. The work separates three regimes. Single-trajectory sequential scaling spends extra compute on one evolving solution path. Sampled inference generates completed candidates and then applies voting, reranking, verifier selection, judge selection, or minimum-Bayes-risk style reduction. Partial-state search allocates compute before completion, expanding and pruning unfinished prefixes. A useful distinction is made between evidence computed per trace and decisions made over a completed candidate pool. Causal stopping is modeled separately because it changes generation cost, not just selection behavior. For evaluation, the work distinguishes shared-bank comparisons, which isolate reducers or verifiers on the same candidate pool, from end-to-end comparisons, which evaluate the full inference system. Candidate-bank diagnostics include repeated-sampling metrics that separate discovery from stability.b:T51b,The contribution is a broad empirical study plus mechanism analysis rather than a new TTS algorithm. The focus is on zero-shot or no-extra-training test-time scaling: prompting, sampling, aggregation, and iterative refinement methods are applied directly to existing instruction-tuned LVLMs, without additional SFT or RL. The study spans multiple model families and sizes, nine TTS methods, and six benchmarks. It also compares against methods designed specifically for VLM reasoning, such as Compositional CoT, to test whether generic LLM techniques are already sufficient.

The stronger part of the paper is the analysis. Instead of reporting only accuracy, the authors examine token budgets, reasoning-chain quality, and attention behavior. Their main observation is that LVLMs have a short early window in which generated tokens attend strongly to the image and absorb visual evidence into the hidden state. After that, attention to the image decays, and later tokens operate more on the internal representation than on direct visual grounding. This explains the mixed effect of test-time scaling: moderate extra reasoning helps on tasks that genuinely require multi-step inference, but excessive reasoning can detach the model from the image and amplify language-prior-driven errors and hallucinations.c:T451,The evaluation is fairly extensive: multiple LVLMs, multiple parameter scales, nine test-time scaling methods, and six benchmarks. Several findings stand out. First, conventional LLM-style TTS methods transfer well to LVLMs and often outperform some approaches designed specifically for VLMs. Second, small models are not inherently bad at TTS. On the contrary, small but competent models such as Qwen3-VL-4B can gain the most, with improvements reported up to about 40%, reaching or even surpassing the baseline performance of a larger model without TTS, such as Qwen3-VL-32B in some settings. Third, TTS can hurt perceptual tasks, not merely because of wasted compute, but because overthinking introduces wrong assumptions and accumulates hallucinations.

The paper also evaluates reasoning-chain quality using both LLM-as-a-judge and human assessment, at global and fine-grained levels. - State: constructed purely from the current set of extracted final answers. Features include sorted top-K answer counts, total sample count |D_t|, and entropy of the count vector as a proxy for convergence/dispersion.
- Actions: discrete choices—stop (return current majority vote) or sample k_l additional candidates in parallel.
- Reward: correctness of the final aggregated answer as positive reward, with penalties for additional samples (compute) and additional rounds (latency). Implemented as a weighted sum and interpreted as a Lagrangian relaxation of maximizing accuracy under compute/latency budgets.
- Policy: a small 4-layer MLP trained with RL; runs on CPU; wraps around any black-box LLM sampler.
- Design trade-off: using only answer-level statistics improves portability and operational simplicity, but forgoes stronger internal signals (logits, token-level uncertainty, process features), which may cap achievable efficiency gains.10:T5a9,The method, FINEVERIFY, has a proposer-verifier structure. A proposer first runs agentic search and samples multiple trajectories, producing a set of candidate answers. The verifier does not assign one global score per candidate. Instead, it first decomposes the original question into a set of checkable sub-questions, each corresponding to a requirement, attribute, or constraint in the task. Then, for every candidate answer, it verifies each sub-question against retrieved evidence and labels the result as supported, not_found, or contradicted. These per-check judgments are aggregated into an evidence-grounded verification score, and the highest-scoring candidate is selected.

Relative to best-of-N or confidence-based reranking, the design changes the problem in three ways. First, it replaces one difficult global judgment with multiple simpler local judgments. Second, all candidates are evaluated against the same explicit checklist, which makes scores more comparable across candidates. Third, the aggregation rule is explicit rather than implicit, so the final decision is inspectable.

The paper also uses a practical early-stopping rule: if a candidate receives full support on all sub-questions, sampling can stop because further compute is unlikely to change the decision. Another useful byproduct is the verification trace itself, which can be used to audit benchmark quality and diagnose why a candidate was accepted or rejected.11:T57f,The evaluation covers four agentic search benchmarks and two models. With only 4 sampled trajectories, FINEVERIFY improves average accuracy for GPT-5-mini from 59.2% Pass@1 to 67.4%, an absolute gain of 8.2 points. For Gemini-3-flash, it improves from 71.3% to 76.9%, a gain of 5.6 points. It consistently beats standard test-time scaling baselines such as Majority Voting, Weighted Voting, Best-of-N, Solution Aggregation, and Confidence Verify.

The more important result is that it keeps improving as the number of samples grows. On BrowseComp-Plus, Figure 1 shows that with 12 samples, FINEVERIFY allows GPT-5-mini to surpass GPT-5, with a 20.5-point gain over GPT-5-mini Pass@1. That matters because it indicates the method is not just a small reranking trick at low sample counts; it converts additional test-time compute into accuracy more effectively than the baselines.

The paper also includes a verification-accuracy analysis: when the correct answer is present in the candidate pool, FINEVERIFY is more likely than coarse candidate-level scoring methods to actually select it. That isolates the source of improvement to better selection rather than better search coverage.
