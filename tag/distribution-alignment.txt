1b:T81a,The paper has two main technical pieces. First is d_NTP, a discrepancy measure between the next-token distributions produced by full ICL and by task-vector-based inference. The key claim is that downstream accuracy is only an indirect signal, while distributional alignment is a more faithful criterion because task vectors are supposed to reproduce the effect of demonstrations, not merely match the final argmax label on average. Empirically, lower d_NTP correlates strongly with better downstream performance.

Second is Linear Task Vector (LTV). Instead of extracting a vector by simple hidden-state differencing or other heuristics, LTV models the effect of demonstrations as a linear transformation on the query representation. The method sets up a regression problem between representations with and without demonstrations and solves it in closed form. The extracted task vector is therefore tied to an explicit approximation objective rather than an ad hoc construction. The novelty is not linearity by itself; the point is that the extraction procedure is derived from a distribution-alignment perspective.

Mechanistically, LTV assumes that in classification settings, much of the useful effect of demonstrations can be approximated as a roughly linear shift in the final hidden representation relevant to label prediction. That is clearly an approximation, since ICL is not globally linear, but it can still be effective when the label space is small and the decision boundary is local. The paper also extends the analysis beyond classification to regression, suggesting that the method is not merely exploiting label-token quirks.

A notable extension is transferability: task vectors extracted from a larger model are applied to smaller models. This is interesting because it implicitly tests whether the task effect learned through ICL has some shared geometric structure across models. The reported gains suggest that at least part of that structure transfers, which is practically useful for smaller models with weaker ICL ability or shorter context windows.1c:T5d2,The experiments cover 8 classification benchmarks and 5 LLMs, with additional results on regression tasks. The headline number in the paper is that LTV improves average classification accuracy by 9.2% over prior task-vector baselines while also reducing inference latency relative to full ICL, since demonstrations are removed at test time. Another central empirical result is the strong negative correlation between d_NTP and downstream accuracy, which is the main evidence that the proposed metric is meaningful.

The empirical story has four parts. First, direct method comparison: LTV is more consistently effective than existing task-vector extraction methods across models and datasets. Second, metric validation: methods with lower d_NTP tend to perform better, supporting the idea that distributional alignment is a useful design criterion. Third, task generalization: the method also outperforms baselines on regression, which weakens the concern that it only works in narrow label-token classification setups. Fourth, cross-model transfer: using task vectors extracted from a larger model improves smaller-model classification accuracy by 6.4%.

The most informative details are likely in the per-model and per-task breakdowns rather than the average alone.
