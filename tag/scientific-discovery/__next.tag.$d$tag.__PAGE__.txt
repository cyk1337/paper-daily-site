9:T52a,Three coupled components:
1) OpenFrontiers Corpus: 12,151 verbatim problem records aggregated from 62 source collections (plus 4,751 catalogue-only pointers). No statement-based deduplication to avoid semantic drift from rewriting. 65.9% have MSC2020 codes; 1,946 include Lean 4 statements. Status uncertainty is explicit: a large fraction is labeled “unassessed” rather than being treated as open.
2) OpenFrontiers-Bench: 200 research-level math problems split into Rediscovery-110 (open into 2026, solved after) and Open-90 (still open). Each item has a Lean 4 statement that compiles in a pinned environment and passes automated probes plus two independent reviews. Evidence is tiered: many solved items come with independently checked Lean proofs or exact certificates that can be recomputed.
3) Evaluation protocol: agents are tested in an isolated sandbox under three access modes (closed-book, frozen-retrieval, live-web). Scoring is strict: only Lean proofs accepted by independent checkers count. Contamination control goes beyond time-anchoring: the benchmark audits AI disclosures and applies per-model lineage exclusion when solution materials credit an AI system tied to the evaluated model family.
The design deliberately trades breadth for adjudicability: math + formal checking to minimize judge noise.a:T688,The paper operationalizes “conceptual change” as four executable transformations (L1–L4) and, for each, distinguishes:
- a descriptive test: checks the defining property (closure, structural agreement, enrichment, etc.), and
- a consequence test: checks an independently specified implication that gives the claim meaning.

L1 (redundancy identification): separates gauge redundancies from genuine conserved symmetries using consequences on observables.
L2 (coarse-graining / reduction): shows closure alone is insufficient via the degenerate reduction Q(x)=0, which is perfectly closed but carries no dynamical variation; adds an observable-preservation consequence test.
L3 (structure transfer): validates transferred structures (e.g., conservation laws) by requiring they produce correct consequences in the target system; uses broken-symmetry controls.
L4 (missing-quantity inference from conservation imbalance): infers hidden quantities and requires the inferred imbalance to exceed noise scales.

It then ports the same logic to neural interpretability:
- CKA: because CKA is invariant to rotations, a random rotation can preserve CKA while breaking the claimed correspondence; the consequence test is functional-effect transfer under localized interventions.
- SAE features: concept enrichment can reject null features, but accepted features may lack causal, concept-specific support under ablations; matched controls and positive controls calibrate both enrichment and intervention sensitivity.
