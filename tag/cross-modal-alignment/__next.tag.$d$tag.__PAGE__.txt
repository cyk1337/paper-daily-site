a:T704,The method has two main pieces: FFD for data construction and TACT for post-training.

FFD, or Factuality-Frequency Distillation, builds a text-anchored corpus of counter-commonsense QA pairs. The central idea is to estimate the prior strength of facts through frequency signals, then route examples according to how strongly they are supported by common language priors. Instead of generic synthetic QA generation, the pipeline focuses on informative low-frequency factual scenarios that are likely to trigger prior-over-evidence failures. This gives the training set a very specific role: it is designed to expose and stress the decoder’s tendency to over-trust dominant priors.

TACT, or Text-Anchored Cross-Modal Transfer, then uses this corpus to reshape the decoder. A key step is prior-aware trajectory curation: the method distinguishes reasoning traces that genuinely follow evidence from traces that merely rationalize the prior. The model is optimized in two stages. The first stage is supervised and reinforces a behavior pattern of grounding observations before making the final judgment. The second stage uses preference-style optimization to suppress prior-driven and over-rationalized trajectories. The paper’s central bet is that because the decoder is shared across modalities, text-only supervision can alter multimodal conflict resolution behavior.

This differs from prior work in a clean way. Methods such as VCD, VDD, M3ID, and MemVR mainly strengthen visual evidence or decoding from the visual side. NoLan-style methods calibrate inference-time behavior. TACT instead targets training-time decoder policy, and it does so with text-anchored supervision rather than extra visual labels. In practical terms, it is trying to teach the model to trust what it already sees.b:T58c,The most informative result is the diagnosis experiment. On CDH-Bench, the authors evaluate three MLLMs with multi-trajectory decoding using n=8 samples at temperature 1.0, and report both pass@k and a custom prior-lock@k metric. As k increases from 1 to 8, pass@k improves by 15–22 points while prior-lock@k drops by 14–18 points across models. This is strong evidence that the correct answer is often present in the decoding space but suppressed by prior-biased selection. Their error analysis on Qwen3-VL-8B points in the same direction: many failures choose the commonsense option, and a simple grounding prompt that asks the model to first describe the queried visual detail reduces such errors noticeably.

The main benchmark results show that TACT consistently improves visual counter-commonsense reasoning across multiple benchmarks while largely preserving general capabilities. The notable part is that these gains come from text-only supervision. The ablations indicate that the full pipeline matters: frequency-aware data construction, trajectory curation, and two-stage post-training each contribute. Plain answer-level SFT is weaker than supervision built around evidence-following trajectories. Another useful observation is the analysis of over-rationalization: longer CoT does not automatically help, because the model can generate visually phrased but incorrect explanations that justify the prior.c:T813,The central method is TokenSwap. Starting from a text-only sample, the pipeline identifies visualizable concepts that matter for the task, replaces them with semantically aligned natural images, and keeps the surrounding text unchanged. This is different from rendering the whole prompt as an image or building a conventional image-question pair. The replacement happens at the concept or phrase level, so the text-only and multimodal versions remain structurally close. That gives a cleaner controlled test: the main change is that some semantic units move from text tokens to visual tokens.

TokenSwap-Bench is built on top of MMLU. The construction has three main stages. First, Gemini-2.0-Flash is used to identify concepts in questions and answer options that can be visually grounded. Second, each concept is turned into an image with a text-to-image model. Third, the benchmark applies several filters to ensure that measured gaps are not artifacts of poor substitutions. The important filters are:
1. Faithfulness verification: the generated image must correctly represent the intended concept in context.
2. Task relevance verification: if removing the replaced concepts from the original text does not affect prediction, the sample is not useful for measuring a meaningful gap and is filtered out.
3. Semantic recoverability: the substituted image is captioned and the caption is inserted back into text form to check whether the original semantics can be recovered.

The training-side intervention is data-centric rather than architectural. The authors augment training with image-interleaved counterparts of text data, and they test this both in pre-training and post-training settings. The intuition is simple and plausible: repeated exposure to semantically matched text and image realizations should force the model to learn more stable cross-modal representations and more consistent reasoning under token-type substitution. Compared with generic image-text alignment losses or caption-style supervision, this directly targets substitution invariance.d:T6bc,There are four main empirical takeaways.

First, the modality gap is pervasive and large. Across 42 MLLMs, replacing text spans with aligned images causes performance drops ranging from 4.2% to 47.4%, with an average decrease of 19.6% and a reported 95% confidence interval of ±3.3%. This is not a small robustness issue; it is a systematic reliability problem.

Second, reasoning-oriented models show much smaller gaps. The paper reports an average gap of about 10.1% for reasoning models versus 25.5% for non-reasoning models. That suggests the difference is likely due to training-time mechanisms rather than prompting alone, such as stronger process supervision, more multimodal reasoning data, or post-training recipes that implicitly enforce consistency.

Third, common prompting strategies do not reliably solve the problem. Chain-of-thought and few-shot prompting provide limited benefit. This matters because it shifts the diagnosis away from inference-time reasoning and toward representation learning and training distribution.

Fourth, scaling compute helps only marginally. A 10× increase in training FLOPs reduces the gap by only about 2.8%. So this is not something current scaling trends fix automatically.

On the mitigation side, TokenSwap augmentation reduces the modality gap in both pre-training and post-training while preserving text-only and standard vision-language performance, with slight gains in some cases. Visual Base (VB) tokens are standard patch embeddings and carry static appearance information such as texture, object shape, and scene layout. Visual Continuous (VC) tokens are the main novelty: each frame gets a learnable query token that absorbs inter-frame dynamics through joint self-attention, aiming to represent motion trajectories, gesture onsets, lip motion, gaze changes, and other high-frequency temporal cues. For positional encoding, the paper introduces Omni-RoPE, a 3D rotary scheme that assigns all token types coordinates in a shared (t, h', w') space. The point is not just to add positions independently to audio and vision, but to make Audio, VC, and VB tokens distinguishable yet comparable inside one spatiotemporal coordinate system, while retaining RoPE-style extrapolation to different resolutions and sequence lengths.

Efficiency comes from two mechanisms.
