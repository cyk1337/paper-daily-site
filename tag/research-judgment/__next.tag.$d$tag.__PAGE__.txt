9:T54b,The contribution is a benchmark called SciΠvot rather than a new agent architecture. The data comes from six months of original, PhD-level AutoResearch projects across six scientific fields. LLM agents ran these projects over weeks to months, while senior researchers periodically intervened to correct poor scientific decisions. The authors convert those intervention points into 248 benchmark cases. Each case presents two candidate plans: the original LLM plan and the human-corrected alternative. At evaluation time, the model sees only the project record available before the intervention, including code, logs, and progress notes, and must choose between the two plans and justify the choice. This design has several strengths. It avoids the instability of scoring open-ended generations by reducing the task to contextual preference judgment. It preserves the long-range project context instead of collapsing everything into a single-turn QA item. And it evaluates preference over scientific corrections rather than stylistic rewrites. The paper also identifies an important failure mode, re-trapping: with more deliberation, a model can move from a correct choice back to the original mistaken plan.
