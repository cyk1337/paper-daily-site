18:T652,The methodology rests on three intentionally independent measurement paths. First, the authors build a 64-shape sweep of LLM primitives that preserve semantics while changing expression form, and they record supported-device sets per operation rather than a vague residency score. This is how they establish that placement is a property of expression, not semantics. A representative example is that fused RMSNorm is fully eligible while an equivalent subln decomposition is not admitted to ANE execution. Second, they train matched real models across architecture families, sizes, and precisions. Quantized checkpoints are byte-identical in structure to their fp16 counterparts, so deployment comparisons isolate encoding rather than graph differences. Third, they read ANE memory-controller byte counters during inference, which lets them verify what actually ran and how many bytes were moved, instead of trusting compile-time plans or fragile power telemetry. From these measurements they derive several mechanism-level conclusions. Placement is jointly determined by architecture, size, and encoding rather than parameter count alone. Once resident, decode behaves like streaming the full weight set every token, so latency is largely a function of bytes streamed per token. They also show that heavily grouped GQA, especially with 2 KV heads, is a smaller and more compiler-friendly operator family on ANE than the conv-heavy alternatives examined. This reverses the usual CPU-side intuition: on this accelerator, the advantage comes from grouping and byte economy, not from an abstract preference for attention.19:T5ff,Several results carry most of the paper’s weight. On placement, semantically equivalent formulations can diverge completely: fused RMSNorm is reported as 100% eligible while the decomposed form is 0%, which directly supports the expression-vs-semantics claim. On architecture and encoding, a 22.3M attention model in fp16 runs on ANE, while a 25.9M conv-heavy fp16 model does not; yet the same conv graph in int8 or 2-bit reaches roughly 83% on-ANE residency. This shows that parameter count is not the governing variable. On decode cost, hardware counters measure about 1.539 / 0.753 / 0.197 bytes per parameter for fp16 / int8 / 2-bit, versus nominal 2 / 1 / 0.25, indicating real-system packing and runtime effects but confirming that streamed weight bytes dominate. On operator size, heavily grouped GQA with 2 KV heads is around 2.5d^2, whereas the LIV conv family is around 4d^2, and the grouped-attention family is admitted at fp16 while the conv-heavy family is the one that falls off ANE. On the end-to-end frontier, ternary models occupy the best size/speed tradeoff among the tested artifacts: around 25M ternary models span roughly 10.0–10.8 MB and 0.62–0.64 ms across operator mixes, while 50M ternary models are about 16.8 MB and 0.86 ms. Relative to the conv-heavy fp16 starting point, the reported gains are 9.8×/6.1× smaller and 3.0×/2.2× faster. An important secondary observation is that once encoding is pushed to ternary, operator mix matters much less for size and speed than the encoding choice itself.1a:T4e9,Read this paper as an evidence chain rather than a benchmark story. Start with the introduction and Table 1 to fix the five headline claims. After that, focus on §6–§8, which map to placement, bytes-per-token, and the operator lever. If time is limited, keep two questions in mind: why does the paper recommend choosing encoding before parameter budget, and why does grouped attention become the smaller operator family on ANE.
