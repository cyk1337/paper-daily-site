18:T490,The model abstraction starts with an unobserved physical state x, an observation o=h(x), a fixed language instruction, and an internal representation λ(x) produced by the VLA backbone. A stochastic policy π then generates a continuous-time open-loop action chunk u(t), which is treated as the control input to a nonlinear control-affine dynamical system. Quantities of interest are defined along the controlled trajectory rather than as static labels. For probing, the work constructs an ideal representation in which multiple physical quantities and their propagated values under candidate action trajectories admit shared linear readouts. For steering, it introduces a path-signature-based exponential-family policy. Path signatures summarize continuous action paths through iterated-integral features, and the exponential-family geometry yields a dually flat statistical manifold. This provides a formal link between linear probes in representation space and linear steering of action-trajectory distributions. The key difference from standard LLM LRH is that the represented direction is tied to future dynamics and policy geometry, not only to a static concept.19:T85f,The paper organizes multimodal LMs into three families: encoder-full, encoder-free, and discrete-token models. Encoder-full systems use a dedicated perceptual encoder to produce continuous semantic features before the shared transformer. Encoder-free and discrete-token systems do not. The central hypothesis is that the missing perceptual computation is internalized by the front part of the shared transformer.

To test this, the authors use three complementary analyses. First, layerwise linear probing asks when modality-token hidden states become linearly decodable for concept identity. In encoder-full models, decodable semantics should already be present at the transformer input. In encoder-free and discrete-token models, semantic organization should emerge progressively with depth. Second, representational similarity analysis compares intermediate states with dedicated perceptual encoders to see whether early and middle layers exhibit encoder-like geometry. Third, causal intervention perturbs image and audio inputs and measures at what depths modality-token states remain causally necessary for the final output, which helps identify where the representation is formed and where it is read out.

Gemma 4 12B is the main target model. Cross-model comparisons include encoder-free EVE and Fuyu, discrete-token Chameleon, and encoder-full controls such as LLaVA-1.5, Qwen3-VL, Qwen3-Omni, and Qwen2-Audio. For aligned image-audio analysis, the paper builds a paired concept dataset from ImageNet-1k and ESC-50 with 31 shared concepts, 40 images and 40 audio clips per concept, for 1,240 samples per modality.

The key claim is not just that some layer performs well. It is that there exists a contiguous band of layers that transforms low-level perceptual structure into a readable semantic representation. That band is the Virtual Encoder. The paper also argues that post-readout routing differs by modality: in Gemma 4, vision briefly aligns with the language subspace near readout, while audio follows a different trajectory, suggesting that shared transformers do not collapse all modalities into one uniform internal stream.1a:T62b,There are four main findings. First, linear probing shows that concept decodability in encoder-free and discrete-token models rises progressively with depth, whereas encoder-full models enter the shared transformer with already organized perceptual representations. This supports the idea that perceptual encoding has been absorbed into the early transformer layers. Second, representational similarity analysis shows that early-to-middle hidden states in models such as Gemma 4 12B resemble dedicated perceptual encoders geometrically, indicating that these layers are not merely doing generic language processing. Third, causal analysis identifies the depth at which modality representations are read out: before a certain layer band, perturbing modality tokens strongly affects the output; after that point, the effect changes, suggesting that the encoded representation has already been handed off to later computation. Fourth, routing is modality-dependent. In Gemma 4, vision transiently approaches the language subspace near readout, while audio follows a markedly different path, implying asymmetric integration mechanisms across modalities.

The paper is more about trends and internal structure than benchmark scores. The paired dataset is also relatively small, with 31 shared concepts and 1,240 samples per modality, so the conclusions should be read as mechanistic evidence rather than a broad statistical law over open-domain semantics.
