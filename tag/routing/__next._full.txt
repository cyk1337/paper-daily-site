18:T747,MoSAR, short for Mixture of Semantic Attention Regimes, inserts a lightweight router on the post-RoPE Q/K pathway. The router is bilinear and conditions on both queries and keys, rather than routing only one side. That choice is important: routing only queries gives row-wise structure, and routing only keys gives column-wise structure, but neither can localize useful two-dimensional regions of the attention matrix before dense similarities are computed. With both sides routed, the interaction between token i and token j selects a regime.

Each semantic regime defines a distance gate g(d). The gate is not just a simple exponential decay. It has a near-distance plateau where RoPE interactions are largely preserved, a transition region with smooth attenuation, and a low-relevance region where interactions become small enough to approximate away. This avoids the semantic discontinuity of hard local masks and makes approximability an outcome of the learned geometry.

The routing is input-conditioned and applied after positional encoding, so regime selection depends on contextual token representations that already carry positional structure. That is why the paper uses the term semantic regimes: locality is not determined by distance alone. MoSAR-Cost adds an explicit pressure toward shorter expected reach, biasing the router toward cheaper regimes and turning the quality-efficiency trade-off into a trainable objective.

Relative to prior efficient attention methods, the differences are clear. It does not predefine a sparse pattern; it predefines a set of decay laws and lets the data choose among them. It does not start by deleting edges; it first learns continuous attenuation and then exploits low-value regions for approximation. And it treats efficient attention as a geometry-learning problem rather than only an operator-design problem.19:T5f3,The main study uses 500M-parameter models trained from scratch and compares dense RoPE, fixed-locality baselines, MoSAR, and the cost-aware MoSAR-Cost. The headline result is that adaptive controlled decay outperforms both dense RoPE and fixed locality among the evaluated variants at 4096 and 8192 tokens, with MoSAR achieving the best perplexity in those long-context settings. The gains appear to be concentrated where long-range structure matters, which is aligned with the method’s goal.

The more interesting result is the inference-time approximation study. The authors replace soft mixture routing with deterministic top-1 routing at inference, effectively discretizing the learned geometry. This substantially reduces expected normalized reach while keeping perplexity competitive under length extrapolation. That suggests the learned regimes are not merely a training-time regularizer; they induce a geometry that can be compressed into a cheaper routing policy. MoSAR-Cost pushes the trade-off further toward lower reach, with some language-modeling cost, making the quality-versus-compute curve explicit rather than reporting a single operating point.

The most useful ablations are likely the ones on one-sided versus two-sided routing, number of regimes, the role of smooth transition regions, strength of cost regularization, and extrapolation behavior. The key question is not only whether perplexity improves, but whether perplexity, reach, and approximation error move in a stable and interpretable way.1a:T412,Start with the introduction and lock onto the paper’s reframing: the target is not a better hand-designed sparse pattern, but a learned decay geometry over attention interactions. Then go to the method section and focus on three pieces: the definition of a semantic regime, the bilinear query-key router, and the MoSAR-Cost objective. It is worth understanding why routing is applied after RoPE rather than before it.

In the experiments, prioritize figures and tables that show perplexity across context lengths, expected or normalized reach, and the gap between soft mixture routing and deterministic top-1 routing. If time is limited, try to answer three questions: do the learned regimes actually separate into different locality behaviors, do those behaviors vary with layer and token semantics, and is the inference-time discretization loss small enough to matter in practice. Around 35 to 50 minutes should be enough for a solid skim. For replication judgment, the appendix on implementation details and extra ablations is necessary.1b:T989,方法核心是 CounterRoute，一个在线 RL 框架，直接从原生支持 thinking / no-thinking 的单一 checkpoint 出发训练，不额外做该方法专用的 SFT warm-up，也不引入外部 router。形式化上，策略先对给定 prompt 采样模式 m∈{TH, NT}，再在该模式下生成回答 z。论文定义了带效率边际 b 的路由目标：只有当 thinking 的估计效用比 no-thinking 高出至少 b 时，才值得走 TH；否则优先走更便宜的 NT。这个 b 不是 token 成本的显式建模，而是 reward space 里的偏置，用来避免在两种模式质量接近时无谓思考。

训练上有两个关键设计。其一是 paired current-policy counterfactual rollouts。对一部分 prompt，强制当前策略分别在 TH 和 NT 下各生成一次回答，得到同一 prompt 下的成对反事实样本。然后比较两种模式的 utility，构造跨模式 credit。因为两条回答都来自当前策略，而不是旧策略、规则标签或外部教师，所以路由监督会随着策略能力一起更新，缓解 non-stationary target 问题。

其二是 hierarchical counterfactual credit assignment。论文采用一种“分离式 token 级赋值”规则：在最初的路由 token 上，不使用模式内 advantage，而是用经过截断或边界控制的跨模式 utility 差值来替换；在后续回答 token 上，只用该模式内部的 GRPO 优势做更新。这样路由 token 学的是“选 TH 还是 NT”，回答 token 学的是“在已选模式下怎么答得更好”，两类梯度不再混在一起。相比把整条序列统一做 policy gradient，这种做法更贴合层级决策结构。

第三个设计是 paired-to-self-routed curriculum。训练早期，为了避免初始模式偏好压制探索，模型会保留较高比例的 paired prompts，让两种模式都持续被采样并获得训练信号；随后逐步过渡到 self-routed prompts，让模型自己决定模式。这个 curriculum 的作用不是提升最终上限，而是稳定 co-learning 过程，避免一开始就被单一模式锁死。

The central idea is to decompose block affinity into two parts: online semantic evidence and offline geometric bias. Existing methods implicitly mix both inside pooled post-RoPE features. SGDR separates them explicitly.

First, semantic aggregation is moved to pre-RoPE space. Query and key features are summarized at the block level before rotary rotation is applied. This avoids destructive interference from averaging rotated vectors, especially in high-frequency dimensions. The result is a more stable semantic summary for each block.

Second, positional structure is reconstructed separately rather than being expected to survive pooling. SGDR introduces an offline structural prior based on relative block geometry.
