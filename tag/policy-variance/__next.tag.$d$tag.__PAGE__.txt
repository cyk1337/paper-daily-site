9:T605,Model generation as a finite-horizon MDP: state = token prefix, action = next token, reward only at the end and normalized to [0,1]. Define policy variance σ^2_π(s)=Var_{a~π}[Q^π(s,a)]. Under deterministic transitions, the environment term in the return-variance decomposition vanishes, yielding a clean identity: trajectory return variance equals the sum over time of expected σ^2_π(s_t). This motivates “critical forks”: states with σ^2_π(s) above a threshold τ.

The paper separates two questions with different complexity regimes:
(1) Local discovery: how many samples from π(·|s) are needed to even observe an action with advantage ≥ c. Using concentration/one-sided inequalities (Cantelli/Chebyshev/Markov variants) on the centered advantage draw A^π(s,a), they derive a polynomial lower bound Ω(c^2/σ^2_π(s)), and show tightness on a canonical two-action fork.
(2) Long-horizon estimation: once such an action is observed, how many full trajectories are needed to estimate its advantage accurately enough to act on; this scales exponentially with remaining horizon, motivating local credit assignment.

They also provide a rollout-free screening step: bound σ^2_π(s) by the Gini–Simpson dispersion 𝒟(s)=1-||π(·|s)||_2^2, computable from a single next-token distribution. Low-dispersion states cannot be critical forks, though high dispersion is not sufficient (many paraphrase/formatting choices can be value-equivalent).
