18:T531,WSR-Tune performs a weight-space reparameterization. Instead of representing each weight matrix in the canonical basis (one basis element per coordinate), it introduces a new basis {\u03d5_ij} with coefficients \u007e{w}_ij. The basis is constructed to be *safety-conditioned*: directions that strongly control safety behavior are placed into a small set of basis directions (a safety subspace), while the remaining directions span a complementary subspace intended for downstream adaptation.

Fine-tuning then becomes: freeze the coefficients associated with the safety subspace; update only the complementary coefficients. Conceptually, this is a coordinate change designed to make “freeze a small part” actually meaningful for safety, because the frozen part is aligned with safety-relevant directions rather than arbitrary native coordinates.

How it differs from prior work:
- Prior safety-preserving FT typically operates in the original parameterization (freeze layers/neurons, constrain update directions, low-rank adapters, post-hoc weight repair). Those methods implicitly assume safety is localized or identifiable in native coordinates.
- WSR-Tune argues safety is distributed in native coordinates and proposes to *change the coordinates* so that safety becomes compact and thus protectable with simple freezing.19:T40e,Skim plan (30–45 minutes):
1) Start with Figure 1 to understand the pipeline: basis construction → reparameterization → freeze safety coordinates → adapt the rest.
2) Read Table 1 in the Introduction carefully; it is the main empirical motivation showing why native-space freezing is insufficient.
3) Focus on the method section that defines how the safety-conditioned basis is computed (what data/labels, what objective, per-layer vs global, computational cost).
4) Check ablations: random/unconditioned bases, varying safety-subspace dimension (freeze ratio), and compatibility with different adaptation methods (full FT vs parameter-efficient FT).

Questions worth digging into:
- Is the basis stable across tasks and checkpoints, or does it need recomputation per model/version?
- Does reparameterization merely help freezing, or does it also reshape gradient flow (optimization geometry) in a way that improves retention?
- Can multiple “protected capabilities” be isolated into separate subspaces for continual training?1a:T6db,The method, Subspace Tuning (SST), computes an SVD for each weight matrix W, takes the top-k left and right singular vectors, and projects the optimizer’s final update ΔW onto the orthogonal complement of those principal subspaces before applying it. The key detail is that SST projects the applied parameter update, not the raw gradient. That distinction matters because stateful optimizers such as Adam transform gradients through momentum and coordinate-wise rescaling; a projected gradient does not guarantee a projected final step. By acting on the actual update written into the weights, SST becomes optimizer-agnostic.

The projection is bilateral: it removes components aligned with principal singular directions on both the output side and the input side. This is stronger than a one-sided constraint and is meant to preserve the dominant mapping structure of the pretrained matrix. Conceptually, SST freezes the high-energy spectral structure and pushes adaptation into the tail subspace.

The paper also addresses a practical limitation of a fixed protected basis. If the SVD basis is computed once and never updated, the allowed complement can become too restrictive as training progresses. SST therefore includes a refresh mechanism that periodically recomputes the SVD on the current weights. This trades off stricter preservation against continued adaptability: no refresh gives stronger protection, while refresh allows the protected subspace to track the evolving model and frees capacity for further learning.

Methodologically, SST is positioned as a composable constraint rather than a standalone fine-tuning paradigm. It can be combined with full-parameter tuning, SDFT-style recipes, and different optimizers such as Adam or Muon.1b:T97c,The empirical section is broad and is designed to test the central claim from several angles: can the model learn new tasks, retain prior capabilities, stay behaviorally close to the base model, and accumulate skills across stages? The domains include Science, Medicine, Pathology, Finance, and Tool Use. The paper evaluates both standard SFT and SDFT, and tests compatibility with Adam and Muon.

The headline result is that SST can preserve an extremely large fraction of principal singular directions while still matching or nearly matching unconstrained fine-tuning on target-task performance. The paper explicitly states that even when protecting the top 99% singular directions, SST still learns the new task, whereas standard SFT causes substantial forgetting. This is strong evidence that the non-principal spectral subspace contains enough adaptation capacity in overparameterized LLMs.

Scaling trends are also important. As model size increases, forgetting under SST shrinks further; from 8B onward, retained performance stays within roughly 1% of the base model, while unconstrained SFT degrades much more. The data-scaling result is equally telling: with more fine-tuning data, standard SFT forgets progressively more, but SST remains comparatively flat while still improving target-task accuracy. That suggests SST is not merely “learning less.”

The paper also goes beyond task accuracy. SST reduces KL divergence from the pretrained model by 2–4× relative to unconstrained recipes. On thinking models, standard SFT collapses natural reasoning length, while SST preserves the original reasoning-length behavior much better. When added to SDFT, SST also reduces fabricated reference-solution attributions, indicating better behavioral fidelity.

The sequential fine-tuning experiments are especially relevant for continual learning. In two-stage and three-stage chains, skills learned earlier remain largely intact under SST as later stages are added, whereas standard SFT degrades dormant skills whenever a new stage is trained. This is closer to the real deployment setting than one-shot forgetting tests.

The most explicit numbers visible from the provided content are: 2–4× lower KL, forgetting within about 1% from 8B onward, and successful learning even while protecting the top 99% singular directions. 第二步是机制诊断。作者把问题归因到 attention-sink corruption。现代 LLM 往往会把大量注意力质量集中到少数早期位置，这些位置的 hidden state 又被少数高幅值固定特征维度主导，形成 sink。论文的假设是，这种 sink 有助于稳定每个位置对关键表面 token 的注意力分配，从而支撑格式敏感任务。VL fine-tuning 会扰动 query/key 投影 Wq、Wk；由于 logit shift 会随输入幅值放大，这种扰动在 sink 位置被特别放大，导致 per-head sink collapse。作者进一步分析不同 QK normalization 设计的差异：如果是 per-head QK-RMSNorm，可以直接去掉对原始输入幅值的显式依赖；如果只是 layerwise normalization，则每个 head 的尺度仍受整层能量分配影响，弱 head 会被进一步压缩，因此到 VL 训练开始前，某些 backbone 的 per-head sink 已经偏弱。

第三步是预测指标与干预验证。作者提出 Sink Strength，一个只在 base LLM 上通过少量 forward pass 计算的标量，用来量化 head-level sink concentration。这个指标不需要做任何 VL 训练，就能预测后续多模态适配后的文本能力损失。然后他们把 base-side sink weakness 与 post-VL gap perturbation 结合起来，区分“骨干先天脆弱”和“VL 更新额外破坏”两部分。最后做两个负对照：一是在预训练后给模型补装 QK-RMSNorm 再去做 VL 训练，发现不能复现原生架构自带的保护效果；二是对已经训练好的 VLM 做多种 weight merging，也无法把丢失的文本能力救回来。这说明问题更像训练过程中的 head-level 结构破坏，而不是事后线性修补能解决的。20:T843,The paper proceeds in three stages.

First, it isolates the phenomenon. The authors compare released VLMs against their reference LLM backbones on a text-only evaluation suite, and add text-only further-training controls plus a matched VL-versus-text training trajectory from the same base model. This is meant to show that the degradation is not just a generic consequence of continued training, but is tied to multimodal adaptation.

Second, it proposes a mechanism: attention-sink corruption. Many modern LLMs place a large fraction of attention mass on a few early positions. Those positions are dominated by a small set of high-magnitude feature dimensions and act as sinks. The paper argues that these sinks help stabilize attention allocation in ways that matter for tasks graded on exact output form. During VL fine-tuning, perturbations to the query and key projections, Wq and Wk, induce logit shifts that scale with input magnitude. Because sink positions have unusually large magnitude, the perturbation is amplified there, causing per-head sink collapse. The architecture analysis then distinguishes per-head QK-RMSNorm from layerwise normalization: per-head normalization removes explicit raw-magnitude dependence, while layerwise normalization can still compress weak heads because each head’s scale is tied to the layer’s total energy.

Third, the paper introduces a predictive metric and tests interventions. Sink Strength is a single scalar computed on the base LLM, before VL training, to quantify head-level sink concentration. It predicts how much text capability will be lost after VL adaptation. The authors then separate base-side vulnerability from update-induced corruption by combining pre-VL sink weakness with post-VL sink-gap perturbation. Finally, they run two negative controls: injecting QK-RMSNorm after pretraining and then doing VL training, and applying several post-VL weight-merging methods.
