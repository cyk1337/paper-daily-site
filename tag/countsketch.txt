18:T748,The method is RISE, short for Readout Influence Sketching Estimator. The starting point is the exact outer-product form of the LM-head gradient for token loss: the gradient with respect to the readout matrix can be written as residual times hidden state transpose. This matters because both factors are available from forward-pass quantities. Once the problem is reduced to readout-side factors, the authors build a dual-channel representation instead of using a single compressed gradient.

The first channel, RH, is a lexical residual channel that keeps token-level error information in vocabulary space. It is intended to preserve precise lexical supervision. The second channel, GH, is a semantic projected-error channel that projects the error through the LM head into a semantic space, making matching more robust to paraphrases and semantically related outputs. The design choice is sensible: a purely lexical view is sharp but brittle, while a purely semantic view is smoother but can lose exact token-level attribution. RISE keeps both.

A second key ingredient is sparsity on the residual side. Although the softmax distribution may remain diffuse over a large vocabulary, the influence-relevant residual energy is shown to concentrate on a small adaptive active-token support. This lets the method avoid full-vocabulary computation while preserving most of the useful signal.

Finally, both channels are compressed with CountSketch, yielding compact representations that support approximate inner-product search. This gives a unified estimator for retrospective attribution and prospective valuation. Compared with gradient-indexing baselines such as RapidIn or ZO-Inf, RISE avoids full-parameter storage. Compared with proxy-model approaches, it stays on the target model rather than relying on a smaller surrogate with potentially mismatched dynamics.19:T639,The evaluation spans Pythia models from 14M to 6.9B and OLMo models from 1B to 32B. The paper emphasizes scalability and faithfulness rather than a single benchmark score. On Pythia-1B, RISE reduces index storage by 28x to 112x relative to RapidIn. At 32B scale, baselines that require per-example gradient indexing become memory-infeasible, while RISE remains usable.

The tasks include Howdy backdoor detection, Finance-Medical domain separation, and Brain Rot data selection. The first two are attribution-style settings that test whether the method can recover harmful or domain-relevant training examples. The Brain Rot setup is the more important one for pretraining researchers because it closes the loop: selected data is used for further training, and the downstream effect is measured. The paper reports 2.8x lower perplexity on control data and 4.1x higher RULER-CWE accuracy compared with baselines when training on RISE-selected data.

The ablations are also informative. One result is that middle-layer hidden-state similarity becomes less discriminative at scale, while the final layer recovers discriminative power, producing a U-shaped pattern. That supports the decision to work at the readout rather than in intermediate representation space. Another result is that the dual-channel formulation outperforms either channel alone, which validates the lexical-versus-semantic complementarity. The important question is not the algebra by itself, but why forward-only quantities are enough and why two channels are needed.

Those sections explain why the method is not just a generic sketching trick but one tailored to LLM representation geometry. In the experiments, prioritize the storage/scalability table and the Brain Rot closed-loop results. If your main interest is data selection for pretraining, the closed-loop section is the one to study carefully.
