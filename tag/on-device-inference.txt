18:T652,The methodology rests on three intentionally independent measurement paths. First, the authors build a 64-shape sweep of LLM primitives that preserve semantics while changing expression form, and they record supported-device sets per operation rather than a vague residency score. This is how they establish that placement is a property of expression, not semantics. A representative example is that fused RMSNorm is fully eligible while an equivalent subln decomposition is not admitted to ANE execution. Second, they train matched real models across architecture families, sizes, and precisions. Quantized checkpoints are byte-identical in structure to their fp16 counterparts, so deployment comparisons isolate encoding rather than graph differences. Third, they read ANE memory-controller byte counters during inference, which lets them verify what actually ran and how many bytes were moved, instead of trusting compile-time plans or fragile power telemetry. From these measurements they derive several mechanism-level conclusions. Placement is jointly determined by architecture, size, and encoding rather than parameter count alone. Once resident, decode behaves like streaming the full weight set every token, so latency is largely a function of bytes streamed per token. They also show that heavily grouped GQA, especially with 2 KV heads, is a smaller and more compiler-friendly operator family on ANE than the conv-heavy alternatives examined. This reverses the usual CPU-side intuition: on this accelerator, the advantage comes from grouping and byte economy, not from an abstract preference for attention.19:T5ff,Several results carry most of the paper’s weight. On placement, semantically equivalent formulations can diverge completely: fused RMSNorm is reported as 100% eligible while the decomposed form is 0%, which directly supports the expression-vs-semantics claim. On architecture and encoding, a 22.3M attention model in fp16 runs on ANE, while a 25.9M conv-heavy fp16 model does not; yet the same conv graph in int8 or 2-bit reaches roughly 83% on-ANE residency. This shows that parameter count is not the governing variable. On decode cost, hardware counters measure about 1.539 / 0.753 / 0.197 bytes per parameter for fp16 / int8 / 2-bit, versus nominal 2 / 1 / 0.25, indicating real-system packing and runtime effects but confirming that streamed weight bytes dominate. On operator size, heavily grouped GQA with 2 KV heads is around 2.5d^2, whereas the LIV conv family is around 4d^2, and the grouped-attention family is admitted at fp16 while the conv-heavy family is the one that falls off ANE. On the end-to-end frontier, ternary models occupy the best size/speed tradeoff among the tested artifacts: around 25M ternary models span roughly 10.0–10.8 MB and 0.62–0.64 ms across operator mixes, while 50M ternary models are about 16.8 MB and 0.86 ms. Relative to the conv-heavy fp16 starting point, the reported gains are 9.8×/6.1× smaller and 3.0×/2.2× faster. An important secondary observation is that once encoding is pushed to ternary, operator mix matters much less for size and speed than the encoding choice itself.1a:T4e9,Read this paper as an evidence chain rather than a benchmark story. Start with the introduction and Table 1 to fix the five headline claims. Then go to §4.1 first: the primitive sweep is the cleanest part of the paper and the best place to understand why the authors insist that expression form, not semantic equivalence, controls placement. Next read §5.1 and §5.2 together. §5.1 explains why matched trained artifacts matter; §5.2 explains why runtime byte counters are necessary and how cross-chip telemetry calibration can fail silently. After that, focus on §6–§8, which map to placement, bytes-per-token, and the operator lever. The figures showing architecture × size × encoding interactions and the relationship between bytes/param and latency are the ones to study carefully. If time is limited, keep two questions in mind: why does the paper recommend choosing encoding before parameter budget, and why does grouped attention become the smaller operator family on ANE. The deeper follow-up questions are whether compiler-admissible graph forms can be enforced during training, whether byte-dominated decode should change how low-bit models are pretrained, and how much of this transfers to other NPUs rather than remaining ANE-specific.1b:T7dc,方法核心是做一套面向 XDNA2 AIE tile 的 fused mixed-precision kernel library，并围绕它重做权重布局和数据流。关键点有三层。

第一层是 kernel fusion。作者不是先把 INT4/INT8 权重展开成 BF16 再做 GEMM/GEMV，而是让压缩权重一路保持低比特格式，直到进入 compute core 后再在同一 kernel 流里完成 unpack、dequantize 和矩阵计算。这样做的原因很直接：如果先物化成高精度权重，带宽和片上存储都会被吃掉，低比特量化的收益会被前处理成本抵消。TileFuse 直接支持 AWQ 风格 W4A16 和 W8A16，对 transformer 线性层做 fused execution。

第二层是 interleaved pre-tiling 与 layout co-design。XDNA2 的 DMA streaming 和 tile 级数据搬运有严格约束，直接按常规矩阵布局喂数据会让流水断掉，尤其在 FFN 的大 shape 上更明显。作者因此在离线阶段对权重做预切块和交错布局，让运行时 DMA 能连续供数，同时让 dequant 后的数据更贴合 AIE 的向量化执行方式。这里的重点不是单纯“换个 layout”，而是把量化分组、tile 尺寸、片上 buffer 和 GEMM/GEMV 访存模式一起设计。

第三层是 end-to-end runtime integration。论文不只停在 microkernel，而是把这些 kernel 接进完整 LLM runtime，覆盖 prefill 和 decode 场景，并处理 practical LLM shapes。前文专门批评已有工作只能跑受限 GEMM 维度，不能覆盖 Gemma-2B 一类模型常见 FFN 形状；TileFuse 的一个卖点就是把 kernel 设计到能覆盖这些真实模型尺寸，而不是只在漂亮的 benchmark shape 上报吞吐。

和已有方法相比，区别主要有两点。相对 Qualcomm 上的 ScalingLLM，它不要求改用 NPU 定制量化方案，而是尽量保留社区现成 AWQ 格式。相对此前基于 IRON 的 BFP16 GEMM 工作，它追求的是可用于准确端到端 LLM 推理的 mixed-precision low-bit 路线，而不是只展示某类 GEMM 的高 TOP/s。1c:T771,The core idea is a close-to-metal mixed-precision kernel library for AMD XDNA2 AIE tiles, coupled with weight-layout and runtime co-design.

First, TileFuse uses fused kernels for quantized linear layers. Instead of materializing low-bit weights into BF16 before matrix multiplication, it keeps weights compressed as INT4 or INT8 until they reach the compute core. Unpacking, dequantization, and GEMM/GEMV execution happen within one kernel flow. This matters because separate weight expansion would erase much of the memory and bandwidth benefit of low-bit quantization.

Second, the paper introduces interleaved pre-tiling and layout co-design. XDNA2 imposes practical constraints on DMA streaming and tile-local buffering, so a conventional matrix layout leads to poor pipeline utilization, especially for large FFN shapes. The authors therefore pre-tile and interleave weights offline so that runtime DMA can stream data continuously and the dequantized fragments align with the vectorized execution pattern of the AIE tiles. The key point is that quantization grouping, tile geometry, memory layout, and execution order are designed together.

Third, the work is integrated into an end-to-end LLM runtime rather than stopping at microkernels. The paper explicitly positions itself against prior results that report strong GEMM throughput on restricted dimensions but do not cover practical transformer shapes or sufficient numerical fidelity for full LLM inference. TileFuse is designed to support real transformer linear layers in both prefill and decode settings.

Relative to prior work, the distinction is clear. Unlike hardware-tailored quantization schemes such as ScalingLLM on Qualcomm, TileFuse tries to preserve standard AWQ-style formats. Unlike earlier BFP16 GEMM work on Ryzen NPUs, it targets deployable low-bit inference with adequate end-to-end accuracy, not just high isolated TOP/s.1d:T418,The main empirical result is that fusion removes a large amount of overhead from weight handling. Compared with full-precision baselines, the paper reports up to 121.6% kernel-level improvement for GEMM and 281% for GEMV. In end-to-end LLM inference, it achieves up to 2.0x lower prefilling latency and more than 64.6% lower energy.

The first is a KV chunk scheduler. The cloud stores precomputed KV caches and partitions them into indexed chunks along token, attention-head, and Transformer-layer dimensions. For each chunk, the scheduler decides whether it should be streamed from the cloud or recomputed on the device, subject to causal and layer-wise dependencies, with the objective of minimizing end-to-end TTFT.

The second component is an overhead model. The paper uses a lightweight MLP to predict per-chunk local computation latency from features such as attention sparsity. This matters because recomputation cost is highly non-uniform across chunks: some are cheap to recompute locally, while others are better streamed. Compared with coarse layer-level or segment-level scheduling, this gives the system a finer cost model.

The third component is a runtime controller. Offline schedules are fragile under real wireless conditions and changing edge workloads. The controller monitors wireless throughput and available compute headroom over sliding windows, then dynamically migrates chunks between the streaming path and the local-compute path. The key point is that SparKV is not just cloud-edge cooperation in a generic sense; it is dependency-aware, cost-aware, and runtime-adaptive.
