17:T518,VBQ assigns each contiguous group of 64 weights a learnable bit-width chosen from {1, 2, 4, 8}. The choice is relaxed with Gumbel-Softmax so it can be optimized jointly with the network. The core optimization problem is that task gradients flowing through the quantizer dominate the bit-budget signal by orders of magnitude, causing the precision logits to stay near initialization.

The paper addresses this with two mechanisms. First, gradient isolation: reconstructed weights are detached along the bit-selection path, preventing the main cross-entropy loss from directly overwhelming the precision logits. Second, alternating optimization: weight updates and precision updates are interleaved, so representation learning and precision allocation are optimized on cleaner timescales. Together, these changes let the bit-selection variables actually commit to discrete allocations.

A notable empirical finding is that the learned allocation stabilizes early, around the first 20% of training. The authors then distill the learned pattern into a fixed recipe, such as mostly 2-bit blocks, a higher-precision first feed-forward block, and a frequency-tiered LM head. This means the adaptive search is useful not only as a deployment method, but also as a way to discover reusable quantization structure.18:T4b8,There are three main empirical takeaways. First, the learned precision allocation is strongly heterogeneous, and the heterogeneity appears within projection types, not just across layers. About 69% of groups collapse to 1 bit, with an average precision of roughly 1.78 bits. The LM head averages around 1.09 bits, while a minority of more sensitive groups retain 4- or 8-bit precision.

Second, the paper makes a bigger-but-smaller argument. A 131M model at 1.82 bits on average, occupying 29MB, outperforms a 55M FP16 model that occupies 109MB. At a larger scale, a 1.46B model using the same recipe matches a 593M FP16 control on FineWeb-Edu while using a storage budget in the roughly 3.7 bits-per-weight regime. The practical message is that, under a fixed memory budget, training a larger model natively at low precision can be better than training a smaller high-precision model.

Third, recipe transfer works. Since the learned bit assignments stabilize early, freezing them into a fixed quantization recipe reproduces most of the adaptive method’s gains. That suggests the paper contributes both an optimization method and a structural observation about where precision matters in decoder-only
