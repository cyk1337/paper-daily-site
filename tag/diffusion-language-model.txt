18:T778,The paper has two technical components: a measurement framework and a decoding intervention.

For measurement, the authors build a step-wise and position-wise analysis framework for masked diffusion language models. At each denoising step, the model predicts clean-token distributions for all positions, but only a subset of still-masked positions is committed. Using this structure, the paper tracks refusal-related token mass across denoising steps and response positions under harmful prompts. It distinguishes several events: whether refusal evidence appears at all, whether refusal tokens are committed, whether that commitment persists or gets overwritten, and how early commitments affect the final safety outcome.

This leads to the paper’s main conceptual finding: shallow-step alignment. The claim is that early denoising steps themselves form a safety-sensitive axis in dLLMs. Tokens committed early can strongly influence the final safety outcome even when they are not all at the leading response positions. Compared with shallow alignment in autoregressive models, diffusion models introduce an additional temporal dimension tied to denoising rather than surface order.

Based on that analysis, the paper proposes Refusal-Aware Early Commitment (RAEC), a training-free decoding method. RAEC changes only the commitment decision during decoding. If a refusal signal appears early and remains sufficiently strong and persistent, the decoder commits it early before later denoising dynamics can wash it out. Importantly, RAEC does not inject an external refusal template; it acts on refusal probability mass already produced by the model. This design is well matched to the diagnosed failure mode. If unsafe trajectories often contain weak or transient refusal evidence, then intervening at commitment time is a more targeted fix than retraining the model or attaching an external safety classifier.19:T6fc,The evaluation uses harmful-instruction benchmarks including JailbreakBench and StrongREJECT, with Llama-Guard-3-8B as the safety judge and Attack Success Rate (ASR) as the main metric. The tested models include masked diffusion LMs such as LLaDA and Dream.

The empirical findings come in three layers. First, the measurement results show that refusal signals are concentrated in early denoising steps and leading response positions, but these are not the same thing. Early-step commitments have a strong effect on final safety, supporting the shallow-step alignment hypothesis. The paper also includes intervention-style analyses: forcing early refusal commitment makes an unaligned base dLLM safer, while forcing early compliance commitment can push an aligned model toward unsafe outputs. That suggests early commitment is not just correlated with safety; it is a causal control point.

Second, the paper analyzes unsafe trajectories and finds that many of them do contain refusal evidence. The issue is that the refusal mass is weak, transient, or overwritten before commitment. Safe trajectories, in contrast, show stronger and more persistent refusal mass and much higher refusal-commitment rates. This is an important mechanistic refinement: the failure is often not “no refusal capability,” but “refusal evidence does not survive to commitment.”

Third, RAEC reduces ASR on both LLaDA and Dream while largely preserving utility. The provided text does not include the full result tables, so it would be unsafe to quote exact deltas here. When reading the original paper, the key numbers to verify are the ASR reductions on each benchmark, the utility trade-off on benign prompts, and the sensitivity to the thresholds controlling early commitment and refusal persistence.1a:T57f,This is a good skim paper for readers interested in mechanism rather than only safety application. Start with the Introduction and Preliminaries to understand the commitment process in masked diffusion LMs: the model predicts all positions at every step, but commits only some masked positions. Without that, the later analysis is easy to misread.

Then focus on the measurement framework and the visualizations. The most informative figures are likely the ones showing: (1) refusal-token mass over denoising steps, (2) refusal-signal distribution over response positions, and (3) differences in commitment persistence between safe and unsafe trajectories. Those figures explain why RAEC works better than the final ASR table alone.

After that, read the RAEC section carefully, especially how the method decides that a refusal signal is strong and persistent enough to commit early, and exactly how the commitment policy is modified. For readers interested in modeling paradigms, this is the most valuable part of the paper because it shows that decoding policy in diffusion LMs is not just an implementation detail; it is part of behavior formation.

Finally, check the benchmark tables for gains on LLaDA and Dream over JailbreakBench and StrongREJECT, and inspect the utility cost. A first pass should take around 30 minutes. If reading more deeply, pair the intervention experiments with the ablations.1b:T4dd,The authors define an exact conditional-marginal oracle model to isolate parallelization error from model approximation error. A deterministic adaptive policy chooses the next reveal set from the observed partial assignment. The selected variables are then sampled independently using their true conditional marginals rather than from their true joint conditional. Approximation error is measured by forward KL, which has a chain-rule decomposition aligned with the reveal process and corresponds to excess expected log loss. Serial depth is the minimum target-averaged number of rounds needed to meet a KL budget. The central proof aligns each round's KL contribution with the conditional total correlation of the variables revealed in that round. The identity applies to random and history-dependent reveal sets. The paper then turns the identity into upper and lower bounds for structured families: logarithmic exact sampling for Bernoulli walks via hierarchical bisection, logarithmic-in-length zero-error schedules for finite-order Markov chains with a factor depending on Markov order, linear expected depth for uniform random permutations at fixed error, and several binary-alphabet constructions with polylogarithmic to polynomial depth.1c:T53b,The theoretical results give the main evidence. For the Bernoulli walk, hierarchical bisection achieves exact sampling in logarithmically many rounds, while contiguous left-to-right schedules require linearly many rounds to maintain fixed accuracy. For uniform random permutations, every fixed-error policy needs a linear number of expected rounds; under a hard round cap, the optimal error reduces exactly to an integer-composition problem, yielding fixed-round asymptotics, a finite-blocklength pairs-first phase, and a joint scaling frontier. Over a binary alphabet, uniform balanced binary strings have depth on the order of squared log sequence length at any fixed positive error, while independent one-hot blocks give matching square-root-depth lower bounds against arbitrary deterministic value-adaptive policies. Rectangular block constructions realize polynomial exponents up to one half. The empirical section is diagnostic: it evaluates generic schedules and deployed decoding rules on 542 sequences from WikiText and model-generated text across four domains using a single 0.5B model and one sampling temperature. A paired self-sampling study on 231 held-out prompts compares teacher-forced pseudo-costs with output quality under an external autoregressive evaluator, and also tests a model-independent spaced-profile schedule.1d:T4bb,A sampling-based watermark adapted to diffusion decoding.
- Injection: replace position-indexed i.i.d. Gumbel perturbations with a smooth, locally auto-correlated Gumbel field (SAC). The field is constructed via a Gaussian copula so that each coordinate keeps Gumbel marginals (under the paper’s assumptions) while introducing controlled local dependence across neighboring positions.
- Rationale: diffusion decoding refines multiple positions in parallel; independent high-frequency perturbations across neighbors can conflict with local consistency during iterative updates, causing oscillations or incoherence.
- Mechanistic analysis: quantify “latent perturbation roughness” via a local-difference energy and show how positive low-lag dependence reduces this roughness (the paper provides an analytic form contrasting i.i.d. vs SAC).
- Detection: a SAC-aware detector using covariance-aware filtering plus native-sample calibration to manage false positives. A global-offset scan is added as a coarse synchronization recovery tool under mild token–signal misalignment.
Overall, it modifies keyed randomness during DLM decoding and pairs it with a matched detector, rather than changing DLM training.1e:T97b,The main idea is to formulate denoising trajectories as a multivariate time series. Diffusion steps define the temporal axis, while the variable axis is not raw token position. Instead, the model learns latent variables through token-to-variable assignment. This design addresses a real mismatch: token positions do not have stable semantic identities across samples, and even within one sample their semantic role can drift during denoising. Treating positions as fixed variables would make standard multivariate time-series modeling poorly aligned with the problem.

To solve this, the paper introduces T2V, a Token-to-Variable assignment module. It reorganizes token uncertainty trajectories into latent variables using uncertainty states, contextual semantics, and positional structure. The goal is to create variables with more stable identities while preserving fine-grained denoising behavior. A trajectory-preservation constraint is added so that local fluctuations are not washed out during assignment. That detail matters because hallucination cues may appear as local rebounds, delayed stabilization, or divergence at specific denoising steps rather than as simple average uncertainty differences.

After constructing latent variables, the paper applies DMTM, Dynamic Multivariate Temporal Modeling. The design is explicitly joint: step-adaptive inter-variable interaction followed by variable-wise temporal modeling. The adaptive interaction suppresses unreliable dependencies at early denoising steps and strengthens interactions once variables become more stable later in the process. Then temporal modeling captures how each latent variable evolves over time. This matches the two motivating patterns in the introduction: inconsistent convergence requires comparing variable states across tokens near the end of denoising, while fault propagation requires modeling evolving dependencies among trajectories.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear. First, it preserves the 2D token-step structure instead of collapsing one dimension upfront.
