9:T5c7,The setup is simple and appropriately controlled. The authors evaluate Qwen3.5-0.8B, Qwen3.5-2B, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Llama-3.2-3B, and Gemma-4-E2B with a minimal prompt of the form “x1 × x2 =”, using greedy decoding to avoid chain-of-thought or tool use masking the raw failure pattern. The dataset contains 16,991 multiplication problems spanning 1x1, 1x2, 2x2, 2x3, 2x4, 3x3, and 3x4 settings. Small spaces are exhaustively enumerated; larger ones are uniformly sampled.
The analysis has three layers. First, they measure exact-answer and per-digit accuracy as operand length grows. Second, they port the carry-potential framework from Wen et al. on addition and test whether multiplication errors also form the characteristic bathtub-shaped curve around integer boundaries. Third, after finding that this pattern does not hold cleanly, they inspect the structure of wrong digits themselves. The main empirical observation is a new regime in small Qwen models: lower-order digit errors often preserve parity, while higher-order errors look more like off-by-one carry failures.
That last point is the real contribution. It suggests multiplication errors are not generated by a single source of discretization noise. Lower columns may be governed by local multiplication-table-like heuristics, while higher columns are more affected by carry propagation. This is a better fit to the computational structure of multiplication than a direct transfer of the addition hypothesis.a:T565,The experimental scale is modest but the design is clean. The dataset has 16,991 problems: 81 for 1x1, 810 for 1x2, 8,100 for 2x2, and sampled subsets for larger settings, including 3,000 each for 2x3 and 3x3, and 1,000 each for 2x4 and 3x4. All models are run with greedy decoding and up to 10 generated tokens, with regex-based answer extraction.
Three findings matter most. First, digit-level error rate versus carry potential does not reproduce the clear bathtub shape seen in addition. The authors argue this is because multiplication has much larger and more variable carry potentials, and because errors from partial products and subsequent summation are entangled. Their 99 x 99 example is illustrative: the tens-column raw sum is 162, and with the previous carry the carry potential can reach 17, far beyond the small bounded values typical in addition.
Second, carry still matters, but it is not a sufficient explanatory variable. Errors increase with more complex carry structure, yet they do not localize neatly around integer thresholds.
Third, small Qwen models exhibit a specific structure: low-order digit mistakes often preserve parity, whereas high-order mistakes resemble off-by-one carry failures.
