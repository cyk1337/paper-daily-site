19:T798,The proposed method, Pareto LoRA, treats text loss and image loss as two coupled objectives and seeks a shared descent direction instead of combining them with a fixed scalar weight. Let g_txt and g_img be the gradients of the two modality-specific losses with respect to the trainable LoRA parameters. The method searches for the minimum-norm vector in the convex hull of these gradients. By the MGDA result, this vector either corresponds to a Pareto-stationary point or provides a common descent direction that improves both objectives. Because there are only two objectives, the optimal mixing coefficient has a closed-form solution, which keeps the additional optimization overhead modest.

An important detail is that the method is not applied blindly at every step. The paper first diagnoses the relationship between modality-specific gradients using two signals: cosine similarity for directional conflict and gradient magnitude ratio for dominance. This is a sensible design choice. If the gradients are already aligned and similarly scaled, intervention is unnecessary. The problematic cases are those where one modality overwhelms the update or where the two objectives pull in conflicting directions. This is precisely where fixed loss-level weighting is too crude, because it cannot adapt to layer-wise, stage-wise, or batch-wise changes in gradient geometry.

The LoRA-specific angle also matters. Since LoRA updates only low-rank adapters, adaptation capacity is limited. If the shared update direction is persistently driven by language gradients, the vision side is more likely to be underfit. Pareto LoRA can be interpreted as using that limited adaptation budget more carefully by steering each update toward a more balanced direction. Third, the direction and severity of imbalance vary across tasks, layers, and training stages, which explains why a fixed loss coefficient is often inadequate.
