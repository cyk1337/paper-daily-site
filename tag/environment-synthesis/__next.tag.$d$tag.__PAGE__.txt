9:T674,WildAWM starts from user personas and daily activities rather than from isolated app templates. It constructs an executable personal-computer world containing websites, desktop applications, MCP services, and local files, with stateful dependencies among them. On top of that world, it synthesizes XAI tasks that require agents to combine GUI interaction, command-line operations, and MCP tool use.

The main methodological contribution is the quality-control layer. Instead of relying on static LLM scoring, the pipeline uses execution-based multi-agent quality gates. Heterogeneous agents, built from different models and harnesses, actually execute the synthesized tasks. Their trajectories provide evidence for three properties: solvability, difficulty, and judge consistency. This is important because synthetic tasks often fail in two ways: they are superficially plausible but impossible under the generated world state, or they come with weak verifiers that produce noisy rewards.

A second notable idea is verifier stress testing via sycophancy probes. The authors inject deliberately incorrect outcome feedback and check whether the judge follows the misleading signal. This is a practical way to test reward robustness and is closely related to reward hacking concerns in RLHF-style pipelines.

Relative to prior work such as AWM, WebArena-Infinity, Gym-Anything, TMax, CUA-Gym, and CUA-Universe, the differences are clear: PC-level multi-interface scope instead of a single interaction mode, connected workspace instead of isolated apps, execution evidence instead of reference-only filtering, and an end-to-end open-source synthesis stack.a:T4de,The pipeline produces 341 environment-task pairs. Each task requires at least three interface types, and many are long-horizon workflows exceeding 100 agent steps. The difficulty is nontrivial: even GPT-6-Astra solves only about 60% of them, suggesting these are not low-complexity scripted tasks.

For training, the paper runs online RL on two Qwen models, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.5-4B, using an OpenCode CLI harness augmented with GUI and MCP tools. The key empirical claim is that training exclusively in these synthetic worlds improves performance on all eight out-of-distribution benchmarks spanning web, claw, and computer-use agent settings. The paper’s emphasis is less on squeezing benchmark maxima and more on demonstrating transfer from synthetic interactive worlds to external environments.

The most important experimental signal is the role of the quality gates and verifier checks. Without execution-based filtering, synthetic tasks are likely to be either unsolvable or weakly verified, both of which would poison RL.
