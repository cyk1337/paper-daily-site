The authors add 25,600 Hindi tokens from the Nanda tokenizer. On 100,000 Sangraha Hindi documents, this reduces Hindi fertility from 1.95 to 1.25 tokens per word.

They compare more than 20 initialization configurations. The design space includes vocabulary averaging baselines, external or learned methods such as FOCUS, top-k semantic retrieval and residual MLP mappings, subword composition, norm calibration, and asymmetric initialization for input embeddings versus the output projection.

The strongest family is subword composition. A new token is tokenized by the old tokenizer, and its vector is initialized from a weighted average of the constituent old-token embeddings. This directly reuses the pretrained embedding geometry, which appears more reliable than projecting from an external semantic space. The best reported configuration uses uniform subword averaging with Hindi-specific norm calibration for the input embedding, and character-length-weighted subword averaging for the output head.
