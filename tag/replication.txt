18:T7a7,The contribution is primarily experimental, but the design is careful. The authors use two families of artificial languages: k-Dyck, which encodes strictly nested dependencies, and k-Shuffle Dyck, which relaxes well-nestedness and allows cross-serial dependencies. They test k=64 and k=128. Bracket symbols are represented as integers, and this turns out to be important because the two tokenizers treat those strings very differently: the Llama tokenizer tends to preserve multi-digit numbers more compactly, while the Gemma tokenizer often splits them into individual digits. That changes the effective surface statistics of the artificial corpus and likely changes what the model actually learns during the pre-pretraining phase.

On the natural-language side, they study English, Albanian, Czech, Danish, Dutch, and Finnish. Training data is a monolingual multi-domain mixture of FineWeb/FineWeb2, OpenSubtitles, and EUBookshop in an 8:1:1 ratio. The artificial-language phase is fixed at 16,000 sequences of length 2,048, i.e. 500 steps with batch size 32. The natural-language phase is fixed at 320,000 sequences of length 2,048, i.e. 10,000 steps with batch size 32. Models are three Llama-3-based sizes paired with two tokenizers, yielding six model variants overall, roughly spanning the low-hundreds-of-millions to sub-billion range.

The main metric follows prior work: token-efficiency gain, defined as how many natural-language training tokens earlier the pretrained model reaches the same validation loss as the corresponding baseline. Negative values indicate harm. Crucially, each setup is run with three random seeds, and the paper also correlates gains with quantified linguistic properties such as sentence length, morphological richness, dependency-tree depth, branching, and crossing dependencies. The technical novelty is not a new algorithm; it is a systematic decomposition of a previously positive result into setup-dependent effects.19:T837,Four findings matter most.

First, variance is high, and not only in fringe settings. Across both small and larger models, identical configurations can produce noticeably different token-efficiency gains under different seeds. This directly limits the claim that the method reliably saves training tokens.

Second, the earlier English result is broadly reproducible for the smallest models. The paper states that, in English, most artificial-language pretraining settings improve the smallest model, with 64-Shuffle Dyck as the main exception. So the prior observation is not invalid, but its scope is narrower than one might infer from English-only evidence.

Third, the most stable positive case is a small model with the Llama tokenizer pretrained on 128-Dyck. The paper repeatedly highlights the 154M+LT+128-Dyck setup as consistently beneficial. By contrast, Gemma-tokenized models are weaker on average and less stable, which strongly suggests that tokenization is not a minor implementation detail here; it is a major determinant of whether the artificial-language signal is preserved in a learnable form.

Fourth, Dyck is usually more reliable than Shuffle Dyck, at least for the Llama-tokenizer models. A plausible interpretation is that strict hierarchical closure provides a cleaner training signal for attention and state tracking than the more permissive crossing-dependency variant, which may be harder and noisier under the tested tokenization and scale regime.

In terms of scale, the artificial-language phase is about 16,000×2,048 ≈ 32.8M tokens, followed by roughly 320,000×2,048 ≈ 655M natural-language tokens. The authors note that these are not Chinchilla-optimal token/parameter ratios, but the loss curves are stable in the final ~3,000 steps, so the relative ordering between baseline and pretrained runs is unlikely to flip with more training. The paper also includes additional experiments on unstable setups and attempts to relate gains to linguistic properties. Those analyses explain some trends, but not enough to turn the method into a predictable or robust recipe.1a:T51b,Start with the Introduction and Figure 1 to get the main message right: the paper is not saying the method never works; it is saying the effect is conditional and high-variance. Then read the dataset and setup sections carefully, especially how the artificial languages are encoded as number strings, how LT and GT tokenize those strings differently, and how small the artificial-language phase is relative to the natural-language phase.

Next, inspect the main multi-seed result plots for the smallest models, then the larger-model plots in the appendix. The key question is not the mean gain alone, but whether run-to-run variance is already comparable to or larger than the average benefit. Also pay attention to the highlighted stable case, 154M+LT+128-Dyck, because it shows the phenomenon is real in at least one regime.

For a 20-30 minute skim, use this order: Introduction → setup tables → Figure 1/3/4 → English results table → appendix plots on tokenizer differences and larger models. The two most useful follow-up questions are: whether tokenization is the dominant hidden variable behind the whole effect, and whether token-efficiency gain is a sufficiently stable metric in such a high-variance setting, or should be paired with fixed-budget final loss and trajectory-level summaries.1b:T7c6,方法上有四个关键部件。第一是 rubric 构建。作者不是先拍脑袋定维度，而是通过 self-evolved discrimination-maximization 过程，让维度集合在迭代中稳定到 9 个行为维度，用来覆盖 emotional accompaniment 中更细的行为结构，例如 empathic reflection、advice restraint、safety calibration、persona stability、emotional trajectory tracking 等。这样做的目的，是避免单一总分把不同能力混在一起，也避免人为先验把量表限制死。

第二是四证书式 instrument trust。作者借 classical measurement theory，把评测有效性拆成四类证据：reliability，要求同一工具多次测量结果稳定；cross-instrument replication，要求不同架构、不同训练世代的 judge 在同一对象上给出相近排序；historical-footprint calibration，要求量表能识别已知架构代际变化带来的行为跃迁；pre-registered prediction，要求量表在未观测数据上做出事先承诺、可失败的预测。这套设计的重点，是把“validity”从“和某一组人一致”转成“能否在世界中稳定工作并经得起外部检验”。

第三是 judge 设计。作者使用 5-judge ensemble，覆盖 5 个模型家族，并刻意拉开 17 个月训练 cohort gap，以降低 judge 与 target 同源带来的循环性。文中还报告了 cross-timeline judges 的一致性，以及在 held-out 真实人类对话上的跨家族收敛情况。这里的核心不是 ensemble 本身，而是“异质 judge + 时间错位 judge”共同提供独立性证据。

第四是预注册与审计。作者在收集 Slice 2/3 数据前，预注册了 10 个可证伪假设和 11 个 forward predictions，并保留 4-artifact audit trail。这个部分很像严肃实验科学中的 protocol discipline，目的是防止在主观评测里事后挑结果、调 rubric、改解释。对 LLM 评测来说，这比单纯多跑几个 benchmark 更接近可审计测量。1c:T7ac,The method has four main components. First, the rubric is not fixed a priori. The authors use a self-evolved, discrimination-maximization procedure and report that it stabilizes to a 9-dimensional rubric for emotional accompaniment. The dimensions include behaviors such as empathic reflection, advice restraint, safety calibration, persona stability, and emotional trajectory tracking. This matters because a single scalar score would collapse distinct behaviors and make regressions hard to detect.

Second, they introduce a four-certificate instrument-trust framework inspired by classical measurement theory. The four certificates are: reliability, meaning repeated measurements by the same instrument are consistent; cross-instrument replication, meaning judges from different architectural families and training cohorts converge on the same readings; historical-footprint calibration, meaning the instrument detects known architecture-era transitions as behavioral shifts; and pre-registered prediction, meaning the instrument makes falsifiable forecasts on data not yet collected. The key move is to shift validity away from “agreement with one rater group” toward “stability and external testability in the world.”

Third, the judge setup is deliberately heterogeneous. They use a 5-judge ensemble spanning five model families and a 17-month training-cohort gap. This is meant to reduce same-cohort circularity and provide a stronger independence argument than a single LLM-as-judge setup. The paper also reports agreement across timeline-separated judges and convergence on held-out real human conversations.

Fourth, the study is pre-registered and auditable.
