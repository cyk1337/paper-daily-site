18:T853,The experimental design is deliberately stripped of task structure. The base condition is a topic-free triadic multi-LLM system with no predefined objective, no role specialization, and no reward signal. Three model instances speak in randomized order over many rounds. A referee module identifies interaction acts and routes messages, but does not inject semantic content into the conversation. Each agent receives a fixed environment instruction, an identity, a short-term dialogue buffer, and a long-term retrieval memory containing only prior interaction history.

A key methodological choice is the lexical-versus-semantic split. The authors explicitly argue that growing vocabulary is not evidence of broadening conceptual exploration. They therefore track both surface-level diversity and semantic movement across long trajectories. The study spans 10 foundation models, including both open- and closed-weight systems. For five primary models, they run three independent 1,000-round simulations each, and they report 73 tested conditions overall.

The intervention suite is broad: decoding settings and algorithms, prompt variants, memory and retrieval changes, population size and model composition, reduced safety-oriented alignment, sycophancy-targeted activation steering, diversity-oriented RL, multi-agent orchestration, and exogenous semantic perturbations. This breadth is important because the paper is trying to rule out easy explanations. The claim is not merely that a default setup collapses, but that many plausible fixes fail to restore sustained, coherent semantic expansion.

The mechanistic section is the most interesting part. The authors combine transcript-level distributional analysis with white-box attention-head diagnostics. Their proposed explanation is that autoregressive feedback reinforces historically dominant representations. In other words, the system keeps reconditioning on its own high-probability semantic traces, which gradually traps it in a narrower region of concept space. That is a stronger and more structural claim than saying the models are simply sycophantic or over-aligned.19:T46e,The clearest empirical result is a robust lexical-semantic dissociation across 73 conditions. Cumulative vocabulary keeps increasing, yet semantic displacement within a run converges and semantic separation across independent runs remains limited. So the system continues to produce new wording without genuinely moving into new conceptual territory.

A second headline result is that none of the 13 intervention families reliably restores broader and coherent semantic exploration. This includes interventions that many practitioners would expect to help, such as decoding changes, memory modifications, orchestration changes, and attempts to reduce alignment-related conservatism.

The main long-run experiments use five named models: GPT-5.6-Terra, Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-4-mini, DeepSeek-V3, and Phi-4. Each receives three independent 1,000-round simulations, for 15 long trajectories in the primary setup. The broader study spans 200- to 1,000-round simulations over 10 model families.
