The paper introduces two principles: Ease of Understanding, meaning demonstrations should align with what the model can already parse and execute; and Smoothness of Knowledge Progression, meaning consecutive demonstrations should not make large conceptual jumps. Based on the second principle, the authors propose Curvilinear Demonstration Selection (CDS). Instead of selecting examples only by query-demo similarity, CDS treats the demonstration list as a trajectory in embedding space and orders examples to minimize total conceptual curvature. This is the key distinction from standard similarity retrieval: similarity retrieval optimizes local resemblance to the query, while CDS optimizes whether the full sequence forms a learnable teaching path.
