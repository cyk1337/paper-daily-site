9:T441,The paper introduces safety offset learning. The key object is the behavioral transition induced by supervision: if an aligned model initially refuses a benign-looking input, but the target answer is helpful and compliant, fine-tuning trains the model to suppress refusal. If that learned offset generalizes beyond the benign training distribution, harmful prompts can also receive more compliant answers. The authors first create controlled benign examples by inserting safety-sensitive keywords into ordinary instructions while keeping the original harmless responses. They then vary keyword count and position to separate two requirements: the induced refusal-to-compliance transition must be large, and the discrepancy must not be solvable through a simple surface shortcut. SAFEOFF operationalizes this idea by rewriting whole benign instructions while keeping their original answers.
