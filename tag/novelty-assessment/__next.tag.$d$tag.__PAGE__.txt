9:T7e0,The contribution is a benchmark plus a cascading evaluation protocol. The dataset is built from two expert-grounded sources.

The first source is ICLR 2023–2026 OpenReview forums. The authors run a two-stage LLM pipeline to extract novelty-overlap claims from official reviews and author rebuttals, then have three PhD-level annotators verify prior-work identity, overlap dimensions, and reviewer evidence. The released high-confidence subset contains 236 pairs. This source is anchored in real review disputes and tends to emphasize method and problem overlap.

The second source is survey co-citation grouping. Papers grouped together by survey authors as similar are used to construct positives after annotation, while negatives are created by cross-group sampling from distinct similarity groups mentioned within the same sentence. This is a better negative construction than random pairing because it keeps topical proximity while preserving dimension-specific non-overlap. It also contributes more task-level similarity cases.

Each instance is labeled independently along three dimensions: Task, Problem, and Method. These correspond to application goal, core technical challenge, and solution approach. A key design choice is that these are fixed ground-truth labels rather than post hoc interpretations of free-form model outputs. That makes error localization explicit.

The evaluation protocol is stricter than standard benchmark scoring. Models must not only predict overlap per dimension, but also provide verbatim evidence spans. Outputs then go through a three-stage cascade: per-dimension correctness, evidence hallucination check, and reasoning mismatch check. Only predictions that pass all three stages count toward Verified F1. In other words, the benchmark measures “correct answer with faithful support,” not just label coincidence. The benchmark also includes 50 multi-paper grouping sets to test whether models can organize related work more structurally than pairwise comparison alone.a:T486,The benchmark is evaluated on 18 LLMs. The main empirical message is not that one model reaches a particularly high score, but that failure modes are systematic: even when the final overlap judgment is correct, models often support it with fabricated evidence or with evidence that is only weakly connected to the stated conclusion. These failures vary substantially across Task, Problem, and Method dimensions. The paper also notes that the two data sources induce different distributions—ICLR-derived pairs skew toward method/problem overlap, while survey-derived pairs skew toward task similarity—which helps expose dimension-specific weaknesses rather than hiding them under a single distribution.

In terms of scale, the benchmark contains 619 paper pairs and 50 multi-paper sets. The ICLR source starts from 42,682 OpenReview forums, indicating substantial upstream mining before strong human filtering at release time. For readers, the most important quantitative pattern is the gap between ordinary correctness metrics and Verified F1.
