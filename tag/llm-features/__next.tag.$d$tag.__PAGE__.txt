5:T535,The authors train sparse autoencoders on mid-layer residual streams of Llama 3.1 8B-Instruct and Gemma 2 9B-IT, then search for literary features using a contrastive minimal-corpus setup plus a three-stage validation pipeline. The minimal corpora are hand-crafted prompt pairs that differ along a single literary axis, such as show-versus-tell scenes, Deep-POV versus ostranenie pairs, and scenes covering the Cowen-Keltner 27-emotion taxonomy. Candidate features are filtered in three stages: logit-lens vocabulary projection for cheap initial discovery, LLM-judge semantic purity scoring for candidate filtering, and causal validation through feature steering judged by a 5-LLM panel.

The paper organizes the recovered features into four classes. Naming-gates promote lexical or near-lexical emission of target affects. Self features cluster around first-person voice and identity register. Stylistic modulators control writing style on a more continuous axis, including show-don’t-tell and defamiliarization. Compositional emotions require multiple features to be steered together. An important methodological point is that cross-model alignment is defined behaviorally rather than geometrically: two features from different architectures need not align by decoder cosine if they produce the same controllable writing behavior.6:T520,The empirical output is a feature catalog plus steering-based causal demonstrations, not a standard benchmark leaderboard. The paper reports 24 canonical paper-grade features across the two models. A single feature controls the show-don’t-tell flip in each architecture: f32213 in Llama and f15097 in Gemma. For first-person register, the authors identify an eleven-feature self cluster in each model, with one feature described as the most RLHF-loaded self feature: f96419 in Llama and f70443 in Gemma. On emotion coverage, Llama reaches full 27/27 coverage of the Cowen-Keltner taxonomy using 24 singleton gates, one partial case, and two multi-feature compositions. Gemma reaches 23/27, with adoration as the strict remaining failure. The paper also reports that 12 Gemma singleton gates transfer cross-lingually at unanimous 8/8 judge agreement.

A notable result is that some emotions, especially joy and adoration, do not appear as singleton features. They require compositions such as gate × self × show-don’t-tell. That supports the paper’s main claim that the internal organization is compositional rather than a flat list of independent affect knobs. Still, the strength of the evidence depends heavily on judge reliability, steering calibration, and the coverage of the hand-built prompt
