Drawing from sensemaking theory, the authors decompose research ideation into eight ordered cognitive stages and use citation neighborhoods as conditioning context, producing full research trajectories rather than short proposals. The dataset, SciSense-Traj, has two modes. In Target mode, given a target paper and its cited works, the model reconstructs a plausible ideation trajectory that could have led from those citations to the known paper. In Infer mode, given the same citation context, the model proposes novel directions without anchoring to a known outcome. These trajectories are then distilled into SciSense-LM models ranging from 3B to 70B.

The most interesting part is not the eight-stage framework by itself, but the supervision design. Intuitively, open-ended Infer supervision might seem more creative. The paper finds the opposite: outcome-anchored Target supervision produces trajectories that are both higher quality and more diverse. The proposed mechanism is that Target trajectories spend more effort on constructive stages such as Schema and Elaboration, which organize prior work into a coherent explanatory structure, whereas Infer trajectories overemphasize early Hypothesis and Questioning stages, leading to broader but less grounded plans. In that sense, the constraint is not merely narrowing the search space; it provides a stabilizing structural prior for a difficult planning problem.

From a training perspective, this is best understood as trajectory distillation: generate structured research plans with a stronger process, then transfer that planning behavior into smaller or more standard LLMs.1a:T50b,The main empirical result is consistent and simple: Target supervision beats Infer on both diversity and quality across model families and scales, without the usual quality–diversity tradeoff. The paper reports a 2.0% gain in aggregate plan quality for Target over Infer, while also showing stronger diversity under multiple automatic metrics. It also includes stage-level analysis showing that Target plans emphasize Schema and Elaboration, whereas Infer plans front-load Hypothesis and Questioning.

The downstream transfer result is more important than the planning metrics alone. When these trajectories are used to condition coding agents, Target trajectories improve the quality of generated scientific artifacts, especially scientific grounding, and also help executability and downstream utility. That suggests the planning text is not decorative; it changes downstream agent behavior in measurable ways.

In terms of scale, the dataset is around 100K trajectories and the distilled models span 3B to 70B.
