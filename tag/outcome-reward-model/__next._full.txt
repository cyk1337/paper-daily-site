18:T4f7,GRACE parameterizes verification granularity continuously with g∈[0,1]: g=0 is outcome-only scoring (ORM), g=1 is step-level scoring (PRM), and intermediate g corresponds to verifying every few steps. The framework couples:
1) A compute model: per-sample generation cost C_gen and verification cost C_ver(g)=c0+c1·g, yielding the number of candidates N(g)=C/(C_gen+c0+c1·g).
2) A difficulty/accuracy model: δ for base success rate of sampled chains; α(g) for verifier accuracy, assumed monotone increasing and Lipschitz in g.
3) A performance objective F(g;C,δ,α): probability of selecting a correct answer among N(g) candidates using the verifier at granularity g.
The key analytical move is decomposing the marginal effect of increasing g into a non-negative “precision gain” term (better discrimination) and a non-positive “sample loss” term (fewer candidates). Under additional regularity assumptions (including log-concavity), they derive a critical compute threshold C* such that coarse verification is optimal below C* and fine verification is optimal above C*, i.e., a regime transition.
