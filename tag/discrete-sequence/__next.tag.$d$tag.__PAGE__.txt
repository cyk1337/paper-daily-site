a:T5ef,The method, SCMDM, applies self-conditioning to masked discrete diffusion in a post-training setting. The training procedure uses two forward passes through the same denoiser. In the first pass, the model takes the current masked/noisy sequence and predicts a clean-state distribution. In the second pass, that prediction is detached and fed back as a self-conditioning signal together with the same current input, producing a refined prediction. The loss is applied only to the second-pass output. Several design choices matter. First, there is no recurrent latent-state pathway and no auxiliary reference model, so the architectural change is small. Second, detaching the first-pass prediction is important: it turns the first pass into a fixed draft for refinement rather than a shortcut gradient path. Third, the paper argues that post-training is a distinct regime from training from scratch. In continuous diffusion, self-conditioning is often dropped stochastically so the model learns both conditioned and unconditioned behavior. Here, once a pretrained MDM already produces informative clean estimates, dropping the self-conditioning signal becomes counterproductive because it mixes refinement updates with weaker unconditional ones. The paper therefore advocates full self-conditioning during adaptation. Relative to prior discrete self-conditioning approaches, the claimed differences are: no extra architectural modules, no auxiliary model, and direct adaptation from an existing pretrained MDM checkpoint.b:T4de,The evaluation spans four discrete-generation domains: natural language, molecules, genomics, and discretized images. The most relevant result for language modeling is on OpenWebText, where generative perplexity improves from 42.89 to 23.72, a 44.69% reduction. That is a large gain and supports the claim that standard MDMs lose useful information across denoising steps. The paper also emphasizes that these gains come with minimal additional compute, which is important for practical adoption. Outside language, QM9 validity improves from 594.2±9.5 to 628.2±15.0 and uniqueness from 539.4±12.6 to 546.6±12.1; genomic distribution fidelity improves by up to 10.73% across denoising-step budgets; and CIFAR-10 FID improves by 9.12%. The most informative ablation is the comparison between full self-conditioning and partial self-conditioning with stochastic dropout. In the post-training regime, full self-conditioning is consistently stronger, suggesting that adaptation should teach the model to refine its own draft rather than alternate between refinement and unconditional prediction.
