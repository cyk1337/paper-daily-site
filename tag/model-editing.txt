18:T6a8,The strongest part of the work is the experimental isolation. The authors build a frozen multi-agent pipeline in which the only changing component is the final decision-layer model. The task is a weekly up/down call on 60 Warsaw Stock Exchange equities over 18 weeks, with five samples per cell, yielding 21,600 decisions. Crucially, the task elicits no refusals: the base models complete all 10,800 base-arm decisions without refusing once. That means any delta after abliteration cannot be attributed to refusal suppression itself; it must be an off-target behavioral effect.

They also control provenance carefully: official BF16 checkpoints, one abliteration recipe, frozen prompts, and a fixed upstream pipeline. This is important because behavioral comparisons are easily confounded by quantization, checkpoint mismatch, or prompt drift. The preregistered primary endpoint is optimism rate—the share of “up” decisions under identical evidence. Additional readouts include verbalized confidence, self-justification length, and uncertainty vocabulary. A capability-covariate control is used to test whether the observed shifts are merely a byproduct of degraded instruction following.

Mechanistically, the paper does not localize the exact geometry of the edited direction inside the network. Instead, it uses behavioral readouts to falsify the “clean surgery” hypothesis. If the refusal direction were truly separable, outputs on a no-refusal task should remain stable. The observed cross-family optimism shift and lexical thinning of uncertainty language suggest that the edited direction is entangled with broader behavioral traits rather than being a strictly isolated refusal feature.19:T5bb,There are four results worth retaining. First, the preregistered main effect is a directional optimism shift that replicates across two model families: abliterated models are more likely to predict “up” under the same evidence. The abstract does not expose the exact percentage change, but the full text treats this as the central replicated finding. Second, exploratory analyses show longer self-justifications and reduced uncertainty vocabulary, indicating that the edit changes not only binary choices but also explanatory style. Third, expressed confidence does not generalize cleanly across families and even reverses sign, which is informative: the side effect is not a simple global increase in confidence but a more structured redistribution of behavioral tendencies. Fourth, the authors revisit an earlier pilot result about “excision of doubt” and show it was an artifact of contaminated provenance—a mismatched quantizer pair. Once provenance is cleaned, that earlier effect disappears or flips.

The design itself is a strength: 21,600 decisions, five repeated samples, zero generation failures, and a frozen upstream pipeline make the comparison unusually controlled for a behavioral study. The main limitation is external validity. The probe task is financially framed and binary, so the paper demonstrates off-target effects convincingly in one controlled setting, but broader generalization to other agentic tasks still needs follow-up work.1a:T698,The paper has three main technical components. First, it characterizes the native discrete gauge of transformer residual streams under coordinate-preserving parameterizations. For LayerNorm, the residual chart has permutation symmetry up to a global sign. For RMSNorm with generic per-channel gain, the correct group is the hyperoctahedral group B_d = S_d ⋉ {±1}^d, meaning arbitrary coordinate permutations plus independent sign flips. The paper also clarifies an important distinction: broader orthogonal freedom can be recovered only after absorbing the RMSNorm gain into adjacent weights, but that changes the coordinate system and no longer preserves sparse coordinate identity. Second, it proposes sign-marginalized activation matching. Instead of matching coordinates by signed correlation, it matches by absolute correlation |<h_i, h'_j>| and recovers the sign afterward from the matched pair. This is the right objective because the true counterpart of a coordinate may be maximally anti-correlated under a negative gauge sign. The authors prove a sharp population-level failure mode for signed-correlation matching in a decorrelated setting: if half of the true signs are negative, the correct entries become the most negative ones in their rows, so maximizing signed correlation systematically avoids them even with unlimited probes. Third, it introduces transport via a checkpoint atlas. Rather than matching only two endpoints, the method composes local B_d gauges saved along a training trajectory. This exploits the fact that nearby checkpoints differ only slightly, so local matches have higher margins and can be chained into a much more reliable global transport map.1b:T62e,The experiments are designed to show how omission of signs fails in practice. On gauge-recorded LLM activations, signed-cost recovery stays near 50%, while sign-marginalized recovery reaches 100%, matching the theoretical obstruction. On a gauge-instrumented Qwen2.5-1.5B finetuning trajectory of 1500 steps, with a random B_d gauge applied at step 0 and the induced correspondence recorded as ground truth, direct endpoint matching recovers 60.3% of coordinates. Transport-via-base raises this to 91.1%, whereas endpoint-via-base without local chained transport achieves only 11.4%, showing that the gain comes from composing many small high-margin local gauges rather than from using the base model as a trivial reference.
