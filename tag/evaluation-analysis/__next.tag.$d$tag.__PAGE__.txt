9:T6c6,The setup models an agent as a fixed solver model M paired with a harness H, where H controls prompting, context management, tool interaction, and execution logic. The central analytical tool is pass@k. pass@1 is treated as a proxy for single-run reliability, while pass@k measures the fraction of tasks solved at least once within k attempts and is used as a proxy for coverage. The key logic is simple: if an optimized harness only improves reliability on already-solvable tasks, its advantage should shrink or even reverse as k increases. If it truly expands the solvable set, the advantage should persist at larger k.

For optimization, the paper uses Meta-Harness. A coding agent proposes harness revisions after inspecting prior harness code, evaluation scores, and execution traces. Candidate harnesses are validated, and the best one H* is compared against the initial harness H0. The novelty is less in the optimizer itself than in the evaluation lens applied to the resulting harnesses.

The paper also introduces stronger references: larger models from the same family and settings with higher reasoning effort. This makes the comparison more informative. It asks whether harness optimization merely makes a smaller model look better at low attempt budgets, or whether it actually approaches the task coverage of stronger systems. Results are further broken down by task difficulty and OOD conditions, which is important because aggregate scores can hide losses on harder subsets.

Methodologically, the strongest part of the paper is this multi-axis diagnosis: attempt budget, task difficulty, OOD behavior, and comparison to stronger references. That is a more faithful capability analysis than a single benchmark number.a:T44b,The headline result is on LawBench. An optimized GPT-OSS-20B harness beats a GPT-OSS-120B baseline by 16.05 percentage points at pass@1, but trails it by 4.00 points at pass@64. That is a clean example of improved reliability without superior coverage.

The same pattern appears elsewhere. On HotpotQA and AppWorld, selected harnesses approach or exceed stronger references in aggregate metrics, yet fail to surpass them on hard subsets. The transferred text-classification harness is even more revealing: despite small pass@1 gains on two OOD datasets, it falls below the baseline at pass@64 on all OOD datasets considered. This suggests that optimization can collapse behavior toward safer, more repeatable trajectories, improving first-attempt success while reducing exploratory coverage over multiple attempts.

The main experimental takeaway is that pass@1 and pass@k measure different things and should not be conflated. Harness gains are heterogeneous: they may help easy or medium tasks through better execution stability while hurting hard or OOD tasks by narrowing the search distribution.b:T476,The evaluation covers 12 multimodal task families and 22 models in total, including non-reasoning models such as Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma-3, and GPT-4.1, and reasoning models such as QVQ, Skywork-R1V2, and Gemini-Thinking.
