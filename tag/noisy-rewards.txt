18:T581,The authors build a chronological market-analysis sandbox and a benchmark called Beta. Each episode is defined by a stock, a decision date, and a forecast horizon. The model can call nine retrieval tools to inspect different views of the historical state at the decision date: price and volume patterns, relative performance, sector context, market context, and indicator-based summaries with configurable windows and resolutions. The episode ends with a terminal action that outputs a numeric return forecast.

Training has two stages. In SFT, the model learns from tool-use demonstrations constructed with historical-outcome supervision. In PPO, it generates its own episodes and receives rewards computed from realized future returns. The paper compares Base, SFT, and SFT+PPO checkpoints to separate imitation gains from outcome-optimization gains.

The technical contribution is not a new RL algorithm. The contribution is the training setup: turning noisy realized returns into a usable optimization signal while preserving a realistic evidence-gathering loop. A key analytical point is that post-training changes the investigation policy itself. The model uses more tools and shifts toward relative-performance, sector, and broader-market evidence.
