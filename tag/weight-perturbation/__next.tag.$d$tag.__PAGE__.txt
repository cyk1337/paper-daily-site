9:T46d,The authors recast Neural Thickets as a Perturb-Select-Vote procedure. PSV samples N perturbed predictors, scores them on a small task-specific validation set, keeps the top K members, and aggregates their predictions by majority vote. Full-weight RandOpt is one instance of this template. The paper then weakens the perturbation space step by step: perturbing all parameters, perturbing a subset of layers, perturbing a single decoder block, and finally perturbing no weights at all. In the logit-only variant, the model is fully frozen and each member receives a fixed random Gaussian bias added to its output logits throughout autoregressive generation, followed by greedy decoding. A first-order argument supports this move: small weight perturbations affect predictions primarily through the logit shifts they induce. The paper also separates the roles of sampling, selection, and voting. On stronger base models, temperature-tuned sampling with majority voting already matches much of full-weight PSV; on weaker models, validation-based selection remains more important because it filters unreliable members before aggregation.a:T413,The paper reproduces the large gains reported by Gan and Isola, including the Countdown example where Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct improves from 10.8% to 63.6% under the original perturbation setup. The central empirical result is that full-weight perturbation is not necessary. Perturbing a single decoder block recovers much of the gain, and logit-only PSV often matches or exceeds full-weight PSV. On MATH-500 with OLMo-3-7B-Instruct, the base model scores 63.3%, full-weight perturbation ensemble reaches 74.7%, and logit-only PSV reaches 81.3%. The PSV runs use large perturbation budgets, typically N from 1000 to 5000, and a selection set of about 200 target-task examples. The paper also evaluates pretrained vision backbones under domain shift. There, logit-only PSV uses one trained linear probe and perturbs class logits, while full-weight PSV perturbs the backbone and trains one probe per member.
