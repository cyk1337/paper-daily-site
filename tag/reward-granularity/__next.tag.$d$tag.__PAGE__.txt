9:T808,The method, S2T-RLHF, uses a two-stage hierarchical credit assignment scheme: sentence level first, token level second. The motivation is simple and well argued. Human preferences are often expressed over semantically coherent units such as claims, instructions, refusals, or stylistic choices, and sentences are a much better proxy for those units than individual tokens. Tokens mostly realize sentence meaning through lexical and syntactic choices. So instead of directly decomposing a sequence-level reward into token-level rewards, the method first allocates reward across sentence-level units and only then performs bounded token-level refinement within each sentence.

A key design choice is that the reward model remains fixed and only sequence-level preference rewards are used. In the first stage, the global reward is distributed over sentence units through a structured cooperative allocation mechanism. The wording suggests that sentences are treated as jointly contributing parts of the response, rather than independently scored fragments. That matters because a response-level preference often depends on interactions across sentences, not just local quality. In the second stage, token-level refinement is explicitly bounded. This is crucial: the paper’s main thesis is that unconstrained fine-grained refinement can inject instability, so token-level signals are allowed only as local adjustments around a sentence-level semantic anchor.

The appendices reportedly provide theoretical discussion around three issues: how sequence-level rewards induce global advantage alignment, how variance amplifies over long horizons, and how gradients scale with response length. The S2T design addresses these by shortening the effective credit-assignment path through sentence-level decomposition, while preserving limited local flexibility through bounded token refinement.
