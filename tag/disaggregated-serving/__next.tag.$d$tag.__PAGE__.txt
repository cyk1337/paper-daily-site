9:T61a,The method has three components. First, PRP, a Prefix Reuse Predictor, estimates whether a prefix will be requested again within a future horizon. It uses lightweight online features rather than offline profiling. The features include structural signals such as prefix length, modality, radix-tree position, and session metadata, as well as temporal signals such as recency, frequency, inter-arrival patterns, and time-of-day effects. Second, CARS, a Cost-Aware Retention Score, converts reuse probability into a keep/evict decision using a closed-form cost model. This is the key step. A miss cost includes recomputation on a prefill node plus transfer to a decode node. An admission cost includes the initial transfer into the shared KV pool and the opportunity cost of occupying pool memory. So the decision is not merely whether reuse is likely, but whether expected reuse value exceeds retention cost under the current system configuration. Third, ATC, an Adaptive Threshold Controller, adjusts the retention threshold as pool pressure, bandwidth conditions, and workload mix change.

The paper’s main strength is the alignment between prediction and systems objective. LRU/LFU can only encode recency or frequency; they cannot express that misses on long multimodal prefixes are much more expensive, while admitting short low-value prefixes may be wasteful. The theoretical section also formalizes when the optimal admission threshold must vary with prefix length, showing that a uniform threshold is structurally suboptimal even in an idealized setting.a:T578,The evaluation covers both language and multimodal workloads, with baselines including No-Cache, LRU-Pool, and Mooncake-LRU. The headline results are on end-to-end TTFT and inter-node transfer volume. KVLearn reduces TTFT by up to 56% versus No-Cache, 38% versus LRU-Pool, and 33% versus Mooncake-LRU. It also cuts inter-node transfer volume by up to 53% relative to LRU-Pool. On MM-Session-style multimodal workloads, it reaches near-Oracle throughput under realistic pool sizes. This suggests the gains come not just from more hits, but from avoiding low-value admissions and expensive misses.

The problem scale is also important. For a 13B model in bfloat16, a high-resolution visual prefix can consume roughly 128–512 MB of KV memory, and recomputing such a prefix on an A100 can cost around 100–400 ms. In that regime, a single wrong retention decision is expensive enough that simple recency-based policies become fragile.

Removing the cost-aware scoring while keeping the reuse predictor should hurt noticeably, showing that reuse likelihood and retention value are different quantities.
