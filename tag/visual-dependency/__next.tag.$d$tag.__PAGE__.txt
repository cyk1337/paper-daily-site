a:T5ca,The central tool is the Visual Dependency Gap (VDG). For each question, the model is evaluated under the original-video condition and under a black-screen condition; VDG is defined from the difference in correctness between the two. This per-question formulation is important because it allows the benchmark to be stratified by actual dependence on visual input rather than only by aggregate averages. The paper then uses paired McNemar tests to show statistical dissociation: if two models differ significantly on original videos but not on black screens, higher accuracy cannot be equated with stronger visual grounding. Beyond the black-screen control, the authors build a four-step diagnostic ladder: black screen, single frame, shuffled frames, and original video. This decomposes what the benchmark is rewarding: any visual content at all, static spatial information, frame diversity, and temporal order. An FPS ablation from 0.5 to 24 FPS provides an additional probe into temporal sensitivity. The paper also revisits compression robustness through CRF sweeps and argues that flat robustness curves can be an artifact of benchmark composition, where visually dependent and visually independent questions cancel each other. The study spans 20 models, 10 architecture families, and 2B–78B parameters, including open-weight and API systems. The authors are careful not to overclaim from 4-bit quantized runs and restrict defended scaling conclusions to full-precision models.b:T640,The most important findings are diagnostic rather than leaderboard-oriented. On MVBench, InternVL2-8B significantly outperforms Qwen2-VL-7B under the original-video condition, with McNemar p = 0.0003, yet the same pair is statistically indistinguishable under black screens, with p = 0.53. That is the clearest evidence that accuracy and visual dependency are separable. Across 20 models, the ranking of task types by VDG is highly stable: Attribute Perception is the most visually dependent category at roughly 0.41, while Temporal Reasoning is close to the language-only baseline at roughly 0.06. The taxonomy also transfers across benchmarks: Video-MME labels reportedly predict MVBench classification perfectly for the mapped categories (7/7), and the pattern generalizes to EgoSchema. The four-condition ladder shows that temporal ordering contributes near-zero accuracy across open-weight models, whereas frame diversity matters much more; in practice, shuffling frames often hurts little, while collapsing to a single frame hurts more. The paper also highlights a diversity-extraction collapse in Qwen3-VL relative to Qwen2.5-VL, showing that newer models are not necessarily better at using video-specific information. In compression experiments, flat CRF curves are shown to be misleading because visually dependent questions are disproportionately compression-sensitive, and their degradation can be masked by many low-dependency questions. The benchmark suite includes a 600-question stratified subset of Video-MME, 462 MVBench questions, and EgoSchema, with models ranging from 2B to 78B.c:T42f,This one is worth a careful read for its evaluation methodology. Start with the introduction and the formal definition of VDG so the target quantity is clear: the paper is measuring dependence on visual input, not some vague notion of multimodal quality. Then go straight to the McNemar dissociation section, which is the strongest statistical argument in the paper. After that, read the task-type VDG spectrum and the cross-benchmark consistency analysis to understand which categories are structurally vulnerable to language-only solving. Next, study the four-condition ladder and the FPS ablation, since that is where the paper decomposes video information into spatial content, frame diversity, and temporal order. The compression section is also worth reading because it explains why apparent robustness can be a benchmark artifact. If you work on video pretraining or benchmark design, pay more attention to the deltas between black screen, single frame, shuffled frames, and original video than to raw accuracy rankings.
