9:T44c,RLDF uses two sources of denoising feedback. Rollout feedback records unmasking probabilities and timesteps along the actual inference trajectory, capturing which positions and denoising stages matter for the final response. Training feedback comes from predicted probabilities during denoising training, from which the method keeps the more informative positions for stable policy updates. Based on these signals, RLDF applies weighted timestep sampling, prioritizing denoising steps with high predictive uncertainty and larger influence on the final output. The policy loss is computed only on selected steps, rather than over all denoising steps or through coarse no-masking and random-masking approximations. A central design choice is to optimize from intermediate noisy states toward a clipped clean-state estimate \(\hat{x}_0\), instead of optimizing toward the next state. The clean-state target is more directly aligned with the final answer and is reported to reduce loss spikes while improving signal utilization.
