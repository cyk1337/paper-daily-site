a:T7ee,The method starts from the rectified-flow prediction objective for the clean image predictor f_theta(x_t, t, c), where x_t is a linear interpolation between a clean image x_0 and Gaussian noise. Standard training minimizes E||f_theta(x_t,t,c)-x_0||^2. AdvantageFlow replaces this with an advantage-weighted version so that higher-reward samples receive larger positive influence and lower-reward samples receive negative influence relative to the prompt-level batch average.

The main technical difficulty is that advantages can be negative. Naively using signed weights makes the per-sample least-squares objective non-convex and unstable. The paper addresses this with rollout policy regularization. Instead of directly fitting noisy signed targets, it introduces a reward-independent reference induced by a moving-average rollout policy. The derivation is framed as fitting a locally reward-improving target distribution, i.e., a linearly tilted distribution around the current policy that shifts mass toward better samples. The regularization term emerges naturally when replacing noisy sampled targets with their conditional means in the reward-independent part of the objective. This reduces variance and restores strict convexity at the sample level.

An additional useful point is unification. The paper shows that a recent negative-aware forward-process RL baseline can be recovered as a special case under a particular adaptive regularization choice. So the contribution is not only a new algorithm, but also a cleaner objective-level view that organizes several forward-process RL heuristics under one framework.

Compared with reverse-process RL, the difference is structural: no stochastic sampler is required for gradient estimation, no denoising-step credit assignment is needed, and the optimized loss remains the same type of forward prediction loss used in pretraining, only reweighted by reward-derived statistics. In practice this makes the method look closer to supervised fine-tuning than to conventional RL.b:T520,Experiments are built on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium for text-to-image reward optimization. The main comparisons are against Flow-GRPO on the reverse-process side and a negative-aware fine-tuning baseline on the forward-process side. The reported result is consistent throughout the paper: AdvantageFlow outperforms both baselines across several reward models and also extends to multi-objective optimization.

The empirical section mainly validates four claims. First, forward-process RL can improve reward without explicit reverse-process credit assignment. Second, rollout regularization is not just a training trick; it is central to both stability and final quality, especially when negative advantages are common. Third, gains are not purely reward-model overfitting: the paper includes GenEval as an external check against reward hacking. Fourth, the unifying view is meaningful in practice, since different regularization choices explain the behavior of prior forward-process baselines.

What is missing, from a pretraining-research perspective, is large-scale scaling analysis. The paper does not focus on model-size scaling, token budgets, or training-dynamics diagnostics in the style of LLM pretraining work. It is primarily an algorithm paper centered on objective design and optimization stability.c:T402,Start with the Introduction and Algorithm sections. Then read the derivation of the advantage-weighted least-squares objective from the local reward-improving target distribution. The most important technical point is the role of rollout regularization: it is doing two jobs at once, variance reduction and repair of the non-convexity caused by negative advantages.
