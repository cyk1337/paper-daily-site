For a prompt-response pair, the prompt remains visible and only response tokens are maskable. The method first samples a noise level and over-masks the response, creating a candidate set of masked positions. It then selects a subset to reveal back to their gold tokens, leaving the rest as supervised targets. The first forward pass, run without gradients, estimates each candidate token's gold-token probability and attention-based support relations between positions. Target priority favors intermediate gold-token probabilities: tokens that are already easy carry little training value, while tokens that are too unlikely may provide weak or noisy learning signal. Reveal selection scores how much a revealed token supports the remaining masked targets, with diminishing returns, while accounting for the cost of losing the revealed token as a target. This produces a non-monotone submodular set-selection problem. After selecting the reveal set, a second forward pass evaluates the remaining masked targets under the selected context. Loss weights are increased for tokens whose gold-token probability improves and whose updated confidence still indicates useful supervision. The weights are capped and normalized so that the average supervised-token weight remains one.
