18:T534,The method, REST (REpresentation-Supervised Thoughts), augments the standard final-answer cross-entropy with four differentiable losses on the latent thought itself. The theoretical setup argues that CE on the final answer does not penalize bad intermediate representations as long as the correct answer remains sufficiently likely, so recursive systems can accumulate representational errors over rounds and across agents.

REST targets four properties. Causality requires the thought to preserve information needed to reproduce the correct output. Minimality pushes it to discard information irrelevant to that output. Separability prevents semantically distinct inputs, especially those with different output distributions, from collapsing to similar latent thoughts. Stability encourages the thought to reflect the distribution over plausible outputs rather than a single sampled answer trace. These losses are added to CE with tunable weights.

Implementation-wise, the paper studies both a multi-agent latent recursive system and a single-agent variant where a model feeds its own hidden states back as the next reasoning step. Importantly, REST does not change the architecture or inference procedure; it only changes the training objective. That makes the paper more about missing supervision than about a new system design.19:T432,The evaluation spans MATH500, GPQA-Diamond, MedQA, AIME2025/2026, LiveCodeBench-v6, and MBPP+. Under matched training budget, identical training data, and the same latent budget, REST improves over CE-only by +3.5 points on average in the multi-agent setting and +3.3 in the single-agent setting. The best configurations reach +7.5 and +6.5 points, respectively. For a skim-level read, these are the main numbers to retain.

The paper also reports that REST decodes 15.4% more tokens on average at inference. The authors attribute this to a higher rate of convergence to a final answer. That is an interesting trade-off: better latent supervision improves accuracy, but not necessarily by reducing visible decoding cost in the current setup.

The analysis sections matter more than usual here. The paper claims that CE-only systems exhibit thought collapse and retention of irrelevant information, while REST-trained systems better satisfy the targeted properties.
