5:T5ac,The paper proposes PrecisionDiff, an automated differential testing framework. The core idea is to treat two precision configurations of the same model as two separate implementations and actively search for inputs that make them disagree. The main challenge is that LLM inputs live in discrete token space, unlike prior differential testing work in continuous domains. To address this, the authors introduce a dual-precision joint optimization objective: optimize for a target behavior under one precision while simultaneously optimizing for the opposite behavior under the other precision. This drives the search toward precision-sensitive decision boundaries.

In the safety-alignment instantiation, the framework constructs adversarial suffixes that keep the model safely refusing under one precision while eliciting unsafe content under another. This is more specific than standard GCG-style jailbreak optimization. The objective is explicitly cross-precision and contrastive, so the discovered suffixes are tuned to expose precision-sensitive triggers rather than generic vulnerabilities. The paper also performs layer-wise divergence analysis to localize where small numerical differences get amplified. The reported hotspots are input-stage layers, early attention blocks, and output-end modules.
