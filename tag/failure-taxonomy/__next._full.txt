18:T6d4,The paper has four main components. First is a six-state control-decision taxonomy: Act, Ask, Refuse, Stop, Confirm, and Recover. This is useful because it does not collapse behavior into a binary do/don’t-do decision. Instead, it isolates the main branching points that matter in deployment. Ask handles ambiguity or missing information, Confirm handles risky or irreversible actions, Recover handles tool failures or observation errors, and Stop handles proper termination. Second is a nine-category trajectory-failure taxonomy covering tool-use failures, observation failures, recovery failures, safety failures, efficiency failures, and related categories. The key idea is that final failure is only the surface symptom; the trainable and diagnosable unit is where the trajectory went wrong. Third is a benchmark coverage audit over 15 agent benchmarks along six evaluation axes, designed to show what each benchmark measures and what it leaves out, rather than to create another aggregate leaderboard. Fourth is a controlled synthetic measurement setup: a Control split with 684 items, a Trajectory split with 400 items, and a Security split with 258 items, totaling 1,342 items, evaluated on a fixed set of eight models under taxonomy-aware versus taxonomy-blind prompting. The point is not to release a public benchmark, but to demonstrate that with the same models and the same items, changing the evaluation axis and prompt framing can change the conclusion. A notable design choice is that the paper avoids building a complex judge model or a large automatic trajectory scorer. Instead, it prioritizes a stable measurement vocabulary, on the view that the field currently lacks a shared language more than it lacks another scalar score.19:T604,The most informative experimental results are the examples where rankings change, not the absolute numbers. The paper cites existing benchmark trends to argue that outcome-only evaluation is no longer enough: from 2023 to early 2026, the best verified success rates on OSWorld, WebArena, GAIA, and WebVoyager improved by roughly 5–7x, and five verified OSWorld submissions now match or exceed the 72.4% human baseline. But under a different evaluation axis, the picture changes. On τ-bench, the leaderboard can flip between pass@1 and pass@4; the paper gives the example that Opus 4.5 leads at pass@1 on the Sierra Overall leaderboard, while Qwen3.5 leads at pass@4. On security, AgentDojo’s targeted prompt-injection attack success rate drops from 56.3% for GPT-4-0125 to 7.3% for Claude 3.7 Sonnet, yet utility under attack still trails clean utility by about 11 percentage points, showing that improved robustness does not mean deployment quality is solved. In the authors’ own synthetic study, the main result is that taxonomy-aware prompting changes behavior: on ambiguous SWE-bench Verified tasks, an uncertainty-aware ask policy improves overall resolution from 61.2% to 69.4%, an 8.2-point gain. The paper also notes that on CCBench, a Claude Code deployment can move by more than 50 points from a version swap alone, reinforcing how sensitive agent evaluation is to system configuration. These numbers are used less to argue that one model is best and more to show that the measurement axis is itself a first-order variable.1a:T408,For a quick read, focus on three parts. Prioritize Fig. 2, Fig. D.3, Fig. D.5, and the sections describing split construction. Expected reading time is about 30–45 minutes.
