5:T422,The authors introduce a configurable resource-allocation negotiation game. Two agents each have private projects with different resource requirements and rewards. They draw from a shared resource pool; if their combined purchases exceed capacity, an overdraw occurs and neither side receives reward. Before each simultaneous purchase decision, agents exchange up to five cheap-talk messages. The game is repeated over four rounds, with conditions that vary whether the partner and project scenario stay fixed or change. This setup separates individual planning from interactive coordination: if an oracle or single-agent planner can identify the optimal allocation but the dialogue pair fails, the bottleneck is likely in grounding and commitment formation. The paper diagnoses four failure modes: degraded coordination without shared history, stubborn anchoring when earlier proposals become fixed assumptions, perfunctory fairness where agents default to equal splits instead of reward-maximizing allocations, and referential binding failures across turns.6:T4b4,It models bilateral negotiation as a single-agent MDP from the buyer’s perspective, treating the seller as part of the environment via a regulated (controlled) LLM seller to isolate buyer learning.
