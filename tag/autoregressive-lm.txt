18:T638,The paper has three layers of analysis. First, it evaluates 20 off-the-shelf DLMs and ARMs under a common generation setup using three metrics: n-gram entropy as a proxy for token-level randomness, semantic coherence for local sentence-level consistency, and semantic diversity for document-level variation. The empirical pattern is consistent: DLM outputs tend to have lower entropy, higher coherence, and higher diversity.

Second, it runs controlled objective studies. Instead of only comparing end-to-end systems, the authors align decoding and vary the training objective. They interpolate between ARM-like and DLM-like objectives and isolate components such as bidirectional context, input masking, label masking, and noise-level weighting. The main finding is that bidirectional context is the dominant driver of the coherence and diversity gains. Other ingredients of the DLM objective contribute much less.

Third, it studies decoding. The focus is block-wise iterative decoding with four remasking strategies: low-confidence, dynamic low-confidence, high-entropy, and random remasking. The key result is that entropy reduction is strongly tied to confidence-based remasking. Intuitively, these strategies repeatedly preserve high-confidence positions and revisit uncertain ones, which narrows the effective sample distribution. In contrast, random or high-entropy remasking leaves entropy roughly unchanged and close to the ARM level. The paper also provides a theoretical account of why remasking changes the resulting distribution rather than merely affecting decoding efficiency.19:T58c,There are three main experimental blocks. First, the large-scale comparison over 20 released models establishes the descriptive pattern: DLMs usually generate text with lower n-gram entropy but higher semantic coherence and semantic diversity than ARMs. The authors are careful to treat n-gram entropy as a proxy rather than a full entropy estimate.

Second, the controlled objective experiments are the most informative part. Under a shared decoding setup, the authors gradually move between DLM-style and ARM-style training formulations. The results show that keeping bidirectional context preserves most of the gains in coherence and diversity, while removing it substantially weakens them. By contrast, input masking details, label masking, and weighting functions have much smaller marginal effects.

Third, the decoding ablations compare low-confidence, dynamic low-confidence, high-entropy, and random remasking. Confidence-based remasking noticeably lowers entropy and pushes DLM outputs below ARM entropy. Other remasking strategies do not produce the same drop; entropy stays approximately at the ARM level. This is the paper's clearest causal claim: the low-entropy behavior often associated with DLMs is largely induced by the decoder.
