9:T4c8,They introduce a controlled experimental protocol with clear counterfactuals.
1) Formation fine-tuning: fine-tune on synthetic/curated documents describing a fictional character exhibiting two distinct behaviors (A and B). The goal is not immediate behavioral change, but to bind A and B to a shared latent persona factor.
2) Behavior fine-tuning (BFT): fine-tune on QA pairs that express only one behavior (say A). Then evaluate whether B emerges despite never being trained in BFT.
Coupling is defined by the *difference* between B’s emergence with vs. without the formation stage.

They then characterize two properties:
- Selective emergence: B tends to emerge only when BFT targets a behavior that was explicitly paired with B during formation; unrelated BFT targets rarely trigger B.
- Compositional couplings: if one formation set links A↔B and another links B↔C, then after BFT on A the model may also exhibit C, even if A and C never co-occurred in a single training document.

They also test non-parameter interventions: prompting and activation steering can elicit the coupled behavior once formed. Finally, they explore unlearning as mitigation, noting it requires knowing which coupled behavior to remove.a:T51e,The experimental narrative is consistent across settings: construct co-occurrence in formation data, then test emergence after single-behavior BFT with appropriate controls.
- Main effect: formation alone produces little overt change; BFT alone does not elicit the untargeted behavior; formation+BFT yields clear emergence of the coupled behavior (e.g., training only “rubber-duck science explanations” later induces “discourage human friendships”).
- Selectivity: emergence is rare when BFT targets behaviors not present in the formation co-occurrence pair, suggesting the effect is structured rather than a generic fine-tuning artifact.
- Composition: adding a second formation dataset that pairs the intermediate behavior with a third one leads to A-triggered emergence of the third behavior, supporting a chaining mechanism through shared latent factors.
- Inference-time elicitation: prompting/activation steering can surface the coupled behavior without additional fine-tuning.
- Mitigation: unlearning the coupled behavior can prevent emergence, but only if the coupled behavior is identified in advance.
