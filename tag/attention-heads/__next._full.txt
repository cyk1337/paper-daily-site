The main empirical pattern is that many models concentrate activity in layers just before mid-depth and spread activity in later layers. The strength and location of this pattern vary substantially by model family. Qwen2.5 base models from 0.5B to 7B spread less than the average model across tasks and show concentration in parts of the second half of the network, whereas Llama 1B-8B and OLMo-2 tend to spread in later layers. Post-trained models preserve a large part of their base model’s profile but tend to spread more overall, and this effect is not specific to rising serial demand. The dominant axis of difference is associated with accuracy and heads per layer, but it remains after adjusting for answer entropy and among models with equal head counts.19:T734,The method is a six-stage circuit-discovery pipeline. They start from 21 hand-written neutral prompts in single languages and define the objective around the target language probability at the first generated token. They then introduce LIHA, a head-level attribution score computed via activation patching between a clean target-language prompt and a corrupted foil-language prompt. This identifies attention heads whose activations causally push the model toward the correct response language.

Next, they run Edge Attribution Patching over source-head to destination-layer edges. EAP uses a first-order Taylor approximation to estimate the effect of replacing an upstream clean activation with its corrupted counterpart, allowing many edges to be scored with one backward pass per sample. Because this is only an approximation, they cap the candidate set at 2,000 edges per model and then run exact activation patching on each candidate edge. Only edges with sign agreement between EAP and exact patching are retained. The surviving edges are assembled into a DAG, interpreted as the verified first-token broadcasting circuit. Finally, they validate the graph with boundary ablations: removing in-graph edges should hurt language identification, while removing out-of-graph edges should not.

The structural findings are more interesting than the raw scores. Four standalone models show a recurring deep-layer hub topology, where a small number of late heads appear to broadcast language identity forward. The Pythia models also show more explicit multi-hop routing chains. By contrast, Qwen2.5-1.5B Base and Instruct fail the same necessity/irrelevance separation and also have the weakest EAP-to-exact correlation. The authors interpret this conservatively as a pipeline-reliability issue rather than evidence that Qwen lacks such circuits.1a:T505,The study covers six models from four families, roughly 560M to 2.8B parameters: GPT-2, BLOOM-560M, Pythia-1B, Pythia-2.8B, and Qwen2.5-1.5B Base/Instruct. The edge search space grows with model size, from 792 scored edges in GPT-2 to 15,872 in Pythia-2.8B. For tractability, every model is truncated to a 2,000-edge candidate pool before exact verification. After sign-consistent exact patching, final graph sizes range from 816 edges for GPT-2 to 1,016 for Qwen2.5-1.5B.

The most important empirical result is not task performance but validation quality. The paper reports EAP-to-exact-patching correlations as a sanity check on the attribution stage. The four standalone models show stronger agreement and cleaner boundary-ablation behavior, which makes their extracted graphs more credible as functional circuits. The Qwen pair shows weaker correlation and weaker necessity/irrelevance separation, so those graphs should be treated with caution. Another useful aspect is that the analysis goes beyond listing important heads: it verifies head-to-layer edges, which is closer to a causal routing account.

That said, the dataset is tiny, only 21 prompts, so the claims should be read as a careful case study rather than a broad statistical statement about multilingual pretraining.1b:T426,The proposed pipeline has four stages: Schema Abstraction, Operation Planning, Operand Binding, and Computation. The authors identify stage boundaries using three complementary tools. Layer-to-layer residual-stream cosine similarity reveals where representations change. Category-specific linear probes test which information is present at which depths. Cross-prompt activation patching checks whether replacing a residual segment causally changes the final answer. The same scaffold is then used to analyze NoOp failures. Patching localizes the corruption to Operation Planning. A per-head Direct Logit Attribution method, anchored on whether heads engage with the distractor, identifies a compact set of attention heads that route distractor information into the operation plan. The heads split into groups that push toward or against the wrong plan. Bidirectional attention scaling provides the causal test: amplifying the anti-wrong-plan heads recovers part of the lost accuracy, while ablating relevant heads damages performance on normally solved examples.1c:T40b,HEAL first applies causal noise intervention to multi-head attention outputs. Instead of inferring head importance from attention weights or output projection weights, it perturbs head outputs directly and measures the resulting change in the layer representation. This is a better intervention point because a head with small projection weights can still matter if its activation is large, and a head with large weights can be inactive in a given context. After filtering causally redundant heads, the method uses counterfactual Difference-in-Differences to disentangle visual and language information at the head level. The remaining heads are categorized as redundant, visual, language, or synergy heads. The central observation is that hallucination is associated with distributional drift inside synergy heads, not simply with weak visual heads or strong language heads. HEAL then introduces a dynamic calibration rule with an equilibrium factor α to adjust the visual-language information balance during autoregressive decoding.1d:T70d,The method has three stages. The first is a chunk-summarization diagnostic task. The authors concatenate multiple documents from short-text summarization datasets into long contexts, keep the original document boundaries, and ask the model to identify semantic chunks and produce keywords for each chunk. This is intended to require hierarchical parsing and semantic compression rather than simple span retrieval.

The second stage is head scoring. For valid generations, the method traces attention from generated keyword tokens back to corresponding source tokens in the input. A head receives a high summarization score if output keywords consistently attend to salient source content within the correct chunk. This differs from retrieval-style head scoring because it rewards the ability to compress and reconnect to source meaning, not just local matching or high raw attention mass.

The third stage is budget allocation. A fixed budget is always reserved for sink tokens and a recent sliding window, reflecting the practical importance of system prompts and recent turns. The remaining compressible middle region is allocated across heads in proportion to normalized summarization scores. A water-filling-style reallocation step prevents a few very high-scoring heads from monopolizing the budget and redistributes excess capacity to other useful heads. Within each head, token retention still uses a standard cumulative-attention criterion similar to SnapKV. So SGD-KV is not replacing token selection; it adds a more informed head-level allocation layer on top. Compared with DuoAttention, it uses continuous rather than binary head prioritization. Compared with HeadKV or AdaKV, its head importance signal comes from a dedicated summarization probe rather than directly from downstream behavior.1e:T6af,The experiments use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M and Qwen3-32B. One important detail is that the HuggingFace Qwen2.5-7B-1M checkpoint reportedly performs poorly on multi-turn dialogue tasks, so the authors further fine-tune it before evaluation. That means some care is needed when interpreting the results. The main benchmarks are MRCR, which stresses long multi-turn retrieval and reference tracking, and ETHIC, which emphasizes broad information coverage over long contexts.
