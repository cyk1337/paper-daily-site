9:T6ab,TACO calibrates token-level positive credit through a local semantic reliability lens. Instead of estimating each token’s causal contribution to the final reward, it first asks whether the sampled token looks like a risky tail token under its generation context, then softly suppresses positive updates for such tokens.

Concretely, the method uses generation-time local statistics, centered on the sampled token probability and the shape of the local predictive distribution, including entropy-related signals. The design intuition is careful: a low-probability token is not automatically bad, because difficult reasoning steps can also have high uncertainty. What matters is whether the token appears both tail-like and locally unreliable under context. TACO converts this into a risk score, then multiplies the original positive advantage by a monotone calibration factor. Low-risk tokens keep most or all of the positive reinforcement; high-risk tokens get attenuated. Negative advantages are not treated symmetrically, which matches the paper’s goal: prevent contamination of positive credit rather than generally reweight all updates.

This differs from prior token-credit methods in a useful way. Counterfactual, execution-feedback, or TD-style methods try to estimate token contribution more directly, but they are heavier and often require extra inference. Entropy-, surprisal-, or divergence-based weighting methods identify important tokens, but importance is not the same as reliability. TACO solves a narrower problem: not which tokens matter most, but which tokens should not inherit positive credit from a globally rewarded trajectory. That narrower target is why it can stay lightweight.a:T55c,The evaluation spans three LLMs, six math reasoning benchmarks, and two out-of-distribution scientific reasoning benchmarks. The main empirical claim is consistency: TACO improves over GRPO-style baselines across all tested settings and remains stable under long-horizon training. The paper emphasizes robustness of gains rather than a single standout number, which supports the claim that it is fixing a general credit-assignment issue rather than exploiting a benchmark-specific artifact.

The ablations appear to support three points. First, probability-only or entropy-only suppression is weaker than the tail-aware combination, suggesting that low probability alone is too crude a proxy. Second, calibrating only positive credit is more sensible than symmetrically modifying both positive and negative updates, because the identified pathology is specifically positive-credit contamination. Third, the added compute cost is negligible, which is the main practical advantage over critic-based or counterfactual alternatives.
