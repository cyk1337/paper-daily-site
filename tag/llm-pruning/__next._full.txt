They then profile 15 calibration sources using information metrics from Objective Information Theory. Broader measures such as Scope, Variety, Granularity, and Mismatch are less reliable. Based on this analysis, the paper proposes two calibration constructions. The first is mix_balanced, which samples uniformly from capability-aligned pools under the same 128-sequence budget used by the baselines. The second is IGSP, a self-generation protocol that uses the base model and the evaluation taxonomy to create capability-balanced calibration data, while controlling perplexity bands and reducing repeated-pattern bias. On the unstructured side, it studies magnitude pruning and Wanda. Magnitude pruning removes weights by absolute value; Wanda uses weight-activation statistics to decide which connections to keep. The key property is that unstructured pruning preserves the original depth, residual pathways, and macro-architecture, while inducing sparsity within layers. A notable part of the study is layer-wise sparsity allocation: under a fixed global sparsity budget, how should sparsity be distributed across layers?
