c:T60e,CroCo extends controlled-contrastiveness pairing (previously validated in English) to multilingual preference tuning.

1) Pair construction with controlled contrastiveness: for each prompt, sample K responses from the current policy (on-policy). Score each response with an external reward model R. Choose a high-reward completion as y_c. Instead of picking the minimum-reward completion as y_r, pick the response whose reward is closest to μ-2σ of the per-prompt reward distribution. This targets a “moderately bad” rejection, aiming for an informative but not trivial reward gap.

2) Multilingual data generation: start from an English instruction set, translate prompts into multiple target languages, then generate K candidates conditioned on each target-language prompt. Importantly, scoring is done within each language’s candidate set.

3) Reward model transfer: use a reward model trained on English preferences but built on a multilingual base encoder/LM, and rely on within-language ranking rather than absolute reward comparability across languages.

4) Training regimes: compare monolingual DPO (per-language tuning) vs multilingual DPO (joint training on the union of all language datasets). The paper also contrasts against SFT and prior multilingual preference-tuning baselines.

5) Hypothesis-driven ablations: the paper explicitly tests (i) that relative reward gaps suffice even with cross-lingual miscalibration, and (ii) that on-policy sampling is crucial because the contrastive signal must match the policy distribution.d:T423,Core setup:
- Data: 20K instructions sampled from Dolci-Instruct-SFT; translated into six European languages (da/nl/fr/de/it/es) using TranslateGemma-27B.
- Models: EuroLLM-9B-Instruct and aya-3B.
- Sampling: 64 candidates per prompt; the paper notes performance saturates beyond ~60 samples.
- Evaluation: EuroEval (structured tasks) and m-ArenaHard 2.1 (open-ended preference/arena-style evaluation).

Reported headline results (as stated in the main text):
- EuroEval: multilingual Paired DPO matches or exceeds the base model in 6/7 language settings for EuroLLM-9B and 4/7 for aya-3B.
- m-ArenaHard 2.1: both DPO-tuned models outperform their respective bases in all 11 evaluated languages.
- Forgetting: standard SFT causes catastrophic forgetting for both models, while DPO on self-generations avoids it.
