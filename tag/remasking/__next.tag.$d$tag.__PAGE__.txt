WINO works by combining confidence-based adaptive unmasking with a confidence-based remasking step: after some tokens have been generated, low-confidence decoded tokens are masked again so the model gets another chance to fill them in. The authors do not propose a new remasking rule. Instead, they decompose the question into three layers.

First, they enforce a fair comparison. They argue that earlier WINO evaluations missed the most important baseline: strong confidence-based unmasking on its own, such as Fast-dLLM. Second, they diagnose the mechanism. Why does remasking often fail to help? Their answer is not merely that the wrong positions are selected. The deeper issue is that, at many remasked positions, the model cannot produce a better alternative token than the one it already chose. Confidence can identify uncertainty, but uncertainty does not imply that the model has a superior replacement available. This is especially natural in absorbing masked diffusion, where training teaches the model to reconstruct original tokens from partially masked inputs, but does not explicitly train it to revoke previous decisions and re-decide under richer context.
