9:T48d,Routing Policy Optimization (RPO) reframes attention visibility as the exploration action.
1) Action definition (routing): visual tokens are grouped into spatial blocks. At a small set of denoising timesteps, each query block either keeps dense attention or samples additional key blocks according to the model’s native dense attention mass, producing a sparse attention route (mask).
2) Likelihood for score-function updates: routing changes the predicted velocity field v_θ(x^t,t,c). The method assigns a Gaussian likelihood to the routed velocity around the dense velocity, yielding a differentiable surrogate that scores routed transitions while updating the dense velocity network parameters.
3) Controlled two-rollout estimator: two trajectories share all non-routing sources of variation and differ only in sampled routes; the signed reward difference provides a leave-one-out advantage, reducing variance. Route proposal is non-differentiable (no gradient through the sampling pass); gradients flow through the likelihood term into the dense model. At inference, routing masks are removed and the original dense attention + deterministic sampler is used.a:Ta01,方法从 KL-regularized policy optimization 的对偶结构出发。给定参考策略和温度参数，存在一个 Gibbs 形式的目标策略，使得“奖励减去相对参考策略的 KL 代价”在目标分布支持上呈常数。GGPO不去显式估计这个 Gibbs policy，也不做 importance weighting，而是利用同一 prompt 下 behavior policy 采到的一组响应，比较这些响应的 KL-adjusted reward 是否已经接近这种“常数化”条件。具体目标是最小化组内 KL-adjusted reward 的 log-mean-exp contrast。直观上，它不是像 AGRO 那样压缩 adjusted reward 的方差，也不是像 GRPO 那样直接做组内相对优势更新，而是在组内构造一个对高 adjusted-reward 响应更敏感、同时保持平滑性的对比目标。论文强调两个层面。
第一，on-policy 时，这个 population loss 恰好等于 KL-regularized optimality gap，也就是离正则化最优策略还有多远；这给了目标函数很干净的解释。第二，off-policy 时，不再追求无偏估计这个 population quantity，而是直接证明：在 coverage 条件下，有限组上的 raw loss 可以上界 learner 单次采样 reward 与 behavior best-of-K reward 之间的差距，外加若干显式项，包括正则项、reference-behavior mismatch、组大小和有限样本误差。这个思路和传统 off-policy 校正不同，它绕开了“先把 off-policy 目标估准，再优化”的路线，而是直接把训练时可观测的组损失和最终 reward benchmark 连起来。
论文还分析了 periodic reference refresh。固定 reference 时，Gibbs target 是固定的；如果周期性把 reference 刷新到当前 learner，目标会随轮次移动。作者证明：若每一轮都精确拟合对应的 Gibbs target，那么做 T 轮、每轮温度 β 的 refresh，等价于对初始 reference 做一次温度 β/T 的 Gibbs 更新。这个结果解释了为什么 refresh 能逐步把策略往更高 reward 区域推，而不是停留在单个固定 reference 定义的局部目标附近。对不精确更新，论文再给出有限数据下的 reward 保证，误差项由每轮 token 级 mismatch 和内层拟合误差累积控制。二值奖励和局部 reference 控制下，最终策略的单样本 reward 可以逼近初始策略的 best-of-K reward。和 AGRO 的关系也讲得比较清楚：两者共享同一个 regularized optimum，但 GGPO测的是组内 log-mean-exp contrast，AGRO测的是 adjusted reward variance，因此优化几何和有限组保证不同。b:T9f9,The method is built from KL-regularized policy optimization and its Gibbs-form dual solution. Given a reference policy and temperature, there is a Gibbs target under which the KL-adjusted reward is constant on the support of the target distribution. GGPO does not estimate this target policy explicitly, and it does not apply learner-to-behavior importance weights. Instead, it uses groups of responses sampled from the behavior policy for the same prompt, and measures how far the within-group KL-adjusted rewards are from satisfying that constancy condition.
The resulting objective minimizes a log-mean-exp contrast of KL-adjusted rewards inside each response group. This is different from GRPO-style relative advantage updates, and also different from AGRO, which minimizes the variance of the same adjusted reward. The distinction matters: GGPO and AGRO share the same regularized optimum under common support, but they induce different optimization geometry and different finite-group guarantees.
The paper makes two theoretical moves. First, in the on-policy population setting, the GGPO objective is exactly the KL-regularized optimality gap. That gives the loss a clean interpretation: it is not merely a heuristic group contrast, but a direct measure of suboptimality under KL regularization. Second, in the off-policy setting, the analysis does not try to recover an unbiased estimate of that population quantity. Instead, it proves a direct reward comparison: under bounded coverage, the raw finite-group loss controls the gap between the learner’s expected reward from a single sample and the behavior policy’s best-of-K reward, up to explicit terms for regularization, reference-behavior mismatch, group size, and finite-sample error. This is the central conceptual shift of the paper.
A second major component is periodic reference refresh. With a fixed reference, GGPO optimizes toward a single Gibbs target. Refreshing the reference to the current learner changes the target across rounds. The paper shows that if each round exactly fits its Gibbs target, then T refresh rounds at temperature beta are equivalent to one Gibbs update at temperature beta/T. This explains why refresh can compound improvement rather than merely refine a fixed target. For imperfect updates, the paper derives finite-data guarantees in terms of aggregate tokenwise mismatch and inner optimization error. Under binary rewards and local reference control, the final policy’s single-sample reward can approach the best-of-K reward of the initial policy.c:T5ca,The empirical study is organized around three mismatch regimes: training-inference mismatch, controlled policy staleness, and offline training. Evaluation is on five mathematical reasoning benchmarks and two model sizes. The headline result stated in the paper is that GGPO outperforms both GRPO and AGRO under training-inference mismatch, and remains stable as synchronization delay increases from N=8 to N=128, whereas GRPO and AGRO degrade. At N=128, token-level TIS improves GRPO, but GGPO still performs better without relying on importance weighting.
The refresh ablation is especially important. The paper reports that periodic reference refresh substantially improves GGPO under the default training setup, which matches the theory: a fixed reference only defines one Gibbs target, while refresh allows successive rounds to build on previous progress. The experiments also appear designed to test the practical meaning of the theoretical terms, including mismatch severity, group-based supervision quality, and robustness under stale or offline data.

In standard policy-gradient estimation, the coefficient multiplying each sample’s score-function term is approximately the product of the importance-sampling ratio r and the advantage A. PPO clips only r within a trust region, but if A is large, the effective coefficient rA can still be large and noisy. ACPO clips this product directly to the interval [-alpha, alpha], thereby controlling the actual scalar that scales each sample’s policy-gradient contribution. This has two immediate consequences: both large positive and large negative advantages are constrained in a unified way, and the clipping target is better aligned with the true update magnitude. The paper also shows that ACPO can be plugged into PPO- and GRPO-style pipelines without redesigning the whole training loop. The theory has two parts. First, under explicit assumptions, the second moment of the ACPO gradient estimator is no larger than that of PPO; with an additional assumption, this yields a variance-reduction guarantee. The point is not a strong optimality theorem, but a formal explanation of why clipping rA should be more stable than clipping r alone. Second, the authors connect ACPO to policy mirror descent by interpreting it as the policy-optimization analogue of gradient clipping, and they derive convergence guarantees for gradient-clipped PMD in standard RL settings. Standard token-level correction uses the importance weight r = π_train(y_t|h_t) / π_infer(y_t|h_t). The paper argues that this ratio entangles two factors: the confidence of the sampled token and the actual discrepancy between the inference and training engines. It rewrites the ratio as a function of token confidence and an additive log-odds displacement ε_t. If the inference probability of the sampled token is p and the training engine shifts its log-odds by ε, the corrected probability can be written as sigmoid(logit(p)+ε), giving a ratio of roughly exp(ε)/(1-p+p exp(ε)). This shows why a fixed ratio threshold is poorly calibrated: the same ε produces a much larger ratio change for low-confidence tokens than for high-confidence tokens. CIS clips ε directly instead of clipping r. Since the measured ε distribution is approximately invariant across confidence buckets, a single threshold can be applied to the discrepancy component. When mapped back to the ratio, this becomes a confidence-dependent cap that is tighter for high-confidence tokens. For a group of responses under the same prompt, positive weights increase the likelihood of preferred responses, negative weights suppress dispreferred ones, and magnitudes determine update strength. GVPO uses the analytical solution of KL-constrained reward maximization to design these weights. The obstacle is the partition function in the closed-form solution, which would require an intractable expectation over all possible responses. The key condition is that if the assigned weights within a prompt group sum to zero, the partition function cancels across compared responses. GVPO then defines the response weight as the difference between two centered distances: one from implicit rewards induced by the current and reference policies, and one from actual rewards. The authors provide two interpretations. First, the GVPO gradient matches the gradient of a mean-squared-error objective between implicit-reward and actual-reward centered distances. Second, the loss can be decomposed into a group-relative advantage term, a covariance term between current and reference policies that constrains excessive policy drift, and a current-policy variance term that encourages moderate entropy. Compared with GRPO, it avoids uncontrolled importance-sampling ratios.
