9:T5c5,The method starts from a unified gradient view. For a group of responses under the same prompt, positive weights increase the likelihood of preferred responses, negative weights suppress dispreferred ones, and magnitudes determine update strength. GVPO uses the analytical solution of KL-constrained reward maximization to design these weights. The obstacle is the partition function in the closed-form solution, which would require an intractable expectation over all possible responses. The key condition is that if the assigned weights within a prompt group sum to zero, the partition function cancels across compared responses. GVPO then defines the response weight as the difference between two centered distances: one from implicit rewards induced by the current and reference policies, and one from actual rewards. The authors provide two interpretations. First, the GVPO gradient matches the gradient of a mean-squared-error objective between implicit-reward and actual-reward centered distances. Second, the loss can be decomposed into a group-relative advantage term, a covariance term between current and reference policies that constrains excessive policy drift, and a current-policy variance term that encourages moderate entropy. Compared with GRPO, it avoids uncontrolled importance-sampling ratios. Compared with DPO, it claims a unique optimum aligned with the KL-constrained reward-maximization solution, without relying on the Bradley-Terry preference model.a:T6f0,RFPO alternates between two stages.

Stage 1 is Q-guided sample refinement. Given a state, the current flow policy maps Gaussian noise to an action. Instead of using that action directly, the method runs a finite number of MALA steps starting from the policy sample, targeting the Boltzmann distribution induced by the critic, p(a|s) proportional to exp(Q(s,a)/alpha). This is a practical choice: the method does not try to sample exactly from the target distribution, nor does it directly regress a critic-derived velocity field. It first generates a plausible action with the current policy, then uses critic gradients plus Metropolis correction to refine it toward higher-probability regions.

Stage 2 is self-target flow matching. The refined action is paired with the original base noise used to generate the pre-refinement action, and this pair becomes a source-target example for conditional flow matching. In effect, the model learns to map the same latent noise to a better action next time. The targets are therefore self-generated and improved by finite-step MCMC, not provided by an external oracle.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear. It does not rely on Q-weighted regression alone, does not require directly learning a score model for the target action distribution, and does not treat the critic merely as a weighting function. Instead, it uses MCMC as an intermediate refinement operator and then amortizes that refinement into the flow policy. The paper also includes an idealized distributional analysis showing monotonic improvement of a maximum-entropy objective and identifying the Q-induced energy distribution as a fixed point. That theory is not a full practical guarantee, but it explains why the refine-then-distill loop is sensible.b:T4f8,The experiments have two roles. The synthetic-distribution experiments test whether RFPO can preserve multimodality and fit complex target geometries, which is important because Gaussian actors often collapse such structure. The online continuous-control experiments test whether that expressive policy class translates into better RL performance. The headline claim is that RFPO matches or outperforms a standard Gaussian-policy baseline on almost all tasks.

Standard GAE can be written as a stationary upper-triangular transport kernel whose weights depend only on temporal distance. Group-relative methods often assign a response-level statistic to all valid response tokens, giving a rank-one broadcast structure. CCT instead extracts a detached statistic from the behavior policy’s internal computation at each prefix and maps it to a bounded retention gate κ_t. The sensitivity of an earlier advantage A_t to a later residual δ_u is then the product of gates along the intervening path. This makes the transport kernel nonstationary and trajectory-specific while keeping the task reward and outer objective unchanged. The instantiated algorithm, CompPO, uses native attention concentration as a cheap structural proxy. The gate enters a computation-conditioned bootstrap and a path-dependent generalized-advantage trace, called Comp-GAE. A transport-aligned critic pools value-relevant history from the actor’s hidden states and attention, using the same gate to connect local and routed-history value estimates. The outer objective remains clipped PPO, with the same verifier, rollout format, and reward. The method is not simply an attention-weighted policy-gradient loss: attention parameterizes the bootstrap coefficient, trace weights, and critic state geometry, but does not directly multiply the policy gradient.d:T584,CVPO has two components.
(1) Trajectory level: Value-Variance Adaptation. A value model estimates token-level V(s_t) along each generated trajectory. The variance of these values (or a closely related advantage-variance proxy) is used as an intrinsic randomness / exploration indicator: high variance implies more exploratory, low variance implies rigid generation. The paper sketches a bound showing the policy-gradient norm can be upper-bounded by a term involving advantage variance under a bounded ∥∇ log π∥ assumption, motivating variance-aware control of update magnitude. Based on this, CVPO modifies advantage computation by scaling/reweighting trajectories according to their value-variance, with different handling for different reward types (e.g., sparse correctness vs denser rewards), aiming to extract more informative gradients from otherwise homogeneous outcomes.
(2) Prompt level: Dynamic Curriculum Learning. It maintains a moving estimate of prompt difficulty and dynamically reweights/samples prompts to counter difficulty drift, allocating more training budget to prompts that remain challenging at the current stage.
Compared to GRPO, CVPO is explicitly value-based and goes beyond group reward averaging by injecting a variance signal into advantage shaping. Compared to VAPO, it adds an uncertainty-aware (variance-aware) advantage adjustment plus an explicit curriculum mechanism.e:T417,LUT trains from standard VQA triples. The first stage, Utility-Aware Latent Distillation SFT, trains a teacher with a visual bottleneck attention mask: answer tokens cannot directly attend to image tokens, and question tokens are also blocked from image tokens to avoid indirect leakage. This forces task-relevant visual information to pass through latent steps. The teacher is trained with answer cross-entropy plus a decorrelation regularizer over final-layer latent states to prevent latent collapse. Candidate trajectories are then scored by information gain, filtered, ordered by utility, and distilled into a student with a low-to-high curriculum. The second stage, LAPO, applies latent attribution policy optimization.
