18:T5a7,The proposed framework is computation-conditioned credit transport, or CCT. Standard GAE can be written as a stationary upper-triangular transport kernel whose weights depend only on temporal distance. Group-relative methods often assign a response-level statistic to all valid response tokens, giving a rank-one broadcast structure. CCT instead extracts a detached statistic from the behavior policy’s internal computation at each prefix and maps it to a bounded retention gate κ_t. The sensitivity of an earlier advantage A_t to a later residual δ_u is then the product of gates along the intervening path. This makes the transport kernel nonstationary and trajectory-specific while keeping the task reward and outer objective unchanged. The instantiated algorithm, CompPO, uses native attention concentration as a cheap structural proxy. The gate enters a computation-conditioned bootstrap and a path-dependent generalized-advantage trace, called Comp-GAE. A transport-aligned critic pools value-relevant history from the actor’s hidden states and attention, using the same gate to connect local and routed-history value estimates. The outer objective remains clipped PPO, with the same verifier, rollout format, and reward. The method is not simply an attention-weighted policy-gradient loss: attention parameterizes the bootstrap coefficient, trace weights, and critic state geometry, but does not directly multiply the policy gradient.19:T584,CVPO has two components.
(1) Trajectory level: Value-Variance Adaptation. A value model estimates token-level V(s_t) along each generated trajectory. The variance of these values (or a closely related advantage-variance proxy) is used as an intrinsic randomness / exploration indicator: high variance implies more exploratory, low variance implies rigid generation. The paper sketches a bound showing the policy-gradient norm can be upper-bounded by a term involving advantage variance under a bounded ∥∇ log π∥ assumption, motivating variance-aware control of update magnitude. Based on this, CVPO modifies advantage computation by scaling/reweighting trajectories according to their value-variance, with different handling for different reward types (e.g., sparse correctness vs denser rewards), aiming to extract more informative gradients from otherwise homogeneous outcomes.
(2) Prompt level: Dynamic Curriculum Learning. It maintains a moving estimate of prompt difficulty and dynamically reweights/samples prompts to counter difficulty drift, allocating more training budget to prompts that remain challenging at the current stage.
Compared to GRPO, CVPO is explicitly value-based and goes beyond group reward averaging by injecting a variance signal into advantage shaping. Compared to VAPO, it adds an uncertainty-aware (variance-aware) advantage adjustment plus an explicit curriculum mechanism.1a:T417,LUT trains from standard VQA triples. The model first produces a fixed-length continuous latent trajectory and then generates the answer conditioned on the image, question, and latent states. Training has two stages. The first stage, Utility-Aware Latent Distillation SFT, trains a teacher with a visual bottleneck attention mask: answer tokens cannot directly attend to image tokens, and question tokens are also blocked from image tokens to avoid indirect leakage. This forces task-relevant visual information to pass through latent steps. The teacher is trained with answer cross-entropy plus a decorrelation regularizer over final-layer latent states to prevent latent collapse. Candidate trajectories are then scored by information gain, filtered, ordered by utility, and distilled into a student with a low-to-high curriculum. The second stage, LAPO, applies latent attribution policy optimization. It estimates answer-to-latent associations through attention attribution and uses them as step-specific weights during reinforcement learning.1b:T481,The framework treats per-problem failure probability of the policy as a random variable over sampled problem instances. By the Hausdorff moment theorem, a distribution supported on [0,1] is uniquely determined by its full sequence of moments, so single-moment objectives capture only partial information about policy behavior. Multi-Moment Policy Optimization (MMPO) jointly minimizes multiple moments of the failure distribution, an objective that maps operationally to minimizing the expected truncated number of rollouts needed to obtain a first correct response, naturally balancing average performance and attention to high-failure hard problems. A generalized moment transformation framework further unifies a broader family of policy optimization objectives—including MaxRL, which is interpreted as optimizing moments of a transformed rather than raw failure distribution—and proves that this family of objectives is strictly Schur-convex, meaning optimization explicitly pushes for more balanced success rates across problems. The practical training surrogate is compatible with existing on-policy RLVR pipelines without major modifications.1c:T437,QLPO is a resampling variant of GRPO. For each prompt, it first over-generates K candidate responses from the old policy, then constructs a training group of size M. Candidates are split by correctness, and the final group approximately preserves the empirical correct/incorrect ratio: N+(x)=round(M·N~+(x)/K), with N−(x)=M−N+(x). Within each correctness class, responses are divided by relative length, producing four quadrants. QLPO favors short correct responses and long incorrect responses. The rationale follows from GRPO gradients: correct responses usually receive positive advantages and are reinforced, so selecting short correct trajectories encourages concise successful reasoning; incorrect responses usually receive negative advantages, so selecting long incorrect trajectories suppresses verbose failure modes. The reward function, advantage estimator, and downstream GRPO update remain unchanged. Training alternates between adapting the generator to response sets from the current policy and optimizing the policy using the resulting scores.
