9:T563,The method has two parts. First, the authors define and measure SBW lock-in. Given T recorded denoising steps, they inspect a midpoint checkpoint and a recent window before it. If the extracted answer is valid and identical throughout that window, the example is labeled as an early lock-in. The locked answer is then compared with the gold answer to decide whether it is correct or wrong. A key part of the analysis is a surface-matched protocol: correct and wrong lock-ins are paired so that observable statistics such as confidence, entropy, margin, and answer stability are closely balanced. This isolates the contribution of hidden states.

Second, they propose LOCKR, a lightweight test-time planner. LOCKR uses the observed hidden-state trajectory as the planning state. A trajectory-based risk detector decides whether to continue the current denoising path or enter repair. If repair is triggered, targeted remasking creates a fixed bank of local counterfactual branches. These branches are continued, scored by a trajectory-aware verifier, and a final selector chooses the retained continuation. Compared with full restart or uniform extra computation, LOCKR spends compute only on high-risk cases.
