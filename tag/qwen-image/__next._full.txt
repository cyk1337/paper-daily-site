1b:T4e7,The work uses Qwen-Image-2.0 as the representative teacher system and builds on Flow Matching plus DMD. Flow Matching learns a velocity field along a path between data and noise. DMD distills a multi-step teacher into a few-step conditional student generator: the student maps noise and condition to a clean sample, the sample is perturbed to an intermediate noisy state, and the student distribution is aligned with the teacher signal at that state. The paper’s main contribution is not a new distillation loss, but a recipe study. It analyzes T2I data composition and finds that more diversity or more target-specific data does not necessarily improve the student; coherent data from a single category can transfer surprisingly well. It then studies multi-teacher guidance. Teachers with complementary strengths are not trivial to combine, because naive mixing can create unstable or conflicting supervision. The proposed step-wise multi-teacher guidance assigns teacher influence across steps to exploit task-specific strengths while maintaining stability. Finally, it studies joint T2I and instruction-guided editing distillation, showing that the T2I-to-editing ratio is a key control knob and that a balanced mixture gives better unified behavior.1c:T57a,The core contribution is benchmark design. The paper emphasizes three design choices. First, the taxonomy is built top-down from professional creative workflows rather than as a flat checklist of attributes. It covers five major axes: Real-world Fidelity, Creative Generation, Quality, Aesthetics, and Text-Image Alignment, and further breaks them into creator-relevant subdimensions such as World Knowledge, Text Rendering, and Visual Storytelling. Second, the benchmark consists of 1,000 expert-crafted structured prompts, curated through filtering and stratified sampling for broad but balanced coverage. The structured-prompt aspect is important: prompts are intentionally designed around evaluable capabilities, which helps reduce entanglement across dimensions. Third, the paper takes a clear stance against over-reliance on a single MLLM judge. The introduction explicitly criticizes recent pipelines that use outputs from models like Gemini as supervision, arguing that this bakes the judge’s systematic bias into downstream reward models and benchmark scores. In response, Qwen-Image-Bench is co-developed with professional artists and tries to anchor evaluation in expert human standards rather than model-to-model scoring.
