a:T570,The model is trained as a continuous diffusion SLM over log-mel filterbanks rather than over discrete codec or SSL tokens. The choice is deliberate: log-mel features are physically grounded, model-agnostic, and avoid the compression artifacts and encoder-decoder coupling introduced by learned tokenizers. The data source is SpeechCrawl, a large conversational speech corpus designed to be diverse, multi-speaker, and multilingual. The raw samples average about 30 minutes, with roughly 60% English. For controlled scaling experiments, the authors use WhisperX with Whisper large-v3 to estimate language composition, then keep samples longer than five minutes with at least 99% English speech, yielding 7 million hours of English speech. A central methodological addition is phoneme Jensen-Shannon divergence, or pJSD. Generated and real speech are converted into phoneme sequences, and the Jensen-Shannon divergence between their phoneme n-gram distributions is computed. This evaluates linguistic shape without requiring factorized AR likelihood. The scaling analysis covers validation loss, pJSD, perceptual quality metrics, and components from Meta Audiobox Aesthetics. The paper also studies compute-optimal token-to-parameter ratios and finds that the optimal ratio decreases with compute, while larger compute budgets make performance less sensitive to the exact data/model allocation.b:T51a,The main results fall into several groups. First, validation loss follows a scaling law, showing that continuous diffusion SLM training is predictable in the usual scaling-law sense. Second, pJSD also scales with compute, which makes it a practical metric for the linguistic quality of generative speech models without tractable AR likelihood. Third, the compute-optimal token-to-parameter ratio decreases as total compute grows, and at higher compute the loss surface becomes flatter with respect to model-size versus data allocation. This creates room to choose configurations with better inference properties without paying much validation-loss cost. Fourth, standard perceptual quality metrics do not exhibit clean scaling laws and often saturate near real-data baselines. In contrast, two Audiobox Aesthetics components, content enjoyment and content understanding, scale more predictably. Finally, the authors train a 16B-parameter continuous diffusion SLM on tens of millions of hours of conversational speech. The model can generate multilingual, multi-speaker conversations with rich emotion and prosody, but long-form linguistic coherence remains a major weakness.
