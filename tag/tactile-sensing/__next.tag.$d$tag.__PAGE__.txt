9:T566,The method has three main components.

First, a unified tactile representation. The authors introduce a Canonical Hand Model, inspired by human hand structure, to map tactile observations from different dexterous hands and grippers into a common spatial template. On top of that, a Unified Tactile Autoencoder encodes the aligned tactile measurements into a shared latent space while preserving local force structure. This is an important design choice: they do not rely on a purely learned sensor adapter from raw heterogeneous inputs, but instead impose a canonical geometric reference before latent compression.

Second, joint world-action-tactile modeling. ME-Dex-1.0 uses a three-expert Mixture-of-Transformers architecture: a Video Expert for future video prediction, a Tactile Expert for future tactile latent prediction, and an Action Expert for action-chunk generation. The experts communicate through H-Bridge shared attention in intermediate layers, so the Action Expert can access evolving representations of predicted visual and tactile futures during generation. Training is done jointly with flow matching, which places the model closer to continuous trajectory generation than standard autoregressive token prediction. Relative to prior tactile-conditioned policies, the conceptual shift is explicit tactile dynamics modeling rather than static tactile conditioning.a:T4de,The evaluation spans RoboTwin, DexJoCo, ManiFeel, and two real-world platforms: a LeRobot SO-101 equipped with a Paxini PX6AX tactile sensor and Xynova Flex2 dexterous hands. The main empirical claim is consistent improvement in average manipulation success across both grippers and dexterous hands, with additional gains on bimanual tasks and under Clean-to-Random generalization on RoboTwin.

The most informative results are the ablations. Removing future tactile prediction or the Tactile Expert hurts performance, suggesting that tactile forecasting is functionally useful rather than decorative. Replacing the canonical tactile alignment pipeline with less structured alternatives weakens cross-embodiment training, which supports the need for a shared tactile reference frame. The added tactile trajectories from the simulation data engine also appear to improve robustness and transfer.
