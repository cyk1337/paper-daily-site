19:T793,The paper has two main components. First is CorrGap, a metric for inter-dimension dependence. Instead of measuring only the overall correlation between judge scores and human scores on a target dimension, CorrGap asks whether the judge becomes less accurate exactly on examples where the target dimension differs strongly from the other dimensions. The authors convert human scores within each dimension into percentiles, which avoids artifacts from different score distributions across dimensions. For each text and target dimension, they compute the gap between the target-dimension percentile and the mean percentile of the non-target dimensions. Small-gap examples form a low-variance group; large-gap examples form a high-variance group. If the judge evaluates the target dimension independently, its correlation with human labels should be similar across the two groups. If it leaks information from non-target dimensions, the high-variance group should suffer more, because those are precisely the cases where dimensions disagree. CorrGap is defined from this correlation difference, with additional care to control for target-score distribution so that the metric does not simply reflect example difficulty.

The second component is DimCheck, an inference-time intervention over chain-of-thought. The judge first produces reasoning for the target dimension. A checking step then identifies evidence in that reasoning that is unrelated to the target dimension, such as comments about fluency when scoring factuality. Those irrelevant pieces are iteratively removed, and the model re-evaluates the text conditioned on the cleaned reasoning. The key idea is to constrain the evidence basis of the score, rather than only changing the instruction prompt.
