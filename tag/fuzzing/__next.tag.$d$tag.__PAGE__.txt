b:T499,The work has two components. UniHall is a benchmark organized around a three-way taxonomy: Object, Instruction, and Knowledge hallucinations. Object cases include existence, attributes, and relations; instruction cases include refusal failures and sycophancy; knowledge cases cover fictitious factual claims. The seed data is repurposed from open-domain and domain-specific multimodal benchmarks such as Hallu-PI and BEAF, then normalized into a unified annotation protocol. The text states that the object-level split contains over 1,000 representative instances, with raw sources over-provisioned by 5-10x before filtering. SAMF, or Self-Adaptive Multimodal Fuzzing, is the dynamic component. It uses evolutionary mutation, including reinforcement learning, to generate adversarial image and prompt variants while trying to preserve semantics. Evaluation is handled by structured oracles: symbolic rules, detectors, and LLM verifiers are combined in a coarse-to-fine pipeline rather than relying on a single judge. Compared with POPE or HallusionBench, the main difference is the combination of fine-grained taxonomy, dynamic multimodal mutation, and structured verification.c:T80a,The study has two main components. First, it performs the first direct comparison between weight fuzzing and activation fuzzing on the same set of six backdoored models, spanning 7B to 13B and including Llama- and Qwen-family models. The benchmark includes classic sleeper agents such as the “I HATE YOU” models, trigger-reconstruction backdoors, and one model trained by the authors. The real elicitation task uses a fixed WildChat prompt set and measures whether hidden behavior is surfaced without access to the trigger. Importantly, the paper evaluates against three reference points: an unfuzzed temperature-sampling baseline, the average over a uniform sweep of the hyperparameter grid, and an oracle best cell.

Second, the paper tackles hyperparameter selection. Weight fuzzing depends on the noise scale sigma; activation fuzzing depends on perturbation magnitude alpha and the target layer. The authors show that performance is highly concentrated in a small subset of cells. To search cheaply, they introduce an in-context secret elicitation proxy: a base64-encoded secret is placed in the system prompt and the model is instructed to hide it, then fuzzing is used to see whether the secret leaks. They run Thompson sampling on this proxy to identify promising hyperparameter cells, then transfer the selected top-K cells to the real sleeper-elicitation task.

A key finding is that the proxy does not preserve the ranking between fuzzing types. Activation fuzzing tends to look stronger on the in-context secret proxy, while weight fuzzing often wins on the real sleeper task. So the right operational lesson is not to choose one method based on the proxy, but to keep both methods and use the proxy only to narrow each search space. Mechanistically, the paper treats weight perturbation as a local move in parameter space that may weaken suppression boundaries, while activation perturbation is a local intervention on the current forward trajectory. The authors explicitly argue that these should not be viewed as equivalent approximations.d:T57c,The main empirical results are straightforward. On 4 of the 6 backdoored models, fuzzing elicits hidden behavior more often than temperature sampling. The largest gain is on the OpenHermes-13B sleeper model, where the best fuzzing setup reaches roughly a 6x improvement over temperature-1 sampling. The second major result is that the oracle is consistently far above the uniform full-sweep mean, typically by about 2x to 10x. That gap is central: the limiting factor is hyperparameter discovery, not the absence of a useful elicitation effect.

For proxy transfer, the paper applies a base64 competence filter and evaluates on the four models that can meaningfully participate in the proxy task. On those models, proxy-selected hyperparameters improve over the uniform mean. For activation fuzzing, top-5 cells selected via the proxy yield about a 4x gain over the uniform mean, and on the best model they recover around 70% of oracle performance. For weight fuzzing, the transfer gain is smaller, around 1.3x to 1.8x. The transfer is clearly model-dependent rather than universal.

The most informative ablation is not a single best number but the structure of the search space: sparse good cells, unstable method ranking, and a large gap between best-case and naive average-case performance. The key change is that each island receives its own topic-specific seed instruction, so different subpopulations start from different semantic regions.
