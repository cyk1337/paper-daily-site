19:T597,The method has two layers. First, the authors reuse a controlled setup from prior work: BERT-style masked token prediction on Markov-chain sequences with vocabulary size 16 and sequence length T=64. The baseline is a pre-norm residual MLP stack with d=64, L=6, MLP width 4d, GELU, final LayerNorm, and a token classifier; training uses Adam at 3e-4, batch size 32, for 10k steps with freshly sampled sequences each step. A matched architecture adds pre-norm multi-head self-attention to each residual block. Second, they treat attention as a perturbing operator around the MLP fixed point and linearize the dynamics near that point, deriving the fixed-point shift formula δ = -M*^{-1}(a+b). Here M* plays the role of the local response/stability operator of the MLP fixed point, while a and b encode first-order and coupling terms induced by attention. The formula is not used to predict neuron-level states directly; instead it yields four measurable predictions: whether the fixed-point geometry shifts or changes attractor class, whether the effective-rank profile changes qualitatively, whether attention acts in a layer-specific way, and whether the perturbation decays, persists, or amplifies with depth. This is the key distinction from standard representation analysis: the paper starts from a perturbative theory and then checks kernel drift, rank evolution, layer transitions, and residual loss gaps in trained networks.1a:T68c,All experiments compare two correlation regimes. In the short-correlation regime, α=10 gives λ2≈0.44 and ξ≈1.2, so sequences decorrelate within roughly five steps. In the long-correlation regime, α=0.05 gives λ2≈0.86 and ξ≈6.7, with mixing time around 31, so a context window of length 64 still carries substantial predictive signal. The main empirical findings are: (1) for long ξ, attention is strongly relevant—it closes a residual loss gap that the MLP cannot bridge and induces a phase-transition-like change in representation space; the abstract explicitly notes an effective-rank jump above the input dimension. (2) For short ξ, attention is largely irrelevant—the MLP already performs the needed coarse-graining, and adding attention changes neither the fixed-point geometry nor the rank profile much, with limited performance gain. (3) Attention is layer-specific rather than uniformly useful across depth; its effect concentrates around one or two transition layers, echoing the earlier observation that kernel drift in the MLP baseline is itself concentrated. (4) The perturbation decay spectrum depends on ξ: under long-range correlations, attention-induced perturbations do not simply die out and may amplify with depth, whereas under short-range correlations they behave more like irrelevant perturbations. The setup is small by design, so the point is not headline benchmark performance but validation of the predicted diagnostics.
