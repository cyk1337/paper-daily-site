An impossibility theorem is then proven: under outcome rewards (scalar reward assigned only at generation end, e.g. 0/1 for correctness) and group-relative baselines, no length-based weighting function can satisfy both P1 and P2 simultaneously. A parametric family of weighting functions f_α(L) = L^(α-1) (α ∈ [0,1]) characterizes the full tradeoff spectrum: α=0 recovers GRPO (length-normalized, length-invariant but gradient-biased), α=1 recovers Dr. GRPO (no length normalization, gradient-unbiased but length-biased). Quantitative analysis shows that for two trajectories with length ratio r, the longer trajectory captures r/(1+r) of the total gradient signal under Dr. GRPO, meaning length bias can be substantial even for moderate length differences.a:T85f,The method models each MDLM denoising step as two actions. First, the model predicts tokens for all currently masked positions. Second, among those newly predicted positions, it chooses which ones remain revealed and which ones are remasked for later steps. This yields a two-stage action MDP. As a result, the trajectory probability factorizes into a token policy term and a masking-policy term, and the policy gradient decomposes accordingly: one term reinforces good token choices, the other reinforces good ordering decisions.

A practical strength is that the masking term does not require a separate policy head. The authors derive unmasking scores from the model’s existing logits and turn them into position log-probabilities via a softmax. So the method adds no extra parameters and no extra forward passes beyond those already needed for token likelihood computation. That is important for diffusion LMs, where generation chains are already longer than AR decoding.

The theoretical claim is that dropping the masking term can miss ascent directions for expected return. Intuitively, this makes sense: in an MDLM, deciding which positions to resolve early and which uncertain positions to defer changes the future conditional distributions and can affect final answer quality. Optimizing only token predictions leaves part of the policy frozen.

Another positive feature is compatibility. The paper does not introduce a bespoke RL framework; it plugs the decomposition into standard policy-gradient style algorithms. Any method that relies on log-probabilities over sampled trajectories can, in principle, incorporate both token and masking log-probs. That makes the proposal easier to adopt than an entirely new optimizer.

Compared with prior work, the differences are clear. Relative to ELBO-based RL approximations, it moves from a reconstruction view to an explicit trajectory-policy view. Relative to token-only trajectory methods, it treats unmasking order as part of the policy.
