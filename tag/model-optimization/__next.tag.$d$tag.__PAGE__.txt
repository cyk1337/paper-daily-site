9:T459,The paper proposes a Model-Algorithm-System (MAS) taxonomy. At the model level, it covers architectural mechanisms such as modality-specific versus unified encoders, sparse expert routing, structural decoding, and modular adaptation. This layer addresses what should not be computed in the first place. At the algorithm level, it reviews ways to reduce effective computation under a fixed architecture, including token compression, pruning, quantization, distillation, speculative decoding, and cache reuse. At the system level, it focuses on execution orchestration: cache management, edge-cloud collaboration, latency-aware scheduling, and hardware-software co-design. A useful part of the survey is its emphasis on vertical synergies rather than isolated tricks. For example, reducing visual tokenization load at the model level changes the payoff of compression at the algorithm level and the bandwidth bottlenecks at the system level. Likewise, autoregressive multimodal generation pushes KV/cache design and scheduling into the foreground.
