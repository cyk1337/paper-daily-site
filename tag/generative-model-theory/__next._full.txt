Only a subset of conditions is observed, and the goal is to sample from an unseen condition whose density can be written as a geometrically weighted product of source densities. At the clean-data level, such a geometric product corresponds to a linear combination of score functions, which explains the common heuristic of adding conditional scores during denoising. The paper then separates two error sources. The first is inference-time approximation error: after forward noising, the clean-distribution score identity no longer holds exactly. Particle-based methods such as Feynman-Kac Correctors attempt to reduce this error by tracking importance weights along the reverse process.
