19:T4ef,GROW replaces trajectory-level policy-gradient RL with advantage-weighted flow-matching regression. For each prompt, the current policy samples a group of utterances. Each utterance is scored by two rewards: intelligibility and speaker similarity. Each reward is standardized within the group, then combined into a signed group-relative advantage. This advantage directly reweights the standard flow-matching loss, so samples above the group mean are reinforced and those below it are downweighted. Two design choices matter. First, the group-mean reward baseline turns reward weighting into advantage weighting, which removes prompt-level scale variation and amplifies subtle but meaningful differences among samples from the same prompt. Second, a Wasserstein-2 velocity penalty anchors the updated velocity field to a frozen pretrained reference, limiting drift and helping preserve naturalness. Compared with Flow-GRPO-style methods, GROW keeps deterministic ODE sampling, avoids ODE-to-SDE conversion, and does not require per-step likelihood evaluation.
