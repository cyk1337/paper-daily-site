9:T75e,The method replaces the usual single drafted block plus prefix rejection with a population-based procedure. A cheap draft model generates multiple particles in parallel over a block of length K. The target model then scores these particles, and instead of accepting the longest matching prefix and discarding the rest, SMC-SD computes importance weights from target-to-draft likelihood ratios and resamples the particle population accordingly.

This differs from standard speculative decoding in three important ways. First, standard SD has stochastic speed because the accepted prefix length is random; SMC-SD has a more regular execution pattern and therefore more predictable throughput. Second, SD is exact by construction, while SMC-SD is approximate. Third, SD throws away much of the information produced during verification once a mismatch appears, whereas SMC-SD reuses that information through weighting and resampling.

On the theory side, the paper gives non-asymptotic error bounds. The constants depend on the chi-square divergence between draft and target, and the approximation improves with the number of particles: L2 bias and mean squared error decay linearly in particle count, while L1 bias decays at the square-root rate. This is a useful result because it makes clear that proposal quality is as important as particle count.

On the systems side, the authors argue that SMC-SD maps well to GPUs because both drafting and verification are vectorizable. The resampling step avoids physical KV-cache movement by using in-place pointer exchanges, and cache size is reduced by exploiting shared prefixes across particles. The roofline analysis is also central: in memory-bandwidth-bound inference, extra arithmetic for weighting and resampling can be relatively cheap, so the method can improve tokens-per-second despite doing more computation than rejection-based SD.a:T485,The evaluation spans both Llama and Qwen model families and includes GSM8K, MATH500, AlpacaEval, and DS1000. The headline result is up to 2.5x higher throughput than an optimized speculative decoding baseline while staying within 3% of the target model’s accuracy. In a multi-GPU setting, the paper reports a 2.36x speedup over state-of-the-art speculative decoding and a 5.2x speedup over plain autoregressive decoding.

The most important empirical point is not just raw throughput, but the speed-quality trade-off. The paper presents SMC-SD as a tunable approximation scheme: by changing particle count, block size, and related settings, one can move along a continuum between fidelity and speed. The key ablations to pay attention to are how gains change as draft-target mismatch increases, whether empirical error decreases with particle count in line with theory, and how much of the end-to-end speedup comes from systems choices such as pointer-based KV handling and shared-prefix cache compression.
