9:T407,The proposed framework, BOT-Orch, formulates delegation as an OT-regularized multi-armed bandit. At each round, a task x_t arrives and the controller chooses one of M agents. Each agent has a latent reward R_t^i and an alignment cost W_t^i for that task. Unlike a standard bandit that only optimizes expected reward, BOT-Orch adds a task-agent alignment term based on optimal transport distances between agent output distributions and task-specific reference distributions. Intuitively, an agent with high mean reward can still be penalized if its output distribution is poorly aligned with the task. The formulation also allows history-dependent task arrivals, time-varying covariance, and cross-agent correlations. The theory claims sublinear OT-regularized regret, structural optimality, robustness to noisy alignment, and consistency/convergence properties.
