9:T51a,The baseline Sinkhorn matrix optimizer repeatedly normalizes the rows and columns of a matrix gradient and applies a scalar update scale. It is attractive because it removes moment state for dense two-dimensional weights. SAGE-like hybrids typically keep lightweight state for vocabulary tensors and use Sinkhorn for dense matrices. The paper shows that this dense-model role assignment breaks for MoE experts. For expert e, the gradient G_{e,t} is drawn from a changing conditional distribution p(x | e is selected by top-k routing), not from the full minibatch distribution. Since Sinkhorn normalization is nonlinear, Sinkhorn(E[G_t]) and E[Sinkhorn(G_t)] can behave differently. MESH addresses this by maintaining a hidden first-moment signal H_t for expert gradients and applying Sinkhorn to H_t rather than to G_t. Non-expert matrices and vocabulary tensors keep the SAGE/Sinkhorn hybrid treatment. The expert momentum is maintained through the gradient-buffer lifecycle instead of being stored as an explicit optimizer-state tensor. MESH-B adds a coarse block/neuron inverse-RMS multiplier, giving a limited form of adaptive scaling. 具体做法分三步。第一步，每个 layer、每个 KV head 选出 k 个 anchor 位置，这些 anchor 的 key 和 value 精确存储。文中强调 recency window 会被并入 anchor 预算，也就是最近一段 token 默认保真，这符合长上下文推理里“近邻依赖强、最近 token 更敏感”的经验。

第二步，对每个非 anchor token，分别在 K 和 V 空间里找到最相近的 anchor，用该 anchor 的方向做一维投影。于是一个原本 D 维向量，被压成“anchor index + scalar coefficient”。这一步不是把 token 聚成簇后共享同一个向量，而是共享方向、保留幅度，因此比纯聚类中心替代更细一些。K 和 V 共享 anchor 位置，但 assignment、投影系数、residual 集合分别独立，这个设计是合理的，因为 key 影响匹配分数，value 影响读出内容，两者误差敏感性不同。

第三步，在总字节预算固定的情况下，只给一部分位置额外存储 residual。residual 先做 randomized Hadamard transform，把能量打散，降低少数坐标异常值对 2-bit 量化的伤害；再按 token 做 absmax 归一化，用面向高斯源的四级 Lloyd-Max codebook 量化，坐标 2 bit 存储。更关键的是 residual 不是平均分配，而是按“恢复 attention output error 的收益”来选位置，并且同一层内各 head 竞争共享 residual 预算。这说明作者优化目标不是向量级 MSE，而是更贴近最终注意力输出的误差。

从方法取舍看，AnchorKV 刻意避开了两类复杂性。它不做 decode-time 动态检索，因此没有 host-device fetch 的调度开销；也不改 attention kernel 主体，因此部署路径比 retrieval/offloading 类方案短。代价是压缩质量高度依赖 prefill 结束时对整段 cache 的一次性分析，以及 anchor 选择和 residual 分配策略是否足够稳健。b:T7c9,The method compresses the KV cache once, at the end of prefill, without changing model weights or the decode-time attention formula.

The representation has three parts. First, for each layer and KV head, the method selects k anchor positions and stores their keys and values exactly. The anchor budget includes a recency window, so recent tokens are preserved with high fidelity by construction.

Second, every non-anchor token is approximated separately in K-space and V-space by projecting it onto the direction of its closest anchor. This turns a D-dimensional vector into an anchor index plus a scalar coefficient. It is more expressive than replacing tokens with a shared centroid, because magnitude remains token-specific even when direction is shared. Keys and values share anchor positions, but assignments, projection coefficients, and residual sets are separate, which makes sense because K and V affect attention in different ways.

Third, only a subset of positions receives an additional stored residual under a fixed byte budget. Residuals are rotated with a randomized Hadamard transform to spread energy and reduce outlier sensitivity, then quantized at 2 bits per coordinate using per-token absmax scaling and a four-level Lloyd-Max codebook. The important part is not just the codec: residuals are allocated according to how much they reduce attention-output error, not according to generic reconstruction loss. Residual budget is shared across heads within a layer, so heads compete for bytes where they matter most.

A notable design choice is what the method avoids. It does not rely on decode-time retrieval from host memory, so there is no fetch scheduling in the critical path. It also leaves the attention arithmetic intact, which shortens the deployment path relative to more invasive kernel or retrieval schemes. The first is operator-level theory. The paper separates Transformer components into linear and nonlinear operators, and studies what happens when the input activation of an operator is compressed. The key result is that if the compressor is unbiased, then for a linear operator the induced gradient error is itself unbiased and does not propagate further upstream to earlier operators. For nonlinear operators, neither property holds. This distinction is the central theoretical contribution because it turns a vague engineering intuition into a concrete rule for module selection. The second layer is model-level convergence analysis. The authors study mini-batch SGD when all linear operators use activation compression, derive an upper bound on gradient variance, and decompose it into two additive terms: standard mini-batch sampling variance and compression-induced variance. The compression term is a sum over operators, controlled by the second-order magnitude of activation errors, so the total increase remains manageable rather than exploding layer by layer. Under the standard L-smoothness assumption, they further show that after the same number of updates, the stationarity metric degrades by at most a factor tied to the fourth root of the cumulative compression error. The proposed method therefore jointly compresses activations and gradients by reusing the low-rank factors of compressed activations to maintain factorized weight gradients throughout training. The first is LAAQ, a layer-aware activation quantization scheme. Instead of forcing all saved activations into a uniform 4-bit format, the paper analyzes sensitivity across layer types and pipeline stages, then assigns different bit-widths accordingly. The motivation is straightforward: attention blocks, MLP blocks, normalization paths, and residual routes do not react to quantization noise in the same way. A uniform policy damages the most fragile activations first. LAAQ therefore treats activation quantization as a structure-aware allocation problem: more sensitive activations keep higher precision, more robust ones are pushed lower, and the average storage cost approaches 4 bits per element without the instability of true global 4-bit activation storage.

The second component is QuanGrad for 8-bit gradient storage and communication. Much of the prior gradient compression literature reduces All-Reduce traffic but still keeps local gradient buffers and accumulation in higher precision, so memory savings are limited. QuanGrad is more aggressive: gradients are stored in FP8, used for local accumulation, and communicated through a precision-preserving 8-bit All-Reduce path. The central issue is that naive FP8 accumulation introduces rounding error that slows or destabilizes training. The paper therefore adds scaling and error-control mechanisms to preserve effective accumulation precision while keeping the communication path in the same low-bit regime. Compared with earlier FP8-gradient efforts, the emphasis here is end-to-end stability of accumulation plus system-level gains.

From a systems perspective, AGoQ is additive rather than replacement-oriented. It sits on top of existing distributed training frameworks: activation quantization reduces forward-saved memory, gradient quantization reduces backward buffers and communication cost, and optimizer-state quantization can still be combined with it. Compared to prior lookup/n-gram approaches (e.g., large re-parameterized tables or explicit n-gram storage), it emphasizes tail-aware compression and diversity-preserving extraction so that increasing table size keeps yielding gains instead of saturating.17:T782,The key idea is to treat the currently loaded expert set as an option, and to model the decision to keep or replace that set using the option-critic framework. The paper formulates dynamic expert loading as a semi-Markov decision process. The state includes context and routing-related information; the action is not a single-token expert choice but a temporally persistent expert mask; an option termination function decides when to switch; and each switch incurs a deliberation cost that stands in for real loading latency or memory movement.

Implementation-wise, each layer gets a lightweight controller. The controller learns two things: a termination policy that decides whether to continue with the current expert set, and a policy over options that selects a new set when switching happens. This differs from standard MoE routing in that routing becomes piecewise constant over spans of tokens rather than independently recomputed at every step. To avoid full retraining, the authors adapt gpt-oss-20b with only small LoRA/adapter updates plus the controller, while keeping the base model largely fixed. Training uses an option-critic style objective with a deliberation penalty, and a self-distillation reward that encourages the modified model to stay close to the original model’s behavior while learning to switch less often.

This is distinct from prior serving-side heuristics. Caching and prefetching optimize around a fixed routing distribution; expert pruning permanently reduces capacity. Here the routing policy itself is changed so the model learns to keep the current expert set whenever the expected quality gain from switching does not justify the cost. The RL analogy is clean: longer option duration yields stronger temporal coherence and easier memory management, but too much persistence reduces token-level adaptability. The deliberation cost becomes the main knob controlling that trade-off.18:T6b3,The main experiments are built on gpt-oss-20b with a small amount of adaptation parameters. The paper first measures switching behavior in several frontier open-source MoEs and finds that active expert sets change extremely often, close to every token. On gpt-oss-20b, the baseline switch rate is above 50%, meaning the model changes expert sets roughly every other token.

With the temporally extended controller, the switch rate drops below 5%, and with stronger switching penalties it can approach 1%. On downstream evaluations such as MATH and MMLU, the adapted model retains up to about 90% of the base-model accuracy. That wording matters: the claim is not near-lossless compression of routing dynamics, but a controllable trade-off. The lower the switch rate, the more quality is sacrificed. The main empirical result is that this trade-off is learnable and tunable, rather than something that must be handled by hand-crafted heuristics.

For each prompt group, responses are split into positive and negative sets, and each response receives an implicit preference score defined in the DPO style as a scaled log-likelihood ratio between the policy and a reference model. Different group-wise methods mainly differ in how they aggregate or contrast these scores across the positive and negative sets, but they all share the same computational issue: the loss depends jointly on multiple responses from the same prompt, so gradients are coupled across samples.

The main technical contribution is a memory-efficient surrogate implementation. The idea is not to change the optimization target, but to change how gradients are computed. The method first performs an extra lightweight no-gradient pass to compute per-sample gradient coefficients implied by the group objective. It then rewrites training as a sample-level token loss whose first-order gradients match those of the original group-coupled objective. In other words, the surrogate is gradient-equivalent at first order, even if it is not numerically the same scalar loss.

This matters because the expensive part of naive group-wise training is not the score computation itself, but the need to keep all group members in one joint computation graph. By moving the coupling into coefficient computation and then doing ordinary per-sample backpropagation, the method removes most of the group-size-dependent activation overhead. The paper also compares several representative group-wise objectives under the same framework in both offline and online alignment settings.

A second important finding is that adding an NLL term on positive responses is often necessary to stabilize group-wise training. A strong point in the experimental design is that the paper does not present only one new objective.
