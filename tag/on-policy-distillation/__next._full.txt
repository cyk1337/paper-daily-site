This is more targeted than generic gradient clipping: it limits the effect of a small number of badly mismatched token entries that would otherwise dominate the update and destabilize adaptation.

Instead of using a fixed candidate length k, the drafter predicts how long the draft should be for the current prefix and confidence pattern. 这里有几个设计点值得注意。其一，为什么强调 on-policy：因为 skill 的价值依赖当前策略会访问哪些状态、会犯哪些错，离线静态 skill 库很快就会失配。其二，为什么用同一个模型同时做 actor 和 analyzer：不是为了省模型，而是为了让“会做事”和“会复盘”一起进化，避免 teacher 固定不动。其三，为什么不把 skill 直接放到推理时：作者希望把 hindsight 变成参数内化的能力，而不是额外的 inference-time memory 机制。

从方法关系上看，它和 verbal reflection、episodic memory、self-distillation 有亲缘性，但区别在于：SEED 不把反思文本当外部记忆长期挂在上下文里，而是把 skill 仅作为训练期 privileged context，通过概率重打分产生密集监督；也不同于纯 outcome RL，因为它提供了 decision-level credit signal；也不同于传统 teacher-student distillation，因为 teacher 不是外部更强模型，而是同一策略在更有 hindsight 的条件下的“增强视角”。23:T914,SEED stands for Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation. The framework has two stages.

Stage one is hindsight-skill supervised fine-tuning. The model is trained to analyze completed trajectories and produce natural-language skills that summarize reusable workflows, decisive observations, and failure-avoidance rules. These skills are not meant as user-facing summaries. They are privileged training-time signals that compress trajectory-level hindsight into a compact, conditionable representation.

Stage two is the self-evolving OPD loop. The current policy collects trajectories on-policy in the environment. The same model then acts as an analyzer and generates hindsight skills for those completed trajectories. Next, the sampled action sequence is kept fixed. The model re-scores those already-taken actions under two contexts: the original context and a skill-augmented context. The probability shift between the two is treated as a dense token-level distillation signal that indicates how hindsight would have changed the policy’s preference over the observed decisions. Training then jointly optimizes the standard outcome-based RL objective and this OPD objective.

Three design choices are central. First, the method is on-policy because useful hindsight depends on the states and failure modes induced by the current policy; a static offline skill bank would quickly become mismatched. Second, the actor and analyzer are the same evolving model, so decision-making ability and trajectory-analysis ability improve together instead of relying on a frozen teacher. Third, skills are used only during training, not at inference time, because the goal is to internalize hindsight into the policy parameters rather than depend on external memory or extra prompts.

Relative to nearby work, SEED is related to verbal reflection, episodic memory, and self-distillation, but it differs in a precise way. It does not keep reflection text as persistent inference-time context. It uses hindsight text only as privileged context during training to produce dense supervision. It also differs from pure outcome-based RL by providing decision-level credit signals, and from standard teacher-student distillation by using the same policy under a more informed hindsight-conditioned view rather than an external stronger teacher.24:T773,The paper evaluates SEED on long-horizon agent settings including embodied interaction, web navigation, and search-based tasks. The main empirical question is whether hindsight-skill distillation improves learning beyond outcome-only RL. The full excerpt provided here does not include the complete result tables, but the experimental structure makes the intended claims clear.

First, adding OPD on top of outcome-based RL improves task success over pure RL baselines, suggesting that hindsight skills provide learnable supervision rather than just extra noise. Second, the gains are not due to inference-time prompting, because the skills are used only during training and are not required at test time. Third, the self-evolving loop outperforms static skills or one-shot distillation, which supports the claim that hindsight supervision should track the current policy distribution. Fourth, token-level re-scoring is more effective than staying at the trajectory level, because it pushes hindsight information down to concrete decision points.

The most informative ablations are likely the following: removing the skill analyzer and keeping only RL, replacing the evolving analyzer with a fixed analyzer or offline skill dataset, using skills as test-time prompts instead of training-time distillation, and replacing token-level probability-shift supervision with sequence-level distillation only. Those comparisons are what establish where the gains actually come from.

From a research perspective, the most important things to inspect are whether the gains are stable across environments, how much extra training cost the analyzer loop adds, and whether skill quality becomes a bottleneck early in training when the policy is still weak. The motivation is that late turns may have little reliable supervision because few trajectories survive to them, while blindly truncating rollouts would remove rare but important deep decisions. The second component is a progressive turn-normalized loss controller. Standard token-normalized KL overweights early turns.
