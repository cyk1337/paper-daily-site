b:T78d,The method has two main components. First, Polestar-Cache uses token-level representational drift to decide where KV-cache entries have become stale and should be refreshed. Instead of assuming that tokens outside the current block remain valid, or relying on coarse proxies such as attention concentration or uncertainty, it directly tracks hidden-state changes across steps and layers. The motivation is specific to dLLMs: even if a token is not explicitly updated at the current step, its representation may still change substantially because neighboring tokens are being denoised under bidirectional attention. Polestar-Cache therefore performs sparse and sporadic KV refreshes only at high-drift positions, while reusing the rest.

Second, Polestar-Commit uses drift as a convergence signal for token commitment. The paper reports that token drift often exhibits a sharp spike near the onset of convergence toward the final output representation, followed by stabilization. This is used to decide when a token can be committed earlier and more safely. Compared with confidence-only policies, drift is better aligned with whether the representation is still being materially rewritten by context, which matters more in iterative bidirectional generation.

Third, the paper includes system-level optimization. Since measuring drift and scheduling refreshes introduce overhead, the implementation uses CPU offloading and latency hiding to overlap Polestar-specific operations with the main forward pass. This matters because many inference-time ideas look good algorithmically but lose their gains once control overhead is included.

Relative to prior work, Fast-dLLM relies on block-wise cache reuse and static commitment thresholds; Elastic-Cache and d2Cache use attention- or uncertainty-based proxies. Polestar’s distinguishing move is to use drift as a unified first-order signal for both cache refresh and token commitment.c:T51d,The evaluation spans multiple diffusion LLM families and reasoning-heavy domains such as math and coding. The headline numbers reported are up to 10.73% accuracy gain over prior baselines, 3.74% on average, up to 3.7× higher throughput, 1.6× on average, and decoding parallelism reaching 3.67 tokens per forward. The paper emphasizes improvement on the accuracy–throughput Pareto frontier rather than a single metric.

The more important experimental story is the validation chain behind those numbers. The paper aims to show: first, that drift is a useful indicator of cache staleness; second, that drift spikes correlate with token convergence; and third, that using drift for both cache refresh and commitment outperforms applying either idea in isolation. The key ablations are therefore likely to be: Polestar-Cache only, Polestar-Commit only, both together, different drift metrics or thresholds, and with versus without the system overlap optimizations.

When reading the full results, the most useful checks are whether gains hold across model families, whether longer generations benefit more, and whether quality degradation under more aggressive parallel commitment is meaningfully reduced. Those details determine whether drift is a robust control signal or a configuration-specific heuristic.d:T451,Start with the introduction and the first two figures. 蒸馏目标的构造很讲究。对每个 masked 位置 i，student 输出该位置的 token 分布；teacher 则读取终态序列 x_0 中该位置之前的 causal prefix，给出 AR 条件分布，作为该位置的监督信号。也就是说，teacher 提供的是“如果按 AR 方式生成到这里，应该偏向哪些 token”，student 学的是“在当前部分去噪状态下，对这些尚未恢复的位置给出与 teacher 一致的分布”。这样做同时解决两件事：一是知识保留，因为监督直接来自原 AR 模型的 logits，而不是只靠 diffusion NELBO 重新学；二是 train-test mismatch，因为训练样本来自 student 自己的 on-policy rollout，而不是离线随机 mask。

论文还加入 rollout-length curriculum。直觉上，训练初期学生模型还不稳定，如果一开始就在很长的去噪轨迹上做 on-policy 学习，状态分布会太差，蒸馏信号也更噪。于是作者逐步增加 rollout 长度，让学生先在较短、较容易的轨迹上对齐，再扩展到更接近真实推理的状态。这个设计和序列决策里的 curriculum / DAGGER 风格很接近，但这里的监督源是冻结 AR teacher。另一个值得注意的点是，作者把 OPDLM 明确定位成 general-purpose DLM 和 task-specific DLM 两种用法：既可以把通用 AR 基座转成通用 DLM，也可以在特定任务上做更便宜的转换。f:T802,The central idea is OPDLM, trained with On-Policy Distillation. The student starts from an ARLM checkpoint whose causal attention is replaced with bidirectional attention, enabling denoising over arbitrary masked positions. The teacher is the original frozen ARLM. The main challenge is that the teacher and student do not share the same state space: the teacher predicts the next token from a causal prefix, while the student denoises partially observed sequences.

The paper resolves this by letting the student generate its own reverse denoising trajectories. At each training step, the student rolls out a trajectory under its own diffusion decoding policy, and one partially denoised state x_t is sampled from that trajectory. Distillation is then applied only to the still-masked positions in x_t. For each masked position i, the student predicts a token distribution conditioned on the partially denoised sequence, while the teacher provides target logits conditioned on the causal prefix of the terminal sequence x_0 up to position i. In effect, the teacher says what an AR model would prefer at that position, and the student learns to match that preference on the states it will actually encounter during inference.

This design addresses both mismatches at once. Knowledge retention improves because supervision comes directly from the original AR teacher rather than from relearning everything through a diffusion NELBO. Train–test mismatch is reduced because training states are on-policy student states, not uniformly random masks. The paper also introduces a rollout-length curriculum: early in training, the student is supervised on shorter and easier trajectories, and the rollout horizon is gradually increased as the model stabilizes. That choice is important for optimization, since long on-policy trajectories from an immature student would otherwise create noisy and low-quality supervision. The framework is presented both as a general-purpose AR-to-DLM conversion method and as a cheaper task-specific DLM adaptation method.10:T597,The most informative results are about efficiency and the necessity of on-policy training. That is not a marginal saving; it turns conversion into something much closer to heavy post-training than continued pretraining.

On task performance, the paper evaluates across general-purpose and reasoning-heavy settings, including math and coding, and also studies task-specific conversion. The emphasis is not on claiming the single best absolute score everywhere, but on maintaining strong performance under dramatically lower training cost.
