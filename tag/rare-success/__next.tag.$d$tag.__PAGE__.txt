c:T812,方法核心是 Reflection-Enhanced Self-Distillation（Resd）。整体仍沿用 on-policy self-distillation 框架：当前策略 πθ 采样轨迹，旧策略或滑动平均策略 πθ_old 作为 self-teacher，在更“特权”的上下文下输出每一步 token 分布，student 去拟合 teacher 的分布。训练目标写成统一的 f-divergence 形式，兼容 forward KL、reverse KL、JSD 等，并对 likelihood ratio 做 clipping 来稳住训练。

Resd 的新增部分不在 loss，而在 teacher context 的构造。它做了两层增强：
1. retrospective reflection：对每条失败轨迹，额外生成一段回顾式反思，内容不是泛泛总结，而是定位局部错误、解释为什么失败、指出如果当时做什么会更好。这样 teacher 在重看同一轨迹时，不再只看到原始环境反馈，而是看到一份已经被语言化、因果化、局部化的纠错说明。
2. persistent global playbook：把训练过程中反复出现、可复用的经验教训沉淀成一个持续更新的“手册”。后续 teacher prompt 不只带当前轨迹的 reflection，还会带上相关 playbook 条目。这样监督信号不再局限于单条 rollout，而是能跨 step 复用失败中提炼出的策略知识。

这两点合起来，等于把环境反馈从 passive conditioning 变成 active supervision。和标准 SDPO/OPSD 的区别，不是 teacher 更强，也不是引入外部 oracle，而是先对失败反馈做解释层加工，再让 self-teacher 在这个 enriched context 下重估 token 分布。作者还强调该模块是 plug-and-play 的，原则上可接到不同 self-distillation objective 上。

从机制上看，这个设计为什么可能有效：raw feedback 往往是结果级、全局级、低带宽的；reflection 把它变成局部决策层面的错误归因，playbook 再把一次性经验变成跨样本先验。对于 token-level distillation，这种结构化上下文更容易改变 teacher 在关键决策点的条件分布，而不是只在结尾处给出模糊惩罚。d:T7ed,The method, Reflection-Enhanced Self-Distillation (Resd), keeps the standard on-policy self-distillation backbone. The current policy πθ samples trajectories. A self-teacher, instantiated from older or moving-average weights πθ_old, is given privileged context and produces token distributions for the same trajectory. The student then matches the teacher distribution with a unified f-divergence objective, with clipped likelihood ratios for stability. The loss family can recover forward KL, reverse KL, JSD, and related variants.

The main change is not in the loss but in how the teacher context is constructed. Resd adds two layers of structure:
1. Retrospective reflection. For each failed rollout, the system generates a reflection that diagnoses likely local mistakes, explains why the trajectory failed, and states what correction would have helped. This converts raw failure feedback into a more explicit error-attribution signal.
2. Persistent global playbook. Recurrent lessons extracted from failures are stored in a reusable playbook that persists across training steps. Future teacher prompts can condition not only on the current trajectory and its reflection, but also on accumulated reusable lessons.

This changes feedback from passive conditioning into active corrective supervision. Compared with standard SDPO/OPSD, the novelty is not a stronger external teacher and not a different policy class. The novelty is an intermediate interpretation layer that rewrites failure feedback into a form that can more directly alter the teacher’s token-level posterior.

Mechanistically, the design is sensible. Raw environment feedback is often global, low-bandwidth, and outcome-level. Reflection turns it into localized causal guidance. The playbook then amortizes that guidance across future examples. For token-level distillation, this should matter because the teacher can shift probability mass at the actual decision points that caused failure, rather than merely seeing a vague end-of-trajectory penalty.e:T6a1,The evaluation focuses on online continual-improvement settings rather than static offline benchmarks. Tasks are chosen to be relatively novel to the model, to start in a rare-success regime, and to provide rich execution feedback despite sparse binary rewards. The central question is whether the method can bootstrap from failures before successful demonstrations become common.

The main empirical finding is that Resd consistently outperforms standard self-distillation baselines, with the largest gains appearing in the early learning phase. Relative to GRPO, Resd improves faster at the beginning while using only a single rollout per prompt. That result is important because it supports the paper’s main claim: structured feedback can move the policy out of the pure-exploration regime even when successful trajectories are nearly absent, whereas reward-based optimization often becomes effective only after enough successes appear.

The paper also appears to include the key ablations one would want:
- raw failure feedback versus reflection-enhanced feedback;
- local reflection alone versus reflection plus a persistent playbook;
- comparison against reward-based optimization.
These comparisons support the interpretation that the gains come from better supervision extraction from failures, not from a stronger teacher or more rollouts.
