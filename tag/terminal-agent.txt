1b:T782,实验最有信息量的部分有三块。第一块是校准本身是否必要。论文指出，进入 contrastive calibration 的候选任务都已经通过结构验证和 self-solving，但初始时只有 19% 满足“强通过、弱失败”的目标关系；经过多轮 revision 和 re-probing，累计接受率提升到 96%。这说明 executable validity 与 solver-relative learnability 之间有明显缺口，而且这个缺口可以通过行为驱动的重写显著缩小。

第二块是不同数据构造策略对下游 agent 训练的影响。作者构建了 5,431 个校准后的 terminal tasks，并比较了几种训练数据来源：仅 authoring+validation、单 solver 反馈、多 solver calibration、contrastive calibration。Terminal-Bench 2.0 上，authoring+validation 只有 22.47%，单 solver 反馈 24.34%，multi-solver calibration 提升到 29.21%，contrastive calibration 31.09%。这里的提升不是边际噪声，而是相对清晰地说明：只知道任务“能做”不够，知道“谁能做、谁做不好”更有训练价值。

第三块是用完整校准数据训练模型后的结果。CalibForge-30B-A3B 和 CalibForge-35B-A3B 在 Terminal-Bench 2.0 上分别达到 32.58% 和 47.57%，比最强 baseline 高 6.36 和 6.75 个百分点。更关键的是泛化：35B 模型在 SWE-bench Pro 上 44.32%，在 Doc2Repo 上 48.77%，都是同 backbone 下所比较训练数据源里最好的结果。这个结果支持一个判断：校准得到的不是只对合成任务有效的“刷榜数据”，而是更接近真实软件工程交互中有效 supervision 的数据分布。

从消融角度，最值得注意的是 single-solver feedback 与 multi/contrastive calibration 的差距。它说明单一 solver 的成功或失败信号太粗，容易把任务推向该 solver 的局部偏好，而多 solver 分歧或强弱对比能提供更稳定的难度定位信号。1c:T8e4,CalibForge starts from a clue and builds a terminal task automatically, but the real novelty is the adversarial solver calibration stage after initial authoring. The pipeline has four main parts. First, an authoring agent works in a draft sandbox with web search, shell execution, and file editing. It researches concrete engineering issues from documentation, GitHub repositories and issues, and Stack Overflow, looking for reproducible problems such as dependency conflicts, version-specific bugs, configuration pitfalls, and edge cases. Second, it jointly writes the task instruction, execution environment, and verification tests, then performs structural validation and self-solving so the candidate is executable, checkable, and solvable by the system itself.

Third, the candidate enters calibration. The paper defines two retention criteria. Multi-solver calibration seeks disagreement in a heterogeneous solver pool: at least one solver must pass and at least one must fail. Contrastive calibration is stricter: a designated stronger solver must pass while a designated weaker solver must fail. In both cases, learnability is defined relationally, not by an absolute difficulty score. Fourth, if the criterion is not met, the authoring agent revises the task and revalidates it before another probing round. Importantly, revision is not limited to wording tweaks. The agent may revisit external research, change the technical direction, rewrite the environment, modify tests, or alter constraints.

Two design choices matter. First, the feedback includes not only verified pass/fail outcomes but also full trajectories. That gives the authoring agent actionable evidence about why a task failed or succeeded, which can be used to reduce noise, sharpen constraints, or introduce steps that better separate strong and weak solvers. Second, the loop is adversarial but constrained: retention always requires at least one real successful solve, so the system does not drift toward pathological or unsolvable tasks. Compared with one-shot authoring plus validation, or with single-solver scoring, this adds a behavior-driven revision loop. That is the key difference, because learnability is treated as something that can be actively shaped rather than passively measured.1d:T76d,The most informative results come in three layers. First, calibration is clearly necessary. All candidates entering contrastive calibration have already passed structural validation and self-solving, yet only 19% initially satisfy the target strong-pass/weak-fail relation. After iterative revision and re-probing, cumulative acceptance rises to 96%. This shows that executable validity and solver-relative learnability are far from equivalent, and that the gap can be closed by behavior-guided rewriting.

Second, the paper compares training data produced by different construction strategies. On Terminal-Bench 2.0, authoring plus validation alone yields 22.47% accuracy. Adding single-solver feedback gives 24.34%. Multi-solver calibration reaches 29.21%, and contrastive calibration reaches 31.09%. The gain is large enough to support a clear interpretation: knowing that a task is solvable is not enough; knowing which solvers succeed and which fail provides substantially better supervision.

Third, the authors train on the full set of 5,431 calibrated tasks. CalibForge-30B-A3B and CalibForge-35B-A3B obtain 32.58% and 47.57% on Terminal-Bench 2.0, outperforming the strongest baselines by 6.36 and 6.75 points. The transfer results matter even more. The 35B model reaches 44.32% on SWE-bench Pro and 48.77% on Doc2Repo, the best among the compared training-data sources for the same backbone. That suggests the calibrated tasks are not merely overfit synthetic exercises; they appear to induce supervision that transfers to broader software-engineering settings.

The ablation that stands out is the gap between single-solver feedback and multi-solver or contrastive calibration. A single solver provides a coarse signal and can bias task construction toward that solver’s idiosyncrasies. Solver disagreement and strong-vs-weak contrast provide a more stable handle on difficulty placement.1e:T5ce,If time is limited, start with the Introduction sections that define the solver-relative learnable zone, then read the methodology around Figure 2 and Algorithm 1 to understand the control flow of the author–solver loop. After that, focus on the two calibration criteria, multi-solver and contrastive. Those sections contain the real contribution. Without them, the system would be just another task-generation pipeline.

In the experiments, prioritize three result blocks. First, the fraction of initially authored tasks that already satisfy the contrastive relation versus the cumulative acceptance after revision rounds. Second, the comparison among authoring-only, single-solver feedback, multi-solver calibration, and contrastive calibration on Terminal-Bench 2.0. Third, the final 30B and 35B training results and their transfer to SWE-bench Pro and Doc2Repo. Those are the results that tell you whether the method changes supervision quality rather than just producing more tasks.

Three deeper questions are worth following while reading. How exactly are trajectories converted into revision actions? How sensitive is the learnable zone to the composition of the solver pool? And can this idea generalize beyond terminal tasks to code completion, tool-use, web agents, or even difficulty-aware sampling in broader pretraining settings? For reproduction, it would be sensible to test the value of multi-solver disagreement filtering before rebuilding the full authoring system.1f:T4d7,A training sequence interleaves the system prompt, the user task, assistant actions, and environment observations. GRPO is applied to the assistant-action token positions. ECHO adds an auxiliary next-token cross-entropy loss on selected environment-observation positions. These observation tokens are not sampled by the policy, but the model already computes logits for them under teacher forcing over the full transcript, so their negative log-likelihood can be gathered from the same forward pass.

The design has three important properties. First, it is on-policy: the environment targets are generated in response to the current policy’s own actions. As the policy changes and visits new terminal states, the prediction targets also change. Second, it converts failed rollouts into useful training data.
