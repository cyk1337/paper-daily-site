17:T606,There are three central design choices. First, the task is a 26-week factored POMDP with six hidden factor processes governing demand, supply, logistics, quality, and related risks. Each week the agent observes a dashboard of symptoms—inventory, in-transit orders, realized demand, freight quotes, berth waiting times, canal transit counts, supplier scorecards, and quality reports—then chooses replenishment actions. Second, the hidden dynamics are intentionally factorized and the event tape is fixed, which makes a fair reference policy tractable: an exact Bayes filter is run per factor, and a rollout policy acts on the same observation stream seen by the LLM. The key point is not that the oracle is strong, but that it is non-privileged. Third, the benchmark defines decomposed metrics instead of relying on total cost alone. A skill score places the agent between a symptom-blind base-stock floor and the fair Bayesian oracle. Detection lag measures how quickly the agent names the active hidden factor in its rationale. Knowing-doing rate measures how often the agent still incurs stockout-like action failures during stress weeks it has already diagnosed correctly. The benchmark also introduces isolated, persistent, and compound stress profiles, keeping the world model fixed while varying overlap among hidden failures. One useful detail is that models are tested with a rules-only prompt and no strategy playbook, which makes the benchmark more about converting diagnosis into action than following a hand-crafted procedure.18:T5ba,The evaluation covers 50 seeds and 200 episodes, grouped by stress profile, with Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.4, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, and Grok 4.5. The main empirical result is a strong mismatch between diagnosis and control. On the perception side, all four models detect hidden-factor episodes at broadly similar rates, around 84% to 88%, typically within one week of onset. In some cases, the lower-skill models even diagnose faster. On the cost side, however, performance diverges sharply: two of the four models finish below the symptom-blind floor overall, meaning that on roughly half of their seeds, a policy that ignores symptoms entirely and follows a fixed inventory rule would have been cheaper. This is the clearest evidence that action quality, not just inference quality, is the main failure source. On persistent seeds, even during stress weeks that were correctly diagnosed, every model still suffers stockout in about 34% to 43% of those weeks. The paper further notes a severity effect: the weeks severe enough to be easy to detect are also the weeks most likely to break the shelf. Even after accounting for that, the main separation between models remains the quality of mapping diagnosis into order quantity, route, supplier choice, and protective actions. The knowing-doing gap is also bidirectional: some models under-react and order too little too late, while others over-protect and pay too much through freight locks, air expediting, or inspections.19:T79f,There are three core technical ideas. First, the task is cast as a POMDP. The model runs an Observation-Thought-Action loop, where actions request raw media slices such as frames, audio segments, or short clips. Crucially, the environment only serves raw media extraction; perception, reasoning, and action selection all happen inside the same model. The most important design choice is the strict split between transient multimodal percepts and persistent textual memory. High-dimensional observations are consumed locally within a turn, distilled into text, and then discarded. Long-horizon state is therefore carried by the reasoning trace rather than by retained raw pixels or audio tokens.

Second, the model is bootstrapped with Agentic SFT built from best-of-N trajectory synthesis plus dual-stage quality control. This is more than a data-generation detail. In active perception, answer correctness alone is not enough: a trajectory may reach the right answer through poor observation choices, and a plausible trajectory may still produce weak memory compression. The dual filtering is intended to keep trajectories that are both correct and learnable, so the model acquires action syntax, observation timing, and memory-writing behavior before RL refinement.

Third, the paper introduces Agentic RL with TAURA. The authors argue that vanilla GRPO is poorly matched to multi-turn agentic reasoning because it assigns a uniform trajectory-level advantage, which blurs the distinction between pivotal discovery turns and trivial actions. They call this advantage homogenization. TAURA uses turn-level entropy as a steering signal to rescale advantages, giving more weight to uncertain and potentially decisive turns. Relative to standard GRPO, this is a more targeted credit-assignment mechanism for interleaved perception and reasoning. Relative to pure supervised trajectory imitation, it preserves room for policy improvement and exploration.1a:T78d,The experimental story has four notable parts. First, the model improves over the direct Qwen2.5-Omni baseline on all ten reported benchmarks. Representative numbers in the paper include 50.5 on LVBench and 71.1 on MLVU. On omni-modal understanding, it reports gains of +4.7 on DailyOmni and +7.8 on OmniVideo. On LongVALE temporal grounding, the gain is an absolute +33.4. This suggests the benefit is not confined to one narrow task type; it extends across long-video QA, audio-visual understanding, and temporal localization.

Second, the gains are tied to efficiency rather than brute-force scale. A 7B OmniAgent reportedly outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-72B on LVBench, 50.5 versus 47.3, while using 73% fewer frames. That is the most practically meaningful result in the paper. It indicates that a learned active-perception policy can trade far fewer raw visual tokens for better task performance, which is more interesting for foundation-model design than another attention-side optimization alone.

Third, the framework shows positive test-time scaling. The paper reports a +6.2 improvement on VideoMME-Long when allowing more inference-time interaction steps. This is important because it extends the idea of spending extra compute at test time from text-only chain-of-thought into multimodal active perception.

Fourth, RL and TAURA appear to matter beyond cosmetic gains. From the full paper narrative, SFT alone teaches basic action execution, but policy quality degrades in longer multi-turn settings through redundant browsing and weak credit on key discovery turns. RL improves the policy, and TAURA further helps by distinguishing pivotal turns from routine ones better than direct GRPO.
