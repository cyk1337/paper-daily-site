9:T786,There are three main design choices. The first is a high-capacity motion vocabulary inside a unified token space. Open-UniMo is built on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and extends the original text vocabulary of roughly 150K tokens with 64K motion tokens. Motion and text share the embedding space and output head. The point is not merely adding special symbols, but making motion a native generative target rather than a side channel. This differs from many prior designs where motion is injected through adapters or auxiliary encoders and therefore has limited influence on the backbone representation.

The second is motion-grounded chain-of-thought. Instead of generating reasoning traces by rewriting text captions, the authors render motions into Blender videos and use Qwen2.5-VL-72B to produce CoT traces grounded in actual motion dynamics. The motivation is straightforward: text-only CoT can hallucinate details that are linguistically plausible but absent from the motion itself. Grounding the intermediate reasoning in rendered motion is meant to bridge coarse language semantics and fine-grained temporal dynamics.

The third is a two-stage training pipeline. Stage one is CoT-guided supervised fine-tuning for bidirectional text-to-motion and motion-to-text learning. Stage two applies GRPO with rewards for format correctness and cross-modal alignment. The interesting part is not GRPO in isolation, but its role in enforcing global properties that teacher-forced token prediction does not capture well, such as robust output structure and semantic consistency across modalities. The evaluation framework, Open-MoBench, is aligned with this goal: it scores text-to-motion outputs on physical plausibility, semantic alignment, and temporal coherence, scores motion-to-text outputs on detail consistency, semantic alignment, and temporal coherence, and adds cycle-consistency protocols for T→M̂→T′ and M→T̂→M′.a:T5a7,From the provided full-text excerpt, the backbone is Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, the unified vocabulary contains about 150K text tokens plus 64K motion tokens, and training uses million-scale open-world motion-language data.
