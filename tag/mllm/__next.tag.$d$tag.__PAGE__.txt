About 37% are visual risk unawareness, where the response follows the text instruction and largely ignores risky visual regions. About 63% are safety implication unawareness, where the model recognizes the visual object or scene but does not treat the request as safety-sensitive. The mechanistic analysis uses activation directions and attention on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct and Gemma-3-12B-IT. The key observation is that explicit harmful text activates stronger safety-related directions, while visually grounded risk receives weaker attention and weaker refusal triggering. The proposed method, Safety-awareness Representation Transfer, extracts coarse safety-awareness directions from harmful-text processing near pre-refusal layers, refines them to balance safety and utility, and transfers them to cross-modal risky inputs while keeping the base MLLM frozen. Compared with adding safety fine-tuning data or relying on prompting, SRT is closer to representation-level steering aimed at repairing the weak link between visual risk perception and safety decision making.a:T816,The method has two stages. First is AHAF, Adversarial Hallucination Attribute Flipping. Instead of using adversarial perturbations for standard classification attacks, the paper uses PGD to flip the model’s object-existence judgment on the same image, for example from present to absent or vice versa. This produces paired images that are semantically almost identical, with differences concentrated around the visual cues that move the model across a hallucination boundary. That is the key distinction from ordinary contrastive fine-tuning with random negatives: random negatives change too many factors at once, such as scene, object identity, and background, so the model can still solve the task through coarse spatial shortcuts.

Second is ACFT, Adversarial Contrastive Fine-Tuning. The model is fine-tuned with a contrastive objective built on AHAF-generated pairs, pulling correct image-text pairs together and pushing apart representations associated with hallucination-inducing perturbations. The important design choice is not merely the use of contrastive loss, but the controlled negative construction. Positive and negative samples share nearly all image content; the only systematic difference is the perturbation that flips the hallucination attribute. That makes the learning signal much more focused on the visual decision boundary.

AHAF also serves as a probe. If tiny pixel-level perturbations can flip the answer, then the visual representation is already sitting close to a fragile decision boundary even on clean inputs. That observation is more general than the specific method: many hallucination mitigation techniques may improve outputs without actually moving the underlying visual representation into a more stable region.

Compared with prior methods, VCD, OPERA, and VTI are more inference-time or post-hoc control methods; RLHF and DPO variants mainly reshape output preference; ordinary OCFT lacks targeted negatives. ACFT differs by directly training against the diagnosed visual misalignment mechanism, with no extra inference cost.b:T7ee,The most informative evidence comes from three groups of experiments.

Unlike threshold-based outlier fallback methods, which detect large values and route them through a higher-precision path, RFQ uses a fully low-bit and differentiable decomposition. It first quantizes the activation into a primary FP4 representation, then explicitly computes the quantization residual and sends that residual through a secondary low-bit quantization path for reconstruction. Conceptually, the approximation is q(x) + q_res(x - q(x)), rather than a hard split between normal values and outliers.

This design has several practical advantages. It is differentiable and suitable for end-to-end training. It avoids threshold tuning, which is difficult when outlier behavior varies by module and evolves during training. It does not require full-precision fallback, making it more compatible with true FP4 execution. And because it models the current quantization error directly, it is better aligned with dynamic training-time activation statistics than PTQ-style calibration methods.

First, the form of D. The paper focuses on explicit visual-semantic tokens rather than prompt rewriting in natural language or implicit latent queries. Within explicit tokens, it compares continuous visual features against discrete semantic tokens. For discretization, the authors study EMA-based tokenizers and embedding-based tokenizers, with and without auxiliary objectives such as MLLM understanding loss or pixel reconstruction loss. Second, how the MLLM should generate those tokens. The paper compares causal attention and full attention over visual tokens. Full attention allows bidirectional interaction and may help holistic representation learning, but causal attention works better in practice for semantic-token generation. The reason is structural: these tokens are used for temporal planning, and autoregressive generation matches the sequential nature of video events.

Third, how the DiT should use the MLLM output. The authors compare latent bridging through learnable queries and connector modules against explicit visual-token conditioning. Multi-layer cross-attention performs best, suggesting that semantic tokens are most useful when they provide persistent structural guidance throughout denoising rather than a weak one-shot hint.
