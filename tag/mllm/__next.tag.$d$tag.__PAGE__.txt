The authors study two variants: abstention, where the model outputs UNKNOWN when uncertain, and numerical confidence, where it emits a confidence score alongside the answer. These methods are easy to deploy, work for both open and closed models, and add almost no inference cost. Their weakness is obvious: they rely entirely on the model’s self-calibration.

The second family is token-level uncertainty. The paper uses entropy and negative log-likelihood from the autoregressive output distribution, aggregated over the response by mean and max. Mean is intended as a length-normalized global uncertainty score, while max captures localized spikes from weakly grounded tokens. A key empirical point is that these signals degrade badly under deterministic decoding, but recover when estimated at higher temperature. That is an important observation because it shows that token-level UE is not just about reading logits; the decoding regime determines whether the uncertainty signal remains informative.

The third family is semantic uncertainty. The study includes Semantic Entropy and multimodal variants such as VL-Uncertainty and VASE. The idea is to sample multiple responses, cluster them by semantic equivalence using bidirectional entailment or perturbation-based consistency, and compute entropy over semantic classes rather than surface forms. Training is nontrivial because argmax-based subset selection is discrete. The paper introduces a trajectory-aware differentiable surrogate built around a variance-preserving noise gate. Second is ACFT, Adversarial Contrastive Fine-Tuning. The model is fine-tuned with a contrastive objective built on AHAF-generated pairs, pulling correct image-text pairs together and pushing apart representations associated with hallucination-inducing perturbations. The important design choice is not merely the use of contrastive loss, but the controlled negative construction. Positive and negative samples share nearly all image content; the only systematic difference is the perturbation that flips the hallucination attribute. That makes the learning signal much more focused on the visual decision boundary.

That observation is more general than the specific method: many hallucination mitigation techniques may improve outputs without actually moving the underlying visual representation into a more stable region.

Using normalized Shannon entropy over 500 hallucinated and 500 non-hallucinated samples, entropy is 5.1% higher for hallucinated cases when the object is present, and 6.2% lower when the object is absent.
