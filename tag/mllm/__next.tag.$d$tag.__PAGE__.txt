The first family is verbalized uncertainty. The model expresses uncertainty in natural language. The authors study two variants: abstention, where the model outputs UNKNOWN when uncertain, and numerical confidence, where it emits a confidence score alongside the answer. These methods are easy to deploy, work for both open and closed models, and add almost no inference cost. Their weakness is obvious: they rely entirely on the model’s self-calibration.

The second family is token-level uncertainty. The paper uses entropy and negative log-likelihood from the autoregressive output distribution, aggregated over the response by mean and max. Mean is intended as a length-normalized global uncertainty score, while max captures localized spikes from weakly grounded tokens. A key empirical point is that these signals degrade badly under deterministic decoding, but recover when estimated at higher temperature. That is an important observation because it shows that token-level UE is not just about reading logits; the decoding regime determines whether the uncertainty signal remains informative.

The third family is semantic uncertainty. The study includes Semantic Entropy and multimodal variants such as VL-Uncertainty and VASE. The idea is to sample multiple responses, cluster them by semantic equivalence using bidirectional entailment or perturbation-based consistency, and compute entropy over semantic classes rather than surface forms. This avoids treating paraphrases as uncertainty. The trade-off is higher cost: multiple generations, an entailment model, and clustering overhead.

The evaluation protocol is one of the paper’s main strengths. The comparison spans four axes: model family, generation, size, and response-length regime. The models come from two MLLM families, InternVL and QwenVL, each with two generations and two scales, roughly 2B and 7-8B. The tasks cover five VQA benchmarks spanning multiple-choice, sentence-length, and open-ended outputs. In addition to AUROC and Coverage at Risk, the paper uses CHDA, a calibrated hallucination detection accuracy metric that calibrates a threshold on a small subset before deployment-style evaluation. That makes the analysis more operational than standard ranking-only metrics.

The contribution is therefore not a new UE algorithm.
