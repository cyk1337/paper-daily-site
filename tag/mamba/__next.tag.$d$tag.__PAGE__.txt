The key design choice is simple but consequential: bidirectional mixing is retained only inside the current active denoising block, while completed prefix blocks are represented through cacheable forward states.

At the architectural level, attention layers cache standard KV tensors at block boundaries, and Mamba layers cache forward recurrent boundary states. This lets completed blocks remain fixed conditioning context across reverse diffusion steps, instead of being recomputed every time.

The main departure from prior full-sequence DiffuMamba-style models is the restriction of the reverse-direction Mamba scan. A reverse scan over the whole denoising window would make prefix states depend on the current noisy block and on the diffusion timestep, which destroys cache reuse. By limiting the reverse scan to the active block, the model keeps local bidirectional context where denoising needs it, while preserving a stable forward-only prefix representation.

Training is built around an all-block objective. A training window is partitioned into blocks, and each block is trained as the current denoising target conditioned on the clean prefix blocks to its left. The important part is not merely summing block losses. During training, the model explicitly constructs the same cache objects used at inference: attention KV caches and Mamba forward boundary states. Losses on downstream blocks therefore directly train the mechanism that builds reusable prefix states.

The paper also introduces timestep-conditioning factorization so that diffusion-step modulation applies only to the active block. This avoids contaminating clean-prefix caches with timestep-dependent activations. That detail is essential: without it, the cached prefix would vary across reverse diffusion steps and would no longer be reusable.

MLA preserves stronger attention capacity but still incurs KV-cache cost, while the linear blocks add no KV-cache overhead and are better suited for long contexts. The MLA-to-linear ratio is treated as the main quality-efficiency trade-off knob, which is a sensible choice because aggressively replacing attention often hurts short-context quality.

Second, the paper develops a checkpoint transformation path from a pretrained Transformer to the hybrid model. Following the spirit of Zebra-Llama, the authors construct pure-MLA and pure-linear variants initialized from the original Transformer weights. For GDN, they provide an explicit attention-to-GDN weight transfer procedure that handles projection mismatches such as grouped-query attention layouts, while keeping the original SwiGLU MLP and RMSNorm sublayers intact. This is an important design choice: the sequence mixer is replaced, but the stable feed-forward and normalization structure of the Transformer is preserved.

Third, and most importantly, HyLo introduces a staged long-context training recipe. Instead of jumping directly to very long sequences, training progresses from 8K to 64K context. This matters because upcycled models already face optimization stress from architectural mismatch, and combining that with immediate ultra-long training can destroy inherited capabilities. To stabilize this process, the paper adds teacher-guided long-context distillation using chunk-wise KL supervision. The chunk-wise design avoids a monolithic full-sequence distillation objective and makes optimization and memory usage more manageable. The paper also acknowledges the trade-off: distillation improves fidelity and stability, but it can constrain the student to remain too close to the teacher's local behavior.

Relative to training hybrid models from scratch, HyLo is about checkpoint reuse and continued training. Relative to earlier upcycling work, its main shift is to center long-context training and distillation rather than treating them as afterthoughts.17:T6aa,The evaluation spans two Transformer families, Llama and Qwen, and two linear block types, Mamba-2 and GDN, which helps show that the recipe is not tied to a single base model. The clearest quantitative claims in the provided text are:
1. staged long-context training is extended from the roughly 24K regime common in prior upcycling work to 64K;
2. after integration into vLLM with tensor parallelism, a Llama-3.2-3B-scale HyLo model can serve up to 2M tokens on 8 AMD MI300X GPUs, about a 30x context extension over the original model.

Empirically, the paper claims better long-context performance than Zebra-Llama-style baselines while keeping short-context quality comparable. A particularly useful part of the study is the analysis of how training sequence length affects long-context generalization. That is more informative than simply reporting a final training length, because it probes whether longer training contexts actually extrapolate and how the curriculum schedule affects stability.

Another key result is the benefit of teacher-guided distillation. Chunk-wise KL supervision significantly improves long-context performance and stabilizes optimization after upcycling. Even without all benchmark tables here, the main pattern is clear:
- architecture replacement alone is not enough to reliably obtain strong long-context behavior;
- staged context-length training helps;
- adding teacher-guided distillation improves long-context quality further;
- these trends broadly hold across different base models and linear modules.
