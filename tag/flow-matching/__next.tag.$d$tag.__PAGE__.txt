5:T4ef,GROW replaces trajectory-level policy-gradient RL with advantage-weighted flow-matching regression. For each prompt, the current policy samples a group of utterances. Each utterance is scored by two rewards: intelligibility and speaker similarity. Each reward is standardized within the group, then combined into a signed group-relative advantage. This advantage directly reweights the standard flow-matching loss, so samples above the group mean are reinforced and those below it are downweighted. Two design choices matter. First, the group-mean reward baseline turns reward weighting into advantage weighting, which removes prompt-level scale variation and amplifies subtle but meaningful differences among samples from the same prompt. Second, a Wasserstein-2 velocity penalty anchors the updated velocity field to a frozen pretrained reference, limiting drift and helping preserve naturalness. Compared with Flow-GRPO-style methods, GROW keeps deterministic ODE sampling, avoids ODE-to-SDE conversion, and does not require per-step likelihood evaluation. The paper also argues against purely positive exponential weighting: in bounded, tightly clustered TTS rewards, it does not provide enough contrast and can be dominated by the shared imitation component.6:T433,Experiments are run on LibriSpeech-PC test-clean and the English and Chinese Seed-TTS test sets in a zero-shot setting. The main metrics are WER, speaker similarity, and UTMOS for naturalness. Averaged over the three test sets, GROW reduces WER from 2.016 to 1.558, an absolute drop of 0.458, and increases speaker similarity from 0.676 to 0.715, an absolute gain of 0.039, while keeping UTMOS at roughly the pretrained level. The paper reports that these gains surpass the DiTAR-GRPO baseline overall. On efficiency, GROW with 10-NFE training rollouts reaches performance comparable to its own 32-NFE setting, and is about 2.9× faster to train than the 32-NFE DiTAR-GRPO baseline. At matched training-rollout NFE, GROW is still about 1.1× faster. All final evaluations use 32 NFE to keep inference quality comparable. Ablations cover rollout NFE, CFG, reward weighting variants, and reward composition. The main empirical message is that group-relative advantage works better than naive reward weighting, and the W2 anchor is important for stability and quality retention.7:T56f,AURORA-LM fully decouples latent space construction and generative modeling to eliminate conflicting objectives between the two stages. First, a high-capacity text autoencoder is trained independently to produce full-width continuous latent sequences of the same length as the original token sequence, with no sequence downsampling. The autoencoder training objective focuses exclusively on exact text reconstruction, with no added regularization to smooth latents or make them easier to generate, ensuring latents carry all information present in the original text. Second, a diffusion model is tailored to model the distribution of these high-dimensional full-width latents, using a block-causal attention structure: sequences are split into contiguous chunks, with strict causal dependence across chunks (later chunks are generated only after previous chunks are fully determined), while diffusion-based parallel generation operates within each chunk. This design preserves the parallel throughput of diffusion models while maintaining the causal structure required for long-form text coherence, avoiding the long-range logical inconsistencies common to non-causal full-sequence diffusion. At generation time, continuous latents produced by the diffusion model are mapped directly to discrete text via the pretrained autoencoder decoder, with no external reranking or correction required.8:T485,Evaluations cover unconditional generation, conditional generation, system-level efficiency-quality tradeoffs, and scaling behavior. Key ablation studies examine three core design choices: Latent capacity tests show that sequence downsampling introduces non-trivial reconstruction error and measurable generation quality degradation, confirming full-width uncompressed latents are necessary for high-fidelity text generation. Block size ablations map the tradeoff between speed and coherence: very small blocks regress to token-by-token serial generation with no throughput benefit, while very large blocks break causal structure and degrade long-form coherence. Scaling tests confirm smooth, predictable performance gains as model parameter count and training data volume increase, with no saturation of returns at evaluated scales—a common failure mode of earlier continuous LM designs. System-level comparisons show substantial quality gains over prior compressed-latent continuous diffusion LMs; the block-causal design matches the generation quality of autoregressive baselines while delivering higher throughput via within-block parallel generation.9:T79f,The method starts with a single-level discrete wavelet transform. An image is decomposed into one low-frequency approximation band and three high-frequency detail bands. The implementation uses the Haar wavelet throughout, mainly because it is orthogonal, invertible, simple, and effectively acts like a lossless 2×2 average-pooling-plus-residual decomposition. Unlike a learned latent space, this transform introduces no reconstruction loss and requires no extra encoder training.

The core change is not architectural. The model keeps the JiT pixel-space transformer and modifies the noise schedule. The LF band is trained over the full time interval t in [0,1], while the HF bands are only active over [t*,1]. During inference, the model first denoises the coarse band alone. At time t*, HF noise is injected, the bands are recombined through the inverse wavelet transform, and the model continues with joint refinement. This preserves cross-band interaction in the later stage, but avoids spending early trajectory steps on HF content that is effectively pure noise.

Conceptually, this is a frequency-aware flow-matching schedule. Compared with standard pixel-space FM, it explicitly respects the natural frequency hierarchy of images. Compared with latent diffusion, it obtains a coarse-to-fine decomposition without a lossy VAE bottleneck. Compared with progressive multi-scale architectures, it is much simpler and cheaper to integrate into an existing transformer pipeline.

A second contribution is the evaluation protocol. The authors argue that standard FID is too forgiving at high resolution because it downsamples to 299×299 and discards much of the fine detail. They therefore add 5-crop FID for native-resolution local structure and high-frequency Fréchet Wavelet Distance for texture fidelity. This matters because the method is explicitly designed to improve high-frequency generation; using only FID would hide part of the gain.a:T5b8,The experiments span class-conditional image generation, text-to-image, and video. On ImageNet 512×512, WaiT reports FID 1.43, 5cFID 1.63, and hFWD 0.67, while reducing sampling GFLOPs by up to 50% relative to the JiT baseline. The largest 2B model reaches FID 1.3 on ImageNet 512 for pixel-space models. The paper also highlights that on hFWD, which isolates texture fidelity, WaiT outperforms both pixel-space and latent-space baselines, suggesting the gain is not limited to global structure.

The same trend appears on OpenImages at 512 and 1024 resolution, which is useful because it reduces the chance that the effect is ImageNet-specific. In pixel-space text-to-image at 1024×1024, the method matches or exceeds JiT on almost all metrics while reaching up to 3× higher throughput. With spatiotemporal wavelets, the recipe transfers to video and achieves FVD 0.84 on Kinetics-600.

The most informative evidence comes from the mechanism-oriented analyses. The trajectory visualizations show that HF bands remain nearly indistinguishable from noise early in sampling, which supports the central premise. Ablations around t* expose a quality-efficiency trade-off: delaying HF helps, but the exact delay matters. The three-axis evaluation also reveals disagreement between metrics, with some methods looking strong on FID but weaker on local detail or texture.
