值得注意的是，这篇方法不改 verifier，不训练新的 MTP head，也不要求重新训练 draft model。它利用的是部署时天然存在的 acceptance/rejection 信号，因此工程侵入性很低。和已有自适应 speculative decoding 方法相比，它的区别在于：一，决策发生在 drafting 之前，适配 MTP 的并行 head 结构；二，目标是 exact verification 下的 throughput，而不是 greedy 近似或额外训练的 confidence proxy。19:T8eb,The key idea is to cast horizon selection as conditional throughput optimization. For each candidate horizon h, the objective is not simply the expected number of accepted tokens. It is the ratio between expected committed tokens and expected round latency. The central statistic is acceptance survival: the probability that the draft prefix up to position i is fully accepted. This quantity determines the expected number of committed tokens for a given horizon. The paper further decomposes survival in terms of conditional rejection hazards, namely the probability that position i is rejected given that all earlier draft positions were accepted. Once these hazards are estimated, the controller can evaluate the expected throughput of each horizon.

The main difficulty is partial feedback. If the system chooses a short horizon, it never observes verification outcomes for later positions. Moreover, the hazards must be estimated under the distribution of surviving prefixes, not merely over all proposed prefixes. To address this, the paper derives a survival-weighted regression objective aligned with the downstream decision problem. AMTP then uses this objective in an online learning loop. Before drafting, it predicts hazards and latency, computes the expected throughput for each horizon, and usually chooses the best one greedily. To avoid blind spots at longer horizons, it occasionally performs randomized full-horizon exploration. Because those observations are collected under a nonuniform exploration policy, the updates use inverse-probability weighting to obtain unbiased estimates of the survival-weighted loss. The paper also provides a regret analysis that upper-bounds conditional throughput regret in terms of cumulative hazard-prediction error.

A notable strength is that AMTP is training-free in the deployment sense: it does not retrain the draft model, does not modify the verifier, and does not add new MTP heads. It exploits acceptance and rejection signals that already exist in speculative decoding. Second, comparisons against simpler adaptive heuristics, such as rules based on average acceptance rate or entropy-style confidence. Third, the exploration-exploitation tradeoff: too little exploration prevents learning about long horizons, while too much exploration hurts immediate throughput. Fourth, error attribution: does performance degrade mainly because latency is hard to estimate, or because survival and hazard predictions are poorly calibrated?
