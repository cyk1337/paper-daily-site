a:T4b6,MDLMPE explicitly conditions positional representations on the denoising state. It starts from a binary availability sequence indicating which positions contain revealed tokens and which positions are masked or attention-invalid. For each target token, a target-centered Gaussian weighting is applied to represent the spatial distribution of visible evidence around that token. For an attention pair, the two centered views are averaged into a joint distribution, so the attention relation can depend on both relative distance and the surrounding availability pattern.

The distribution is then projected through a cosine basis to produce features. The method injects these features through two paths. Token-level features are added to the token representation. Pair-level features are mapped by a lightweight MLP into angular offsets for RoPE. This keeps the relative-position geometry of RoPE, but makes the rotations responsive to the current mask pattern. The method is different from long-context RoPE adaptations such as LongLLaDA and UltraLLaDA, which mainly target extrapolation beyond the pretraining length; MDLMPE targets the changing availability topology within the current denoising sequence.b:T85f,The method models each MDLM denoising step as two actions. First, the model predicts tokens for all currently masked positions. Second, among those newly predicted positions, it chooses which ones remain revealed and which ones are remasked for later steps. This yields a two-stage action MDP. As a result, the trajectory probability factorizes into a token policy term and a masking-policy term, and the policy gradient decomposes accordingly: one term reinforces good token choices, the other reinforces good ordering decisions.

A practical strength is that the masking term does not require a separate policy head. The authors derive unmasking scores from the model’s existing logits and turn them into position log-probabilities via a softmax. So the method adds no extra parameters and no extra forward passes beyond those already needed for token likelihood computation. That is important for diffusion LMs, where generation chains are already longer than AR decoding.

The theoretical claim is that dropping the masking term can miss ascent directions for expected return. Intuitively, this makes sense: in an MDLM, deciding which positions to resolve early and which uncertain positions to defer changes the future conditional distributions and can affect final answer quality. Optimizing only token predictions leaves part of the policy frozen.

Another positive feature is compatibility. The paper does not introduce a bespoke RL framework; it plugs the decomposition into standard policy-gradient style algorithms. Any method that relies on log-probabilities over sampled trajectories can, in principle, incorporate both token and masking log-probs. That makes the proposal easier to adopt than an entirely new optimizer.

Compared with prior work, the differences are clear. Relative to ELBO-based RL approximations, it moves from a reconstruction view to an explicit trajectory-policy view. Relative to token-only trajectory methods, it treats unmasking order as part of the policy. Relative to approaches that would need an extra network for ordering, it reuses the model’s own logits and keeps the engineering overhead low.c:T678,The main empirical focus is mathematical reasoning and code generation. The headline result is consistent improvement from jointly optimizing the token and masking terms over methods that optimize only the token component. The abstract reports gains of up to +4.0% on MATH500, +2.9% on HumanEval, and +2.5% on GSM8K and MBPP. The paper also claims top-tier performance across these tasks, suggesting the benefit is not isolated to a single benchmark.

The most informative part should be the ablations. The critical comparisons are likely: token-gradient only, masking-gradient only, and both together; sensitivity to remasking schedules; and alternative constructions of the position log-probability. If the joint objective consistently wins, that supports the claim that the masking policy contributes genuine learning signal rather than acting as a noisy regularizer.

The paper should also be read against prior ELBO-based RL and token-only trajectory RL baselines. For those comparisons to be meaningful, one should check whether rollout count, denoising steps, reward normalization, and the underlying MDLM backbone are controlled. RL results for diffusion LMs can otherwise be confounded by sampling budget and decode schedule.

A practical point is efficiency. The authors emphasize that the masking term comes at no extra forward cost beyond token-likelihood computation. That means the gains are not simply purchased with more compute; they come from extracting more supervision from the same sampled trajectory. If the full paper includes wall-clock or sample-efficiency numbers, those would be especially useful for judging real adoption value.d:T5c9,Do not start from the result tables. Start from the formalization. The central idea is that one MDLM denoising step consists of two actions—token prediction and remasking—not that remasking is an implementation detail.

First, read the preliminaries that define the MDLM trajectory and sampling process. Make sure you understand the exact remasking semantics: whether the method only decides which newly predicted positions stay revealed, or whether previously revealed tokens can be changed again. That choice determines the MDP state and action space.

Second, read the two-stage MDP and policy-gradient derivation carefully. This is the core of the paper. Focus on how the trajectory probability factorizes, how the position log-probability is derived from logits, and how the theory section argues that ignoring the masking term can miss improving directions. If you only read one section, read this one.

Third, inspect the main comparisons and ablations. Prioritize token-only versus token-plus-masking, consistency of gains across tasks, and any signs of instability or reward hacking. For RL on diffusion LMs, variance and training stability often matter more than the best single score.
