9:T4ff,The analysis uses the online-to-nonconvex conversion framework. The optimizer’s update rule is treated as an online learner, and the learner’s regret is converted into a Goldstein-style stationarity guarantee for nonsmooth nonconvex objectives. For a momentum matrix M = U Sigma V^T, the ideal Muon direction is the polar factor U V^T, which preserves singular subspaces and maps all positive singular values to one. This hard singular-value map is discontinuous. Finite Newton–Schulz replaces it with a polynomial map obtained by q iterations of an odd matrix polynomial, implemented with matrix multiplications rather than an SVD. The paper shows that this finite-depth map is the gradient of a smoothed spectral potential evaluated at the momentum matrix. This turns Muon into a gradient-based prediction algorithm. The discounted regret decomposes into a penalty term and a stability term. The penalty decreases as the smoothed potential approaches the nuclear norm; the stability term is controlled by the Lipschitz constant of the finite Newton–Schulz map. Choosing q = O(log(1/epsilon)) balances these terms.
