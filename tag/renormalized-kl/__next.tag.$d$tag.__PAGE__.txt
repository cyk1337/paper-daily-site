9:T6a5,UBTree has two main ideas. The first is architectural factorization. Drafting is split into a unigram proposer, a bigram selector, and a tree constructor. The proposer is a block-parallel drafter in the DFlash style: it predicts candidate tokens for all positions in a drafting block independently, preserving the throughput advantage of parallel drafting. The selector then scores adjacent candidate-token pairs instead of restoring full autoregressive dependence. It uses proposer hidden states together with frozen target embeddings transformed by separate MLPs into predecessor and successor representations, and predicts transition scores for candidate pairs. The method also learns depth-dependent weights to combine proposer logits with selector scores, which effectively lets shallow tree levels rely more on marginal token quality and deeper levels rely more on local path consistency. The second idea is tree-native training. Instead of standard distillation on target rollouts sampled at inference temperature, UBTree uses high-temperature regenerated target trajectories and a renormalized KL objective. High-temperature trajectories expose more plausible alternatives beyond the top path, while renormalized KL focuses supervision on these meaningful alternatives rather than wasting signal on low-quality tail mass. The result is a drafter trained not just to produce one strong path, but to allocate probability mass across multiple branches that are useful for tree verification. Compared with prior tree drafting methods, the contribution is not merely “using a tree”; it is explicitly matching both model structure and training objective to the multi-branch verification setting.a:T432,The paper evaluates UBTree on both academic-scale and production-scale settings. On a production model, Ling3-Flash-124B, UBTree is described as competitive with frontier baselines such as DSpark. The paper also emphasizes stronger durability on long-context tasks and in high-entropy scenarios, which is exactly where single-path parallel drafters tend to degrade. From the method description, the key ablations are likely to show that removing the bigram selector hurts branch quality, replacing tree-native training with standard single-path training reduces the usefulness of lower-ranked branches, and depth-dependent score fusion improves node ranking across tree levels.
