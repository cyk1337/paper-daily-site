17:T842,The method, VEPA (Visual Evidence Pre-Alignment), factorizes answering into two steps. First, the MLLM generates textual visual evidence conditioned on the image and the question. Second, a frozen blind reader—an LLM that does not see the image—receives only the question and the generated evidence, and tries to recover the ground-truth answer. In probabilistic terms, the paper rewrites direct answering as a decomposition through a latent bottleneck e: an evidence policy P(e|v,q) followed by a reasoning policy P(a|e,q). The bottleneck is intentional. It forces the model to externalize the visual content that is actually needed for answering.

The optimization target is built around two properties. Sufficiency means the evidence should contain enough information for the blind reader to infer the answer. Visual dependence means the evidence must genuinely come from the image rather than from question priors or direct answer leakage. To train the evidence policy, the paper uses GRPO. For each sample, the model generates a group of candidate evidence sequences, and learning is driven by relative rewards within the group.

The reward design is the critical part. The paper does not simply reward the reader for getting the answer right. It adds constraints to prevent degenerate solutions: penalties against answer leakage, so the evidence cannot collapse into a disguised answer channel; penalties against repetitive or low-information evidence, so RL does not reward verbosity or templated outputs; and a decoupling between grounding and final answer generation, so the optimization pressure stays on evidence quality rather than downstream response style. This is the main difference from direct answer-level RL, where the model can still exploit shortcuts from language priors. Compared with replacing captions by dense descriptions, VEPA is also different in supervision structure: the evidence is question-conditioned, not a static attempt to describe the whole image.
