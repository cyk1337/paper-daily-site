Under SDC, confidence is the posterior probability that the chosen answer is correct, conditioned on a latent decision variable that encodes noisy task-relevant evidence. The paper then builds around three experiment families.

First, single-layer isolation. Each transformer layer is trained independently under RL. Second, stability analysis. Mechanistically, the paper points toward a plausible interpretation: lower layers are tied more to lexical and local representation building, upper layers are closer to output formatting and surface realization, while middle layers are where RL can most effectively reshape reasoning transformations and decision boundaries. Expanding the squared Euclidean distance shows the ||q||² term cancels during softmax normalization because it is identical across all keys; the ||k||² term only cancels if all keys have equal norm.
