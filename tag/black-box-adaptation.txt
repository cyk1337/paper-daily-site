18:T7be,The method learns a vector δ over the vocabulary and adds it to the base model logits at every step, yielding πδ(yt|x,y<t)=softmax(l(x,y<t)+δ). Because δ is context-independent, it can only induce a global reweighting of token preferences; it cannot implement prefix-specific decisions. The theory starts from a KL-regularized RL objective with terminal reward r(x,y1:T). If arbitrary policies are allowed, the optimum is an exponential tilt of the base policy, equivalent to a prefix-dependent logit correction derived from soft value or advantage terms. The proposed fixed-bias policy is then treated as a restricted approximation to that optimum. The paper derives a reward-gap bound showing that the approximation error depends on how much the optimal correction varies across prefixes. In plain terms, the method should work when the desired correction is mostly a stable token-level preference, such as favoring EOS, concise endings, or formatting tokens, and should fail when the correction must depend strongly on local context.

On the estimation side, the paper derives a closed-form IPS-style estimator that uses black-box rollouts, sequence-level rewards, and token probabilities. No backpropagation through the model is needed. Operationally, the estimator compresses systematic token-level differences between high-reward and low-reward trajectories into one shared bias vector. The paper also compares full-vocabulary bias against more restricted parameterizations and argues that full-vocabulary control is more robust, because many useful behavior changes require coordinated reordering across groups of tokens rather than a few manually selected ones. Relative to prompt optimization, this consumes no context and acts directly on the output distribution. Relative to PEFT or fine-tuning, it is almost non-invasive. Relative to generic black-box RL, the policy class is much smaller, which improves stability and sample efficiency but also lowers the ceiling.19:T556,The experiments are organized around three questions. First, can a fixed logit-bias vector produce measurable gains at all? The paper reports improvements over the base model on math and reasoning benchmarks while using far fewer trainable parameters and much less compute than conventional fine-tuning. Second, where does the method help most? The empirical pattern is that it works better when the target behavior has a strong global token-bias component, such as verbosity control, formatting, stopping behavior, or small distribution shifts with recurring answer patterns. It helps less on tasks that require fine-grained conditional decisions. Third, do the empirical results match the theory? The ablations indicate that full-vocabulary bias is better than restricted token subsets, and that KL regularization plus the IPS estimator improves training stability. The setup appears to rely mainly on RLVR-style rewards, where prompts have verifiable answers and rewards are computed from correctness or related rule-based checks.
