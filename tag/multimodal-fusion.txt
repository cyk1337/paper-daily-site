First, the form of D. The paper focuses on explicit visual-semantic tokens rather than prompt rewriting in natural language or implicit latent queries. Within explicit tokens, it compares continuous visual features against discrete semantic tokens. For discretization, the authors study EMA-based tokenizers and embedding-based tokenizers, with and without auxiliary objectives such as MLLM understanding loss or pixel reconstruction loss. They also test hierarchical token structures inspired by recent VLM designs, but find limited gains for video generation.

Second, how the MLLM should generate those tokens. The paper compares causal attention and full attention over visual tokens. Full attention allows bidirectional interaction and may help holistic representation learning, but causal attention works better in practice for semantic-token generation. The reason is structural: these tokens are used for temporal planning, and autoregressive generation matches the sequential nature of video events.

Third, how the DiT should use the MLLM output. The authors compare latent bridging through learnable queries and connector modules against explicit visual-token conditioning.
