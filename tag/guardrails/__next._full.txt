19:T8a9,方法核心是一个分阶段课程式训练，把显式安全推理逐步内化成固定步数的 latent recurrence。Stage 0 先做显式 reasoning warm-up：模型输入 instruction、prompt、response，目标是生成结构化安全 rationale，再输出最终的 prompt/response 安全标签。这样做的目的不是为了部署时继续生成 rationale，而是先把“如何分析风险”这件事教会模型。后续阶段逐步把原本要生成的 rationale token 替换成 latent state，让模型在若干个隐式 recurrent step 中推进内部表征，再直接预测标签。

这里的关键难点不是 recurrence 本身，而是 hidden state 回灌后的输入分布不再像正常 token。作者指出 vanilla Coconut 式做法在 guardrail 场景下会遇到明显失配：模型预训练时见过的是词表嵌入空间中的离散 token 序列，而 latent recurrence 喂回去的是上下文相关的连续状态，语义锚点不稳定。为此他们引入 Context-Prediction Fusion：一部分保留 contextual hidden state，另一部分引入来自 vocabulary embedding space 的 predictive semantic guidance，相当于用“当前状态最可能对应哪些词义”的信息给 recurrent state 加一个词表语义锚。这样做不是把 latent step 再离散化成 token，而是在连续状态里混入预测语义，减少 recurrence 漂移。

训练上采用 stage-wise internalization curriculum。先让模型会显式推理，再逐步减少对显式 rationale token 的依赖，增加 latent step 的占比，直到推理时只保留固定预算的 latent computation 和最终标签解码。这个设计和直接端到端训练 latent reasoning 的区别在于：作者认为安全推理的监督信号比较稀疏，若一开始就完全隐式化，模型很难学到稳定的中间决策过程；先显式、后内化，能把 rationale 作为脚手架。全文还分析了 latent step 中表征如何演化，发现 CoLaGuard 的安全相关表示会沿 recurrent step 逐步偏移，而 vanilla Coconut 的这种渐进变化弱得多，这说明课程式内化确实让隐状态承担了“多步 deliberation”的功能，而不只是多跑几层无效计算。1a:T68b,CoLaGuard uses a stage-wise curriculum to internalize explicit safety reasoning into latent recurrent computation. In Stage 0, the model is trained as an explicit reasoning guardrail: given an instruction, a user prompt, and a model response, it generates a structured safety rationale and then predicts the final moderation labels. The rationale is used as training scaffolding rather than a deployment requirement.

In later stages, rationale tokens are progressively replaced by latent recurrent steps. Instead of decoding the reasoning trace, the model performs a fixed number of hidden-state updates and then directly predicts the safety labels. The central technical issue is that hidden-state recurrence creates an input distribution that differs from standard token embeddings seen during pretraining. Vanilla Coconut-style recurrence therefore becomes unstable or semantically weak in this setting.

To reduce this mismatch, the paper introduces Context-Prediction Fusion. The recurrent state combines contextual hidden-state information with predictive semantic guidance derived from the vocabulary embedding space. In effect, the model keeps the continuous contextual state but anchors it with token-space semantic predictions, which helps prevent drift during recurrence. The curriculum matters: the model first learns explicit safety reasoning, then gradually compresses that reasoning into latent steps. The paper also analyzes representation trajectories across latent steps and argues that CoLaGuard induces progressive safety-relevant state transitions, unlike weaker recurrence baselines where hidden states do not evolve in a meaningfully deliberative way.1b:T671,The evaluation spans 10 prompt- and response-moderation settings across 8 safety benchmarks. The main empirical claim is that CoLaGuard improves macro-F1 over classification-only guardrails while preserving much of the robustness of explicit reasoning guardrails, but with substantially lower inference cost because it avoids rationale generation. The relevant comparisons include single-pass classifiers, explicit reasoning guardrails, and vanilla latent-recurrence variants.

The ablations are more informative than the headline. Removing the stage-wise curriculum hurts performance, which supports the idea that explicit rationale supervision is needed as a scaffold before latent internalization. Replacing Context-Prediction Fusion with plain latent recurrence also degrades robustness and stability, consistent with the hidden-state distribution mismatch argument. The number of latent steps shows a budget-performance tradeoff rather than monotonic gains, suggesting that useful latent reasoning requires structured state evolution rather than simply more recurrent compute. The representation analysis further shows that safety-relevant features shift progressively across latent steps in CoLaGuard, which is the strongest evidence that the model is doing more than compressed classification.
