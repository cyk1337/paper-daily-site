9:T679,The work has two main components. First, it introduces the AgentMeter benchmark. Benchmark90 is the full 90-task validation set spanning realistic local workflows: code editing, repository inspection, shell usage, data processing, and document manipulation. Tasks are drawn from SkillsBench, Terminal-Bench, and DA-Code, then calibrated into 30 Easy, 30 Medium, and 30 Hard tasks. Core30 is a lower-cost subset used to compare more model–CLI configurations at scale. In the main setup, Core30 covers 24 complete configurations formed by 6 model families and 4 CLI families. A key design choice is to separate source provenance from effort tiers: difficulty tiers are calibrated independently rather than inherited from the original benchmark source.

Second, the paper proposes AgentMeter Score (AMS), a success-anchored, cost-aware, tier-calibrated metric. AMS is designed to combine three deployment-relevant signals: task quality, quality under cost budgets, and penalties for expensive failures. The tier structure matters because the desirable behavior changes with task effort. On Easy tasks, many configurations succeed, so efficiency becomes the main discriminator. On Medium tasks, the success–cost tradeoff dominates. On Hard tasks, avoiding long, expensive failed trajectories is part of what makes a pairing usable. AMS is meant to reflect this changing preference structure better than raw pass count, token/pass, or USD/pass alone.

The paper’s main methodological move is not a new agent algorithm but a redefinition of the evaluation unit. The main Core30 comparison evaluates 24 complete model–CLI configurations from a 6-by-4 grid. The authors then validate overlapping configurations on Benchmark90 to test whether the ranking structure persists on the larger set.
