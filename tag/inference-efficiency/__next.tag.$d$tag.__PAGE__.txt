For a given position, if the token is only committed at a later timestep, the model’s predictive distribution at that final commit timestep is treated as the teacher. Earlier-timestep distributions at the same position are then trained to match that teacher. The target is therefore not the ground-truth one-hot token directly, but the model’s own eventual decision.

This design has several notable properties. First, it is on-policy: the supervision evolves with the model, instead of being frozen in an offline teacher-generated dataset. Second, it is cross-time rather than cross-model distillation: teacher and student are the same model evaluated at different denoising stages. Third, it is tightly aligned with fast decoding. If early distributions already resemble the eventual commit distributions, confidence-based parallel unmasking becomes much safer.

From the full text, TSD is added on top of the standard masked diffusion objective as an auxiliary temporal distillation loss, likely implemented as a KL-style objective with stop-gradient on the teacher side.
