ActGuide-RL then injects action guidance as a reference plan. The model can use this guidance to navigate toward promising states, but the guidance is not meant to become a permanent crutch. The theory starts from a simplified policy-gradient objective without clipping or importance weighting and studies how predictive entropy changes after an update. The key result is that entropy change is negatively related to advantage, and the effect is amplified when the model is already highly confident. Intuitively, positive-advantage samples reinforce selected tokens, sharpen the distribution, and reduce entropy; negative-advantage samples do the opposite and tend to flatten the distribution. In high-confidence regions, this sharpening or flattening effect becomes stronger, which explains why entropy collapse can become self-reinforcing once the policy has already narrowed.

Entrocraft uses this result operationally. Instead of adding entropy regularization to the loss, it performs rejection sampling on rollout samples before the policy update. If the observed entropy is below the target schedule, it preferentially keeps samples whose advantages push entropy upward and filters out samples that would reduce entropy further. If entropy is above target, it does the reverse. In effect, it biases the advantage distribution so that the resulting gradient steers the policy toward the desired entropy level.
