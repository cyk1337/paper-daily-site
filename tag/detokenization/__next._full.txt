19:T9e3,方法上最关键的是数据构造和干预设计。作者不直接在自然文本里做相关性分析，而是利用“同一个词既可以被 tokenizer 表成单 token，也可以被人工拆成多个 subword”这一事实，构造 canonical form 与 artificial split form 的对照。然后定义 canonicity：比较 canonical 单 token 运行时该词位置的最终层 residual，与 split 运行时最后一个 subword 位置的最终层 residual 是否对齐。这个指标用来判断 split 后模型有没有在内部成功恢复出 canonical 词表示。

为了把 attention 与 MLP 的作用拆开，作者设计了两类配对样本。LST pair 共享最后一个 subword，不同的是前文子词；FST pair 共享第一个 subword，不同的是最后子词。这样可以分别隔离“跨位置传来的信息”和“本地末尾子词本身的贡献”。在这些配对上做 activation patching：把 source run 某层某位置的 attn_out 或 mlp_out 替换到 target run，再看 target 最终重建成哪个词。结果显示，在 Layer 1 的最后子词位置，patch attention 输出就足以把 target 的重建词拉向 source 的词，说明 attention 已经携带了决定性跨位置信号；而 patch MLP 输出则对应把这个信号与本地子词组合成更完整的词级表示。

论文提出的机制可以概括为：
1. 对于两到六个 subword 的词，真正发生组合的位置始终是最后一个 subword；
2. 如果词更长，中间 subword 位置主要充当 relay，把前面子词的信息逐步传到最后位置；
3. Layer 1 attention 负责传输 token-specific signal，这个信号较弱、线性可读，但已经足以决定后续会往哪个词收敛；
4. Layer 1 MLP 负责把该信号和末尾子词 embedding 做连续变换，得到更接近 canonical token 的表示；
5. 后续若干早层继续完成 detokenization，但关键起点已经在第一层建立。

作者还把分析扩展到 12 个 transformer LM，比较不同架构和位置编码。结论不是“所有模型都在同一层完成 detokenization”，而是 detokenization 集中在前 6% 到 30% 的层数区间，跨度主要由 positional encoding 决定。也就是说，位置编码如果更利于局部顺序信息在早层传播，detokenization 会更快完成；如果不利，则需要更多层。最后，作者在 activation patching 找到的关键深度上拟合一个线性方向，用早层激活预测最终 detokenization 成败，形成一个可操作的 probe。1a:T42f,The paper asks a precise mechanistic question: how do transformer LMs convert subword-fragmented input back into a usable word-level representation, and why does this process sometimes fail? Prior work had already suggested that detokenization happens in early-to-middle layers and involves both attention and MLPs, but the causal chain was still coarse. This work narrows it down substantially. Using controlled canonical-vs-split word pairs and activation patching, the authors show that English detokenization in Llama2-7B, and similarly in a broader set of transformer LMs, starts as a two-stage computation in Layer 1. A small set of attention heads first transmits a token-specific signal from earlier subwords to the final subword position; then the Layer 1 MLP combines that imported signal with the local subword representation to produce a continuous word-level representation. The paper also studies how this mechanism scales with word length, how it varies across architectures, and whether early-layer activations can predict eventual detokenization success.1b:T4df,This matters for pretraining research because it turns the tokenizer-model interface into something mechanistically inspectable rather than merely behaviorally observable. Tokenization artifacts are known to hurt robustness, arithmetic, and rare-token behavior, but this paper identifies where the model repairs the mismatch between subword input and word-level semantics. That is useful for thinking about tokenizer design, early-layer representation formation, and failure modes induced by poor segmentation. A second important result is that the depth over which detokenization unfolds is governed more by positional encoding than by model scale. That is a concrete architectural claim, not just an interpretability observation, and it suggests that early semantic composition depends on how positional information supports cross-token aggregation. Third, the paper provides a linear probe from early-layer activations that predicts final detokenization success with AUROC 0.94 on isolated inputs and 0.97 on natural text. That makes detokenization a measurable training signal. It could plausibly become a diagnostic for comparing tokenizers, monitoring training dynamics, or studying when word-level concepts become stable during pretraining.1c:Ta04,The core methodological contribution is the combination of a tightly controlled dataset construction with causal intervention. Instead of probing arbitrary natural text, the authors exploit words that can appear either as a single canonical token or as an artificially split sequence of subwords. They define a canonicity measure by comparing the final-layer residual stream at the canonical token position with the final-layer residual stream at the last subword position in the split run. High similarity means the model has successfully reconstructed the canonical word representation from fragments.

To separate the roles of attention and MLPs, they build paired examples with shared subwords. In one setup, pairs share the last subword but differ in earlier fragments, isolating the contribution of cross-position information. In another, pairs share the first subword but differ in the final fragment, isolating the contribution of the local final-subword identity. They then perform activation patching: replace attn_out or mlp_out from a source run into a target run at a specific layer and position, and observe which word the target ultimately reconstructs. This is the key causal move. In Layer 1 at the final subword position, patching attention output is already sufficient to redirect the target toward the source word, showing that attention carries the decisive imported signal. Patching the MLP output then shows how that signal is transformed together with the local final-subword representation into a more canonical word-level state.

The resulting mechanism is fairly crisp. For words split into two to six subwords, composition consistently happens at the last subword position. Intermediate positions mainly act as relays when the word is longer. Layer 1 attention transports a weak but linearly readable token-specific signal from earlier fragments. Layer 1 MLP composes that imported signal with the local subword embedding through a continuous transformation. Subsequent early layers continue the process, but the decisive initialization happens immediately.

The authors extend the analysis to 12 transformer LMs and compare architectures and positional encodings. Their claim is not that every model detokenizes in exactly one layer, but that detokenization is concentrated in the first 6% to 30% of layers, with the span largely determined by positional encoding. Finally, they fit a linear direction at the depth identified by patching and use it to predict whether detokenization will succeed, based only on early-layer activations.1d:T7c6,There are four experimental results worth retaining.

First, the mechanism localization result. In Llama2-7B, Layer 1 is already causally central. Patching Layer 1 attention output at the final subword position can change which word is reconstructed at the end of the network, while Layer 1 MLP output further sharpens that representation toward the canonical token. This supports a sequential division of labor: attention transports, MLP composes.

Second, the word-length generalization result. The authors test words split into 2 to 6 subwords and find that the final subword remains the composition site throughout. Longer words do not induce distributed semantic composition across all positions; instead, intermediate positions mostly relay information forward until the last position can assemble the word-level representation.

Third, the cross-model comparison. Across 12 transformer language models, detokenization is concentrated in the first 6% to 30% of layers. The important claim is that this depth tracks positional encoding more than parameter count. That weakens a simple scale-based explanation and points to an architectural determinant of early semantic aggregation.

Fourth, the predictability result. A linear probe trained on early-layer activations at the patching-identified depth predicts final detokenization success with AUROC around 0.94 on isolated contextless sequences and 0.97 on natural text. This suggests that success or failure is largely decided very early, and that the signal transfers beyond the synthetic split-word setup.

The paper also discusses failure cases where the imported early-layer signal is too weak or ambiguous, making later recovery incomplete. That aligns with known tokenization-related failures on undertrained tokens and difficult segmentations.
