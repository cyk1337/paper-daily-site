9:T6e7,DenMark replaces sentence-level candidate selection with rollout-guided semantic lookahead over local denoising updates.
The method has three main components:
1. Fixed region partitioning. The output sequence is divided into token regions that serve as the basic units for watermark injection and detection. These regions are not assumed to align perfectly with syntax; they provide a stable operational granularity during iterative denoising.
2. Temporary rollouts for semantic estimation. A local token update inside a DLM does not immediately define a complete semantic unit, so its watermark value cannot be scored directly. DenMark temporarily rolls out unresolved positions to form approximate completions, evaluates them with a key-dependent semantic scoring function, and estimates the expected watermark gain of each candidate local update. The update with the highest estimated score is then committed. In this way, watermark evidence is accumulated progressively throughout denoising rather than injected only after full sentence completion.
3. Calibrated scanning at detection time. Semantic attacks can shift token boundaries and break the original region alignment. To recover evidence under such shifts, the detector scans over multiple candidate region sizes and calibrates the resulting statistics.
The main conceptual difference from prior semantic watermarking is that DenMark does not force DLMs into an AR-style candidate-rejection loop. Instead, it works with the native iterative denoising process and moves semantic evaluation into local decision making. The paper also provides an analysis interpreting the final semantic score gain as the accumulation of many local choices, which helps explain why the method works beyond empirical results.a:T4ba,From the provided text, the experiments cover multiple DLMs and datasets, with the main comparison against token-level watermark baselines under semantic attacks. The headline claim is that DenMark preserves stronger watermark detectability after semantic-preserving rewrites, which is consistent with its design: the signal is encoded in meaning rather than surface token bias.
The excerpt does not include full result tables, so exact model names, AUC values, TPR@FPR numbers, attack-strength breakdowns, or rollout-cost figures cannot be stated reliably. Still, the experimental agenda is clear:
1. Demonstrate generality across more than one diffusion LM;
2. Compare robustness against token-level watermarks, especially under paraphrasing and semantic rewriting;
3. Measure how calibrated scanning recovers evidence under region-boundary shifts;
4. Analyze how rollout-guided local decisions accumulate into stronger final semantic watermark scores.
