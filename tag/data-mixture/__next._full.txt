18:T417,The paper compares standard pretraining, SPT, with parallel pretraining, PPT. In SPT, source documents and their translations are sampled as independent examples. In PPT, each document is concatenated with its translation inside the same 2,048-token training context. Each segment is prefixed with a language label such as English: or French:, and the language order is alternated so that both translation directions receive cross-lingual context. The objective remains ordinary autoregressive next-token prediction, with loss on both languages and no additional contrastive or translation-specific objective. The key mechanism is that when predicting the later segment, the model can attend to a semantically corresponding document in another language. The paper then evaluates two-stage schedules. PPT→SPT first builds cross-lingual use of parallel context and then adapts the model back to independently sampled documents. SPT→PPT tests the reverse order and helps isolate the importance of ending training on the target usage distribution.19:T444,The authors train 1.3B dense Llama 2-style models from scratch on TransWebEdu for English–French and English–Swahili. The language pairs cover a higher-resource and a lower-resource case. Single-stage SPT and PPT runs are trained for roughly 200B tokens. The two-stage comparisons use matched total budgets, including a reported 120B-token setting with a 20B-token second stage, and compare SPT-only, PPT-only, PPT→SPT, and SPT→PPT. PPT alone does not consistently improve general language understanding. Behavioral analysis shows that PPT models benefit more from cross-lingual context, but this advantage is not fully exercised by standard single-language tasks. PPT→SPT improves average general language understanding in both language settings and gives the best translation performance. Relative to SPT-only, BLEU improves from 19.25 to 34.03 on English–French, a 77% relative gain, and from 9.07 to 13.18 on English–Swahili, a 45% relative gain. Continued SPT improves monolingual language modeling while retaining an advantage in using cross-lingual context over SPT-only.1a:T433,The main empirical results are consistent and useful. More relevant data improves in-corpus performance, with large gains in Minecraft as the data budget grows. Mixture diversity improves transfer to related worlds but gives limited benefit on distant worlds, suggesting that virtual worlds share less stable structure than physical environments. Larger models are more data-efficient in the early phase where they learn self-control and action primitives, but under single-game training they can generalize worse. The authors describe a two-phase dynamic: an early interface phase for basic control and action primitives, followed by a world phase where additional gains are tied more closely to trained or related environments. An empirical scaling law over validation loss, data volume, mixture diversity, and model size reaches R²=0.982 on Minecraft loss and R²=0.995 on in-corpus loss. The models are also reported to be competitive with Minecraft specialists and general foundation models in 3D worlds, and up to 5× better than frontier models in real-time settings.1b:T46f,Africa-Papers is built via a four-pillar ingestion architecture designed to capture both research about Africa and research authored by Africa-affiliated institutions:
- DOAJ African journals: harvest full-text PDFs from 944 journals across 31 nations (74,606 PDFs reported).
- CGIAR/CGSpace: filter for verified bitstreams and extract 50,534 agricultural manuals, pest guides, soil maps, and extension documents.
- arXiv: from a 3.19M-paper snapshot, select preprints by topical/keyword focus on African contexts and/or Africa-based affiliations, yielding 42,530 full texts.
- OpenAlex: query the global scholarly graph for open-access works with African institutional affiliations and valid PDF URLs, indexing 1,517,281 candidate papers for streaming ingestion.
Pipeline highlights include streaming-to-parquet ingestion, storage-conscious binary pruning, layout-aware Markdown parsing to preserve structure (tables/headings/formulas), and multi-key deduplication. The excerpt explicitly mentions DOI normalization (matching against 235,461 known DOIs) and indicates additional stages (e.g., title slug) beyond the truncated section.1c:T689,The pipeline begins with outlet identification. The authors use State Media Monitor as the source of media organizations and their control labels. The original seven-level typology is collapsed into controlled versus independent for analysis, while the released dataset retains the finer labels. They then map media assets to web domains in two stages: an LLM is prompted to propose URLs in context, and humans manually verify or correct them. The resulting inventory contains 640 state media outlets, 1,910 assets, and 1,183 unique URLs spanning 173 countries.

Instead of crawling the web independently, the paper extracts pages directly from existing LLM pretraining corpora, namely English FineWeb and FineWeb-2. This design choice is important. It makes the dataset reflect content that models are plausibly trained on, rather than a generic snapshot of the web. It also enables year-by-year and language-by-language audits over a corpus that has already undergone quality filtering and deduplication.

The analysis has three layers. First, a corpus audit quantifies the prevalence of state media in English and multilingual web data, including temporal trends, language distributions, and the balance between controlled and independent outlets. Second, a content study uses Russian and Ukrainian state media as a case study, applying decontextualized fact extraction and information pursuit to compare coverage patterns and territorial narratives. Third, a system-level analysis places these sources into a RAG setup on the BorderLines territorial-dispute benchmark to test how retrieval over state media affects model responses across languages and target audiences.1d:T605,Four findings stand out. First, in English web crawls, the share of state media pages coming from editorially controlled outlets rises sharply over time, from 39% in 2013 to 93% in 2025. This suggests that the English-language state media slice entering open-web corpora has become increasingly dominated by more tightly controlled sources.

Second, in several low-resource languages, state media accounts for a nontrivial share of all web data. The paper reports values up to 17.8% for Uyghur. For multilingual pretraining, this is important because when the total amount of web text is small, source concentration can have a much stronger effect on the learned distribution.

Third, the Russia-Ukraine case study shows that the issue is not merely ideological disagreement. Russian English-language state media covers international affairs broadly, while Ukrainian state media is more focused on Ukrainian affairs. On topics such as Crimea, the extracted factual claims and narrative framing diverge in systematic ways.
