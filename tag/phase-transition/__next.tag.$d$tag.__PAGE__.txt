9:T5c0,The paper introduces Constraint Saturation Evaluation (CSE). Two design choices define it. First, every constraint must be deterministically verifiable, with no LLM judge in the loop. This keeps the evaluator stable exactly where composition becomes hard. Second, the benchmark varies the number of simultaneous constraints k systematically from 1 to 12, instead of testing only a few fixed compositions. CSE contains 36 constraint types across 8 processing dimensions, ranging from local lexical constraints to document-level structure, relational reasoning, and self-referential tasks. Each verifier returns both a binary pass/fail decision and a continuous compliance score in [0,1]. The benchmark construction also includes a composability vetting stage, which is important: a constraint that implicitly locks multiple output dimensions would distort high-k behavior and confound the analysis. The paper then analyzes three levels of behavior: per-constraint pass rate as k increases, probe-level joint success, and co-failure structure. The main empirical finding is that failures are only weakly correlated, with mean phi around +0.067, so joint success is well approximated by multiplying per-constraint success rates.
