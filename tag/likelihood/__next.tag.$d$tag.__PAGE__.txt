9:T467,The proposed method is likelihood-array regression, or LAR. For each target token, a fixed scoring language model evaluates the token under a sequence of nested left-context windows rather than only under the full prefix. These evaluations form a structured array indexed by context length and relative token position, with additional features derived from the model’s predictive distribution. Arrays from documents of different lengths are aligned onto a common domain. LAR-1 learns additive contributions from individual cells to the class log odds and aggregates them over tokens and context lengths. LAR-2 adds within-path second-order features for the same target token, including same-context quadratic terms and products across different context lengths. Basis expansions and random squared projections produce finite-dimensional representations, which are estimated with ridge logistic regression. Conditional entropy, and more generally data-model cross-entropy, can be expressed as the integral of its derivative with respect to the noise parameter. The nontrivial step is to turn the derivative of a joint quantity into something local and usable. The paper imports the EXIT/GEXIT machinery from coding theory and proves that for memoryless channels, the derivative decomposes into a sum of coordinate-wise local derivatives.

The main result is a mismatched conservation law for cross-entropy. Instead of assuming the denoiser equals the true posterior, the analysis allows a learned posterior family and shows that the global data-model CE is exactly the integral of local information derivatives along the noise axis. This yields several concrete consequences. One, the framework is channel-agnostic at the level of admissible memoryless corruption and recovers familiar special cases such as the Gaussian I-MMSE relation. Two, there is a locality property: the relevant derivatives depend only on marginal posteriors P(X_i | Y(τ)), not on the full joint posterior P(X | Y(τ)). This is a major simplification for high-dimensional sequence modeling. Three, the paper derives an implementation-oriented implication: it is sufficient to model an extrinsic posterior of the form P(X_i | Y_{-i}(τ)) and combine it with the known channel likelihood through a tilt. That connects the theory to practical token-wise denoisers and to leave-one-out style parameterizations. Four, the paper makes a sharp distinction between matched and mismatched regimes. In the matched limit, all admissible channels conserve the same CE. In finite-capacity settings, however, different channels define posterior families of different complexity, which leads to channel-dependent performance gaps.c:T68e,The experiments are designed to validate the finite-capacity claim rather than to optimize benchmark rankings.
