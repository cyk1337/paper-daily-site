The important design choice is to avoid rewarding generic non-answer behavior as if it were calibrated epistemic caution.18:T506,KARL combines (1) a Knowledge-Boundary-Aware Reward (KAR) and (2) a two-stage RL schedule.
1) KAR: an online knowledge-boundary estimator per query, implemented via sampling-based within-group response statistics. By sampling multiple responses for the same question and checking whether correct answers appear in a group, the method estimates whether the query lies within the model’s current competence region. Rewards are then adjusted dynamically: if the estimator suggests the model can answer correctly, correct answering is strongly rewarded and unnecessary abstention is discouraged; if the estimator suggests the model is unlikely to answer correctly, guided abstention is rewarded to avoid confident fabrication.
2) Two-stage RL: decouples boundary exploration from abstention calibration.
- Stage 1 (boundary exploration) uses a mixed reward biased toward accuracy to avoid the early abstention surge induced by static ternary rewards.
- Stage 2 (abstention calibration) converts persistent errors into abstentions while trying to preserve the accuracy gains from Stage 1.
