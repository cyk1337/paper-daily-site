The central design is to augment DINOv2 with a pixel-space depth reconstruction head, so the model keeps DINOv2’s self-distillation-based representation learning while also predicting pseudo depth maps. The key choice is what the paper does not do: it does not build an RGB-D encoder that requires depth at inference, and it does not follow the standard MAE-style route of feeding depth as an input modality. Instead, depth is used as auxiliary supervision during pretraining only. This matches the deployment constraint in surgery, where reliable depth input is often unavailable.

The motivation in the paper is comparative. Off-the-shelf DINOv2 and DINOv3 pretrained on natural images do not transfer well enough to surgical imagery. MAE-family methods, including RGB-D variants such as MultiMAE, also fail to close the gap in the authors’ setting; the paper explicitly notes that MultiMAE underperforms a single-modality MAE baseline in their surgical pretraining setup. That negative result is important because it justifies moving depth supervision into a DINOv2-style framework rather than continuing along the MAE path.

Technically, the training objective is a combination of the original DINOv2 objective and a depth reconstruction loss. The depth supervision comes from off-the-shelf pseudo-labeled dense depth rather than real depth sensors, which lowers the data barrier and makes the recipe scalable.
