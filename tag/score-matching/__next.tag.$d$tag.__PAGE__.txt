a:T515,The construction places a smooth inward superlinear perturbation in a remote region with negligible mass under the forward marginals. This keeps the on-path L2 score error arbitrarily small. It also keeps the learned continuous-time reverse process nonexplosive, with moments of all orders, and arbitrarily close to the exact reverse process in path-space total variation. The failure appears after explicit Euler–Maruyama discretization. Rare numerical trajectories can enter the remote perturbed region, and the explicit updates then repeatedly amplify their magnitude. The probability of such events is tiny, but the amplification dominates the probability cost, so all positive moments of the endpoint diverge. Weak convergence, and even convergence in probability, can coexist with divergence in every Wasserstein-Wp distance. The paper also shows that the same issue can occur in arbitrary dimension, for probability-flow ODEs, and even within a fixed finite GELU architecture. A positive result is obtained for bounded data: if the data support is contained in a known closed convex set, projecting the learned denoiser onto that set before forming the score yields grid-uniform moment bounds. For image diffusion, this gives a stability interpretation of clipping the predicted clean sample.b:T443,The paper formulates compositional generation over a perturbation space A and an outcome space X. Only a subset of conditions is observed, and the goal is to sample from an unseen condition whose density can be written as a geometrically weighted product of source densities. At the clean-data level, such a geometric product corresponds to a linear combination of score functions, which explains the common heuristic of adding conditional scores during denoising. The paper then separates two error sources. The first is inference-time approximation error: after forward noising, the clean-distribution score identity no longer holds exactly. Particle-based methods such as Feynman-Kac Correctors attempt to reduce this error by tracking importance weights along the reverse process. The second is score estimation error: typical samples from the composed target may lie in low-density regions of each source distribution, where the learned score is poorly constrained. The main claim is that this second error often dominates, and FKC-style correction can amplify it rather than repair it.c:T45c,The paper proposes a collapse-and-refine mechanism and instantiates it as Score-induced Latent Diffusion (SiLD):
- Stage 1 (low-noise, manifold learning): due to the score’s singular behavior at small noise, the induced denoising map rapidly collapses onto a projection to the data manifold. Using mean-field gradient flow analysis for two-layer networks, they show exponential decay of a geometric alignment risk.
- Stage 2 (moderate-noise, density-on-manifold learning): once geometry is captured, training refines the intrinsic density. They analyze this via random-feature regression on the manifold, deriving generalization bounds where excess risk scales polynomially with k and sample size n, independent of d.
- End-to-end sampling guarantee: decompose error into a manifold-regime term (k-dependent) and a high-noise term (polynomial in d but exponentially damped by integration time), avoiding the ambient curse.
