18:T4af,The authors formalize search as a candidate graph G(C,E), where nodes are partial candidates and directed edges encode dependencies. A candidate is represented as a set of literals, and each round expands and verifies leaf candidates. A binary verifier indicates whether a candidate remains consistent with the target. There are N agents, each verifying up to m candidates per round and sharing verification results with d neighbors. The key dynamical variable is A_t, the total number of active incorrect candidates across agents. When A_t decays to zero, the system reaches the absorbing solved state; when it grows or persists, the search process remains active and unsuccessful. The paper introduces four search types: needle, chain, hierarchical, and factorizable search. These classes differ in how much a verification result from one branch can prune other branches. The theoretical analysis derives task-dependent critical degrees d_c. Factorizable search benefits most from communication because local evidence can be reused across otherwise separate parts of the search space.
