9:T479,The method has two layers. First, the paper defines and estimates step advantage. A CoT is segmented into steps. For each step, the authors take the prefix up to that point and run Monte Carlo rollouts to sample continuations and final answers, estimating expected reward from that prefix. Comparing expected reward with and without the step gives the step’s advantage. This avoids prompt rewriting and avoids relying on manually designed perturbations; it measures marginal contribution directly on the original trajectory. Second, they turn noisy step-level estimates into analyzable structure. A changepoint-based procedure identifies whether a trace contains steps whose advantage confidently exceeds an effect-size threshold, separating informative from uninformative traces. They then ask whether judges can predict these labels or scores from text alone, using both prompted judges and critics fine-tuned on advantage-derived supervision. Performance is compared not only to simple baselines but also to a noise ceiling induced by rollout uncertainty, which is important for interpreting how much decodable signal is actually available.a:T504,The experiments span multiple generator models, datasets, and both thinking and non-thinking settings. Three findings stand out. First, important steps are sparse: many sentences that look like reasoning have near-zero advantage. The opening geometry example captures this well: two self-checking steps look similar in text, yet one changes success probability negligibly while another raises it from about 52% to about 94%. Second, judge performance is strongly asymmetric. Larger zero-shot judges beat a prevalence baseline, but remain far below the noise ceiling. Fine-tuned critics improve substantially and get close to the ceiling on incorrect traces, yet recover only a small share of the decodable signal on correct traces. This suggests that in successful reasoning, the state changes that matter are often not fully externalized in the text. Third, the paper uses advantage trajectories to study reasoning dynamics and finds that gains from scale and from thinking mode are driven mostly by stronger answer priors before the first reasoning step, rather than by the answer being discovered progressively during the CoT.
