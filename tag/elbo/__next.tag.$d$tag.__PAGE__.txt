从概率建模上，作者给出了这个多 mask 前向过程的精确定义，并推导出闭式 ELBO。训练目标可以理解为两部分：一部分是标准的 clean-token reconstruction，即从 xt 预测原始 token；另一部分是 intra-mask identification，即当位置已经落入 mask 子空间时，模型还要识别它属于哪个 mask。这个额外项不是装饰性的，它正对应 multi-mask 设计引入的信息结构：如果模型不学会区分不同 mask，终态熵虽然存在，但无法被有效利用。

论文另一个关键点是“如何做离散 consistency distillation”。连续扩散里可以借助 probability-flow ODE 把随机过程对应到确定性流，但离散 jump process 没有这么直接的对应物。作者的处理是构造 low-entropy shared-Gumbel coupling：在不同时间点共享 Gumbel 噪声，使得两条离散路径之间具有较强耦合，从而定义稳定的一致性训练目标。目标不是简单让不同时间的网络输出相同 token，而是让学生去逼近沿耦合路径的 future-filtration posterior，也就是给定未来噪声信息后对 clean token 的后验。这个定义比朴素 self-consistency 更严谨，因为它尊重离散随机过程本身的条件结构。

The contribution has three layers. First, MultiMDM replaces the single absorbing mask with a multi-mask subspace, preserving explicit noise markers while restoring nonzero terminal entropy. Second, it derives a closed-form ELBO/KL objective that remains native to the discrete setting, rather than relying on continuous relaxations. Third, it proposes a discrete consistency-distillation recipe based on shared-Gumbel low-entropy couplings and a future-filtration posterior target. That interface is highly relevant for anyone working on diffusion LMs, any-order LMs, or AR+diffusion hybrids.c:Tb06,The key idea is to replace the single mask token with a set of mask states. Let V be the normal vocabulary and M={m1,...,mK} be a set of mask tokens. Each clean token x0 is assigned a designated mask. In the forward process, a token does not jump directly toward one universal [M]; it is first biased toward its designated mask and only later mixes across the full mask subspace. This changes the geometry of the diffusion process in two useful ways. First, the terminal distribution is no longer a single deterministic all-mask sequence; it retains entropy over the mask subspace and can therefore serve as a meaningful starting point for few-step generation. Second, mask states remain separate from the normal vocabulary, so the model still knows which positions are corrupted. That preserves the clean/noise separation that makes MDMs stronger than uniform-state diffusion.

The probabilistic formulation is developed explicitly, and the resulting ELBO has a clean interpretation. One term is the usual clean-token reconstruction objective: predict the original token from a corrupted state. The second term is an intra-mask identification objective: once a position has entered the mask subspace, the model must also identify which mask it belongs to. This is not a cosmetic addition. It is exactly what allows the model to exploit the information carried by the multi-mask terminal state. Without learning to distinguish masks, the extra entropy would exist formally but would not be useful for generation.

The second major technical contribution is the consistency-distillation construction for the discrete setting. In continuous diffusion, one can often move from a stochastic SDE to a deterministic probability-flow ODE while preserving marginals. There is no direct analogue for finite-state jump processes. The paper addresses this by introducing a low-entropy coupling based on shared Gumbel noise across time points. This creates strongly coupled discrete trajectories and enables a stable consistency target. Importantly, the target is not a naive requirement that outputs at different times should match. Instead, the student is trained toward the future-filtration posterior over clean tokens along the coupled path. That is a more principled target because it respects the conditional structure of the discrete stochastic process.

A practical strength of the framework is the continual-training recipe. Because MultiMDM stays close to the MDM parameterization and training interface, a pretrained MDM can be adapted into a multi-mask model rather than retraining from scratch. Given the cost of diffusion-LM pretraining, this is a meaningful design choice. This produces two groups of latent states: K prologue positions h_p and N visual positions h_v, with defaults K=16 and N=256. The two groups are quantized separately, using a small codebook for prologue tokens and a larger one for visual tokens. Only visual tokens are sent to the decoder for image reconstruction, trained with standard L1, perceptual, and adversarial losses. Prologue tokens do not participate in reconstruction at all. The AR model then learns the concatenated sequence [z_p; z_v].

The crucial design choice is gradient routing. Using Prob-STE, the direct gradient from the AR cross-entropy is sent only to the prologue tokens, not to the visual tokens. This changes the optimization geometry completely. Instead of forcing reconstruction and AR losses to fight over the same variables, each objective gets its own token subset: z_v for reconstruction, z_p for AR-friendly summarization. Visual tokens can still be affected indirectly through the shared bidirectional encoder, but that path is much weaker. The paper also proposes Prologue-Post, where the visual tokenizer is fully frozen and the prologue is learned afterward. That variant is important because it shows the idea does not depend on joint end-to-end retraining.

The theoretical framing is built from the ELBO view of two-stage AR generation. In the standard setup, the prior must model the aggregate posterior marginal q(z_v), which is often complex and multimodal. With prologue tokens, the AR model first conditions on z_p and then predicts z_v, effectively replacing q(z_v) with q(z_v|z_p). If z_p captures information useful for predicting z_v, the conditional distribution is easier to model. The paper also addresses a natural concern: why would z_p not collapse into uninformative codes? The answer is that reconstruction is already handled by z_v, so collapsing z_p does not help reconstruction. At the same time, uninformative z_p cannot reduce AR loss.
