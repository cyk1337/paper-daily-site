18:T5cb,FAIR-Calib has two stages. Stage I is teacher probing. A full-precision teacher is used to estimate a position-aware prior that combines two signals: whether a position is at the write frontier, and how reliable the teacher is at that masked-stage position and timestep. Intuitively, positions that are both frontier-exposed and intrinsically unstable should receive more protection during calibration. The authors argue that this prior is mechanism-driven and therefore transfers reasonably across calibration corpora. Stage II is off-policy, layer-wise calibration. Instead of running expensive end-to-end diffusion rollouts with the quantized model, the method uses a teacher-forcing surrogate and aligns hidden states layer by layer under teacher trajectories. The loss is a reweighted hidden-state MSE, where the weights come from Stage I. This means calibration no longer treats all positions and timesteps equally; it explicitly prioritizes fragile frontier states. The paper also motivates an additive time-by-position weighting scheme and explains why weighted hidden-state MSE is a reasonable surrogate for preserving commit stability under mild assumptions. At the quantizer level, the implementation follows a FlatQuant-style learnable affine reparameterization to flatten weight and activation distributions before symmetric uniform quantization. The novelty is therefore not a new quantizer, but a dLLM-specific calibration target and an efficient off-policy procedure.19:T4d8,The experimental story is built around both end-task quality and mechanism-level diagnostics. The main setting is W4A4 PTQ on diffusion LLMs such as Dream and LLaDA. FAIR-Calib consistently outperforms naive transfers of AR-LLM PTQ baselines across multiple benchmarks, with the largest benefit on harder reasoning tasks where dLLMs are more brittle under perturbation. More importantly, the paper reports improvements on three internal metrics: fewer teacher-forced commit-step flips, reduced post-commit mismatch including both mean-disagree and more severe never-agree cases, and flatter probability-MSE traces showing weaker stepwise error amplification after a false commit. The provided excerpt does not include the full numeric tables, but the full text indicates several meaningful ablations: removing frontier-aware weighting, removing reliability gating, or dropping the off-policy weighted calibration each hurts performance. This suggests the gains come from the full two-stage design rather than a single heuristic.
