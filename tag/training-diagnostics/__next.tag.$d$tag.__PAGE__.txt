9:T53f,The method has a diagnostic part and an intervention part. For diagnosis, the authors define Advantage Collapse Rate, the fraction of training batches whose group-relative advantages are fully or nearly degenerate. In GRPO, advantages are computed by standardizing rewards within a group. If all rewards are identical, the group mean equals each reward and the group standard deviation is zero, so every advantage becomes zero. Near-homogeneous rewards lead to the same problem in practice. ACR is cheap because it reuses statistics already computed during GRPO training.

For mitigation, they introduce Adaptive Virtual Sample Policy Optimization (AVSPO). The key idea is not to redesign the policy objective or add a critic, but to inject virtual reward samples when collapse is detected, so that a non-zero relative comparison signal can still be formed. This creates usable advantages for groups that would otherwise be gradient-dead. The paper emphasizes that AVSPO is plug-and-play: no extra response generation and no additional LLM forward passes are required. Compared with critic-based PPO or GAE, it avoids a value network; compared with process reward models, it does not require step-level supervision; compared with entropy regularization, it directly targets reward homogeneity rather than applying generic exploration pressure.a:T43d,For a quick read, focus on three parts. First, read the problem setup carefully to distinguish this from mode collapse or reward hacking; the paper is about zeroed group-relative advantages under homogeneous rewards. Second, inspect the ACR definition and the correlation plots linking early ACR to final performance; that is the most informative part of the paper. Third, read the AVSPO algorithm section with one question in mind: how virtual samples alter advantage computation without requiring extra model passes.

If considering implementation, then read the ablations on model scale, benchmark variation, and out-of-domain behavior.
