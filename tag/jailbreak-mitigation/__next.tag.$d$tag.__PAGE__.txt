For the same inputs, the method records key and value activations from both the frozen model and the DSA-perturbed model. Their token-level residuals are stacked per layer and head, and independent SVDs on K and V residuals produce low-rank harm-calibrated displacement subspaces. The method decomposes activation residuals rather than adapter weights because the realized KV shift depends on token states, modality, and context. After subspace extraction, the DSA is discarded. Stage 2 projects the KV cache away from these subspaces and learns lightweight residual repairs to recover useful information lost by projection. The repair is constrained by key-side structural grounding, a reconstruction penalty, and query-side separation so it does not reconstruct the suppressed directions. At inference time, SPARK computes the energy of each head's current KV state in the learned harmful subspace and derives a closed-form mixing coefficient g_h* between the original memory and the projected-and-repaired memory. This coefficient is not a learned probability of harmfulness; it is a representation-energy-based intervention strength.
