9:T853,The method is NC-GRPO, Noise-Contrastive GRPO. For each rollout group, half of the branches are standard GRPO rollouts, while the other half receive Gaussian noise injected into the last hidden layer during the prompt-encoding prefill pass. The noisy branches therefore start generation from displaced latent states. If a branch still reaches the correct answer despite the displacement, it is reinforced relative to branches that fail, so sensitivity to that latent perturbation is converted into policy-gradient signal through group-normalized rewards.

Several design choices matter.

First, the noise is injected only once during prefill, not throughout decoding. The paper is careful about this distinction: it changes the departure state of the rollout, but does not keep perturbing the cached representation during generation. This keeps decoding cost identical to standard GRPO and makes attribution cleaner.

Second, the noise scale is calibrated to the hidden-state norm. The perturbation magnitude is proportional to the norm of the original hidden vector, so sigma becomes a dimensionless relative scale rather than an architecture-dependent absolute one. The paper also shows that the relative perturbation magnitude concentrates with variance 2/d, which means the actual perturbation size is tightly controlled in high-dimensional hidden spaces.

Third, the noise follows an annealing schedule: stronger exploration early, decaying to zero later. This mirrors prior noisy-rollout schedules, but moves the perturbation from pixel space into latent space.

Fourth, the paper includes antithetic and steered probes. These are designed to test whether the gain comes from simply adding more noise budget or from creating independent stochastic branches. The conclusion is that independent stochastic diversity is the active ingredient, not a specific direction or just larger perturbation energy.

Methodologically, this is a clean intervention. The GRPO objective, reward, decoding, and evaluation are kept fixed as much as possible, so the observed differences can be attributed mainly to the diversification mechanism.a:T6f9,The experiments focus on multimodal mathematical reasoning and hallucination robustness. The paper reports significant gains over vanilla GRPO on five out-of-domain multimodal math benchmarks, with pooled McNemar significance ranging from p=0.001 to p<1e-4. It also reports improvements on in-domain accuracy, suggesting that latent perturbation is not merely acting as domain-shift regularization.

Compared with image-space noise, NC-GRPO performs better on hallucination robustness: significant on POPE with p=0.007, and +0.6 points on HallusionBench. This matters because it suggests latent diversification is not just making outputs more varied; it may be encouraging policies that are more stable to perturbations in internal representations. It also highlights a limitation of pixel-space noise: it can only diversify along directions expressible by the visual encoder, whereas latent noise acts after multimodal fusion.

The mechanism studies are the most informative part. Matched antithetic and steered probes indicate that the benefit comes mainly from independent stochastic branching rather than a particular perturbation direction or total noise budget. The noise-scale study is also useful: sigma_0 acts as a specialization dial. Larger noise tends to favor reasoning gains but can trade off against general capability, while sigma_0=0.2 is reported as a regime that improves reasoning without measurable loss in general capability.
