17:T785,The paper introduces two variants. The base version is a Dirichlet-process cache. For each incoming representation, the model compares it to existing memory-slot keys. If the nearest slot is farther than a threshold, it allocates a new slot; otherwise it merges the input into the nearest slot and updates the corresponding key/value. This is the DP-means rule, the small-variance MAP limit of a Dirichlet-process mixture, but used here as an online write operator rather than latent-variable inference. Readout remains content-based, so the model retains some of the retrieval behavior of attention while avoiding per-token storage.

The second variant is a surprise-adaptive Dirichlet-process cache. A fixed concentration works for stationary streams, but not when novelty arrives in bursts. The paper therefore adapts the concentration using a surprise signal derived from recent allocation rate. When novelty rises, the cache becomes more willing to open new slots; when the stream becomes predictable again, the effective budget tightens and memory consolidates. The two variants share the same allocate-on-novelty and read mechanism. They differ only in whether concentration is fixed or dynamically scheduled.

The distinctions from prior work are carefully drawn. This is not clustered or routing attention, which approximates full softmax within a bounded context and usually uses a fixed number of clusters. It is not Memorizing Transformer, kNN-LM, or other external-memory systems that still store token-level entries. It is also not fixed-budget sparse caching such as StreamingLLM or H2O, where capacity is controlled by eviction. The closest family is novelty-gated memory such as Titans or EM-LLM, but here novelty is defined by a DP-means-style distance threshold rather than a purely learned write gate.
