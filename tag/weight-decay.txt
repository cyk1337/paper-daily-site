For weights feeding into normalization layers such as BN or LN, rescaling the weight vector may leave the network function largely unchanged. The loss is therefore insensitive to radial scaling, but the curvature is not. When the norm of such a weight block becomes small, the relevant Hessian eigenvalues can be amplified, roughly following the inverse scale of the norm. Weight decay supplies a persistent shrinkage force, so increasing the decay coefficient drives these blocks toward a high-sharpness region. The paper then defines a weight-norm criticality indicator combining local Hessian information and the learning rate. A practical feature is the component-wise decomposition: one can evaluate the restricted Hessian for a specific scale-invariant block instead of the full parameter Hessian. This makes the criterion applicable to architectures with many normalization modules and allows attribution of instability to individual layers.19:Ta5f,论文先做“理解”，再做“改造”。

第一部分是必要性与机制分析。作者先在 0.12B 级别 Llama 预训练中实证比较带 γ 与去掉 γ 的模型，发现即便 γ 参数量几乎可以忽略，去掉后训练明显变差。理论上，他们分析 Pre-Norm 结构中 Norm(x)⊙γ 后接线性映射 W 的组合，指出在 Pre-Norm 下，γ 的作用可以被吸收到后续线性层中，因此它不增加函数类表达能力；但在梯度流上，γ 会改变后续线性层不同方向的有效步长，形成 self-amplifying preconditioning。直观地说，某些坐标上的 γ 变大，会放大这些方向进入线性层的激活，也会相应改变这些方向的梯度更新，从而进一步强化有利方向的学习速度。这解释了为什么一个很小的向量会持续影响训练动态。

第二部分是 weight decay 的区分分析。作者把 Norm 层分成 Input-Norm 和 Output-Norm。所谓 Input-Norm，是 γ 后面立刻接线性层，典型就是 Pre-Norm；Output-Norm 则是 γ 更直接地决定输出表示本身。理论结论是两类 γ 的 wd 作用相反：对 Input-Norm γ，适度 wd 有利，因为它能诱导更平衡的动力学，控制 Hessian sharpness，提升稳定性和收敛速度；对 Output-Norm γ，wd 会压缩其表达自由度，因此有害。这个结论很实用，因为很多训练代码里对 norm 参数是否做 wd 一直没有统一共识，论文给出了结构相关而不是经验拍脑袋的答案。

第三部分是三类改进。
1）Heterogeneity：在标准 Transformer 里，一个共享的 Pre-Norm 输出会同时喂给 Q/K/V 以及其他分支，但这些分支的训练动态并不相同。作者为不同分支引入 branch-specific scale vectors，让每个分支拥有自己的预条件器，而不是共享一套 γ。
2）Placement：标准 Pre-Norm 的 γ 放在线性层输入侧，主要提供 row-wise 的预条件效果。作者提出新的放置方式，使其同时产生 row-wise 与 column-wise preconditioning，从而更充分地改善后续线性映射的优化几何。
3）Reparameterization：作者把 γ 做 magnitude-direction 形式的重参数化。理论上，这会诱导更强的各向异性预条件效果，让优化更快地沿有用方向推进，而不是被单一尺度约束。

The central questions are threefold. First, does gamma actually matter for pretraining despite being a negligible fraction of the parameters? Second, in Pre-Norm Transformers, is its benefit about expressivity or about optimization dynamics? The paper’s answer is clear: scale vectors matter a lot for LLM pretraining; in Pre-Norm architectures they do not increase expressivity; their main role is to act as a self-amplifying preconditioner for subsequent linear layers; and this understanding leads to several low-cost design improvements that consistently reduce terminal loss.1b:T497,This is a meaningful pretraining paper because it turns a widely used but poorly understood architectural detail into a principled optimization object. The contribution is not a large new module. It is a careful explanation of why a tiny parameter vector can have a persistent effect on training dynamics, and how to redesign it in a way that scales.

There are three reasons it matters for foundation-model research. First, it is a reminder that important training behavior often comes from small parameterization choices rather than headline architectural blocks. Those choices can affect conditioning, Hessian sharpness, anisotropy, and effective learning rates throughout training. Second, it gives a new lens on normalization in Pre-Norm LLMs: gamma should be viewed less as an expressivity component and more as a learnable preconditioner. Third, the proposed modifications add almost no compute or parameter overhead, yet they improve terminal loss across dense and MoE models, from 0.12B to 2B, under AdamW and Muon, and across multiple learning-rate schedules. The first stage establishes necessity and mechanism. Empirically, the authors compare standard Llama-style pretraining with and without scale vectors and show that removing gamma substantially hurts training, despite gamma accounting for a negligible parameter fraction. Theoretically, they analyze the composition Norm(x) ⊙ gamma followed by a linear map W in Pre-Norm architectures. Their key result is that gamma does not enlarge the function class in Pre-Norm settings, because its effect can be absorbed into the following linear layer. So the gain is not expressivity. Instead, gamma changes the effective optimization geometry of the subsequent linear map. It rescales coordinates before the linear transformation, which in turn changes gradient magnitudes and update speeds across directions. This creates a self-amplifying preconditioning effect: directions that become larger through gamma can receive stronger updates, which further reinforces useful learning directions.

The second stage studies weight decay. The paper distinguishes Input-Norm from Output-Norm layers depending on whether the scale vector is immediately followed by a linear map. This distinction is important. For Input-Norm scale vectors, including standard Pre-Norm layers, weight decay is beneficial because it promotes more balanced dynamics and controls Hessian sharpness, improving both speed and stability. For Output-Norm scale vectors, weight decay is harmful because those scale vectors directly affect expressivity and should not be unnecessarily constrained. This is a practical result with immediate recipe implications.

The redesign stage introduces three complementary modifications.
1) Heterogeneity: in standard Transformer blocks, one shared normalized representation feeds multiple projections such as Q, K, and V, even though these branches have different training dynamics. The paper assigns branch-specific scale vectors so each branch gets its own preconditioner.
2) Placement: in standard Pre-Norm, gamma sits on the input side of the next linear map and mainly induces row-wise preconditioning. The paper proposes alternative placements that provably induce both row-wise and column-wise preconditioning, improving optimization more fully.
3) Reparameterization: gamma is rewritten in magnitude-direction form. The analysis argues that this yields a more anisotropic preconditioner and can accelerate optimization further.
