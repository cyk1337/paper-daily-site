c:T6ff,The method is simple and fairly clean. For a decoder-only transformer, at each layer and token position the authors extract three internal states: the attention write a^l, the FFN write m^l, and the updated residual stream x^l. Each state is projected into vocabulary space using the model’s own unembedding matrix together with the final normalization layer, following a logit-lens-style readout. The Shannon entropy of each resulting distribution is then computed, producing H_a^l, H_m^l, and H_x^l for every layer. Concatenating these values across depth yields the TriLens feature, which is fed to a lightweight probe for hallucination detection.

The underlying hypothesis is that hallucinations are often accompanied by slower sharpening or persistent uncertainty in internal pathways. When contextual routing and parametric memory agree, the corresponding vocabulary distributions should sharpen with depth. When they disagree or support competing continuations, entropy remains higher or decays more slowly. Measuring entropy separately on MHSA and FFN writes is important because the residual stream alone mixes these sources. The pathway split is meant to expose whether context-derived evidence and parametric recall converge or diverge during generation.

Two comparisons are especially informative. First, the paper tests an explicit intra-layer module-disagreement feature and finds that it adds little, suggesting that most useful signal is already present in the entropy trajectories themselves. Second, it compares against DoLa-style cross-layer contrast features and finds that simple per-layer entropy captures much of the same signal. The contribution is therefore not a more elaborate probe, but a more structured and lower-dimensional summary of internal uncertainty.d:T5f2,The evaluation covers three instruction-tuned LLMs and four benchmark settings, for 12 model-benchmark cells in total. TriLens improves over the strong white-box baseline ICR Probe on all 12 cells, with an average AUROC gain of 12.1, and achieves the best AUROC in 11 of the 12 cells. That is the main empirical claim: a compact 3L-dimensional entropy trajectory can outperform stronger-looking probes built on richer hidden-state features.

The ablations are the most useful part of the paper. First, MHSA, FFN, and residual-stream entropies each contribute useful signal, and the combination works better than any single pathway, supporting the claim that the pathways are complementary rather than redundant. Second, adding explicit intra-layer disagreement features yields little extra benefit, which suggests that the entropy traces already encode most of the relevant instability. Third, in a head-to-head comparison with DoLa-style cross-layer contrast features, TriLens matches or exceeds them with a simpler statistic, implying that much of the useful detection signal lies in when and where intermediate distributions sharpen.

The layer-wise analysis is also informative. The depth at which discrimination peaks varies systematically across architectures and benchmarks, indicating that different models schedule contextual integration and parametric recall differently across layers.
