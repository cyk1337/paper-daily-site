The squared norm contains a quadratic term, ε²/d · ||∑r_n||². In an ordinary non-shared residual stack, different residual branches are approximately independent, so this term grows like Θ(ε²N), which motivates ε=1/√N. In a looped network, the same W generates all updates. The activations are produced by the same recurrence, the residual directions remain positively correlated, and the quadratic term grows like Θ(ε²N²). Keeping the residual stream bounded therefore requires εN=O(1), i.e. ε=1/N. The same accumulation appears in the optimization analysis: the output change after one optimizer step scales as ηεN, so ε=1/N makes the stable learning rate roughly independent of N. For a practical block with L unique layers repeated N times, the paper separates two effects: a linear N factor from within-layer reuse and a √L factor from across-layer random-walk accumulation.
