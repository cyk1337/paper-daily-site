9:T597,The setup treats a benchmark as a fixed list of M questions. For each question, generated answers have a verifier score S and a correctness bit Y. At budget k, the selected answer is the one with the highest verifier score among k samples, and the curve reports the average correctness over the fixed question list. The audit may adaptively choose questions, extend answer paths, query correctness labels, and stop when it can return a simultaneous confidence band with half-width ε and failure probability δ. The analysis counts three resources: generated answers, correctness queries, and question visits. For a fixed list, the main cost law for certifying all budgets up to K is, up to logarithmic factors, K/ε + min(M, ε^{-2}) + s/ε^2. The three terms correspond to rare high-scoring answers at large budgets, the identity of the benchmark questions, and within-question variance accumulated along the curve. Matching lower bounds show that these terms are intrinsic. A key technical device is a coupling of nested winners on one answer path, which lets a single path serve all budgets. The practical algorithm is a paired audit: each round draws two fresh paths per question, uses an exponential inequality for paired Bernoulli draws to build anytime-valid simultaneous bands, and then applies a corrected exact-binomial final look to stop at a fixed round. Combined with a multilevel audit, it attains near-minimax cost.a:T45b,The empirical study uses 185 held-out score pools. The curves often plateau early: the median curve is within 1/32 of its best value by k=10, so small differences matter. On 100 MMLU-Pro questions, a fixed exact-binomial design needs 192,000 generated answers to certify all budgets up to K=64 within ±1/32 at 95% confidence. By contrast, one path of 64 answers per question uses only 6,400 answers and gives unbiased estimates of all points, but does not provide a sufficiently tight simultaneous guarantee. At budget 64, roughly three quarters of the variance in selected-answer correctness comes from between-question variation, which explains why balanced revisits to every fixed question can save cost. In experiments, the paired audit uses 0.74, 0.66, and 0.53 times the generated answers of the cheapest competing certified audit at K=64, 256, and 1024. It is cheaper on all eight generator-benchmark answer sets, misses none of 3,255 runs against exact curves, and its cost follows the three-term theory closely with R^2=0.995.
