The authors first run systematic block-size sweeps and show two empirical facts: the sample-wise optimal block size is not constant, and for most samples it lies near the training block size rather than anywhere in a large global action space. This motivates a local discrete classification problem instead of online search or a heavier RL-style controller.

For state representation, BlockPilot does not consume the full hidden-state sequence or add a complex sequence encoder. Instead, it uses the target model’s predictive distribution for the final token after prefilling. The paper argues that this distribution already aggregates full-context information through autoregressive attention, so it captures both local uncertainty and the stability of the upcoming continuation. The action space is then defined as a small neighborhood around the training block size, which keeps labeling and deployment simple.

A notable design choice is how conservative the integration is. The method does not modify the target model, the diffusion draft model, or the verification procedure. It only decides which block size to use before decoding starts. In other words, the gain comes from policy selection on top of an existing speculative decoding stack, not from improving draft quality itself.

The paper also leans heavily on the locality observation. If optimal block sizes were globally scattered, policy learning would be noisy and brittle. Third, it reports end-to-end gains on top of existing diffusion-based speculative decoding.
