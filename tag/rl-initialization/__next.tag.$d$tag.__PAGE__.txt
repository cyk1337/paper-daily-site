9:T445,The central concept is strategic diversity: substantive variation in how a problem is approached, not lexical variation or embedding-space distance. The paper proposes two ways to generate strategically diverse self-training data using the model itself. The first adapts Verbalized Sampling: the model lists multiple possible approaches with estimated probabilities, and each approach is then used as a hidden instruction for generating a full response. The second method, Groot, asks the model to build a hierarchical decision tree over approaches and samples distinct root-to-leaf paths. This is natural for competitive programming, where solutions branch by algorithm, data structure, and implementation choices. For story planning, where narrative decisions are interdependent and less hierarchical, the unstructured VS-style approach is more suitable.
