9:T583,The setup is deliberately controlled. The base stack is a decoder-only, LLaMA-style transformer trained on TinyStories. Everything is held fixed except the FFN module, which is replaced by a Mixtral-style MoE block in the sparse variants. The main sparse recipe uses 4 experts, top-2 routing, dropless dispatch, Switch-style load balancing, and optional router z-loss. The authors explicitly avoid token dropping and capacity truncation so that the comparison stays focused on routing behavior rather than execution shortcuts. The training objective combines next-token cross-entropy with a load-balancing auxiliary loss and an optional router z-loss; the best sparse recipe uses lambda_bal = 1e-2 and lambda_z = 1e-3. Fairness is evaluated in two ways. The active-match dense baseline is width-resized to approximately match the MoE’s per-token active parameter count. The total-match dense baseline is resized to match the MoE’s full stored parameter count. The headline configurations are: dense active-match at about 14.89M parameters, MoE at about 21.06M total and 14.77M active parameters, and dense total-match at about 21.06M parameters. Matching is tight, with less than 1% error for active counts and less than 0.1% for total counts.
