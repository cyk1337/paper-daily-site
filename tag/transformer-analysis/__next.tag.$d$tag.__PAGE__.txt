5:T6a0,The paper models a token sequence as a vector field over the token-position graph. Each token position carries a feature fiber, and an attention update is written as a connection walk: a nonnegative walk matrix aggregates messages from neighbors, while each edge applies a linear transport map that moves features from the source fiber to the target fiber. Within this formulation, single-head attention is proved to be exactly a connection propagation step with constant transport. The walk is determined by attention weights, while the transport is shared across edges through the value/output linear maps.

The more important result is for multi-head attention. The paper shows that standard MHA can be rewritten exactly as a single edge-dependent connection walk. The effective transport on each edge is not fixed; it is an attention-gated mixture of the headwise transports induced by the value/output maps of different heads. This is the key structural clarification: MHA should not be interpreted only as separate heads followed by concatenation, but as one coupled operator whose edgewise transport depends on the attention pattern.

The paper then studies when the corresponding generator reduces to a random-walk connection Laplacian. This requires additional conditions, such as suitable normalization of the walk and constraints that make the layer update behave like a discrete propagation step. When those conditions hold, the layer dynamics align with diffusion on vector fields; when they do not, the operator is more general, potentially directed, asymmetric, and reaction-like. The discussion also connects this decomposition to advection-diffusion-reaction intuitions in depth.6:T61b,The method has three parts. First, the paper formalizes geometric continuity using the absolute cosine similarity between adjacent layers’ top right singular vectors v1 or top left singular vectors u1, and adds a sigma-squared-weighted all-spectrum variant to distinguish head-direction alignment from broader subspace alignment. Second, it runs controlled ablations on a 16-layer, width-256 residual MLP. The main finding is that residual connections make cumulative gradients across layers point in similar directions, so weight updates accumulate coherently across depth. But this alone is insufficient if the model retains rotational symmetry: layers can still drift independently within equivalent rotated coordinate systems. Activations and normalization matter because they break that symmetry. A particularly clean experiment introduces a nonlinear yet rotation-preserving activation and shows that it fails to produce continuity, isolating symmetry breaking rather than nonlinearity per se as the operative factor. Third, the paper maps the mechanism back to transformers. Q, K, Gate, and Up read from the residual stream and therefore show strong input-space continuity; O and Down write back to the residual stream and therefore show strong output-space continuity; V lacks a nearby symmetry-breaking interaction and exhibits much weaker continuity. The paper also distinguishes the roles of activation and LayerNorm: activation tends to concentrate continuity in the leading singular direction, while LayerNorm spreads it across multiple directions.7:T52d,Experiments are split between toy MLPs and small transformers. The toy setup is a residual MLP with hidden size 256 and depth 16 trained on MNIST. The authors ablate residual connections, activations, normalization, and related factors, then track adjacent-layer singular-vector similarity and effective rank. The main empirical results are: residual connections are necessary because they substantially increase cross-layer similarity of cumulative gradients; standard activations and LayerNorm both break rotational symmetry but in different ways, with activations concentrating alignment in the top singular direction and LayerNorm distributing it more broadly; and a rotation-preserving nonlinear activation does not sustain continuity. The transformer experiments on WikiText-103 reproduce projection-specific continuity patterns, and the paper further reports the same structure in pretrained Llama-3.1-8B: Q/K/Gate/Up show high input-space continuity, O/Down show high output-space continuity, and V remains weak. The most informative direct ablation removes the MLP activation, dropping Gate v1 continuity from 0.66 to 0.37 with only about a 3% perplexity change. That result is important because it shows continuity is not just a side effect of overall model quality; it is tied to specific architectural
