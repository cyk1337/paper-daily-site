18:T49b,The authors formulate recursive retraining as a distributional dynamical system. At each iteration, K candidates are sampled from the current model distribution p_t. One candidate is selected by a Bradley-Terry choice rule under a sampled reward, and retraining is idealized as unconstrained maximum likelihood, so p_{t+1} becomes the selected-sample distribution. In the large-candidate regime, the BT selection update becomes an exponential tilt of p_t by exp(r_i(x)), followed by normalization. With pluralistic preferences, the reward is sampled from a fixed mixture, for example with weights q and 1-q for two rewards. The analysis defines near-optimal basins for the rewards and proves that, under an outside-basin gap condition, mass outside the union of these basins decays. Once mass is concentrated inside the basin union, the final split is governed by leakage: how much one reward also reinforces the other reward’s basin. As leakage vanishes under stronger separation, the basin weights approach the reward sampling weights.
