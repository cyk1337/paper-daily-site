19:T5c9,HRM-Text is built on a Hierarchical Recurrent Model with two timescales. The fast L module performs local iterative refinement, while the slow H module maintains a more stable semantic state. In the described forward pass, the model runs two high-level cycles; each cycle applies three L-module updates followed by one H-module update. Token logits are produced from the final H state. This reuses parameters across recurrent steps, increasing effective computational depth without increasing parameter count proportionally.

The difficult part is making deep recurrence trainable for language modeling. The paper introduces MagicNorm and warmup deep credit assignment. At the beginning of training, gradients are backpropagated only through the final two recurrent steps; later this window expands to the final five steps. This reduces the optimization burden early on and gradually exposes the model to longer credit-assignment paths. The modules also use parameterless RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, and sigmoid-gated self-attention.

The training objective departs from standard causal LM pretraining. HRM-Text is trained from scratch on instruction-response pairs, optimizing negative log-likelihood only on the response, i.e. P(answer | instruction). It uses a PrefixLM mask: instruction tokens can attend bidirectionally to one another, while response tokens remain causal.
