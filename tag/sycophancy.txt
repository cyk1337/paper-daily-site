For each question, the policy first produces an initial solution y0. It then receives critic feedback c and generates a critic-stage solution y1 conditioned on the question, the initial answer, and the feedback. A judge labels both y0 and y1 as correct or incorrect, and the pair is mapped into one of four transition types: Correction (wrong to right), Sycophancy (right to wrong), Robustness (right to right), and Boundary (wrong to wrong). Training assigns calibrated rewards to these quadrants: strong positive reward for Correction, strong penalty for Sycophancy, weak positive reward for Robustness, and weak penalty for Boundary.

The design choice is deliberate. If one only rewards final correctness, the model can improve by becoming more willing to change its answer, which may increase both useful corrections and harmful capitulations. The quadrant reward separates these behaviors explicitly. The paper also samples different critic styles, including opposing, neutral, and supportive feedback, so the policy must learn conditional revision rather than unconditional deference. Optimization uses group-normalized advantages, suggesting a GRPO-style relative update rather than plain scalar-reward PPO. On the systems side, the authors introduce dynamic asynchronous rollout with tail-adaptive completion: samples that finish the initial stage can move to the critic stage immediately, instead of waiting for the slowest trajectories in the batch.
