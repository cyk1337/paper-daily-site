9:T70f,The method has three main components. First, the authors build a controlled set of moral dilemmas from four classic works in PG19 instead of relying on synthetic dilemmas. Gemma-4 31B extracts a situational premise P and two competing duty assertions D1 and D2. A Z3 SMT solver verifies that P∧D1 is satisfiable, P∧D2 is satisfiable, and P∧D1∧D2 is unsatisfiable. This filters out cases where a trivial compromise would satisfy both duties. Out of 185 raw candidates, 156 pass all three checks. Second, each dilemma is paired with five ethical lenses: Kantian deontology, utilitarianism, virtue ethics, care ethics, and social contract theory, yielding 780 training prompts. The policy is initialized from instruction-tuned Gemma-4 E4B, described as 4.5B effective parameters and 8B total parameters. For each prompt, it generates 8 completions with a separated thinking block and visible answer. Third, rewards come from a self-judged tournament. The same policy judges its own completions pairwise using a prompt based on Wachsmuth’s 15-dimensional argument-quality taxonomy. It reasons over the dimensions but emits four preferences: logic, rhetoric, dialectic, and overall quality. The overall preference is used as the reward. All 28 pairs among 8 completions are judged in both presentation orders, producing 56 judgments per prompt. Presentation flips are treated as ties. Because ties are explicit, the authors use a Rao-Kupper preference model rather than a plain Bradley-Terry model, then convert latent strengths into RLOO advantages. Masked thinking restricts the policy gradient to visible answer tokens. Compared with scalar outcome rewards or label-only moral training, this setup directly optimizes relative argument quality while controlling for trivial compromise and order bias.a:T47b,The experiments address judge reliability, training dynamics, and transfer. First, the self-judge’s argument-quality preferences broadly align with human annotators. Increasing judge scale mainly reduces presentation flips rather than changing the task into a different preference problem. Second, RL induces a prescriptive register. The policy becomes more decisive, uses more forward-looking obligations, and relies less on caveats and retrospective justification. The change is not simply dogmatism; it is a shift toward a confident normative style. Third, transfer to external moral judgment benchmarks is negative or mixed. On MoralChoice, ELEPHANT, and MORABLES, presentation flips are roughly halved, showing that consistency improves. However, sycophancy stays flat, framing acceptance rises, and accuracy falls. Ablations over dilemma source and masked thinking reproduce the same pattern, which suggests that the consistency-validity split is not an artifact of a single data source or masking choice. The training set is small by LLM standards, so the paper should be read as a controlled mechanism study rather than a scaling recipe.b:T54f,Training uses an IPD environment with a Tit-for-Tat opponent, 200 episodes, early stopping with patience 30, LoRA, and effective batch size 32. The full evaluation grid covers 3 models, 4 training conditions, 6 attack types, and 230 scenarios, for 16,560 evaluations, plus 2,880 fiction-variation and defense evaluations. At 27B, moral RL reduces mean adversarial degradation by 5.2x but costs about 11 percentage points on Ethics accuracy. Across 205 scenarios and 5 seeds, reasoning-level moral reward gives a 5.8x robustness gain, while the matched random reward does not. Baseline agentic ethical scores are already low, around 0.032–0.086, indicating safe default behavior without attack. Gemma-27B baselines are around 0.032–0.048, while Llama-8B is around 0.065–0.086.
