A normal forward pass produces the final-layer hidden state h0. A lightweight extractor compresses this into a low-dimensional reasoning state s0, which acts as a bottleneck summary of the current internal reasoning status. Instead of decoding immediately, the model performs a sequence of refinement steps in latent space. Two learned controllers govern the process: a depth controller decides how many refinement steps to take, and an action controller chooses the direction or type of refinement at each step. Both controllers consume only the compact reasoning state, which keeps the control mechanism lightweight and avoids direct search over the full hidden tensor. Training uses policy gradients with a step-wise likelihood improvement signal as reward: if a refinement step improves the likelihood of the desired output, it is reinforced. This gives denser credit assignment than using only final-task reward and avoids explicit supervision on natural-language reasoning traces. Relative to prior latent intervention work, the novelty is iterative and adaptive hidden-state revision rather than one-shot editing or static steering.
