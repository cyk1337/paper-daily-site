b:T6f8,CAST, short for Credit Assignment from Solver Teachers, uses the solver as a turn-level teacher. For an action a sampled by the LLM at state s, the solver evaluates the state before and after the action, V(s) and V(s'). Their difference defines a solver advantage indicating whether the action moved the trajectory closer to success. This scalar is then injected into the RLVR objective as a turn-level process signal, weighting policy updates at the action level.

The important point is not just reward shaping. The paper gives a distillation interpretation. Under a soft-optimal solver assumption, the log-probability of an action under the solver’s implicit action distribution is strictly proportional to its one-step state-value change. Therefore, maximizing a solver-advantage-weighted objective is equivalent to on-policy distillation toward the solver’s action preferences. This avoids the usual requirement for teacher logits: a solver that can provide state values or cost-to-go is enough.

To stabilize training, the method applies two lightweight transformations. First, an asinh transform compresses extreme solver signals while preserving resolution near zero; the paper interprets this as a robustified KL-style constraint. Second, batch-level RMS normalization removes scale mismatch across games and difficulty levels. Together, these steps make the process signal numerically stable and comparable across domains.

Compared with expert-trajectory SFT, CAST remains useful when the model visits off-expert states. Compared with cross-trajectory comparison methods such as GiGPO, it uses direct local feedback. Compared with learned process reward models, it does not require a separate dense-labeling pipeline, provided a solver or a good value estimator exists.c:T563,The experiments are run on Sokoban, Minesweeper, and Rush Hour, chosen to cover long-horizon planning, partial-observation inference, and constrained combinatorial search. CAST outperforms outcome-only RLVR baselines and the process-level baseline GiGPO in both in-domain and unseen-difficulty settings. It also reaches DAPO’s peak validation performance in fewer training steps, indicating better sample efficiency.

The abstract does not include the full numeric tables, but the paper emphasizes several findings. Solver overhead is negligible because the method only needs local value evaluations rather than expensive full-trajectory search. Both asinh compression and RMS normalization matter; removing them hurts stability or cross-game robustness. Replacing the exact solver with a learned value network retains much of the gain, which is important for settings where exact solvers are unavailable. The trained policy also shows some zero-shot transfer to held-out ALFWorld and WebShop without further fine-tuning, suggesting that the method is not only memorizing game-specific trajectories.

For judging the paper, the most informative results are the ablations: solver-advantage weighting versus outcome-only RLVR, the necessity of the signal transformations, the gap between exact and learned teachers, and whether the advantage persists on unseen difficulty levels.d:T473,For a skim, start with the method overview and the theory section. The central question is why a scalar value difference can stand in for teacher logits. Once that equivalence is clear, the rest of the method is straightforward. Then read how the objective is integrated into RLVR or PPO-style updates, followed by the two stabilization tricks, asinh compression and RMS normalization. In the experiments, prioritize the main tables on the three games, the sample-efficiency curves, the unseen-difficulty generalization results, and the exact-solver versus learned-value comparison.

Keep the boundary conditions in mind. The method assumes access to a strong solver or at least a reliable value estimator, so it is best suited to verifiable environments with clear state transitions. It is much less directly applicable to open-ended language generation. Also, this is primarily a post-training credit-assignment paper rather than a pretraining one.
