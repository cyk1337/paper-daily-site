9:T65a,DataPraxis is built around verified workspace evolution. The starting point is a seed workspace containing data assets and an execution environment. Instead of generating a task immediately from that initial directory, the framework lets an agent analyze the workspace, produce scripts, intermediate datasets, findings, and artifacts, and then writes verified results back into the workspace. This creates an evolved state that can support deeper follow-up analysis. Different analytical branches can also be inherited or combined, which allows later questions to depend on earlier discoveries.

Once a chain of findings and dependencies has been accumulated, the framework constructs a task backward from those dependencies. It removes the intermediate support that existed during workspace evolution, so the final solver must reconstruct the analysis from the raw materials. In this way, the hidden dependency structure discovered during evolution becomes a challenging long-horizon task.

To address reliability, the paper introduces dual-evidence verification. One source of evidence comes from validated findings produced during workspace evolution. The second comes from independent solution trajectories used to build and validate rubrics. The verifier therefore checks not only final answers but also deliverables, supporting evidence, and overall analytical quality. Compared with direct task generation from data descriptions, this approach is better at surfacing questions that only emerge after prior analysis. Compared with exploration-only task mining, it puts more emphasis on dependency accumulation and reuse.a:T53c,The paper builds DataPraxis-5K, a dataset of 5,000 long-horizon professional analytics training examples across 15 domains, including business, finance, sports, and entertainment. It trains two models: DataPraxis-27B and DataPraxis-35B-A3B, the latter being a 35B MoE with 3B active parameters.

A useful motivation experiment studies whether deeper questions really depend on prior analysis. In 50 workspaces, an agent proposes three successive questions, and Qwen3.6-27B is used to measure the perplexity of each question under different context conditions. When only the initial workspace is provided, mean perplexity rises from 16.87 to 31.07 for later rounds. When prior findings are also included, perplexity drops by 46.1% and 53.0% in rounds 2 and 3. This supports the paper’s main premise: deeper analytical questions are often not obvious from the raw workspace alone and become much more predictable once earlier findings are available.

The abstract also claims that DataPraxis-27B achieves leading performance among the evaluated models on data analysis and long-horizon professional tasks.
