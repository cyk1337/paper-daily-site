18:T6f9,GrowMTP trains a multi-token draft head inside the RL loop using speculative verification signals. The main challenge is not the existence of labels, but how to make those labels valid. The paper identifies three constraints. First, supervision must be applied under rollout-consistent draft-conditioned states. The k-th drafted token is predicted conditioned on previous drafted tokens, not on the final executed prefix after verification. To avoid train-test mismatch, GrowMTP records the draft trajectory and reconstructs the hidden states corresponding to the actual draft-conditioned context that produced each proposal. Second, learning across drafting depths is coupled. A deeper token only matters if earlier drafted tokens are accepted, so depth-wise training should reflect that dependency rather than treating each position as an independent classification target. The paper introduces a depth-coupled acceptance (DCA) loss based on an acceptance-chain surrogate, with a logarithmic transform to increase relative gradient weight on low-chain-value cases. Third, supervision beyond the first rejection is invalid for execution, because deeper drafted tokens are conditioned on a rejected token and therefore lie off the executed prefix. GrowMTP gates the loss after the first rejection and excludes those suffix positions. A further design choice is to detach draft-head updates from the policy backbone, so the online drafting module does not directly perturb policy learning. This sacrifices possible gains from joint optimization, but keeps the method modular and stable. An important practical point is that no offline warm start or depth curriculum is required: the first draft position always yields valid supervision, so training can begin from random initialization.19:T4a3,The headline result is from-scratch online growth on a headless model. On Qwen3-4B with a randomly initialized K=5 draft head, trained for 500 math RL steps, the acceptance length rises to 2.91 from the autoregressive floor. This yields 2.13x rollout speedup and 1.60x end-to-end step speedup, with head-training cost included. Over the same 500-step budget, GrowMTP reduces total GPU-hours by 9.4% relative to using an offline-trained head with comparable acceptance, which is the key economic result. The paper also studies online adaptation when a draft head already exists. On Qwen3.5-4B-Base, online updates provide an additional 1.36x rollout speedup over a frozen pretrained head. On MiMo-7B-SFT, extending the head from K=1 to K=5 increases acceptance from 1.65 to 4.0 and improves rollout speed by 1.93x. Another useful finding is domain specialization: heads trained on math rollouts and code rollouts achieve higher in-domain acceptance, supporting the claim that rollout-specific adaptation matters. The introduction does not expose all ablation numbers, but the experimental structure appears to cover from-scratch growth, continued online adaptation, and domain dependence.1a:T46d,Read this paper with a systems question in mind: why has speculative decoding been standard in serving, yet still depended on offline draft-head preparation in RL? After the introduction, focus on three technical pieces: reconstruction of draft-conditioned states, the DCA loss, and loss gating after the first rejection. Those are the parts that make the method more than simple distillation from verification logits. If time is limited, start with the method figure, the loss definition, and the rollout/training pseudocode, then move to the main training curves showing how acceptance length grows over RL steps and when net speedup appears. The most informative ablations are likely: removing draft-conditioned reconstruction, replacing DCA with independent depth losses, and training beyond the first rejection. While reading, it is worth asking whether the same online-growth idea could extend to stronger native MTP or EAGLE-style drafting modules, and whether partial backbone updates would help or destabilize RL.
