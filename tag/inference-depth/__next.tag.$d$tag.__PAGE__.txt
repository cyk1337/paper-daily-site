9:T5d9,The model backbone is kept frozen. Lightweight readouts are attached to intermediate layers, trained to predict the model’s final token from the current residual stream. A confidence test then decides whether to emit that token immediately or continue through deeper layers. This design is deliberate: it avoids retraining the base LM and keeps adaptation cheap enough for deployment-specific tuning.
Key elements of the approach are:
1. A unified cost metric in layer-equivalent units, combining saved computation and saved parameter movement, which is especially relevant for edge inference.
2. Traffic-specific training or calibration of readouts, rather than assuming one global exit rule works everywhere. This is the paper’s main thesis: deployment-conditioned calibration matters a lot.
3. Explicit treatment of the KV Cache Absence issue as a systems constraint that any practical early-exit setup must handle, even though the paper does not center its novelty on a new cache-repair mechanism.
4. Self-supervised supervision: the readout is trained only on the frozen model’s own full-depth outputs over ordinary traffic, not on human labels.
Relative to prior work, the novelty is less about architecture and more about problem framing and methodology. Earlier papers focused on generic early-exit criteria or cache handling.
