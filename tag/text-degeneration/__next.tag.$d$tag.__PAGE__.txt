9:T8d1,全文的核心思路是把重复退化看成一个离散动力系统中的“入环”和“困环”问题，而不是把所有重复都归因于单步概率偏差。作者先用 coverage-based 的视角刻画 repetition loop：生成序列中往往存在一个短 N-gram 循环，覆盖了后续大部分 token。基于这个观察，他们把退化拆成两部分。

第一是 loop entry risk，也就是模型在某个关键上下文第一次踏入重复轨道的概率。作者发现这通常是一个 sharp entry event：只要在入环点替换掉少数几个高概率 token，后续轨迹就可能完全摆脱重复。第二是 loop persistence，即一旦进入循环，模型在连续多个时间步里持续留在循环中的倾向。作者指出 persistence 才是长程重复的主导项，因为它会在每一步累乘放大。

在这个分析上，论文进一步解释了为什么剪枝和朴素蒸馏会恶化问题。剪枝后的学生模型容量更紧，若仍用标准 distillation，它容易把概率质量泄漏到 teacher 原本压低的 token 上，这会提高 entry risk；同时它还会压扁 nucleus 内几个接近并列的合理候选，使模型更早承诺到单一路径，降低 escape mass，从而提高 persistence。这里的 escape mass 指的是在当前采样集合内，分配给“可接受但不重复”的替代 token 的总概率质量。

基于这两个失真来源，作者提出两个训练目标。FOCUS 用于抑制 leakage。它不是均匀模仿 teacher 全分布，而是对高置信 teacher 区域加权更高，让学生优先学稳 teacher 明确支持的 token，减少容量不足时向低支持区域漂移。RePAIR 用于降低 persistence。它围绕退化 onset 构造 continuation pair：一个是实际走向重复的 negative continuation，另一个是从同一起点重新生成得到的 non-degenerate continuation。然后用 margin-based objective 提升 onset 附近那些合理替代 token 的相对得分，增加 escape mass，避免模型过早锁死在循环轨道上。

从方法定位看，FOCUS 更像是改造蒸馏损失，RePAIR 更像是针对 loop-sensitive context 的对比式修复。两者互补：前者减少“误入”，后者增加“脱困”能力。a:T96f,The central idea is to treat repetition as a dynamical process with two stages: entering a loop and staying in it. The paper adopts a coverage-based view of repetition loops, where a short recurring n-gram accounts for a large fraction of the generated continuation. Based on this, degeneration is decomposed into two quantities.

The first is loop entry risk: the probability that generation first falls into a repetitive trajectory at a sensitive context. The authors argue that this is often a sharp event. Replacing only a few high-probability tokens near the onset can be enough to divert the trajectory away from repetition. The second is loop persistence: once the model has entered the loop, how likely it is to remain there over successive decoding steps. Persistence is the dominant factor for long-horizon degeneration because it compounds across in-loop transitions.

This decomposition is then used to explain why pruning and naive distillation make the problem worse. Under reduced capacity, standard distillation can leak probability mass into tokens that the teacher had strongly suppressed, which raises entry risk. At the same time, it can collapse several plausible near-tie alternatives inside the nucleus set, causing the model to commit too early to a single path and reducing the escape mass available to non-repetitive continuations. Here, escape mass refers to the total probability assigned to acceptable alternative tokens that would let decoding leave the repetitive trajectory.

The paper proposes two complementary training objectives. FOCUS addresses leakage. Instead of matching the teacher distribution uniformly, it reweights distillation toward high-confidence teacher regions so that the student prioritizes tokens the teacher clearly supports, reducing drift into low-support regions. RePAIR addresses persistence. Around detected degeneration onsets, it constructs paired continuations: a repetitive negative continuation and a regenerated non-degenerate continuation from the same onset. A margin-based objective then increases the relative score of plausible alternatives near the onset, enlarging escape mass and reducing early commitment to the loop.

Conceptually, FOCUS is a distillation correction objective, while RePAIR is a contrastive repair objective for loop-sensitive contexts. They target different parts of the failure mode and are designed to be complementary.b:T6ba,Experiments are conducted in a post-pruning pipeline on Llama-family models, with LoRA-based finetuning after pruning. Evaluation covers both open-ended continuation on WikiText-103 and instruction-style generation on Self-Instruct. Importantly, the paper does not stop at perplexity. It also reports generation-quality and degeneration-sensitive metrics such as MAUVE, CREP, EAD, and semantic similarity measures including BERTScore.

The main empirical result is consistent: FOCUS and RePAIR reduce repetition and improve distributional and semantic quality, while incurring only a small increase in perplexity. The paper contrasts this with standard anti-repetition decoding heuristics, which can suppress repetition but often damage the language-model distribution and hurt coherence. The advantage here is that the method does not blindly penalize previously generated tokens; it selectively reshapes probability allocation at loop-sensitive contexts.

The most useful ablation-level insights are threefold. First, long-run degeneration is driven more by persistence than by the one-time entry event. Second, under nucleus decoding, preserving probability mass on several plausible alternatives is critical for avoiding lock-in to a repetitive trajectory. Third, FOCUS is compatible with existing training-based mitigation objectives and still improves generation quality when combined, suggesting that it addresses a relatively distinct source of distortion.
