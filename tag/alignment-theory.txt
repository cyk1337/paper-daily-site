19:T538,The paper revisits the derivation of DPO from KL-regularized RLHF under the Bradley-Terry preference model. The critical step is the substitution of the RLHF-optimal policy into the reward parameterization. The authors argue that this substitution is valid only if the optimal policy itself respects the observed preference ordering. RLHF does not guarantee this, because its objective trades off reward against KL divergence from the reference model. When the reference model strongly prefers the wrong response, the KL term can pull the optimum toward the wrong ordering. Under this violation, DPO no longer optimizes the same objective as RLHF: it optimizes relative advantage over the reference policy. The paper formalizes a pathological solution region where DPO loss can decrease while the learned policy still prefers dispreferred responses. To address this, it introduces Constrained Preference Optimization, which augments the RLHF objective with explicit preference constraints. The paper also gives a geometric view through soft margin ranking loss: DPO can induce an effective margin that becomes negative, while CPO enforces non-negative effective margins. A conservative variant, E-CPOC, is proposed to avoid explicit reward modeling while maintaining a formal connection to constrained RLHF via a loss-to-delta argument.1a:T49b,The authors formulate recursive retraining as a distributional dynamical system. At each iteration, K candidates are sampled from the current model distribution p_t. One candidate is selected by a Bradley-Terry choice rule under a sampled reward, and retraining is idealized as unconstrained maximum likelihood, so p_{t+1} becomes the selected-sample distribution. In the large-candidate regime, the BT selection update becomes an exponential tilt of p_t by exp(r_i(x)), followed by normalization. With pluralistic preferences, the reward is sampled from a fixed mixture, for example with weights q and 1-q for two rewards. The analysis defines near-optimal basins for the rewards and proves that, under an outside-basin gap condition, mass outside the union of these basins decays. Once mass is concentrated inside the basin union, the final split is governed by leakage: how much one reward also reinforces the other reward’s basin. As leakage vanishes under stronger separation, the basin weights approach the reward sampling weights.
