First, it evaluates 20 off-the-shelf DLMs and ARMs under a common generation setup using three metrics: n-gram entropy as a proxy for token-level randomness, semantic coherence for local sentence-level consistency, and semantic diversity for document-level variation. The empirical pattern is consistent: DLM outputs tend to have lower entropy, higher coherence, and higher diversity.

Second, it runs controlled objective studies. Instead of only comparing end-to-end systems, the authors align decoding and vary the training objective. They interpolate between ARM-like and DLM-like objectives and isolate components such as bidirectional context, input masking, label masking, and noise-level weighting. The main finding is that bidirectional context is the dominant driver of the coherence and diversity gains. Other ingredients of the DLM objective contribute much less.

Third, it studies decoding. The focus is block-wise iterative decoding with four remasking strategies: low-confidence, dynamic low-confidence, high-entropy, and random remasking. The key result is that entropy reduction is strongly tied to confidence-based remasking. Intuitively, these strategies repeatedly preserve high-confidence positions and revisit uncertain ones, which narrows the effective sample distribution. In contrast, random or high-entropy remasking leaves entropy roughly unchanged and close to the ARM level.
