技术贡献可以拆成四块。

第一块是 knowledge flow。作者先在真实数据上看语言、视觉理解、视觉生成之间的迁移，再在严格控制的合成环境里做因果剥离。这里的关键不是简单比较多任务训练是否涨点，而是看哪一种能力会给另一种能力提供可迁移表征，以及这种迁移是否对称。结论是明显不对称：语言对视觉理解、视觉生成的帮助方式不同；视觉生成与视觉理解之间也不是天然互利，而是依赖概念类型。作者用合成数据把概念级 transfer 拆出来，这一点比只在自然图文语料上做 ablation 更干净，因为后者很容易被数据共现和任务难度混淆。

第二块是 synergy vs. competition。作者把“模态是否互相帮助”追到数据复杂度和网络结构两个层面。数据层面，过于复杂的语言或视觉任务会让模态争夺容量；相反，适度简化的一侧可以给另一侧提供辅助监督，形成协同。结构层面，作者分析 Transformer 内部哪些部位更适合共享，哪些部位更适合隔离。结论是 shared attention 更有利于跨模态信息交换，因为注意力负责 token 间路由；而 separate FFN 更适合隔离模态特有变换，减少在参数空间里的直接冲突。这个设计判断很像把 attention 视作通信层，把 FFN 视作模态私有计算层。作者还验证这种现象在不同 visual tokenizer 下都成立，说明不是某个图像表示偶然带来的结果。

第三块是 early unification。作者系统改变视觉数据引入的时间点，以及模态训练的调度方式，比较从一开始 joint training、先文本后视觉、分阶段 curriculum 等方案。核心发现是视觉必须尽早进入，而且要与语言同时共训练。若先把语言模型训得很强，再做视觉接入，模型会出现作者称为 vision laziness 的现象：优化更倾向复用已有语言先验，而不是认真更新视觉相关子网络，导致视觉表征学习不足。这一点对当前很多“先训 LLM、再补视觉”的路线是直接挑战，因为它说明后接视觉不只是效率问题，而是会改变最优解的形态。

第四块是 recipes。作者把前面三类发现合并成可执行 recipe：采用不对称的数据混合比例，使用 shared attention + split FFN 一类的参数共享设计，并坚持早期统一、同步联合训练。然后在大规模 MoE 训练中做单变量对照，验证这些设计在更大参数和更长训练下仍然成立。这里的价值在于，它不是提出一个新模块，而是把多模态预训练里最容易拍脑袋决定的几件事，变成了有实验支撑的默认选择。1c:Ta3c,The model is a decoder-only Llama-3-style backbone trained under the Transfusion framework, where text uses discrete next-token prediction and image generation uses continuous flow matching inside one unified model. The large-scale validation uses a 13.5B MoE model trained on 2T tokens. The technical contribution is organized into four parts.

1) Knowledge flow. The authors first measure transfer among language, visual understanding, and visual generation on real-world data, then move to synthetic controlled environments to remove confounders. The key point is not just whether multitask training helps, but which capability transfers to which other capability, and whether the transfer is symmetric. Their answer is no: transfer is strongly asymmetric, and the interaction between visual understanding and visual generation is concept-dependent rather than universally positive.

2) Synergy vs. competition. The paper traces modality interaction to both data complexity and network structure. On the data side, simplified tasks in one modality can help the other modality, while high complexity can create competition for capacity. On the architecture side, the authors analyze where sharing helps and where separation is safer. Shared attention appears to support cross-modal routing and communication, while separate FFNs reduce destructive interference by keeping modality-specific transformations private. This is a useful architectural decomposition: attention as the communication layer, FFN as the modality-specialized computation layer.

3) Early unification. The paper varies when vision is introduced and how modalities are scheduled during training. The main result is that vision should be present early and trained jointly with language, not added after a strong language prior has already formed. Late introduction leads to what the authors call “vision laziness”: the model leans on existing language priors and under-optimizes the vision-related components. This is an important challenge to the common retrofit paradigm.

4) Recipes. The final recipe combines asymmetric data mixing, selective parameter sharing such as shared attention plus split FFNs, and early simultaneous multimodal training. The paper then validates these choices with controlled single-variable comparisons at larger scale.1d:T6d1,The experiments operate at two levels. The first level is controlled mechanism analysis. The default model is a 16-layer decoder-only Transformer with hidden size 2048, GQA 32/8, SwiGLU, RoPE, QK-norm, and FlashAttention. The backbone is 1.5B, and the split-FFN multimodal version totals about 2.3B parameters. Using both real and synthetic data, the paper studies transfer among language, visual understanding, and visual generation, then runs systematic ablations over task complexity, parameter sharing, visual tokenizer choice, vision introduction timing, and training schedule. The main findings reported in the text are: transfer is not symmetric; visual understanding and generation interact differently depending on concept type; modality synergy versus competition is largely controlled by data complexity; shared attention plus separate FFNs is a strong structural compromise; and late alignment induces vision laziness.

The second level is large-scale recipe validation. The authors train 13.5B MoE models on 2T tokens and run controlled single-variable comparisons to test whether the earlier findings survive scale. The excerpt provided here does not include all result tables, so exact benchmark numbers are incomplete, but the paper clearly positions the large-scale section as a scaling check rather than a small-model anecdote. When reading the full paper, the most important numbers to extract are: the trade-off curves under different data mixtures; the gains or losses from different sharing patterns such as shared attention versus split FFNs; and the gap between early joint training and late or staged introduction of vision, ideally together with optimization diagnostics that support the vision-laziness interpretation.1e:T641,A good reading order is problem, mechanism, then recipe.

Then read Section 3 and Section 5 carefully. Section 3 is the center of the paper because the knowledge-flow analysis motivates the later data-mixing recipe. Pay attention to how real-data experiments and synthetic experiments play different roles: one gives external realism, the other gives cleaner causal interpretation.
