18:T7bf,The method is deliberately simple but well targeted. The authors define two axes. Direct rate (DR) measures how often the model repeats its top answer over 30 samples of the original prompt at temperature 0.6. This captures concentration under one wording. Framed rate (FR) then asks whether that same direct top answer survives 30 matched scenario reframings of the underlying question. Importantly, FR is not the concentration of answers under reframing; it is the reappearance rate of the original direct top answer. That design choice is the core of the paper, because it measures persistence of a specific preference rather than generic consistency. The final bias depth score is π = DR × FR, which is high only when the model is both concentrated under direct prompting and robust to reframing.

The dataset construction is also central. They build 4,442 opinion prompt families, each grounded in a real Olmo-3-7B-SFT training example, and create matched everyday-scenario reframings for each family. Because the task is open-ended generation rather than multiple choice, answer normalization is necessary to merge semantically equivalent outputs and avoid contaminating FR with superficial phrasing variation. The contribution is therefore less about a new model and more about turning a causally meaningful question—does a preference survive paraphrastic and situational variation?—into a scalable evaluation protocol.

The paper then runs this protocol across the full Olmo-3-7B release pipeline: Pretrained → SFT → DPO → RLVR. That allows stage-wise tracking of bias depth. It also adds two attribution analyses: whether the post-SFT top answer matches the pretrained top answer, and whether a biased answer can be traced back to a specific SFT training example. Finally, it tests removability using continued LoRA-SFT and prompt-based debiasing with GEPA, asking whether Deep and Shallow biases differ not just in origin but also in how hard they are to erase.19:T5c3,There are four results that matter most. First, across four post-SFT models, 35.8% to 77.7% of the 4,442 prompt families look biased under direct prompting, but only about a quarter of those direct biases survive reframing. So most of what a single-prompt protocol would count as bias is shallow. Second, along the full Olmo-3-7B pipeline, most Deep bias is already present at the pretrained stage, whereas Shallow bias keeps increasing through SFT and every later post-training stage. That is the clearest empirical support for the paper’s main interpretation. Third, biases that survive reframing are much more likely to match the pretrained model’s top answer, suggesting inheritance from pretraining rather than creation by SFT alone. At the same time, clean attribution to one specific SFT example works only for a small minority of cases, which argues against a simplistic single-example memorization story and points instead to broader distributional effects. Fourth, debiasing is asymmetric: continued LoRA-SFT reduces Deep bias by only 4.5 percentage points versus 10.3 points for Shallow bias, and GEPA reduces Deep bias by 1.6 points versus 5.8 points for Shallow. Those removability gaps line up with the stage-of-origin analysis.

For a pretraining reader, the main signal is not any one absolute score but the consistency of the whole picture: biases with stronger evidence of pretraining inheritance are more robust to reframing and harder to remove later.1a:T483,This is a skim paper, but a careful skim is worthwhile. Start with the introduction and Section 2 to internalize the DR/FR split. The key detail is that FR tracks recurrence of the direct top answer under reframing, not the top answer under each reframed prompt. Without that detail, the paper can look like a generic robustness study, which it is not. Then read the dataset section closely enough to understand how prompt families, scenario reframings, and answer normalization are built; the validity of the Deep/Shallow distinction depends heavily on these choices. In the experiments, prioritize the stage-wise analysis, the pretrained-versus-SFT attribution, and the debiasing section. When looking at figures, focus on whether the same answer persists across checkpoints and how much each debiasing method moves Deep versus Shallow cases.

A solid read takes about 25–35 minutes.
