9:T679,For each behavior, the authors build a matched contrastive dataset with positive examples that elicit the behavior and negative examples that suppress it. At a chosen layer of a decoder-only Transformer, they take the last-token residual-stream activation and compute paired contrast vectors between positive and negative examples. These vectors are centered and decomposed with SVD. Instead of representing a behavior by a single steering direction, the method represents it as a top-k low-rank subspace.

A key step is removing decision-aligned variance. The leading singular components can reflect the model’s final positive-vs-negative decision rather than behavior-specific structure. The paper defines a scalar decision margin using the log-probability difference of label strings, identifies components aligned with this margin, and separates them from the behavior subspace analysis. This is meant to avoid conflating decision readout with the internal representation of the behavior.

The diagnostic then studies two quantities. The first is geometric overlap between behavior subspaces, measured through projection-style or principal-angle-style comparisons. The second is decision coupling, which measures how strongly a behavior subspace is connected to the model’s decision direction. Projection-based interventions are applied to a source behavior subspace, and the induced changes are measured on target behaviors. Overlap explains whether an intervention can reach another behavior; decision coupling explains whether that shared representation actually affects the output.
