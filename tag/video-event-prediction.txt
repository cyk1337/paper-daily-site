1b:T7b6,The core method is Future-L1. Starting from a standard video MLLM backbone, the model is augmented with a latent visual reasoning channel controlled by three special tokens: <|latent_start|>, <|latent|>, and <|latent_end|>. Generation begins in text mode. Once <|latent_start|> is emitted, each subsequent latent position produces a hidden state that is not projected to the vocabulary; instead, that hidden state is fed back as the next-step input embedding. This creates an autoregressive continuous-state process inside the latent span, and those latent states remain in the KV cache so later text tokens can condition on them. Span length is variable, and multiple latent spans can appear in one response, allowing adaptive allocation of visual reasoning budget.

Training has two stages. The first is supervised cold start. The authors build Future-L1-50K from TwiFF-2.7M using visual-gain curation rather than naive sampling. For each candidate example, they compare a strong base model under two conditions: observed video prefix only, versus prefix plus an intermediate future-frame hint. They keep examples where the future visual hint provides measurable marginal utility. This removes easy cases that do not need future imagination and noisy cases where the hint is not informative. During SFT, the model learns both when to open latent spans and how to align latent hidden states with future-frame embeddings.

The second stage is LA-DAPO, a latent-aware RL objective. It optimizes sampled latent trajectories using outcome-contrastive rewards and temporal-diversity rewards. The first favors latent futures that lead to correct event prediction; the second discourages repetitive or collapsed latent thought patterns. The motivation is clear: if optimization only rewards the final answer, the latent channel may become decorative.
