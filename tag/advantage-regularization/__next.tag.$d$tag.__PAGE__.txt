c:T610,The method, MARGO, stands for Mixed-Mode Advantage Regularization for Grounded Optimization. The conceptual starting point is a residual decomposition: the non-thinking response reflects the model’s direct-answer tendency, while the thinking response adds a reasoning residual on top. That residual can be helpful, recovering the right fact, or harmful, introducing unsupported associations, entity confusion, or speculative drift.

MARGO modifies the rollout grouping used in group-based RL such as GRPO. Standard setups typically compare only thinking trajectories for the same question. That makes the direct-answer baseline invisible: if all thinking samples are wrong, or all are right, the training signal cannot tell whether explicit reasoning helped or hurt relative to the model’s own non-thinking behavior. MARGO instead forms mixed rollout groups containing both thinking and non-thinking trajectories. The non-thinking output becomes a same-model reference inside advantage estimation.

This changes the optimization target in a meaningful way. Thinking trajectories that corrupt an already correct direct answer receive lower relative advantage, while trajectories that genuinely repair an incorrect direct answer are reinforced. The novelty is not a complicated RL algorithm, but the choice of reference signal. Rather than relying on an external teacher or a separate verifier, the method uses mode contrast within the same model to expose harmful reasoning residuals. That is well aligned with the failure mode the paper identifies.d:T455,The strongest empirical point is the instance-level transition analysis. On TriviaQA with Qwen3-8B, thinking corrects 11.74% of examples that are wrong under non-thinking, but it also overturns 7.50% of originally correct non-thinking answers into incorrect ones. That is the key evidence that thinking-induced hallucination is real and not a corner case.

The training evaluation covers two models and six factuality-oriented QA benchmarks. The paper reports that MARGO consistently improves factual reliability over fixed-mode inference, mode selection, supervised fine-tuning, and RL baselines trained on the same curated data. It also evaluates on mathematical benchmarks and claims that the factuality-oriented mixed-mode RL does not noticeably damage general reasoning ability. The excerpt provided does not include the full result tables, so the exact benchmark-by-benchmark gains cannot be reproduced here.
