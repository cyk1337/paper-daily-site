18:T641,The central surrogate is a pre-trained Gaussian Process over the performance function. The GP prior is not learned from scratch. For established benchmarks, its covariance can be derived from historical evaluation records across models, capturing that two inputs are related if models tend to succeed or fail on them together. For new domains or modalities, the method uses off-the-shelf text or image embedding models to represent inputs, then pre-trains encoder parameters and GP hyperparameters so the kernel reflects semantic or visual similarity.

On top of this surrogate, ProEval handles two objectives. For aggregate performance estimation, it frames the metric as an integral, ∫ f(x)p(x)dx, and uses Bayesian quadrature. Unlike Monte Carlo averaging, BQ treats the integral as a posterior random variable and provides an analytic estimate of its uncertainty. The acquisition rule then selects inputs that most reduce posterior variance of the aggregate metric. For failure discovery, the method defines failures as a superlevel set, {x | f(x) ≥ λ}, and selects points by balancing predicted failure probability with epistemic uncertainty. This prevents the search from repeatedly sampling only known failure types.

The paper also extends evaluation beyond a static dataset. It uses an LLM generator conditioned on discovered hard examples as in-context anchors. To reduce collapse into a single failure mode, it introduces bi-level sampling: first sample high-level semantic topics, then generate concrete test cases under those topics.
