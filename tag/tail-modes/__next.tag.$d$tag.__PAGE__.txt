9:T8c4,The paper starts by separating recursive training into three paradigms: replace, accumulate, and selection. Replace uses only newly generated synthetic data and is already known to collapse quickly. Accumulate keeps the original real data and appends later generations, which can prevent variance blow-up in theory, but in low-resource settings the real seed set is often too small to dominate later synthetic rounds. The main focus is selection: generate a candidate synthetic pool, score it with a verifier, and keep only the “good” subset for the next round.

The key move is to model the verifier’s reference distribution explicitly as a biased local observation rather than an approximation to the global truth. Under this view, the selection operator is not a denoiser. It is a directional projection toward the local manifold. Samples are retained in proportion to how well they match the silo’s own support, so repeated selection trims away globally important but locally underrepresented regions. The paper argues that this is not a transient artifact. In recursive training, the bias compounds and drives diversity contraction at an asymptotic power-law rate, with worsening Wasserstein distance to the true global distribution.

To mitigate this, the authors propose a collaborative verifier framework under a no-raw-data-sharing constraint. Instead of pooled verification, they build proxy reference distributions across silos using Wasserstein geometry. Two constructions are emphasized: geodesic interpolations between local empirical distributions, and Wasserstein barycenters across multiple silos. These proxies are then used as collective references for scoring synthetic samples. The design choice is important. Simple feature averaging, parameter averaging, or majority voting would not preserve distributional geometry well enough, especially for tail coverage and mode structure. Wasserstein proxies are intended to recover a more faithful global reference at the distribution level while respecting silo boundaries. The paper also notes that access to the true global distribution would theoretically fix the problem, but that option is unavailable in the target regime, which is why collaborative proxies matter.a:T6d2,The experiments are designed to validate mechanism rather than to chase a single downstream score. Section 6 is the main place to read. The setup simulates low-resource recursive training with multiple silos and compares no selection, local selection, global-reference selection, and collaborative selection based on geodesic or barycenter proxies.

The main findings are consistent with the theory. Local selection can improve short-term alignment with the silo’s own data, but it accelerates global diversity loss: variance contracts, tail modes disappear, and Wasserstein distance to the true distribution grows. This degradation compounds across generations rather than appearing as a one-off effect, matching the paper’s power-law collapse analysis. When selection is performed against a global reference, collapse is much weaker, which isolates the real issue: not selection per se, but insufficient reference coverage. Under the realistic no-sharing constraint, both Wasserstein geodesic and barycenter proxies outperform single-silo selection, with larger gains when silo heterogeneity is stronger.
