9:T56c,The experimental design is intentionally simple. Invisible reasoning is defined as consequential computation in internal representations during the forward pass without an interpretable trace in output tokens. The authors insert a fixed filler-token sequence before the model’s answer. The key control is that the same filler sequence is used for every problem, so it cannot encode information about a particular question or answer at the token level. In evaluation, few-shot examples include the question, an assistant-side filler span, and the ground-truth answer; the test query is also followed by an assistant-side filler prefill before the model generates the answer. The baseline uses the same setup without filler. The paper proposes three diagnostic criteria: performance improves with filler tokens; the improvement depends on filler content; and filler preferences differ across models. These criteria make it less plausible that the effect comes from ordinary semantic prompting, and more plausible that model-specific representations and attention dynamics are being used. The tasks are short and verifiable: 4-digit multiplication, 5-to-7-step nested arithmetic, and counting distinct assigned variables in code snippets. The authors are careful not to reduce the mechanism to “more compute” alone, since inserting tokens also changes positions and attention patterns.a:T461,The study evaluates 13 frontier language models. Many models benefit from filler tokens on the synthetic reasoning tasks, with gains up to about 13 percentage points. The gain depends strongly on the filler sequence, and the best filler content varies across models. This model-specific variation suggests that the effect is tied to internal token representations, positional interactions, and learned computation patterns rather than to a shared human-readable meaning of the filler. A safety-relevant experiment adds a hidden modular arithmetic objective alongside the primary task. For Claude Opus 4.5, filler tokens allow the model to satisfy the hidden constraint without sacrificing primary-task accuracy, while the extra objective remains invisible in the output text. The training experiments are more mixed: RL on Qwen3-235B induces strong preferences over filler content, but the filler-token benefit does not persist robustly at test time; supervised fine-tuning also fails to transfer it.
