18:T5fa,The key methodological move is preregistration plus a full audit trail. The authors froze the protocol in advance, including acceptance thresholds: same-window repeat rankings had to reach Spearman 0.90, and byte-identical next-day replays had to reach 0.99 agreement. They then refused to repair the protocol after seeing the data. This is what gives the negative result force.

Several design choices matter. First, they built an append-only audit chain: request bodies, hashes, exclusions, valid task groups, replay pairs, and recorded metadata are all pinned and traceable. That rules out the usual explanation that the requests were not actually identical. Second, they decompose instability into distinct observable modes: within-window fluctuation, cross-day drift, and amplification caused by structured outputs such as rankings, where a tiny token-level change can invalidate or reorder an entire record. Third, they add prospective attribution experiments rather than stopping at a failed gate: same-day versus cross-day comparisons, four-provider comparisons, self-hosted controls, and constructed-error calibration. These help separate endpoint instability from task difficulty or judge incapacity. Fourth, the paper connects systems-level nondeterminism to measurement failure. Batch-dependent kernels, serving-time batching, and silent deployment changes were already known in systems work; the paper shows how these low-level effects become fatal once the output is used as a structured ranking or thresholded gate.19:T56e,The headline numbers are strong and easy to interpret. Across two campaigns, the audit covered 52,988 request attempts. The authors are careful not to treat this as the effective sample size; the actual analyses rest on 31 valid task groups, 100 replay pairs, and 10 constructed-error conditions. The first preregistered gate failed badly: same-window repeat rankings achieved only Spearman 0.400 against a required 0.90. The second gate also failed: byte-identical next-day replays agreed at 0.78 against a required 0.99.

Equally important is what did not fail. Requests were delivered successfully, schemas were valid, request-body hashes matched the frozen plan, and all recorded metadata fields remained constant. In other words, the engineering record looked clean while the measurement record was unstable. That is a major empirical point: standard API logs are not enough to establish reproducibility. The supplementary comparisons appear designed to answer whether the issue is specific to one provider or endemic to black-box shared serving, and whether self-hosting reduces the instability floor. The constructed-error calibration is also important because it asks a sharper question than average accuracy: even if the observer can detect obvious errors on average, is its variance low enough for threshold-based scientific use?
