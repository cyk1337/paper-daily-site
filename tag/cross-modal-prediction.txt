19:T442,The method combines bidirectional cross-modal latent prediction with per-modality distributional regularization. Given an image encoder and a text encoder, image embeddings are passed through a modality-specific predictor to predict stop-gradient text embeddings, and text embeddings predict stop-gradient image embeddings in the reverse direction. There are no in-batch negatives, no softmax denominator, no sigmoid pair classification over all image-text pairs, no learned temperature, and no momentum teacher. Collapse is handled by SIGReg applied separately to each modality, encouraging the marginal embedding distribution to be close to an isotropic Gaussian. During training, LeVLJEPA embeddings are not L2-normalized because the regularizer operates in Euclidean space; normalization is used only at evaluation time when cosine similarity is needed. This differs from CLIP, which uses symmetric InfoNCE; from SigLIP, which uses independent pairwise sigmoid losses; and from VL-JEPA, which uses a JEPA-style prediction framing but still relies on InfoNCE negatives to avoid collapse.1a:T461,The method is evaluated at two data scales: CC12M with 12 million image-text pairs and Datacomp-L with 92 million pairs. At the CC12M scale, LeVLJEPA is reported to be competitive with CLIP and SigLIP on both zero-shot transfer and linear probing, indicating that negative-free training can learn usable cross-modal alignment at this scale. At the Datacomp-L scale, contrastive baselines retain an advantage on zero-shot transfer, which is expected because their loss directly optimizes global image-text similarity. LeVLJEPA remains on par under linear probing of the global class representation and improves background robustness. The largest separation appears in dense-token evaluations: LeVLJEPA outperforms contrastive baselines on semantic segmentation. When used as a frozen visual backbone for a VLM, with only a lightweight bridge trained between the frozen encoder and a frozen language model, it obtains the best reported accuracy on GQA, VQAv2, and POPE across two language-model families.
