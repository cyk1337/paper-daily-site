Muon can be viewed as applying approximate orthogonalization to momentum for matrix-valued parameters. More abstractly, it fits into a linear minimization oracle (LMO) framework. This unifies Muon with Scion, SignSGD, and normalized SGD as different geometries of direction selection over norm balls.

The key theoretical observation concerns the expected clipped gradient, μ(g)=E[clip_c(g+ξ)]. The paper shows that it can be decomposed into a positive scalar multiple of the true gradient g plus a residual term r(g) that changes direction. Since an LMO is invariant to positive rescaling, LMO(λg)=LMO(g) for λ>0, the dominant effect of clipping is invisible to the update rule. Only the directional residual enters the optimization error, and that residual is controlled by the centered gradient variance. This is the central idea of the paper. It avoids the usual strong assumption of bounded raw per-example gradients and does not require the clipping threshold to grow with the horizon.

Building on that decomposition, the authors derive a convergence guarantee for private momentum-LMO methods on smooth nonconvex objectives in any finite-dimensional norm. The bound separates optimization error, momentum tracking error, sampling noise, privacy noise, and a clipping floor. The floor scales as ρ1 min{σ, σ^2/c}, where ρ1 is the norm-conversion constant into the relevant dual norm. The interpretation is that passing clipped gradients through an LMO costs at most a geometric conversion factor relative to clipped SGD, rather than introducing a qualitatively new instability. At fixed noise scale, the rate is about O~(K^-1/4); with noise calibrated to an (ε, δ) privacy budget, the dependence matches the familiar DP scaling.

Algorithmically, DP-Muon still follows the standard central example-level DP pipeline: per-example clipping, Gaussian noise addition, averaging, then momentum and Muon orthogonalization. Privacy comes entirely from the first stage; the optimizer-specific part is post-processing. The novelty is therefore not a new privacy mechanism, but an explanation of why Muon-style post-processing remains analyzable under active clipping.e:T5e6,The empirical study has two parts: validation of the theory’s implications and optimizer comparisons under DP. For hidden channel j in block ell, it couples the corresponding row of the MLP up-projection with the corresponding column of the down-projection, and assigns an allocation μ_j^ell in [0,1]. All such channels across all MLP blocks share a single budget K, with sum_{ell,j} μ_j^ell = K. For a fixed allocation, BMuon inserts a diagonal reweighting into Muon’s spectral-norm constraint. The allocation is then chosen globally to maximize first-order loss decrease, so channels across layers compete for the same budget rather than being controlled by fixed layerwise sparsity targets. The paper shows that the resulting oracle is exactly normalized steepest descent under a norm Ω_{α,K}. The parameter α controls the strength of channel reweighting, and K controls the global channel budget. The construction recovers Muon when α=0 or K=N. This differs from NuMuon: NuMuon targets low-rank compressibility through a nuclear-norm constraint, while BMuon targets structured hidden-channel compressibility.
