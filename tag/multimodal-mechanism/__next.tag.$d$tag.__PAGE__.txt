9:T804,The method has three parts. First, the authors introduce an evidence-grounded protocol to identify OCR heads. They decode with greedy autoregression, then replay attention along the model’s own generated trajectory instead of teacher-forcing on reference tokens. Predictions are aligned to references at the character level, and only tokens fully contained in exact-match spans are kept for scoring. For each aligned visible span, they define a visual evidence set using patch regions whose IoU with annotated evidence exceeds 0.1. A head gets an OCR hit if its top-attended key falls inside the evidence set for the current generated token. Candidate OCR heads are then selected using sample-level hit rates and activation frequency across examples.

Second, they separate discovery from validation and perform held-out causal interventions. The identified OCR heads are ablated on a disjoint validation split, and the resulting degradation is compared against matched random head ablations. This is important because it distinguishes heads that merely correlate with evidence from heads that are causally important for transcription.

Third, they investigate the origin of these OCR heads. Heads found from visual transcription are compared against retrieval/copy heads independently identified from text-only retrieval tasks. The comparison includes top-k overlap, all-head rank correlation, and cross-task causal effects. The main finding is strong overlap in general VLMs. They also compare models before and after OCR tuning and show that the dominant head population and layer organization are largely preserved, while functional and causal importance is redistributed. In other words, specialization is closer to reweighting than rewiring.

Relative to prior work, the paper moves beyond localized visual retrieval tasks, studies full-sequence transcription, and combines attention-based identification with held-out causal validation. It also frames OCR as reuse of a broader retrieval/copy mechanism rather than as an isolated visual skill.a:T6a5,The experiments cover three OCR-specialized models—GLM-OCR, MinerU2.5, and PaddleOCR-VL-1.6—and two general VLMs for the reuse analysis. First, OCR-relevant heads are sparse and stable. Across running text, tables, and formulas, the authors identify small reproducible head sets whose targeted ablation hurts transcription substantially more than matched random ablations. That supports causal relevance rather than incidental correlation.

Second, visual OCR heads strongly overlap with textual retrieval/copy heads. The abstract reports untuned top-20 intersections of 73.3% in two general VLMs, with all-head Spearman correlations ranging from 0.677 to 0.886. Those are strong numbers for a mechanistic overlap claim.

Third, OCR tuning preserves the main head population and layer layout while changing relative importance. The paper argues that specialization does not create a new circuit family; instead, it amplifies and redistributes the role of an existing one. The excerpt does not include the full tables, but the narrative suggests this pattern is consistent across model generations and OCR subdomains.

And the analysis spans text, tables, and formulas, which makes the mechanism claim less likely to be an artifact of plain text OCR.
