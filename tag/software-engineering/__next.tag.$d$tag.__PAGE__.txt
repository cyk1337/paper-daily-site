9:T4ec,The paper proposes and documents the Salt method as a disciplined workflow rather than a new learning algorithm.
- Fixed deliverable bundle per iteration: (i) implementation, (ii) formal specification, (iii) kernel-checked proof that the implementation meets the spec, (iv) tests including adversarial controls to ensure the proof has “bite,” and (v) certificates—simplified restatements of the spec designed for human comprehension.
- The key contract is implication: the kernel proves the formal statement entails the certificate statement, so the certificate can only be weaker than what was proved. This makes statement-level human review tractable without trusting the full formal development.
- Emphasis on specification risk: correctness is meaningless if the spec is wrong; therefore the workflow centers on active interrogation, where the human queries the system and receives kernel-checked evidence (unfolding binders, justifying hypotheses, producing variants under changed assumptions).
- End-to-end verified stack culminating in a RISC-V tapeout, with strict provenance (commit-verified theorem trails) and append-only error logs.
