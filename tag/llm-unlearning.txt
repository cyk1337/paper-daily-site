17:T501,The "margin cliff" phenomenon is first documented: across 14 unlearning methods spanning gradient-based, preference-based, and distillation-based families, converged unlearned models have per-token answer margins (log-probability gap between the gold token and its strongest competitor) on forget samples that cluster in a narrow band above the retain-only reference model's margin, rather than falling to the reference level. The position of this margin directly predicts how easily content can be recovered via relearn attacks. KKT stationarity analysis shows the cliff arises when the retain regularizer holds the diagnostic log-odds of forget content above a floor, a condition induced by token-saturating losses whose gradients vanish once forget tokens are suppressed. Margin Calibration (MC) is a non-saturating correction: a softplus hinge loss anchored at the reference model's per-token margin, whose gradient scales with the margin gap and thus does not vanish even when forget tokens are suppressed. A KL constraint on a disjoint instruction corpus limits drift on non-forget behavior. MC runs as a short LoRA polish on any already-unlearned model, with a deployment-phase variant that anchors at the pre-unlearning model instead, requiring no retain-trained reference.18:T409,The proposed method, Mansu, stands for Mechanistic-Aligned Null-Space Unlearning. It has three components. First, EAP-IG circuit attribution identifies a sparse subgraph that causally supports the forget-set answers, avoiding uniform updates across the whole model. Second, updates are restricted to this circuit and projected into the null space of the retain-set Fisher information, aiming to reduce forget-set performance while bounding damage to retained capabilities. Third, a magnitude floor tied to the NF4 bin width is applied to cumulative updates, so that the relevant weight changes survive 4-bit quantization by construction. The paper also introduces Circuit Attribution Divergence as a post-hoc mechanistic check for whether the knowledge circuit has collapsed rather than being bypassed.
