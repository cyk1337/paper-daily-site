18:T7dc,The setup is deliberately controlled. Each document is a sequence of assignment statements of the form [ent] [attr] [val] [SEP], followed by a query [QUERY] [ent] [attr] [ARROW]; the target is the value currently bound to that slot. Bindings are resampled per document from disjoint vocabularies, and the stream is infinite, so the task cannot be solved by memorizing weights. For the queried slot, an old value v_old is written and then repeated R_old times, later replaced by a new value v_new written once, and the query appears ΔD statements after the final update. Because the current value appears exactly once while the superseded value appears R_old times, “most recent” and “rarest” always select the same answer on training data.

To read out the mechanism, the authors introduce a minimal causal edit: they invert the multiplicities of the competing values while holding the ground truth, total token count, answer position, and all other tokens fixed. They then measure the signed change in answer log-odds, Δ. Positive Δ indicates sensitivity consistent with rarity; negative Δ indicates that repetition boosts the value that should be suppressed. This is stronger than a correlational probe because it directly tests causal sensitivity to one statistic while preserving task semantics.

A second technical contribution is the analysis of an early training shortcut. Before a retrieval circuit forms, models learn to copy a value from a fixed token offset near the end of the document. That strategy depends on layout regularities rather than slot matching plus temporal retrieval. Its accuracy ceiling is predicted by 1/|supp(ΔD)|, and the paper shows the models nearly saturate that ceiling when the support is narrow. Applying the intervention during this phase yields strongly negative Δ, which would look like a clean frequency-sensitive regime if one ignored training stage. The paper uses this to argue that mechanism readouts must be interpreted jointly with training dynamics.19:T613,The main experiments use 26M-parameter transformers, with 75 total runs across redundancy settings. Several findings stand out. First, every analyzed cell reaches held-in accuracy of at least 0.999, including strata where the trivial heuristic “the last update is the answer” fails. So standard evaluation cannot distinguish opposite internal dependencies on repetition count. Second, under the causal edit, the same nominal configuration can separate sharply across seeds: in 13 of 25 cells, the sign fraction differs by more than 0.3 across three seeds, with the largest difference reaching 0.879, while the document-level standard error is only 0.025. That gap is too large to attribute to sampling noise. Third, within a single run, post-formation checkpoints can still vary substantially, with within-run standard deviation up to 0.303; doubling the training schedule also moves cells that the data distribution does not distinguish in advance. By contrast, the timing of retrieval formation remains stable across these comparisons. Fourth, a control edit on a non-queried slot changes the same number of tokens but stays within 0.015 nats, supporting the specificity of the intervention. Fifth, when pooled across seeds, sign fraction increases monotonically with redundancy level through at least R_old = 12, showing that redundancy changes the tendency of the readout without uniquely fixing the mechanism. The paper also explains why R_old = 1 and 2 are excluded and why 16 is the structural upper bound under the document layout constraints.1a:T48d,Start with the setup and the exact equivalence construction. The paper is best read as a cautionary note for mechanistic claims: before attributing a circuit to a corpus statistic, check whether the training distribution ever made the alternatives distinguishable.1b:T66e,The method is a two-stage Diagnose-and-Generate loop. In the first stage, Diagnose & Allocate, the system estimates the effective supervision yield of the current practice distribution: given the current student and a set of prompts, how likely is it that sampled rollouts will contain both correct and incorrect trajectories, producing a usable preference pair? Rather than making topic-level decisions from raw counts, DIAG uses Empirical Bayes shrinkage to stabilize estimates under low sample counts. This prevents overreacting to noisy topics that happen to look good or bad due to small numbers. It also allocates budget between exploration and exploitation, so the curriculum does not collapse too early onto a narrow subset of topics.

In the second stage, the teacher generates follow-up problems conditioned on the student’s recent mistakes. This is the key departure from standard active learning. Instead of selecting uncertain prompts from a static candidate pool, DIAG actively creates new prompts that target the student’s current failure modes. The paper also uses uncertainty-weighted resampling to concentrate compute near the competence boundary rather than spreading it uniformly.

Conceptually, the method optimizes two quantities at once: pair yield and pair quality. Pair yield determines how much supervision is available per unit budget. Pair quality determines whether preference learning happens on examples that are actually informative for the current student. Compared with brute-force scaling of rollout width, DIAG tries to increase the probability that each sampled problem produces useful contrastive signal.1c:T5af,From the provided full-text content, the experimental emphasis is not on a single benchmark headline but on showing that DIAG produces more informative preference pairs under the same or lower budget, and converts that higher signal density into better mathematical reasoning performance. The introduction explicitly claims that static practice distributions cause a rapid decline in natural contrastive supervision, while DIAG improves preference-pair yield through signal-aware pacing, Empirical Bayes topic scoring, and mistake-conditioned generation.

Because the supplied text does not include the full result tables, the confirmed findings are directional rather than fully numeric. First, fixed prompt pools become less effective over iterations as the student improves. Second, robust topic allocation outperforms greedy allocation based on raw observed yields. Third, mistake-conditioned generation is better than practice-agnostic generation at staying near the student’s competence boundary, which increases the chance of obtaining discriminative preference supervision. When reading the full paper, the most important numbers to verify are: the valid-pair rate per iteration, final benchmark gains under matched training budgets, and the variance or stability of different topic-allocation strategies. - Controlled setup: keep the student always in non-thinking mode during both training and evaluation; vary only the teacher’s generation mode (thinking vs non-thinking) and the teacher’s access to references (correct, incorrect, none, or from other problems).
- Decomposition: for trajectories generated by the student at checkpoint c, compare next-token distributions:

  Using log-probability vectors, decompose total teacher supervision direction:

The motivation is straightforward. If training starts only from the composite task, supervision is too sparse to teach which intermediate forms are useful for certificate assembly. The supporting tasks provide denser algebraic supervision and decompose the search skill into learnable pieces.

The second layer is supervised fine-tuning. Starting from Phi-4-reasoning-plus, they apply assistant-token QLoRA SFT on direct solutions and simulated symbolic traces. In other words, the model first learns the semantics and trajectory patterns of tool use rather than memorizing an API protocol. Native tool calling is introduced only at deployment through four SymPy-backed functions. This separation is methodologically clean: it tries to teach when and why to use tools, not just how to format a call.

The third layer is verifier-grounded GRPO. The model is further optimized with task-specific symbolic rewards. For weighted-SOS, a terminal candidate is accepted only if it satisfies strict structural constraints: it parses into a finite sum, every weight is an exact positive rational, every summand is a polynomial square, and the expanded identity matches the target polynomial coefficient by coefficient. The key point is that reward is anchored to decidable correctness rather than fuzzy preference signals. Compared with generic math RL, the room for reward hacking is smaller because terminal acceptance is exact.

The main novelty is not a new optimization algorithm. It is the full loop: curriculum-style algebra supervision, simulated traces, native symbolic tools, and exact verifier-based rewards. Unlike many tool-use agent papers, this one deliberately stays inside a fully checkable algebraic domain, which makes it easier to separate tool reliability from strategy failure. The paper is also careful not to overclaim beyond this setting.20:T80c,The experimental setup is fairly clear. Training uses 1.35 million synthetic examples spanning eight supporting tasks plus weighted-SOS. Evaluation uses 90,000 separately generated test instances. The complete system reaches 91.75% macro-average across the nine polynomial tasks. On the main weighted-SOS task, the Full system achieves 78.96% verified success, compared with 44.73% for the same backbone with the same tool interface but without the full post-training pipeline. That gap is the central empirical result: tool access alone is not the main source of gain; the algebra curriculum and verifier-grounded optimization are.

The paper compares Base, SFT, Base+Tools, and Full. The ablation is informative but not fully causal. In particular, it does not include SFT+Tools without GRPO or GRPO without tools, so the independent contribution of each component cannot be isolated cleanly. Still, the existing comparisons are enough to support the claim that simply attaching tools is insufficient, while the full training recipe materially improves composite symbolic search.

Two details matter for interpretation. First, the eight supporting tasks are solved directly without native tool calls in both training and evaluation, so their scores should be read as evidence of learned algebraic priors and intermediate-form competence, not direct evidence of tool-execution skill. Second, every weighted-SOS test polynomial is SOS by construction. Therefore, failure does not mean the instance is non-SOS; it means the search failed to find a certificate. That makes error analysis cleaner because the issue is search quality, not task infeasibility.

The paper also reports terminal failure categories.
