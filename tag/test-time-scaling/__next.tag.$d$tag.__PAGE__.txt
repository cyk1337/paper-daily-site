It first samples n complete trajectories from the same generator, then scores them with a reward model. Instead of using an absolute reward threshold, it converts rewards into rank-normalized scores in [0,1], and applies two thresholds, theta_low and theta_high, to route candidates into Keep, Refine, or Discard. The motivation is practical: absolute reward scales are often unstable across tasks and prompts, while rank is more robust.

The main novelty is the refinement stage. For borderline candidates routed to Refine, SRD does not resample the whole trajectory. It scans the trajectory at fixed intervals m, evaluates prefix-level reward behavior, and finds the earliest point j where quality appears to degrade relative to the preceding prefix. It then truncates the suffix after j and regenerates only that suffix, using a higher sampling temperature to encourage local exploration and avoid repeating the same failure mode. The repaired trajectory is rescored and reranked jointly with the remaining candidates, and is kept only if it survives the final routing.

The paper also provides a theoretical analysis. Under mild assumptions, SRD achieves strictly higher expected best-trajectory quality than rejection sampling and improves sample efficiency by about 1.28–1.36x, with gains increasing as the candidate pool grows. The gain is characterized by a term of the form (1 + rho * p_M / p_H), where p_M and p_H capture the mass of repairable medium-quality samples and already-high-quality samples, and rho reflects refinement success. This matters because it clarifies when SRD should help: only when there is a meaningful population of salvageable trajectories and the reward signal can identify degradation reliably.

Relative to prior work, best-of-N only selects at the end and never recovers partial structure; speculative rejection can stop early but is irreversible once a prefix is rejected; Controlled Decoding uses a learned prefix-aware value function for token-level steering, whereas SRD needs only a generator and a reward model, with no larger teacher and no separately trained value function. The trade-off is that SRD still requires full-trajectory sampling up front and extra reward evaluations, and its boundary detection is fairly coarse, based on interval scanning rather than token-level attribution.10:T6b0,The experiments cover MATH500, GPQA, HotpotQA, and AlpacaEval, spanning mathematical reasoning, science QA, multi-hop QA, and instruction following. The clearest number stated in the paper is the theoretical 1.28–1.36x sample-efficiency gain. In Stage 1, the model samples K full solution traces. Each trace must output not only a final answer but also exactly M decision-critical claims: concise intermediate propositions whose failure would undermine the answer. These are meant to be semantic anchors such as key constraints, transformations, maximality conditions, or intermediate conclusions, not generic summaries or answer restatements. The purpose is to compress a long reasoning trace into a small set of high-information, checkable propositions. In Stage 2, the same model is not asked to solve the problem again. Instead, it receives only the original problem and the extracted claims, and it actively searches for contradictions, counterexamples, missing conditions, factual mistakes, or unsupported inferences. The central design choice is falsification rather than proof. There is a useful asymmetry here: constructing a fully correct solution is hard, but invalidating a wrong trace only requires finding one decisive flaw. After verification, CLR maps claim-level verdicts into a nonlinear trace-level reliability score and aggregates answer groups by total reliability. This allows a reliable minority to overturn an incorrect majority. Relative to self-consistency, the method reallocates part of the budget from generating more traces to verifying the semantic anchors of existing traces. Relative to step-level verification, it avoids exhaustive checking and does not require process supervision or a separately trained verifier.15:T4ca,The evaluation spans four LLMs and multiple difficult reasoning benchmarks, with comparisons made under matched model-call budgets.
