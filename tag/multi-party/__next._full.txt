18:T6cc,The work has three main components. First is a data engine for synthetic long-form conversation. It combines persona- and memory-conditioned dialogue planning, multi-speaker bilingual TTS, and acoustically faithful multi-channel mixing to produce parallel-stream audio with codec-frame-level alignment suitable for Moshi-style training. The important point is that the generator is not just reading text dialogues aloud. It explicitly controls conversation length, number of participants, turn-taking, overlap, backchannels, interruptions, addressee shifts, and long-range coreference, so the training corpus contains the structural patterns that define real multi-party interaction.

Second is MultiTalkBench, an interactive benchmark for end-to-end speech-to-speech systems. It jointly evaluates three axes: long conversations beyond ten minutes with probes for entity tracking and topic coherence, one-model-many-user interaction with metrics for addressee selection and turn-taking, and English-Chinese bilingual ability. This differs from prior long-audio benchmarks, which mostly test passive understanding rather than interactive generation, and from prior speech-to-speech benchmarks, which are usually short and dyadic.

Third is a bilingual Moshi-style full-duplex model trained on the new corpus. The paper stays within the end-to-end unified modeling paradigm rather than reverting to an ASR→LLM→TTS pipeline. For pretraining researchers, the main question is how changing the training distribution changes behavior: with both input and output represented in codec-token space, long context, multi-party interaction, and bilingual switching become part of the language modeling objective itself rather than external modules.19:T469,The strongest asset is the released 57.6k-hour synthetic training corpus, which the authors claim exceeds the combined size of prior open full-duplex speech corpora. On the model side, they train a bilingual Moshi-style full-duplex system and report substantial gains over open baselines including Moshi, MiniCPM-o-4.5, and Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct on MultiTalkBench. The excerpt provided does not include the full result tables, but the introduction makes clear that evaluation spans long-horizon interaction, multi-party interaction, and bilingual conversation rather than only short-turn fluency.

When reading the full paper, three result types are worth prioritizing. First, scaling behavior: whether long-context and multi-party abilities improve smoothly with more synthetic data. Second, data-component ablations: what happens when overlap, addressee shifts, memory conditioning, or bilingual mixing are removed. Third, attribution of gains: whether improvements come mainly from corpus scale, richer interaction structure, or model changes.
