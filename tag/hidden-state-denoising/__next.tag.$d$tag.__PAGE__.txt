9:T4bc,The method, DiHAL, follows a Locate-and-Replace strategy. In the locate stage, the paper avoids exhaustive training of a diffusion bridge at every layer. Instead, it defines geometry-based proxies for each hidden-state space: local compactness, global stiffness, and effective rank. These are intended to capture whether a layer’s representation is easy to denoise, stable under imperfect score estimation, and not overly complex. After selecting a layer, DiHAL replaces the lower transformer prefix with a conditional diffusion bridge. The bridge does not predict tokens and does not reconstruct embeddings directly; it reconstructs the hidden state at the chosen internal layer. The reconstructed state is then passed through the retained upper transformer layers and the original LM head for token prediction. This design matters for two reasons. It delegates the difficult continuous-to-discrete mapping to the already trained upper network, and it turns language diffusion into hidden-state reconstruction rather than direct text recovery. The paper also proposes a fixed geometry score that predicts promising insertion layers under a shallow one-epoch bridge-training protocol, reducing the search cost.a:T4be,Experiments are conducted on 8B-scale backbones. The empirical goal is not broad benchmark dominance but a controlled test of two claims: whether the geometry score can identify good insertion layers, and whether hidden-state interfaces outperform embedding-level, latent-level, and continuous-to-discrete diffusion interfaces under a fixed diffusion/recovery budget. The reported findings are that the geometry score correlates strongly with hidden-state reconstruction quality after a lightweight bridge-training setup, and that effective insertion points tend to be relatively shallow rather than deep. That is an important result: later semantic layers are not automatically better diffusion targets. On the generation side, DiHAL improves perplexity and diversity over several alternative interfaces, supporting the idea that reconstructing an internal state and letting the remaining AR stack decode it is more robust than direct token-space recovery.
