9:T7ec,The method centers on building long-horizon reflective trajectories. A task environment is formalized as e=(sigma, S), where sigma is the task specification and S is an executable scoring function. The agent works in the same environment for multiple rounds. In each round it proposes a candidate solution y_t, receives feedback f_t, and decides how to revise the solution next.

The data pipeline has three stages. First, a teacher model converts raw source material from GitHub and online judges into executable environments. In machine learning engineering, these environments include training pipelines, validation metrics, and task constraints. In algorithmic programming, they include problem statements, tests, and judging logic. Second, an agent interacts with each environment over many rounds, generating revision trajectories. Third, trajectories are selected as whole sequences rather than filtered step by step. A trajectory is kept if its final score is high and the process satisfies task requirements, even if intermediate rounds contain failures, regressions, or dead ends. This is the key design choice in the paper, because it preserves the behavioral signal of recovering from setbacks and continuing to improve.

AREX-2 is trained from Qwen3.8-27B on these long-horizon trajectories together with the deep-research data used in the earlier AREX recipe. The underlying hypothesis is that reflection and long-horizon execution are meta-skills. Therefore, the best training domains are not necessarily those that look most similar to the target domain, but those that provide clean supervision, verifiable feedback, and room for sustained improvement.

Compared with common agent-training pipelines, the differences are clear: the target behavior is multi-round improvement rather than one-shot success; the filtering unit is the full trajectory rather than individual successful steps; and domain choice is driven by supervision quality and verifiability rather than surface similarity to the downstream task.a:T54e,The empirical section is the main strength of the paper. Starting from Qwen3.8-27B, AREX-2 reaches 81.8 on MLE-bench Lite and 70.7 on Frontier-CS, with the latter reported as the best among the open-weight models compared in the paper. More importantly, the gains transfer beyond the training domains. Without adding new deep-research-specific training data, AREX-2 achieves 84.0 on BrowseComp, 52.6 on HLE, 92.2 on GAIA, and 93.8 on DeepSearchQA. That supports the claim that long-horizon reflection learned in verifiable environments can generalize to more open-ended research tasks.

Two experimental patterns matter. First, performance continues to improve as the round budget increases, which suggests the model has learned to convert extra test-time budget into better solutions instead of stalling early. Second, the model is only 27B, yet compares favorably with substantially larger systems, implying that the gain is tied to the training recipe and data rather than scale alone.
