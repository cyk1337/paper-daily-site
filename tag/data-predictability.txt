18:T638,The setup has three main ingredients. First, the paper adopts the earlier observation that magnitudes of trained Transformer linear weights are well summarized by a Weibull distribution, with a shape parameter k that stays nearly constant across layers and models, around 1.2. That reduces the training-side movement to the scale parameter λ. Second, on the data side, the authors use bigram conditional entropy D = H(next|prev) as a training-free measure of local next-token predictability. To normalize against a matched randomization baseline, they define a predictability margin (Hr - D), where Hr is the entropy reached by matched-budget shuffling. Third, they fit an empirical law
λ^2 - λ0^2 = C0(η) + C1(η)(Hr - D)^0.59,
where learning rate η only affects the two coefficients C0 and C1. A notable claim is that the exponent 0.59 is not chosen by directly fitting the growth curve; it is tied to an independently measured data-side saturation exponent p ≈ 1.69 through 1/p.

The paper is careful about scope. It does not claim D is a universal currency across corpora. The law is established within a fixed corpus while moving along a corruption gradient that changes the next-token mapping. The authors also extend the analysis from whole-model λ to per-block λ. The Weibull shape k remains nearly uniform, but the scale response is heterogeneous and concentrated in feed-forward blocks. Because k is stable, the second moment <w^2> = λ^2 Γ(1 + 2/k) is approximately additive, which lets pooled λ^2 be approximated by a parameter-count-weighted average of blockwise λ^2.19:T4b7,The main empirical results are fairly specific. Across 23 runs spanning roughly an order of magnitude in learning rate, after removing the per-η coefficients C0 and C1, the data collapse onto y = (Hr - D)^0.59 with slope 1.00 and R^2 = 0.941. The paper explicitly distinguishes this collapse result from direct predictive accuracy: the stricter per-learning-rate prediction has R^2 around 0.82. In held-out end-to-end self-validation, predicting λ from the pre-training data statistic alone yields about 5.7% relative error. At the block level, the same functional form still works with block-specific coefficients, and the median per-block R^2 is about 0.84. The Weibull shape parameter stays in a narrow range, roughly 1.16 to 1.21 across blocks. On scope tests, mapping-rich text remains broadly compatible with the relation, while code is systematically over-predicted, which motivates the authors' proposal that redundancy is a second data dimension missing from D.

What matters most is the experimental control. The paper fixes the corpus and budget, then varies predictability through controlled corruption. That makes the observed relation more informative than a loose cross-dataset correlation.1a:T402,Read the introduction first for the exact problem framing and, more importantly, the boundary conditions. The paper is easy to overread as a universal law, but it is explicitly a within-corpus result. Then go to the section with the main growth law and the learning-rate collapse. Check carefully what is shared across runs and what is fitted separately per learning rate. After that, read the section deriving the 0.59 exponent from an independently measured data-side saturation relation; this is the most methodologically interesting part. Then skim the held-out self-validation and the per-block analysis. The former tells you whether the claim is genuinely forward-predictive; the latter shows where in the network the scale response concentrates. Finish with the limitations section, especially the discussion of code and redundancy.
