9:T6cd,The core method is replay pairing. For a fixed trajectory, the authors run two matched conditions: one keeps the original plan in history, the other replays the same trajectory with the plan removed from history. At each step and layer, they measure cosine distance between hidden states across the two runs. This distance is treated as a plan-fidelity signal. If the model has internalized the plan, removing the text should not quickly change later hidden states. If the model depends on rereading the plan from context, the difference should be large near the plan and then collapse once the plan is no longer actively represented.

They test this on ReAct agents built from Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, replicate within family on Llama-3.1-8B, and compare against DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B. A key methodological contribution is the reasoning-trace confound. In reasoning models, prior <think> blocks often restate the plan. If one removes only the explicit plan text, both replay conditions may still contain plan information, making the measured distance artificially small. Their strict stripping fix removes prior <think> blocks from the plan-removed condition, yielding a cleaner estimate of whether the plan remains available in context.

They also train Ridge probes on hidden states to predict signal magnitude and classify active versus decayed plans. The best layer is L32 in Llama. Probe transfer from Llama to R1 is only partial, and self-probes indicate that R1 uses a substantially different representational direction. The paper is careful about scope: these are representational claims, not strong causal claims about behavior. The authors explicitly note confounds from step index, history length, and discourse structure.a:T721,On ALFWorld with Llama-3.1-70B, the plan signal peaks at 0.453 one step after the plan is written, then drops by 4.1x within a single action-observation cycle, settling near 0.027 by step +5. On HotpotQA, the decay is even faster at 12.4x. This is the main empirical basis for the claim that plans behave like context-time objects rather than persistent internal state.

The probe analysis is strong numerically but also revealing about confounds. A Ridge probe at L32 predicts signal strength with R^2 = 0.875 and separates active from decayed plans with AUROC 0.999. But step index is also almost perfectly decodable at the same layer, with R^2 = 0.978, so part of the classification power is clearly tied to trajectory phase.

For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B, naive replay pairing suggests a much smaller step+1 signal than Llama, 0.022 versus 0.099, which would imply weak plan encoding. After strict stripping, the signal increases by 163% in-sample and 153% on held-out data, while Llama changes by only 4.8%, supporting the claim that the original measurement was contaminated by reasoning traces. A Llama-trained L32 probe transferred to strict-stripped R1 states reaches AUROC 0.748 with n = 19 and p = 6e-4. R1 self-probes reach 1.000 AUROC on two distilled families, but along a direction 89.3 degrees away from Llama’s, suggesting near-orthogonal representational geometry.
