18:T836,The core method adapts activation patching to the vision encoder and measures how much a single layer-token activation restores the final image-text similarity score. The procedure has three runs. In the clean run, the model sees the original image and produces a similarity score s_clean. In the corrupted run, the image is perturbed with Gaussian noise and yields s_corr. In the patched run, the corrupted image is used again, but one intermediate activation at layer l and token t is replaced by its clean counterpart, producing s_patch. From these three scores, the paper defines a causal contribution Γ(l,t), interpreted as the fraction of the clean-corrupted gap recovered by restoring that activation. This is stronger than attention visualization or gradient saliency because it measures functional recovery, not just correlation.

The second key step is to connect causal importance to spatial grounding. Since ViT tokens correspond to fixed image patches, each token is mapped back to its patch region Pt, and the overlap with the ground-truth object box B is measured using IoU(Pt, B). For each layer, the paper computes the correlation between Γ and IoU, denoted Corr_l. If Corr_l is high, causally important tokens tend to fall on the target region. If it is low, the model may still perform well while relying on evidence outside the queried object. This layerwise analysis is important because it asks whether spatial grounding emerges progressively through the encoder or never becomes causally dominant.

The full text indicates that the analysis starts with CLIP as a controlled setting and then extends to larger VLMs, multiple corruption settings, and different generations of visual encoders, including ViT-based and SigLIP-based systems. The paper also probes whether models preserve visual structure when appearance cues are removed. The answer is nuanced: visual cues are indeed used for structural understanding, but that does not imply localized causal grounding. This is a meaningful distinction from prior work that relies mostly on attention maps or attribution heatmaps.19:T6d2,The clearest findings in the provided full content are threefold. First, on CLIP, the authors run token- and layer-level causal tracing over 100 samples and find only a weak relationship between causal contribution and target-box IoU. The qualitative conclusion is explicit: highly causal tokens often do not lie inside the target region. In other words, the activations that recover the image-text score are frequently outside the object referred to by the text.

Second, this is not presented as a CLIP-specific artifact. The same pattern is reported in larger VLMs from 2023 onward and under different corruption procedures. So increased model scale and stronger multimodal benchmark performance do not automatically produce better spatial causal alignment. The excerpt does not include the full numeric tables, but the textual claim is clear that the phenomenon persists across models.

Third, the paper studies structure understanding under reduced appearance cues and finds that models do use visual information to infer structure. That matters because it rules out the simplest interpretation that the models are ignoring the image altogether. The issue is subtler: they use visual evidence, but the evidence that is causally important is not necessarily localized where a human would expect it to be.

From an experimental-design perspective, the useful details are the layerwise Corr_l analysis, reporting mean and standard deviation across seeds, and comparisons across encoder families. Then read the part that maps tokens back to image patches and computes IoU and Corr_l. This is what makes the paper a causal grounding analysis rather than a standard saliency study.

In the results, focus on three kinds of figures. First, token heatmaps on individual examples, which make the off-target causal tokens visually obvious. Second, layerwise Corr_l curves, which show whether spatial grounding emerges anywhere in the encoder. The most interesting follow-up questions are practical. Would the conclusion hold under occlusion, patch dropout, or frequency-domain corruption instead of Gaussian noise? Does the same mismatch appear when the target is a generative VLM next-token logit rather than an image-text similarity score? And can region-level alignment losses, object-centric crops, or stronger visual discretization constraints materially increase Corr_l during pretraining? Those are the places where this paper connects back to model design.1b:T747,方法核心有两个表示层 readout，都是用“策略相关的反事实干预”来做校准目标。第一个 readout 作用在 prompt state 上，预测 closed-book sufficiency，也就是当前策略在不检索时是否已经足够回答；其补量就是 retrieval necessity。第二个 readout 作用在 answer commitment 阶段，估计 evidence reliance：把已检索证据删除后，答案是否会变化，用这种 answer-stage sensitivity 作为 operational grounding 的代理。

关键点不在于直接在线做反事实，而在于先用干预构造标签，再训练轻量 readout 去近似这些判断。这样 rollout 时只需读取 policy representation，就能得到 necessity 和 reliance 分数，避免了过程标注和额外 LLM 调用。

奖励设计是 correctness-first 且 normalization-aware 的。正确的 searched trajectory，只有在 necessity 高时，reliance credit 才有意义；正确的 direct trajectory，则拿 closed-book sufficiency 的信用；重复搜索会有有界惩罚。这个设计针对 GRPO 一类 group-relative 更新里的一个实际问题：如果一个 group 里样本都答对，纯 outcome reward 会让 advantage 退化到接近 0，训练无法区分“少搜、必要搜、证据驱动搜”和“乱搜但也答对”。Search-G1 用 shaped reward 在这些 tied groups 里恢复了可学习差异。

另一个重要机制是 periodic refitting。因为 readout 依赖 policy representation，而 RL 会持续改变表示空间，所以 necessity/reliance 估计器如果固定不动，很快就会失准。论文把 search-agent 训练看成 measurement-credit-adaptation 的闭环：定期用最新策略生成轨迹，重新做校准并重训 readout，让奖励测量始终跟着 policy knowledge boundary 走。这一点比静态 reward model 更贴近实际训练动态。1c:T7ae,Search-G1 builds two representation-based readouts, both calibrated using policy-relative counterfactual interventions. The first readout operates on the prompt state and predicts closed-book sufficiency: whether the current policy could answer correctly without retrieval. Its complement is retrieval necessity. The second readout operates near answer commitment and estimates evidence reliance by measuring whether the answer would change if retrieved evidence were removed. This is framed as an operational proxy for grounding rather than a full causal certificate.

The key design choice is to use interventions for calibration, not for online reward computation. Counterfactual tests are run offline to create labels, and lightweight readouts are trained to predict those labels from the policy’s own representations. At rollout time, the reward can therefore be computed cheaply, without process supervision or extra model calls.

The trajectory reward is correctness-first and normalization-aware. Correct searched trajectories receive reliance credit only when retrieval was necessary. Correct direct-answer trajectories receive credit for closed-book sufficiency. Repeated search incurs a bounded penalty. This directly addresses a practical issue in group-relative RL such as GRPO: when all trajectories in a group are correct, pure outcome reward yields near-zero advantage and cannot separate efficient grounded search from redundant search. Search-G1 restores reward variation inside these tied groups.

A second important component is periodic refitting. Because the readouts depend on policy representations, and RL changes those representations over time, a frozen estimator will drift out of alignment.
