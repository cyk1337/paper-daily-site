9:T624,SkillGym is built around a skill-to-task pipeline. The input is a registry of human-written skills spanning 12 major categories and 63 sub-categories. Each skill is first organized into a structured skill card containing descriptions, examples, pseudocode or scripts, and execution constraints. For each sub-category, the authors manually design reusable templates that instantiate concrete tasks from these reusable workflows, including input assets, interaction interfaces, target conditions, and code-based verifiers. This turns a static skill document into an executable environment.

A key design choice is contrastive validation. Under a reference agent configuration, the system runs tasks with and without the target skill and keeps tasks that show empirical skill dependence. This matters because many tasks associated with a skill document may still be solvable by generic capability alone. Without this filtering step, the resulting supervision would overstate the contribution of the skill itself.

Once environments are validated, the authors collect successful trajectories using multiple teacher models and harnesses. These trajectories can support supervised fine-tuning and outcome-based reinforcement learning. The paper mainly demonstrates the SFT path: verified long-horizon workflows are used to fine-tune Qwen3.5-35B-A3B into SkillGym-Agent. The novelty is less about a new RL algorithm and more about closing the loop from human workflow documents to instantiated tasks, verifiers, contrastive dependency checks, and high-quality interaction traces.a:T670,The scale of the resource is substantial: 2,756 environments across 12 major categories and 63 sub-categories, plus 8,364 successful trajectories. Each trajectory averages 49 tool calls and more than 60k logged text tokens, which indicates genuinely long-horizon, multi-tool interaction rather than short completion-style supervision.

On the model side, supervised fine-tuning of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on these trajectories yields broad gains over the base model. The abstract reports, under Claude Code, +199 Elo on GDPval-AA v2, +19.10 percentage points on Terminal-Bench 2.1, and +28.13 / +12.38 points on SkillsBench v1.1 with and without skills, respectively. Another notable result is that on skill-assisted SkillsBench, the 35B model reaches 51.47%, exceeding the reported scores listed in the paper for Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.4 Mini, and DeepSeek V4 Pro (Preview). The more interesting claim is not the leaderboard comparison, but that the trained model still outperforms its skill-assisted base even when no inference-time skills are provided. That supports the paper’s central argument about internalization rather than better external skill usage.

When reading the full experiments, the most important checks are: (1) whether gains are consistent across harnesses, so the result is not an artifact of one execution stack; (2) how much improvement remains in the no-skill setting, which is the cleanest evidence of reusable procedural competence; and (3) ablations on environment filtering and teacher sampling, to verify that the benefit comes from skill-dependent verified trajectories rather than simply adding more long-form SFT data.b:T51e,For a first pass, start with Figure 2 and the main SkillGym pipeline. Once you understand the five stages—skill cards, template instantiation, verifiers, contrastive skill-dependence filtering, and trajectory sampling—the method is mostly clear.

Then read the environment construction details. The most important parts are the template design and verifier implementation, because they determine whether the tasks are executable, scorable, and scalable. If your interest is data generation rather than benchmark ranking, this section matters more than the final headline numbers.

In experiments, prioritize Table 4 and the SkillsBench results. The key questions are not the absolute scores, but whether the model still improves without inference-time skills and whether the gains hold across different harnesses. Those two points are the strongest support for the internalization claim.

A 20-minute skim is enough to capture the main idea. If you work on agent post-training data pipelines, spend more time on the appendix examples, failure cases, and the teacher/harness combinations.
