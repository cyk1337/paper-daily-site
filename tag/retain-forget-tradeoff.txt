18:T7e8,The setup is deliberately controlled. All models are trained on English Wikipedia, about 3.7B tokens, with article-level topic labels used to define three domains: a forget domain consisting of Biology articles, about 3.7% of tokens; an adjacent domain including Medicine and Health, Chemistry, and Earth and Environment; and a retain domain containing the rest. The architecture is fixed across runs: a 254M-parameter GPT-Neo-style transformer with 16 layers, hidden size 1024, 32 attention heads, and MLP width 4096. In each layer, the authors designate a tiny biology subnetwork: one attention head and 64 MLP hidden units. Everything else is treated as the non-biology subnetwork.

To control entanglement, they repurpose Selective Gradient Masking during pretraining. For biology examples, gradients to the non-biology parameters are zeroed after backprop, forcing biology knowledge to flow mainly into the biology subnetwork. For a sampled subset of non-biology examples, the biology subnetwork is zeroed in the forward pass, training the model to handle retain and adjacent data without relying on that specialized pathway. The remaining examples are trained normally. By varying the fraction of training steps that use SGTM, they produce six models with graded levels of disentanglement. This is the central design choice: the dataset and the downstream unlearning algorithms stay fixed, while the internal routing of knowledge during pretraining changes.

They then apply three standard unlearning methods to every model: Weighted Gradient Ascent, Weight Divergence Regularization, and RMU. The logic of the paper has two stages. First, show that the six models indeed span a meaningful range of disentanglement, using three separate metrics rather than relying on naming or training settings alone. Second, compare retain cost at matched forgetting levels. This makes the experiment much cleaner than prior studies that varied data mixtures or unlearning procedures and then attributed the outcome to entanglement.19:T5e8,The main result is consistent and easy to interpret: more disentangled models achieve better retain-forget trade-offs. At a fixed level of forgetting, the most disentangled model incurs about 4x lower retain cost than the most entangled one under WGA and RMU, and about 1.3x lower under WDR. That is large enough to suggest that entanglement is not a minor nuisance variable but a substantial driver of collateral damage in unlearning.

The experimental scale is moderate but well controlled: roughly 3.7B training tokens, 1024-token blocks, a forget domain that is 3.7% of the corpus, and a fixed 20% subsample of each test split used for unlearning sweeps and reporting. All models have the same 254M parameter count, which removes model-size variation as a confound.

Two details are especially worth noting. First, the adjacent domain is explicitly separated from the retain domain, which makes the setup more realistic than a simple biology-versus-rest split. It allows the paper to probe whether forgetting one domain also harms nearby knowledge. Second, the gain from disentanglement depends on the unlearning method: WGA and RMU benefit much more than WDR. That suggests an interaction between representation structure and the mechanics of the editing algorithm, rather than a single universal effect size. For a closer read, the exact definitions of the disentanglement metrics and the retain-cost / forget-level measures are important, because they determine how far the conclusions can be generalized.1a:T435,This is a good skim for methodology rather than for direct algorithm adoption. Start with the introduction, where the paper clearly separates localization from entanglement and explains why earlier work did not isolate the latter. Then read the experimental setup carefully, especially the forward-pass and backward-pass interventions used by SGTM. That is the core experimental manipulation. After that, inspect the section that validates the disentanglement ordering across the six models; without that, the later unlearning comparison is much less convincing. Finally, read the retain-forget curves for WGA, WDR, and RMU and compare how sensitive each method is to the underlying representation structure.

If you only want the main takeaway, 20 to 30 minutes is enough.
