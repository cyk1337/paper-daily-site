17:T589,The method has two stages. Stage one is Task Feature Identification. Hidden states from a chosen layer are passed through an SAE, which maps dense activations into a high-dimensional sparse feature space. Using a small prior task set, the method first performs a coarse recall step: features that activate frequently at a task-critical token position are kept as candidates. The intuition is that mechanisms central to a task should recur consistently during task execution. Since frequency only captures correlation, the paper then applies interventional filtering. Candidate features are perturbed one by one, and only those whose intervention measurably affects task performance are retained as causal task features. Stage two is Feature-Based Data Scoring. Each candidate training sample is scored by how strongly it activates the validated causal features, again at the relevant token position. High-scoring samples are treated as feature-resonant data and used for supervised fine-tuning. Compared with standard data selection methods, the differences are clear: it does not rely on external labels or teacher scoring, it selects by internal activation structure rather than surface similarity, and it inserts a causal validation step before optimization.
