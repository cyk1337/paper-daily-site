19:T764,The setup distinguishes the as-of date t0 from the model training cutoff T. Text written after t0 but before T is bridge data. It can contain information caused by the later world state that also determines the final outcome Y. A model trained on this bridge data may obtain outcome information without ever seeing the benchmark item itself. The paper formalizes this as a back-door leakage path from the later world state through bridge data into the model prediction.

The first technical result is a non-identifiability theorem. A passive backtest observes forecasts, outcomes, timing relative to the cutoff, and possibly covariates, but it cannot separate recency from leakage. The identified set for leakage inflation is a sharp interval, and collecting more items does not make it collapse. A flat pre/post profile is therefore not proof of cleanliness, and a non-flat profile is not proof of leakage.

The second part derives a per-question leakage law. Inflation is the product of honest uncertainty and an extraction factor. The extraction factor has a strong nonlinear payoff: extracting half of the leaked signal already yields about three quarters of the full inflation. This explains why weak recall can still move benchmark scores. The analysis predicts that leakage concentrates on questions that surprised the crowd and had dense training coverage.

The measurement strategy requires an external reference. With a known cutoff, the paper uses a regression-discontinuity design to identify cutoff-localized leakage. With a matched clean control, it uses a difference-in-differences design to estimate global leakage and report a leakage-adjusted score. Matching is central: naive pre/post comparisons are replaced by comparisons that control for timing, question characteristics, and baseline difficulty. Paraphrase probes are treated as detection tools, not measurement tools.1a:T4ee,The empirical section starts with ForecastBench. The authors evaluate five flagship models on the official panel, restricted to questions that resolved after each model’s documented training cutoff. These outcomes could not have been memorized under the documented cutoffs. Even so, four of the five models fail the standard pre/post check, with spurious leakage gaps as large as +0.061. A matched-window check collapses the gaps across all five models, supporting the interpretation that the apparent signal is recency rather than leakage.

The estimators are validated using twin models with planted leakage. One model is kept clean, while the other receives controlled leakage. The proposed estimators recover the planted dose, location, and per-question profile, and return a null result on the clean twin. On deployed frontier systems, the estimators identify one cutoff-localized signature and clear five models whose apparent advantages were explained by recency at the design’s power floors.
