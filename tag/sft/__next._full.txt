18:T53f,Localization-Head Alignment (LHA) has three key pieces:
1) Identify localization heads: within Qwen3.5-4B’s full-attention layers (128 heads), find a small subset that performs evidence selection. Causal importance is validated via head ablations (ablating four localization heads hurts much more than four random heads).
2) Supervise at fact tokens inside the chain: instead of supervising attention at the final answer, apply supervision at the token positions where the model states a visual fact (e.g., when predicting the object word in “the kite is blue”). This matches the observation that perception happens when facts are written, and that attention fades later.
3) Contrastive box-based attention loss: using grounding boxes, train those heads to attend more to the evidence region than to an equal-area background. The loss is formulated as an evidence-vs-background ratio so the softmax normalizer cancels, emphasizing where attention goes rather than how much total visual attention is used.
The same auxiliary loss is used in both SFT (on the model’s own correct trajectories) and GRPO-style outcome RL (on correct rollouts). A gradient decomposition is used to argue outcome rewards select trajectories, while the attention term provides a consistent routing gradient. Inference is unchanged (no extra zoom/crop passes).19:T568,Backbone: Qwen3.5-4B in thinking mode (32K token budget, temperature 1). Evaluation: 1,396 high-res fine-grained MCQs (V* 191, HR-Bench 4K 800, TreeBench 405). Answers are rule-parsed; an LLM judge (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) is used only when parsing fails, and judge decisions are manually audited.
Diagnostics:
- Evidence masking: masking the ground-truth region costs 22.1 points more than masking an equal-area distractor region; the smaller the evidence, the larger the drop.
- Localization heads are causal: ablating four localization heads causes an additional 13.2-point drop vs ablating four random heads; their evidence focus decays with longer reasoning.
Main results (as reported):
- LHA reaches 76.6% overall (Figure 1), the best single-pass Qwen3.5-4B variant in their comparison.
- Beats inference-time interventions including ViCrop (two extra passes) and outperforms the attention-supervised RL baseline EASE by 3.0 points.
- Controlled gains: on identical self-generated trajectories, alignment SFT improves +1.4 over CE-SFT; after identical outcome RL, the gap grows to +1.8 (95% CI [0.5, 2.9]). Adding the attention term during RL yields an additional +0.9.
Efficiency: SFT updates only 2.6% of the LM; aligned policies produce 19% shorter rollouts (15% faster RL steps) and use 24% fewer reasoning tokens at inference. Transfer: +2.5 points on direct-answer Qwen2.5-VL-3B.1a:T48e,Instead of only comparing four binary transitions, AdamW-to-AdamW, AdamW-to-Muon, Muon-to-AdamW, and Muon-to-Muon, the paper constructs mixed optimizers. At each update step, it samples a Muon update with probability p and an AdamW update otherwise, with p in 0.0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, and 1.0. This turns optimizer identity into a continuous axis and enables a p_PT by p_SFT grid. The score in each cell is decomposed into a global mean, a pretraining row effect, an SFT column effect, and a residual interaction term. If matching optimizers had an intrinsic benefit, the residuals should be systematically better along the p_PT = p_SFT diagonal; the experiments do not show a strong pattern of that kind. The continued-pretraining experiments use the same logic and show that apparent switching costs largely disappear after learning-rate tuning. AdamW uses decoupled weight decay and gradient clipping. Muon follows the paper’s Algorithm 2 and uses the 0.2 RMS update scaling from prior Muon work.
