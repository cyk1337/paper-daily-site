9:T90f,TailSFT 的核心很简单：在 SFT 时做 sequence-level filtering，把那些相对于初始策略已经“fit 得够好”的训练序列过滤掉，只对 under-modeled tail 继续更新。作者的出发点是，标准 cross-entropy 会继续奖励所有 demonstration 的概率提升，包括模型已经很容易采样出来的响应；而概率质量守恒意味着，这种继续加压可能把质量从别的潜在有用响应上挤走，导致 coverage 下降。也就是说，CE 和 coverage 不是同一个目标，甚至可能冲突。

论文先在理论上 formalize 了 coverage。设任务有二值可验证奖励，RL 初始阶段真正有用的是：对一个 prompt，模型在 k 次采样内至少出现一个 reward-bearing response 的概率，这正是 pass@k 的含义。large-k pass@k 越高，RL rollout 越容易拿到正信号。TailSFT 因而不再试图均匀提升所有监督样本的 likelihood，而是优先学习那些初始模型还不容易覆盖到的序列。

关键设计点有两个。第一，过滤是 sequence-level，而不是 token-level reweighting。因为 coverage 关心的是“完整响应是否可被采到”，不是局部 token 拟合。第二，过滤标准是相对于 initial policy，而不是相对于当前训练中的 policy。作者强调这一点并给出理论说明：若按当前策略动态过滤，训练过程会不断改变“谁算已经学会”，容易引入不稳定或错误的偏置；相对于初始策略做过滤，则更接近“保留初始模型已有覆盖、把增量学习预算投向尾部”的目标，并且能在文中的设定下给出优于标准 CE 和一些朴素过滤方案的 coverage 保证。

从算法形态看，TailSFT 是一个 drop-in SFT objective modification，不需要改模型结构，也不依赖 RL 阶段信息。实现上更像在 SFT 数据上做基于 base policy 的 hard example selection，但它不是为了提升训练时 loss 或 pass@1，而是为了提升后续 RL 可利用的探索起点。论文还提出了一个 coverage-ratio diagnostic，用 base model 和标准 SFT run 的统计量来判断 TailSFT 是否有望改善 coverage。这个诊断给了方法一个实用入口：不是所有数据集、所有任务都一定适合过滤，先看 coverage ratio 可以减少盲试。a:T85d,TailSFT is a sequence-level filtering method for supervised fine-tuning. During SFT, it removes sequences that are already sufficiently well fit by the initial policy, and concentrates updates on the under-modeled tail of the response distribution. The motivation is that standard cross-entropy keeps rewarding likelihood increases for every demonstration, including responses the model can already sample easily. Because probability mass is limited, pushing those easy responses further can reduce mass assigned to other potentially useful responses, hurting coverage.

The paper formalizes coverage in the setting of binary verifiable rewards. For a prompt x, what matters for RL initialization is the probability that at least one of k samples receives reward 1. This is exactly what pass@k measures. Large-k pass@k is therefore a proxy for how much positive learning signal RL can expect from the initialization under a realistic rollout budget.

Two design choices are central. First, filtering is done at the sequence level rather than the token level, because coverage is about sampling complete reward-bearing responses, not about local token fit. Second, filtering is defined relative to the initial policy rather than the current policy during training. This is not a cosmetic detail. The authors argue, and partially prove, that filtering relative to the initial policy better preserves useful pre-existing coverage and avoids the instability or myopia of more naive dynamic filtering schemes. Under their setup, this criterion yields better coverage guarantees than standard CE and simpler alternatives.

Operationally, TailSFT is a drop-in modification to SFT rather than a new architecture or an RL-dependent method. It resembles hard-example selection based on the base model, but the target is not better training loss or pass@1; it is better downstream RL initialization. The paper also introduces a lightweight coverage-ratio diagnostic, computed from the base model and a standard SFT run, which provides a sufficient condition for TailSFT to improve coverage. That diagnostic makes the method more actionable in practice.b:T5c1,The experiments are organized in three layers. First, the paper uses a controlled graph-navigation task to isolate the tension between CE and coverage. In that setting, filtering already-fit examples improves large-k pass@k even when cross-entropy becomes worse and pass@1 does not improve. This is an important result because it shows TailSFT is not just an empirical trick on benchmark tasks; it is optimizing a genuinely different objective.

Second, the method is tested on OLMo-3 7B for math and coding. According to the reported headline numbers, TailSFT improves pass@16 by up to 16.8 absolute points on coding and 3.1 points on math. More importantly, the gain transfers through RL: after GRPO, final pass@1 improves by up to 3.9 points. That is the strongest evidence in the paper, because it shows the method improves not only multi-sample accessibility but also the eventual post-RL model.

Third, the paper includes design ablations and diagnostics. It compares different filtering criteria and motivates why filtering relative to the initial policy is preferable to more naive alternatives. It also introduces the coverage-ratio diagnostic and shows that it can predict when TailSFT should help. A recurring theme in the results is the trade-off: TailSFT may sacrifice CE and small-k metrics in exchange for better large-k coverage and better post-RL performance.
