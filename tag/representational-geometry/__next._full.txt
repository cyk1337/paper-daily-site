1a:T58a,The paper does not claim to directly observe a global 'reversion manifold'. Instead, it introduces an operational proxy based on local witnesses. Starting from the same base model, the authors construct several short helpful-only fine-tuned checkpoints and treat them as local witnesses of an earlier behavioral region. They then define the displacement from the current safety-aligned model to such a witness as v_rev, the local reversion direction. The technical argument has three parts. First, geometric measurement: during benign post-alignment fine-tuning, they track how strongly the update trajectory aligns with v_rev, and compare it against isotropic random-direction baselines. Second, predictive analysis: they test whether early alignment with v_rev predicts later behavioral degradation. Third, causal intervention: they explicitly block motion along v_rev during fine-tuning and measure whether harmfulness is reduced with limited task cost. A key detail is that 'manifold' is used behaviorally and operationally, not as a directly recovered object in weight space. So the method is a combination of proxy directions, local linearization, and behavioral validation. Relative to prior work showing that safety alignment is shallow and low-rank, or that safety holds only within a narrow basin, this paper goes one step further by proposing a concrete reversion direction and testing it with intervention.1b:T45c,The main evidence is reported in an 8B setting. During early benign fine-tuning, alignment with v_rev rises from 0.429±0.052 after the first update to 0.647±0.021 by step 20, and this same window coincides with substantial unsafe drift. The geometric signal is also predictive: across checkpoints, alignment with the reversion direction correlates strongly with downstream degradation, with Spearman r = 0.877. The most important result is the intervention study. Blocking motion along v_rev during benign fine-tuning reduces harmfulness from 19.0%±4.0% to 8.5%±1.5%, while incurring little task cost. Matched random-direction controls do not reproduce the effect, which argues against a generic regularization explanation. The paper also reports stability across different witness constructions, which is important because otherwise v_rev could be a fragile post hoc artifact. The experiments do not yet yield a general mitigation recipe, but they do support the narrower claim that a meaningful part of post-training drift can be explained and manipulated through a history-dependent low-dimensional direction.1c:T5b6,The paper frames Adversarial Concept Search (ACS): given a set of concepts, extract a linear direction/encoding for each atomic concept from the model, then use pairwise geometry (primarily angular separation) to predict whether composing those concepts will succeed. The mechanism hypothesis is “lossy superposition causes interference”: while sparse feature activations can in principle allow lossless decoding even with non-orthogonal features, real models decode imperfectly; when multiple task-relevant features are simultaneously active and their directions are too aligned, they interfere and compositional generalization fails.
Operationally, the paper instantiates ‘atomic representations’ differently per task:
- In SCAN, atomic primitives are cleanly defined, enabling direct measurement of concept-pair interactions and angles.
- In multi-hop QA, a two-hop question is decomposed into two single-hop components; the angle between the two single-hop representations predicts whether the composed two-hop query will be answered correctly.
- In multilingual factual recall, geometry between fact representations and language subspaces/directions is used to predict recall accuracy for fact×language combinations.
The key distinction from standard error prediction/active learning is that ACS aims to work without running the model on candidate composed inputs; it tries to rank failure-prone scenarios “upstream” using concept-level probes.1d:T4d5,Define two token-level quantities.
- Contextual curvature: treat token hidden states at a given layer as a trajectory in representation space. Compute first-order differences between adjacent token states; define curvature at position k as the angle between adjacent difference vectors; then average over a backward-looking window (size 3) to get a localized contextual curvature.
- Next-token entropy: entropy of the model’s output distribution at that position.

The paper builds three evidence pillars:
1) Correlation + emergence: curvature predicts entropy in GPT-2 XL and Pythia-2.8B, and the relationship strengthens over training using Pythia checkpoints.
2) Targeted perturbations: perturbations aligned with the trajectory structure modulate entropy more effectively than nonspecific perturbations, supporting functional relevance rather than a mere correlate.
3) Auxiliary curvature objectives: adding curvature-targeting losses can alter entropy while preserving task performance, implying curvature is a controllable representational feature tied to uncertainty.
