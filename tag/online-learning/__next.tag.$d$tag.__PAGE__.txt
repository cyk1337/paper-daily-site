5:T5c7,The setup starts from a next-token generator f: Σ*→Σ, mostly in the binary alphabet case. Given a prompt x, the model repeatedly predicts one token and appends it to the context for M steps. This induces an M-step chain-of-thought trajectory and an end-to-end label given by the last generated token. A base class F of next-token generators therefore induces both a trajectory class F^{CoT-M} and a final-output class F^{e2e-M}.

The paper studies online learning of F^{e2e-M} under two feedback models. In the end-to-end model, after each round the learner only observes the final token. In the CoT model, the learner observes the entire M-step trajectory. The analysis is framed in the standard online mistake-bound style, using combinatorial online learnability tools such as Littlestone-style complexity. The main technical contribution is to characterize how autoregressive composition affects online learnability, and to show that revealing intermediate trajectories can substantially reduce the effective complexity of the problem.

The key results are upper bounds, lower bounds, and separations. The upper bounds show that under CoT feedback the mistake bound can track the complexity of the underlying next-token class much more directly, with milder dependence on M. Under end-to-end feedback, hidden intermediate states induce a harder composed prediction problem.
