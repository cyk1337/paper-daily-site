18:T6bd,The method has three pieces. First, the paper constructs behavior directions in two ways: data-driven difference-in-means directions from activations, and hand-picked directions from lm_head rows associated with behavior-specific tokens. Detection is built both ways; intervention, especially refusal, is mainly derived from lm_head token directions. Second, the authors do causal testing rather than stopping at probing. They inject directions into the residual stream with forward hooks and measure behavioral changes layer by layer at inference time. Third, they analyze the geometry and the mechanism behind the mismatch. For formatting, detection and control collapse onto essentially the same axis. For hallucination, the cosine between detection and intervention is only about 0.12, roughly an 83-degree separation. They also rule out a construction artifact: even a detector built purely from activations, without hand-chosen tokens, remains misaligned with control, with cosine around -0.06. A key conceptual point appears later in the paper: cosine is not a steerability oracle. Detection is not a single privileged vector but a high-dimensional equivalence class, while steerability is functional. What matters is whether the control direction also serves as a detector, not whether one static angle is large. Mechanistically, the paper traces the hallucination mismatch to the output mapping, where an entity-copying pathway dominates the epistemic signal. As a result, pushing along the detection direction can strengthen name completion and make fabrication worse instead of triggering refusal. The authors then try a partial fix by rotating the detection direction 15 degrees toward the intervention direction.19:T5dd,The main experiments are run on Gemma 2-2B-it, a 26-layer model with 2304-dimensional residual stream, using inference-time hooks only and no fine-tuning. Two behaviors are contrasted. For output format, detection and control are effectively aligned, which supports the single-axis story for some shallow behaviors. For hallucination, real vs fake entities become perfectly linearly separable from layer 5 onward, with AUC = 1.000, yet the cosine between the detection direction and the refusal intervention direction is only about 0.12. A second detector built directly from activations, without hand-picked tokens, gives cosine = -0.06 against the intervention direction, reinforcing that the gap is not an artifact of direction construction. The cross-model study includes Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Gemma 2-9B-it, and Gemma 2-2B base/instruct, spanning 1B to 9B parameters. The cosine stays in the narrow range [0.12, 0.20]. The base vs instruct comparison on Gemma 2-2B is especially informative because the values are nearly identical, pointing to a pretraining origin. In the repair experiment, rotating 15 degrees from the detection direction toward the intervention direction raises refusal rates to 73% and 60% on two held-out entity categories, with only 1.8% false positives on N=115 examples. That result suggests the gap is bridgeable to some extent, but only after explicitly searching for a compromise direction rather than reusing a probe vector as a control knob.1a:T48d,First, a taxonomy of 18 fine-grained misalignment indicators is constructed, each corresponding to a unitary cognitive process (e.g., strategic omission, fact fabrication) and meeting three design criteria: single representational feature (learnable by a linear classifier), low false positive rate in benign reasoning, and high coverage across diverse misalignment behaviors. A meta-plan-guided automated pipeline is then designed to generate multi-turn training conversations containing each indicator, paired with hard negatives and benign control samples, for training linear probes (lightweight linear classifiers trained on intermediate model activations to detect specific internal representation patterns). Independent linear classifiers are trained on cached intermediate layer activations for each indicator. To reduce false positives, a cascade monitoring system combining white-box (probe-based) and black-box (LLM judge) checks is introduced. For rigorous generalization evaluation, an out-of-distribution test suite is built by combining automated behavioral elicitation and established misalignment benchmarks with natural misaligned model behaviors.1b:T4c4,The authors build a balanced three-class dataset with N=750 examples: 250 from LogiQA 2.0 for deductive reasoning, 250 from ARC-Challenge for inductive reasoning, and 250 from αNLI for abductive reasoning. Prompts are standardized, decoding is greedy with a 2048-token budget, and thinking mode is disabled; <think> blocks are stripped to avoid confounding hidden-state geometry with output-style differences.

For each layer, the representation is the hidden state at the last input token. A linear logistic-regression probe with L2 regularization predicts the reasoning-mode label. The best layer is then analyzed geometrically using intrinsic dimensionality, local curvature, inter-class separation, and convex-hull contamination. The key contribution is the deconfounding pipeline: compare reasoning-mode probing with source-identity probing, test whether option count alone predicts labels, restrict to four-choice tasks, and residualize hidden states with respect to source identity, option count, and response length before re-probing. The paper also runs causal steering experiments with 20 random-direction controls.1c:T451,The testbed is Dyck-1 and Shuffle-k (k=2/4/6/8), where correct continuation depends on stack-like memory. The model is an encoder-only transformer with a causal mask for next-token prediction, but the output is multi-label: it predicts the set of valid next tokens as a k-hot vector via a sigmoid head, trained with MSE.
Method pipeline:
1) Train a fully converged LM on Shuffle-k.
2) Train linear probes to predict stack depth at each position from hidden states; use a control probe with randomized labels and report selectivity to argue the feature is genuinely encoded.
3) Extract a principal “stack direction” from the probe weight matrix W using SVD; take the top right singular vector w.
4) Perform causal intervention at inference by ablating that direction: h ← h − (h·w)w. Compare against ablating random directions as a control.
The design choice of using the top singular vector (instead of a single class weight) aims to capture the most shared discriminative direction across depth classes, and random-direction ablations help rule out trivial damage-from-any-ablation explanations.1d:T7e9,The setup is straightforward but broad. The authors evaluate five transformer families spanning 1.4B to 9B parameters: Pythia-1.4B, Gemma-2-2B, Gemma-2-9B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.1-8B. For each base model, they create two LoRA-tuned variants: an honest model trained only on correct answers, and a deceptive model trained only on plausible but incorrect answers. This gives a clean controlled comparison because architecture, base weights, and adaptation method are fixed while the supervision target changes.

The analysis has two layers. First, they test separability. Mean-pooled hidden states are extracted from every transformer layer, and both linear probes (logistic regression) and nonlinear probes (MLPs) are trained to classify honest versus deceptive activations. If linear probes already perform near perfectly, then dishonesty is encoded in a largely linear direction.

Second, they characterize the geometry of that direction rather than stopping at AUC. The paper measures Fisher Discriminant Ratio to quantify class separation relative to within-class spread; effective rank to estimate intrinsic dimensionality; centroid L2 distance and possible phase transitions across layers; adjacent-layer cosine similarity of the dishonesty direction to test directional stability; cross-domain directional alignment between TruthfulQA and MMLU; and expected calibration error to assess whether probe confidence is meaningful. This is a stronger design than a simple probing paper because it asks not only whether dishonesty is decodable, but also where it forms, how stable it is, how low-dimensional it is, and whether it generalizes.

The choice of synthetic dishonesty is deliberate. It removes many confounds present in alignment-faking or strategic-deception settings. The trade-off is equally clear: the resulting conclusions are about how supervised objectives induce false-output representations, not about whether frontier models already possess strategic deceptive circuits in realistic deployment settings.1e:T79d,The experiments cover five models from 1.4B to 9B parameters. The headline result is strong linear separability in four of the five architectures: linear probes reach AUC >= 0.99 as early as layers 1 to 3. Pythia-1.4B is the notable exception, with a peak AUC of only 0.705. That exception is important because it suggests that model scale and architecture materially affect whether dishonesty becomes a clean linear feature.

Cross-domain transfer is another central result. Probes trained on TruthfulQA transfer to held-out MMLU subjects with almost no loss, which argues against the probe merely memorizing dataset-specific lexical cues. Instead, it suggests a more general representational shift associated with deceptive fine-tuning.

The paper also runs an adversarial robustness analysis by injecting Gaussian noise into hidden states. Later-layer dishonesty representations are substantially more robust than early-layer ones, and Gemma-2 models are especially resistant across noise levels. This complements the early-layer AUC result: the signal appears early, but becomes more consolidated and robust deeper in the network.

The geometric analyses add architectural nuance. The authors report differences across model families in Fisher Discriminant Ratio, effective rank dynamics, centroid-distance phase transitions, adjacent-layer directional consistency, and calibration. So the paper does not claim a single universal geometry of dishonesty; it shows that the phenomenon is real but architecture-dependent. Another useful experiment compares the tuned variants against pretrained baselines, showing that supervised optimization amplifies dishonesty-related directions rather than merely exposing a strong pre-existing feature.
