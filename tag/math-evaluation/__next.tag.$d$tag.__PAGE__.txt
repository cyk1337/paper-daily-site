9:T5eb,FaithSieve has three stages: localization, verification, and synthesis. In localization, the system decomposes coarse benchmark steps into finer EdgeUnits using a proof-state-tree representation. Each transition in this tree corresponds to a local reasoning responsibility, which makes the evaluation target much sharper than judging an entire paragraph-like step. In verification, the method extracts typed proof obligations from suspicious transitions. These obligations are not treated uniformly; they encode different kinds of reasoning responsibilities such as algebraic transformation, implication transfer, existence construction, boundary-condition handling, or case transitions. The system then gathers evidence through both lightweight checks and Lean-based validation. Lean is used as a structured evidence source rather than a direct judge. A key safeguard is the semantic alignment score, which measures whether the formalized statement preserves the assumptions, directionality, quantification, and strength of the original informal obligation. Lean evidence is only trusted when this alignment is high enough, preventing drifted or weakened targets from producing false reassurance. In synthesis, local evidence is aggregated back to the original step level to predict the first erroneous benchmark step. The main novelty is the full pipeline: decomposition into local transitions, typed obligations, formal checking gated by semantic alignment, and evidence aggregation for first-error localization.a:T506,The paper introduces two expert-verified datasets: ProofLoc-Olympiad with 350 algebra and number theory problems, and ProofLoc-University with 200 advanced mathematics problems. The evaluation follows the standard first-error localization setup, reporting both binary accuracy and exact accuracy, with the latter requiring the correct first-error index. The experimental story is important because it tests whether local formal evidence, when filtered for semantic faithfulness, actually improves early-error localization. The likely key ablations are removing EdgeUnit decomposition, which should make local flaws harder to isolate; removing semantic alignment gating, which should increase false positives from target drift; and relying only on natural-language judging, which should weaken precise localization.
