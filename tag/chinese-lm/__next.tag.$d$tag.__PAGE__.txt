9:T628,There is no new model architecture here. The contribution is the challenge design. Systems must be pretrained from random initialization under a BabyLM-style data budget. Participants can either use the official 102-million-word corpus or build their own corpus under the same word budget. This preserves the core low-data constraint while leaving room for research on corpus composition, filtering, and tokenizer design.

Evaluation is organized into three tracks with 14 tasks in total. The NLU track combines zero-shot minimal-pair evaluation and supervised transfer tasks from CLUE. The zero-shot part includes ZhoBLiMP and a hidden XCOMPS-ZH benchmark: the former emphasizes syntactic and semantic acceptability, while the latter targets concept-property compatibility. The Hanzi track directly tests whether models acquire structural and phonological knowledge about Chinese characters. Importantly, if the tokenizer maps the target character or component to UNK, the model automatically fails that item. This turns tokenization coverage into an explicit research variable rather than an implementation detail. The Cognitive Modeling track uses Chinese fMRI benchmarks from MulCogBench, evaluating whether hidden states can predict human neural responses during word-level and discourse-level comprehension.

From a pretraining perspective, the interesting part is that all three dimensions are evaluated under the same data budget. Character tokenizers, BPE, word segmentation, and mixed-granularity schemes are therefore forced to expose their trade-offs more clearly.a:T44d,The paper is mainly a task and benchmark description, not a method-heavy experimental study.
