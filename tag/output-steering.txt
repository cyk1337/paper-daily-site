After removing the FFN, each layer is approximated by value/output projections, attention mixing, and normalization. In the shared-weight case, the effective layer matrix is fixed across depth; for multi-head attention it is formed from the output projection and the concatenated value projections. This makes the analogy to power iteration especially clean: repeated multiplication and normalization increase alignment with the dominant eigenvector when the spectral conditions are favorable. At consensus, full attention becomes close to a uniform matrix, so the perturbation induced by attention weights weakens and the projection effect becomes clearer. For standard layer-varying models such as GPT-2, GPT-Neo, and BERT, the effective matrix changes from layer to layer, so convergence to a single eigenvector is not expected. The authors instead examine whether passing through a layer increases, in aggregate, the correlation between token states and that layer’s dominant eigenvector.
