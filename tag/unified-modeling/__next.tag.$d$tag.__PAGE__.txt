a:T521,From the available full-text excerpt (intro only), the approach is organized around three coupled issues and corresponding design choices:
1) Unified formulation: treat generation, reference-based synthesis, temporal extension, and editing—now extended to joint audio-video—as one conditional denoising family with varying condition/target configurations.
2) Explicit token roles: instead of implicit concatenation, attach role information to tokens so the model is told which segments are reconstruction targets, which are to be preserved, and which are guidance-only references.
3) Conditioning duality: implement both a semantic pathway (instruction following, reasoning over text + visual evidence) and a latent pathway (high-fidelity preservation of identity/texture/layout/motion). In audio-video, this must also handle modality absence and asymmetric roles per modality.
4) Data consistency & balance: emphasize truly aligned audio-video pairs and a task mixture that does not let data-rich objectives dominate.

However, the excerpt stops before the method section; details such as the exact role representation, backbone topology (single-stream vs dual-tower), fusion mechanism, loss weighting, and sampling of condition-output configurations cannot be stated faithfully without the missing sections.b:T869,方法上有两个关键部分。第一部分是功能 token 设计。作者把一类视觉操作直接注册为 tokenizer 词表中的离散 token，例如 zoom、draw line、draw shape、add arrow、insert label 等。模型生成这些 token 时，表面上仍是在做标准自回归解码；但在执行层面，这些 token 对应预定义的视觉操作，会作用在当前视觉上下文上。这样做和 agentic 方法的区别是，模型不再输出冗长代码或工具参数串，而是用单个 token 触发内部化的操作接口；和 latent reasoning 的区别是，这些中间单元仍在显式 token 序列里，训练目标仍是标准 CE，不需要额外连续隐变量监督，也不破坏并行 teacher forcing。

第二部分是训练配方。作者采用两阶段训练。SFT 阶段构建了 ATLAS-178K 数据集，覆盖 40 多类视觉推理任务，并为每个样本标注 functional-token trajectory，让模型先学会何时调用、调用什么操作。这里的关键不是图像级 supervision，而是序列级 supervision：模型只需在推理轨迹中学会插入正确功能 token。RL 阶段在 SFT 模型上继续做 outcome-driven optimization，直接使用标准 GRPO，因为功能 token 已经是普通词表 token，不需要像某些 latent/action 方法那样改 PPO/GRPO 的 credit assignment 结构。

作者在 RL 中发现一个很重要的问题：功能 token 在整段输出里占比很低，但对结果影响很大；如果直接按标准 sequence-level GRPO 更新，优势信号会主要分配给大量普通文本 token，功能 token 的学习强度不足。为此提出 LA-GRPO。它在标准 GRPO 目标外，加了一个针对功能 token 词表的静态加权 token-level auxiliary loss，相当于给这部分稀疏控制 token 一个持续的锚定梯度。这个设计不是重新定义 RL，而是在不破坏现有训练框架的前提下，改善稀疏关键 token 的优化密度。论文的一个隐含判断是：对于这类“少数 token 决定外部状态变化”的任务，sequence-level reward 本身不够，需要在 token 粒度补一条稳定监督通道。c:T840,The method has two main components. The first is the functional-token interface. Visual operations such as zooming, drawing lines or shapes, adding arrows, and inserting labels are added as discrete tokens in the tokenizer vocabulary. During decoding, the model still performs standard autoregressive token generation. At execution time, however, these tokens trigger predefined visual operations on the current visual context. Compared with agentic methods, this avoids verbose code generation and external tool syntax. Compared with latent reasoning, the intermediate units remain explicit sequence elements, trained with standard cross-entropy, without introducing continuous latent supervision or recurrent hidden-state dependencies.

The second component is the training recipe. ATLAS uses a two-stage setup. In the supervised stage, the authors build ATLAS-178K, covering more than 40 visual reasoning tasks, with annotated functional-token trajectories. This gives the model a cold start on when and how to invoke the operations. Importantly, the supervision is sequence-level rather than image-level: the model learns to place the right functional tokens in the reasoning trace. In the RL stage, the SFT model is further optimized with outcome-driven rewards using standard GRPO. Because functional tokens are ordinary vocabulary items, no custom RL machinery is needed.

A central technical observation appears during RL: functional tokens are sparse in the output sequence but disproportionately important for the final result. Under standard sequence-level GRPO, their learning signal is diluted by the much larger number of ordinary text tokens. To address this, the paper introduces Latent-Anchored GRPO (LA-GRPO), which augments GRPO with a statically weighted token-level auxiliary loss anchored on the functional-token vocabulary. In effect, this gives sparse control tokens a persistent gradient path without changing the overall AR training framework. The broader lesson is that sequence-level rewards alone may be insufficient when a small subset of tokens controls external state transitions.d:T6a6,The experiments are organized around three claims: functional tokens are more compact than explicit agent-style calls, easier to train and generalize than latent reasoning alternatives, and benefit materially from the proposed RL modification. On the data side, the paper introduces ATLAS-178K, a 178K-example dataset spanning 40+ visual reasoning task types, used for supervised cold start. The training pipeline compares SFT, RL on top of SFT, and RL with LA-GRPO.

From the full paper, the main findings are qualitative and structural rather than tied to a single leaderboard. After SFT, the model can reliably insert functional tokens into reasoning traces and outperforms text-only reasoning baselines on tasks that require intermediate visual operations. RL further improves overall task success, and adding LA-GRPO yields more consistent gains, especially on tasks where correct visual manipulation is central. The authors attribute this to better optimization of functional-token usage and better reward credit assignment.

The most informative ablations remove functional tokens, replace them with longer textual or code-like formulations, or remove LA-GRPO. These variants show worse efficiency and weaker task performance. Without LA-GRPO, RL still helps, but functional-token learning is less stable, with behavior resembling 'explains but does not act' or inconsistent invocation. The provided excerpt does not include the full numeric tables, so the exact gains should be checked directly in the main results and ablations. The most important numbers to verify are the output share of functional tokens, their token-level advantage statistics, and how LA-GRPO changes those distributions.e:T41d,The relevance to pretraining is straightforward. The paper revisits the long-standing question of whether generative visual pretraining can rival discriminative visual pretraining, but frames it in a much more LLM-like way: first do large-scale generative pretraining, then apply lightweight instruction tuning to align the model to downstream task formats. If this recipe holds, visual foundation models may not need to choose between generation and understanding objectives. Instead, generation pretraining could serve as the common base, with post-pretraining alignment unlocking understanding capabilities, much like in text LLMs. This is especially relevant for multimodal and unified modeling work, because it suggests that modern image generators already contain rich internal world models; the bottleneck is often the interface and output protocol rather than the underlying representation. In contribution type, this is more a paradigm validation than a new backbone, but it materially strengthens the case for generative pretraining in vision.f:T849,The method is conceptually simple and closely mirrors the base-model-to-instruction-tuned-model pipeline in NLP. Starting from a pretrained image generator, Nano Banana Pro, the authors do not add task-specific heads for segmentation, depth, or normals, and they do not redesign the architecture into a specialized perception model. Instead, they keep the unified generative architecture and apply lightweight instruction tuning. The key design is to re-encode classical vision-task outputs as RGB images, so the model continues to 'generate images', but those images can be deterministically decoded back into structured outputs such as segmentation masks, depth maps, and surface normals. For example, in segmentation, the prompt may explicitly ask the model to render a target category in pure yellow <255,255,0>, after which the output can be parsed into a mask by color clustering or thresholding. This gives three practical benefits. First, one shared set of weights can support many 2D and 3D tasks, with prompts acting as the task switch. Second, the amount of new training data is relatively small because instruction tuning mainly teaches the model to answer in the requested format. Third, because the output space remains RGB images, the model is more likely to retain its original image generation and editing capabilities.

A central claim in the paper is that prior work already hinted that generators can produce outputs resembling segmentation maps or depth visualizations, but they underperformed on benchmarks largely because the outputs were not standardized or reliably decodable. The authors therefore shift the problem from 'improving visual representations' to 'calibrating the output protocol'. They also avoid the route of full finetuning with task-specific modules, since that tends to reduce generality and can damage the original generative behavior. Vision Banana is thus positioned as a visual analogue of an instruction-tuned LLM: the base capability comes from generative pretraining, and the alignment stage turns that capability into a controllable, benchmarkable, multi-task interface.10:T7f1,The experiments support the paper’s main claim: after lightweight instruction tuning, Vision Banana performs strongly on both visual understanding and image generation. On the understanding side, the paper reports state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art results across several 2D and 3D benchmarks. The headline examples are that it surpasses the specialized segmentation model SAM 3 on multiple segmentation tasks and beats the 3D expert Depth Anything 3 on metric depth estimation. The public excerpt does not include all tables or full numbers, but the framing is important: the claim is not just that the model can do these tasks, but that a unified generator, after a small alignment step, can match or exceed specialized understanding models. On the generation side, Vision Banana largely preserves the capability of its base model, Nano Banana Pro. On GenAI-Bench, it achieves a 53.5% win rate against the base model, suggesting no obvious degradation in generation quality. On the ImgEdit image-editing benchmark, it is reported to perform on par with the base model. The paper also notes that only 500 randomly sampled queries from SA-Co/Gold were evaluated to save compute, so some benchmark conclusions should be read with that caveat.

From an experimental-design perspective, the most important detail is the instruction-tuning recipe that mixes original generation data with newly added vision-task data. This directly addresses the key risk of catastrophic forgetting. If the model were tuned only on vision-task supervision, it could collapse toward a specialized CV model. Mixed training helps preserve the original generative distribution. Another critical element is the decodable visualization scheme itself. The reliability of benchmark metrics depends on whether the color coding, depth visualization, and normal encoding are stable enough to survive generation noise. The fact that the model reaches strong quantitative results suggests that this output protocol is carefully engineered rather than ad hoc.11:T42c,Start with the problem framing in the introduction.
