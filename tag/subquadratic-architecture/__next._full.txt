19:T6b5,The paper is organized around three tightly connected components. First, it performs controlled empirical comparisons among xLSTM, Mamba-2, and Gated DeltaNet in matched hybrid settings. The authors compare them under from-scratch pretraining, Transformer-to-subquadratic distillation, and downstream code generation, while keeping parameter count, layer count, and the number of retained self-attention layers comparable. This is important because it reduces the chance that observed differences come from unrelated recipe choices. Second, the paper introduces a unified formulation of the three architectures. Instead of treating them as unrelated families—state-space models, linear attention, gated recurrence—it rewrites them in a common state-update language and compares how they write to state, forget state, overwrite state, and read from state. This yields the paper’s central hypothesis: the architectures should differ most on two primitive capabilities, accumulation and finite-state tracking. Third, the authors test that hypothesis directly using synthetic length-generalization tasks. Counting tasks isolate accumulation beyond the training horizon, while state-tracking tasks isolate ordered finite-state updates. xLSTM performs well on both, including beyond training length, whereas the alternatives are weaker on at least one side of this trade-off. A key interpretive point is that xLSTM is not a single mechanism but a combination of matrix-state linear-attention-style layers and recurrent layers. The paper argues that this combination gives it both stronger accumulation behavior and stronger state-machine-like tracking, which in turn explains its advantage on complex structured data.1a:T5cc,The strongest empirical section is the code-pretraining comparison. The authors train 400M-parameter inter-layer hybrid language models with 24 total layers, retaining only 3 standard self-attention layers and replacing the rest with the tested subquadratic operator. They compare Gated DeltaNet, Mamba-2, and xLSTM[7:1]. Training is run under three data settings: Nemotron-CC-Code-v1 for 20B tokens, the same corpus for 100B tokens, and a 20B-token mixture of Nemotron-CC-Code-v1 with FineWeb-Edu. The main evaluation is HumanEval pass@{2, 8, 16, 64}. The headline numbers reported in the excerpt are on pass@64: xLSTM beats the next-best backbone by 1.43 points at 20B code tokens, 0.90 points at 100B code tokens, and 1.81 points on the code-plus-FineWeb mixture. The paper also reports HellaSwag, PIQA, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, and WinoGrande, where the trend remains favorable but margins are smaller, reinforcing the claim that structured dependency tasks are where architectural differences become visible. Beyond from-scratch pretraining, the paper includes code-focused Transformer distillation and time-series foundation-model pretraining, showing that the pattern is not tied to one training regime or one data modality. Finally, the synthetic length-generalization experiments are crucial for the explanatory argument: counting tasks probe accumulation, state-tracking tasks probe ordered finite-state updates, and xLSTM generalizes further beyond training length on both.1b:T51f,Begin with the motivation and Figure 1 to understand why the paper deliberately avoids relying on standard web-LM benchmarks as the main evidence. After that, spend most of the time on Section 3, the unified formulation. This is the intellectual center of the paper. Do not read it only as notation conversion; focus on how the three operators differ in write, forget, overwrite, and read behavior, and how those differences motivate the accumulation-versus-state-tracking hypothesis.
