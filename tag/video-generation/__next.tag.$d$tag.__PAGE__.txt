9:T87f,The proposed method, QuantWM, is a training-free 2-bit KV-cache quantization framework. Its goal is not merely to reduce Key/Value reconstruction error, but to explicitly preserve attention logits and temporal consistency. It builds on the centroid-residual representation used in QVG: each Key is assigned to a BF16 centroid, and only the residual is quantized to low bitwidth. The paper argues that QVG is limited because centroid selection is driven mainly by K-means geometry, which optimizes feature-space distance rather than downstream impact on QK^T.

The first component is QSAC, quantization-sensitivity-aware clustering. Instead of selecting centroids purely by Key-space clustering quality, QSAC jointly considers Query sensitivity and residual dynamic range, aiming to reduce the effect of INT2 residual quantization on attention logits. Intuitively, some Key directions may have small Euclidean error but still be highly dangerous if they align with Query-sensitive directions and alter token ranking. QSAC tries to avoid such residual structures already at the clustering stage.

The second component is PSAC, principal-subspace attention compensation. Even with better clustering, Key quantization error can still be amplified along dominant Query directions and distort logits. PSAC extracts the principal Query subspace and uses low-rank projections to represent and compensate the Key error components along those sensitive directions during inference. The idea is not to fully reconstruct Keys, which is unrealistic at 2 bits, but to correct the error components that matter most for attention allocation. This is a different philosophy from simply increasing quantization fidelity: it accepts that full preservation is impossible and spends the available budget on the subspace that most affects retrieval behavior.

The overall design is coherent: QSAC makes Keys more quantization-friendly before compression, and PSAC compensates the most harmful attention shifts after compression. Because the method is training-free, it is intended as a practical drop-in replacement for existing KV-cache compression pipelines in video generation and world models.a:T53a,The experiments cover several representative models, including LingBot-World-v2, Matrix-Game-2, LongCat-Video, HY-World 1.5, and Causal-Forcing, which suggests the authors are aiming for architecture-agnostic evidence rather than a single-model result. The headline outcome is that QuantWM substantially reduces temporal flickering, blur, and artifacts under 2-bit KV quantization while maintaining strong benchmark performance on metrics such as VBench. It also achieves up to 6.20x KV-cache memory compression with limited inference overhead.

The most important evidence comes from the diagnostic ablations. The paper separates Key-only and Value-only quantization and shows that most degradation comes from Keys. It then compares reconstruction errors and finds that Keys can have smaller numerical error than Values despite causing worse output quality. That result supports the paper's central claim: KV quantization should not be judged mainly by reconstruction magnitude, but by its effect on attention logits and token ranking. The method components are built directly around that diagnosis. Even without all table values in the provided text, the experimental story is clear: the goal is not just to preserve benchmark scores, but to fix temporal-consistency failures that benchmarks under-measure but humans notice immediately.b:T4d5,The proposed framework is DIAL, or Dual-phase Intrinsic Attention Leveraging. The first component is an analysis of cross-attention in a text-conditioned latent video DiT trained with rectified flow matching. The authors obtain subject masks from a semantic segmentation pipeline and compare them with attention maps across layers and timesteps. They find that a subset of attention blocks consistently produces spatially concentrated maps aligned with the reference subjects. These maps are called ISGM, Intrinsic Spatial Grounding Maps. In late denoising stages, where ISGM is sharp and reliable, DIAL extracts and binarizes the maps into subject-specific spatial guides, then injects them as controllable attention biases into attention blocks. This allows inference-time control of reference fidelity without retraining, including separate control for multiple subjects. In early denoising stages, the maps are noisier, so the paper does not use them as direct hard guidance. Instead, it scores denoising trajectories by ISGM-mask alignment, constructs preference pairs automatically, and applies RL fine-tuning to improve reference grounding. The same internal signal is therefore used differently depending on the denoising regime.c:T938,The framework has three main components. First, a VLM-based coding agent takes a reference image and natural-language instructions, initializes the world, identifies entities, assigns attributes and states, and writes executable programs that define interactions, triggers, and objectives. Second, a state executor maintains an explicit global world state and updates it according to user actions and programmed rules. This state includes off-screen entities and non-visual fields, so the system does not depend on the renderer to remember the world. Third, a generative renderer produces observations conditioned on compiled controls derived from the current state.

The key design choice is the intermediate representation. The paper compares text descriptions, 2D boxes and masks, detailed 3D scenes, and dense rendering controls such as G-buffers. It then chooses a state-augmented 3D oriented bounding box for each entity. The OBB encodes position, extent, and orientation in a shared world coordinate system, and is augmented with persistent identity, semantic category, dynamic state, and appearance information. This is a deliberate compromise. It is more geometric and view-consistent than text, less camera-tied than 2D controls, and much lighter than full 3D assets that would require explicit pose and geometry synthesis at inference time.

A deterministic state compiler then projects the evolving OBBs along the target camera trajectory and rasterizes them into pixel-aligned spatiotemporal controls. This is important. Instead of asking the video model to infer camera projection and entity-pixel correspondence implicitly, the compiler resolves those constraints explicitly. The renderer is left to synthesize fine geometry, articulation, texture, and secondary dynamics that are not specified by the compact state. In effect, the system splits the problem into explicit state evolution plus conditional generative completion.

The training side includes a data curation pipeline that recovers the spatial and semantic supervision needed for this interface from videos. Even from the available text, it is clear that the authors treat this pipeline as necessary for scaling: without extracting entity-level structure from video data, the proposed interface would remain a hand-built control mechanism rather than a trainable pretraining target.d:T461,The visible experimental emphasis is on state-video consistency rather than generic video quality. The benchmark focuses on combat scenarios and measures whether the number of visible alive characters is correct and whether death states are visually realized. That is a sensible choice because these are exactly the kinds of failures that expose a mismatch between explicit state execution and generative rendering.

Those failure modes matter more than a single benchmark number for judging whether this decomposition is viable.e:T5bd,The method is ActionSplice, built around Counterfactual State Transport (CST). Consider a frozen action-conditioned world model that generates an active chunk of T latent frames with a K-step sampler. If a new action arrives after solver step r, the current internal state s_r^- lies on the trajectory induced by the old action a^-. The target is a matched counterfactual state s_r^*, defined at the same solver step under the revised control schedule c^(m), while keeping the initial state, committed history, solver schedule, and stochastic inputs fixed. During training, rollback is used to construct these matched state pairs, but rollback is not used at inference time. Instead, a lightweight corrector predicts a masked residual in the backbone-native transport representation, moving s_r^- toward s_r^*, after which sampling resumes for the remaining K-r steps. There are two variants. CST_R retargets the entire active chunk when the action changes asynchronously. CST_T preserves the prefix and edits only the suffix starting at temporal boundary m, enabling finer-than-chunk action changes. An important design choice is that the method does not force a shared latent space across backbones. It defines transport in each model’s resumable internal state space: clean-prediction transport for one backbone and Euler solver-state transport for another. That makes the method more practical, though it requires separate correctors per backbone and per variant.f:T419,The reported gains are concrete. Relative to direct condition swapping, CST_R reduces rollback-relative LPIPS by 61.5% on minWM–Wan Action2V and by 75.9% on HY-WM1.5. CST_T reduces suffix LPIPS by 56.1% and 77.5% on the two backbones, while delivering 2.73x and 1.69x pixel-ready speedups over waiting. The evaluation protocol is one of the stronger parts of the work: it covers interruption step, directed action transitions, temporal boundaries within a chunk, and repeated interruptions, and it uses rollback as a matched counterfactual reference rather than relying only on visual plausibility.
