9:T541,The benchmark is built around three design choices. First, the authors structure latent mental variables with a BDI-P taxonomy: Perception, Belief, and Desire, expanded into 12 secondary aspects and 34 fine-grained subdimensions. This turns a vague notion of Theory of Mind into a controlled evaluation space. Second, they introduce Zero-Action Prompting. Prompts describe only internal state and never specify the action, so the model cannot rely on lexical action cues and must infer intention before behavior. Third, they use counterfactual prompt pairs under a fixed visual world state. Only one mental variable is changed, such as true belief versus false belief, or seeing versus not seeing an object. Success on both paired prompts is treated as stronger evidence that the model is tracking latent state rather than exploiting shortcuts.

The evaluation pipeline is also more careful than a standard video benchmark. Instead of only scoring visual quality, they use an automated large-vision-model judge to assess visual quality, commonsense plausibility, intention accuracy, and world-state maintenance. The judge is prompted with structured reasoning steps rather than a single scalar query. They also compare the automated pipeline against judgments aggregated from 10 human experts on 100 generated videos, reporting strong agreement.a:T49b,MindWorldBench contains 372 expert-designed images and 744 prompts arranged as counterfactual pairs. The paper evaluates 11 image-to-video models, including Veo 3.1, Kling V3, Wan 2.6, and CogVideoX. The main empirical pattern is consistent across models: visual realism and physical commonsense are noticeably stronger than mental-state consistency. In other words, models often produce plausible actions, but not the action that follows from the character’s subjective state.

The most informative experimental finding is the Omniscient Bias. In false-belief scenarios, a character should act on what they believe, even when that belief is wrong. Models frequently ignore that distinction and generate behavior aligned with objective truth. The paired counterfactual setup is important here because single-prompt success can be accidental; paired success under minimal mental-state changes is a stricter test. The abstract does not expose the full numeric table, but the full text makes the ranking less important than the shared failure mode: even strong proprietary and open models remain much better at physical continuation than at belief-conditioned behavior generation.b:T425,Rollout-Marginal Distillation keeps the autoregressive rollout mechanism unchanged: the generator still predicts each new chunk conditioned on its generated history. The change is in the teacher signal. Video-level DMD minimizes a reverse KL between the noised student rollout distribution and a pretrained teacher video distribution, using the difference between a frozen real score and an online fake score. RMD instead scores each generated chunk independently, without exposing the teacher to the imperfect history or future chunks. The objective matches the student’s chunk marginal distribution to a teacher-defined chunk distribution. The chunk teacher is prepared by adapting a video diffusion model on individual chunks extracted from videos generated by the original bidirectional model. Since independent chunk scoring does not enforce cross-chunk relations, the method adds a later video-level DMD refinement stage to improve temporal transitions. First, a physical mechanism interpreter maps each action-outcome branch to a distribution over latent mechanisms rather than a point estimate. This matters because different interventions constrain the underlying physics to different degrees: a strong push may sharply identify friction, while a weak interaction may leave many mechanisms plausible. Second, the paper introduces a shared-world evidence score. Instead of forcing branch posteriors to be identical, it measures whether all branches retain common support for a shared mechanism relative to an initial-scene prior p(z|I0). The prior correction is important: it prevents uninformative branches from appearing falsely consistent simply because they remain broad and uncertain. Third, the learned compatibility signal is injected into both training and sampling. The interpreter is trained using same-world groups versus mismatched-world groups, then frozen. Its evidence score is used during flow training and also as sampling-time guidance, so the generated rollouts are nudged toward sets of futures that admit one common physical explanation. A key design choice is weak coupling: the model does not force videos or latent posteriors to collapse to the same representation, only to remain jointly explainable by one mechanism. The system is formalized as a prompt enhancer πθ feeding a video generator Gϕ. The goal is to generate a textual condition y that is easier for Gϕ to realize faithfully. Instead of starting from a user request x and expanding it forward into a richer prompt, WanPE starts from a high-quality real video v, derives a hierarchical cinematic condition y from that video, and then reconstructs a compatible but information-sparser user request x. The motivation is straightforward: y is grounded in real video and therefore better aligned with the conditioning distribution seen by the generator, while x approximates realistic user input at inference time.

The hierarchical cinematic condition appears to be organized at the shot level rather than as a single long paragraph. It captures scene setup, entities, actions, camera language, lighting, audio or dialogue, and narrative progression across shots. This matters because cinematic planning is a coupled control problem: action, camera, pacing, editing, and atmosphere evolve together and cannot be handled well by local prompt embellishment.

After SFT, the model is optimized with SC-GRPO. The key contribution here is the reward design rather than GRPO itself. The paper defines a nine-dimensional semantic-consistency reward to check whether user requirements are preserved across the entire plan. The reward targets omissions, semantic drift, incorrect bindings between entities and attributes or actions, and temporal inconsistencies across shots. Compared with rewards based only on fluency or overall text quality, this is much closer to structured fidelity. In effect, the RL stage acts like a global verifier for long-form planning: the model is encouraged not only to sound cinematic, but to remain faithful to the original request throughout the generated plan.

Its goal is not merely to reduce Key/Value reconstruction error, but to explicitly preserve attention logits and temporal consistency. It builds on the centroid-residual representation used in QVG: each Key is assigned to a BF16 centroid, and only the residual is quantized to low bitwidth. The paper argues that QVG is limited because centroid selection is driven mainly by K-means geometry, which optimizes feature-space distance rather than downstream impact on QK^T.

The first component is QSAC, quantization-sensitivity-aware clustering. Instead of selecting centroids purely by Key-space clustering quality, QSAC jointly considers Query sensitivity and residual dynamic range, aiming to reduce the effect of INT2 residual quantization on attention logits. Intuitively, some Key directions may have small Euclidean error but still be highly dangerous if they align with Query-sensitive directions and alter token ranking. QSAC tries to avoid such residual structures already at the clustering stage.

The second component is PSAC, principal-subspace attention compensation. Even with better clustering, Key quantization error can still be amplified along dominant Query directions and distort logits. PSAC extracts the principal Query subspace and uses low-rank projections to represent and compensate the Key error components along those sensitive directions during inference. The idea is not to fully reconstruct Keys, which is unrealistic at 2 bits, but to correct the error components that matter most for attention allocation. This is a different philosophy from simply increasing quantization fidelity: it accepts that full preservation is impossible and spends the available budget on the subspace that most affects retrieval behavior.

The overall design is coherent: QSAC makes Keys more quantization-friendly before compression, and PSAC compensates the most harmful attention shifts after compression. Because the method is training-free, it is intended as a practical drop-in replacement for existing KV-cache compression pipelines in video generation and world models.12:T53a,The experiments cover several representative models, including LingBot-World-v2, Matrix-Game-2, LongCat-Video, HY-World 1.5, and Causal-Forcing, which suggests the authors are aiming for architecture-agnostic evidence rather than a single-model result.
