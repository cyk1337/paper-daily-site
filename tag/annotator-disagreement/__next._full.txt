训练上，作者把 LLM 视为 policy，对每个样本采样多个 rollout。每个 rollout 解析成一个预测分布，再与真实 annotator distribution 比较，得到 reward。SHALA 的关键不只是用分布匹配 reward，而是根据样本的歧义程度动态重加权 reward，使高歧义样本在优化中占更大权重。直观上，这相当于告诉模型：在大家都一致的样本上，学会主标签就够了；在意见分裂的样本上，重点不是押中某个单标签，而是学会保留多种 plausible interpretation 的概率质量。作者把这一点嵌入 GRPO 的 group-based rollout 优化里，而不是做普通监督学习下的 soft label cross-entropy。

从与现有方法的区别看，论文主要区别于三类路线。第一类是 majority vote 或 label smoothing 式训练，它们仍然默认存在一个主真值，只是在局部做平滑。SHALA 直接把目标改成 annotator distribution。第二类是 inference-time uncertainty elicitation，只在测试时让模型报概率或多答案，但训练目标没变。SHALA 把分布学习放进训练过程。第三类是一般的 distributional alignment 或 pluralistic alignment 工作，往往强调多视角输出，但未必显式利用样本级 annotator disagreement 来调节优化强度。SHALA 的“smart handling”主要体现在这个动态加权机制上。

Instead of a single gold label, each example is represented by an empirical label distribution derived from multiple annotators. The model is asked to produce a verbalized distribution, meaning a textual output that specifies probabilities over candidate classes. This choice avoids adding a separate classifier head and lets the model express uncertainty through the same generative interface used by standard LLMs.

Training is formulated as policy optimization. For each sample, the model generates multiple rollouts. Each rollout is parsed into a predicted class distribution, which is then compared against the annotator distribution to produce a reward. The distinctive part of SHALA is not only that it uses a distributional reward, but that it dynamically reweights rewards according to the degree of annotator disagreement. Highly ambiguous samples receive greater influence during optimization. Intuitively, the model is encouraged to do different things on different regions of the data: on low-ambiguity examples, it can concentrate on the dominant label; on high-ambiguity examples, it should preserve probability mass over multiple plausible interpretations rather than collapse to a single answer.

Relative to prior work, the paper differs from three common approaches. First, majority-vote or soft-label training still assumes a dominant truth and only smooths around it. SHALA makes the annotator distribution itself the target. Second, inference-time uncertainty elicitation asks the model to report probabilities or multiple answers at test time, but leaves the training objective unchanged. SHALA moves distribution learning into optimization. Third, broader pluralistic or distributional alignment work often focuses on diverse outputs without explicitly using sample-level disagreement to modulate training strength. The “smart handling” in SHALA is precisely this ambiguity-aware reweighting.

There are clear boundaries. The method requires multi-annotator distributions, so applicability depends on data collection. Verbalized distributions introduce parsing and calibration issues. The analysis appears to support the intended mechanism. Gains are larger under high-ambiguity conditions, which matches the design of the weighting scheme. Benefits are stronger for inherently ambiguous semantic categories, suggesting the model is learning multi-interpretation structure rather than simply outputting flatter distributions. The paper also reports more uncertainty-aware reasoning behavior, meaning the model is more willing to acknowledge multiple plausible interpretations in its outputs.
