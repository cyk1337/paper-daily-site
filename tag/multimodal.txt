Under fixed top-k input settings, the authors evaluate three multimodal DLMs on five visual QA benchmarks using the same VisRAG-ranked candidate pools, prompts, decoding budgets, and answer scorer. They show that Recall@k increases with k, while macro-average answer accuracy drops. They then isolate the cause and find that semantic conflict, not merely the presence of multiple images, is the main driver of degradation. The theoretical analysis focuses on the first unresolved denoising state. When a correct page and a conflicting page each support a different coherent answer, the correct latent structure requires multiple answer positions to share one source choice. A position-factorized proposal breaks this dependence and allocates probability mass to cross-source answers; the unsupported mass grows as the conflict spans more answer positions. Second, the paper proposes ECF, a training-free evidence admission framework. It combines multi-granularity evidence construction, where both full pages and layout-derived regions are indexed, with generator-side candidate filtering. For each additional candidate ci, ECF compares the target DLM’s first-step answer-block entropy under {c1, ci} versus {c1, blank(ci)}. This yields a conditional confidence gain that controls for the structural effect of adding another visual slot and measures whether the candidate content itself reduces competing-source risk.
