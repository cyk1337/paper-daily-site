18:T5d2,The method, VIGOR (VarIance Guided Online Rollout allocation), starts from an analysis of GRPO under binary rewards. The key observation is that the magnitude of the learning signal is governed by within-group reward variance. If all sampled responses for a prompt are either all correct or all incorrect, the group-relative advantage carries little discriminative information. If the group contains a mix of successes and failures, GRPO gets a meaningful comparison signal. Based on this, VIGOR replaces uniform rollout allocation with iterative refinement. Each prompt first receives a small initial number of rollouts. The algorithm estimates group-level reward variance from these partial samples, selects the prompts with the highest variance, and allocates additional rollouts only to them. This repeats until a fixed total budget is exhausted. The distinction from DAPO-style approaches is important: VIGOR does not first over-generate a larger pool and then filter. It allocates budget online during generation, which avoids paying extra rollout cost purely for selection. Compared with methods that improve utilization through stale rollouts or auxiliary signals, it leaves the GRPO objective unchanged and only changes where the rollout budget goes. The paper also provides a theoretical speedup analysis under a Pareto reward-variance model, arguing that the benefit of iterative refinement grows rapidly with the number of refinement rounds when informativeness is heavy-tailed.19:T40d,The evaluation covers both math reasoning and coding RLVR. On math, the authors test Qwen2.5-1.5B, 3B, 7B, and Phi-4-Mini-Instruct across six benchmarks. The main result is consistent: VIGOR reaches the same accuracy with fewer rollouts, with up to 2.3x rollout-efficiency improvement on Qwen2.5-3B. Under rollout-matched comparisons, where methods are given the same total rollout budget, VIGOR achieves the best average score across all four model settings. On coding, using Qwen3-8B on LiveCodeBench v6, VIGOR reaches GRPO’s final full pass rate with 1.49x fewer rollouts and improves average test pass rate by 3.4 points. The most informative evidence is in the training curves rather than the final table alone: gains appear early, which supports the claim that online allocation is most valuable when the policy is still changing quickly. A reasonable caveat is that the method likely benefits most when prompt informativeness is heavy-tailed; if nearly all prompts are uniformly easy or uniformly hard, the margin should shrink.1a:T43b,C-MTP is a direct-supervision method. Indirect methods, such as Coconut-style training, recurrently produce latent states and feed them back into the model. This matches inference better, but training requires sequential autoregressive passes. Prior direct-supervision methods such as CoLaR aggregate teacher CoT embeddings and ask the model to predict a latent distribution, often a Gaussian in hidden space. C-MTP takes a simpler route. It treats each latent as representing a span or step of the teacher CoT, aggregates the embeddings of the tokens in that span, and trains the model with a multi-token prediction objective over the compressed tokens. The model therefore learns latent reasoning through token likelihood rather than through explicit vector regression. This enables parallel teacher-forced training over the CoT trace. The trade-off is that the method inherits the structure and length of the teacher trace, and at inference it can suffer from accumulated errors because the model must condition on its own latent states rather than teacher-forced textual context.1b:T514,The analysis first disentangles two correlated but distinct signals: position-level predictive entropy measures the model's total uncertainty at a sequence position, while sampled token probability measures the likelihood of the specific token that was generated at that position. High-entropy positions can produce both high- and low-probability tokens; low-probability tokens can appear either at high-entropy positions (where the model is uncertain, providing useful learning signal) or low-entropy positions (where the model is highly confident but sampled an outlier, producing noisy, unstable gradients). The Relative Surprisal Index is defined to combine both signals, measuring the relative unexpectedness of the sampled token against the model's full predictive distribution at the position. Under mild assumptions, RSI has a direct analytical connection to the magnitude of token-level policy gradients, making it a direct proxy for token importance. The RSI-S selection scheme filters two categories of unproductive tokens before gradient updates: low-surprisal tokens at low-entropy positions (already mastered, near-zero gradient) and high-surprisal tokens at low-entropy positions (outlier samples, unstable gradients), retaining only tokens that provide stable, useful learning signal.1c:T42e,The analysis treats latent CoT through an information-theoretic lens. Trajectory supervision injects dense stepwise signals by training latent states to predict suffixes of explicit reasoning traces, which helps with temporal credit assignment. Space supervision controls the geometry of the latent manifold. The paper distinguishes geometric compression, which rigidly aligns latent states with token embeddings or a similar semantic geometry, from generative reconstruction, which requires latent states to remain decodable into natural-language reasoning.
