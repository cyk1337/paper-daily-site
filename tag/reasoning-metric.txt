18:T75c,TRACE has two stages. First, it performs sentence-level constructive-element tagging. A reasoning block is segmented with spaCy, and each sentence is passed to TRACE-DeBERTa, a multi-label classifier built on DeBERTa-v3-base. The labels are derived from Toulmin's argumentation model and Flavell's metacognition framework, covering roles such as claim, data/evidence, warrant, qualifier, and monitoring-like elements. The classifier uses an 8-dimensional sigmoid head with BCEWithLogitsLoss and softened class weights to handle imbalance. Training data consists of roughly 100k reasoning sentences annotated by GPT-5.1 and Claude 4.5 Sonnet in alternating fashion, which is meant to reduce single-annotator stylistic bias.

Second, TRACE converts the predicted label sequence into a score using explicit rules. It computes State Validity, which checks whether the label set of an individual sentence corresponds to an allowed constructive state, and Transition Coherence, which checks whether transitions between adjacent sentences belong to a predefined transition set. Some transitions are treated as good, such as Evidence to Claim, because they indicate argumentative progress. Others are treated as bad, such as Monitoring to Qualifier, because they suggest hesitation or structural drift. The final TRACE score combines these two components.

The main distinction from prior work is that TRACE does not directly ask an LLM to judge the whole trace, and it does not rely on outcome labels. Instead, it inserts an interpretable intermediate layer: local structural tagging followed by rule-based scoring. That improves interpretability and makes the metric easier to reuse as a reward. The trade-off is also clear: the framework depends on a hand-designed ontology and transition rules, so it may be less robust for compressed, implicit, or stylistically unusual reasoning traces.19:T5a6,The evaluation has three main parts. First, the authors test whether TRACE correlates with actual task performance. Across 7 reasoning models, 39 benchmarks, and 26.3K QA samples, they report a Pearson correlation of 0.741 between TRACE score and benchmark accuracy. This is strong enough to show that the metric captures meaningful process information, though not strong enough to replace task accuracy.

Second, they validate the sentence classifier itself. TRACE-DeBERTa is trained on about 100k automatically annotated sentences and evaluated against 400 human-labeled sentences from three senior NLP researchers. Human agreement reaches Cohen's kappa of 0.672, which already indicates substantial ambiguity in the task. The model achieves macro-F1 of 0.666 against human labels, close to that ceiling. Category-wise, Qualifier is easiest at F1 0.821 due to explicit lexical cues, while Warrant is hardest at F1 0.547 because inferential links are often implicit.

Third, they test downstream utility. On Arena-Hard-v2.0, TRACE reaches 64% agreement with LLM-as-a-judge in the MATH category, suggesting partial alignment but not equivalence. More importantly, in RL on GSM8K, using TRACE as a reward signal yields better gains than accuracy-only rewards, with a reported +9.9% improvement over the base model.
