9:T60e,The method has three pieces. First, a formalization: evaluator validity is represented as a pair (S, R), not a single scalar. The paper argues and proves that the two coordinates are independent, and that R is not interpretable without S, because a noisy judge can appear responsive for the wrong reasons. This motivates a validity frontier instead of one-number ranking. Second, an intervention design around scientific overclaiming. The authors define scope edits that widen the set of situations a claim covers—broader populations, adjacent domains, stronger quantifiers, turning an observation into a standing property. They define strength edits that keep scope fixed but increase commitment—removing conditions, deleting hedges, adding unsupported intensifiers. Edit direction is set by human annotators rather than inferred from model outputs. Third, a measurement and validation protocol. They evaluate 7 judges across 4 domains, separately scoring invariance and sensitivity, and separately analyzing scope versus strength. They also introduce validation power, which asks whether a dataset forces the evaluator to rely on the construct rather than on provenance or surface cues. A key point is the distinction between item mode and paired mode: only paired minimal-edit evaluation can expose sensitivity failures that are invisible in ordinary per-item scoring.
