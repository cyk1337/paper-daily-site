18:T4e9,The approach is a three-way alignment between (i) corpus exposure, (ii) an external popularity proxy, and (iii) model judgments.
1) Full-corpus exposure measurement: the authors index the complete OLMo training corpora (pretraining, midtraining, long-context) with Infini-Gram. The combined data is ~27TB / 7.4T tokens; indexing is sharded (128 shards) and yields ~50TB of indexed artifacts, enabling exact entity/alias counting at scale.
2) Entity set construction: entities are sampled from Wikidata across five types (Person, Location, Organization, Art, Product). Aliases are used to improve recall, but alias ambiguity is treated as a key confounder, motivating validation/cleaning steps.
3) External popularity signal: Wikipedia pageviews are used as a real-world popularity proxy.
4) Model elicitation: two protocols on OLMo-3-7B and OLMo-3.1-32B—Direct (ask for popularity) and Pairwise Comparison (which entity is more popular). The paper treats pairwise comparison as a more stable behavioral readout, less sensitive to calibration and free-form generation artifacts.
5) Analysis strategy: correlations are examined not only globally but also by entity type and popularity quantiles to surface head vs tail differences and type-specific effects.19:T564,The main technical contribution is the RedirectQA dataset. Starting from Wikidata factual triples, the authors map each subject entity to a Wikipedia article title as the canonical surface form, then use Wikipedia redirects to collect alternative mentions of the same entity. A useful detail is that redirects are not treated as uniformly valid aliases; the dataset construction includes filtering and category annotations, separating alternative names, abbreviations/initialisms, spelling variants, diacritic changes, punctuation variants, and common erroneous forms. The evaluation protocol holds the relation and gold object fixed and changes only the subject string, which removes confounds from template choice or fact difficulty. The analysis uses both answer accuracy and prediction consistency across canonical–redirect pairs, allowing the paper to distinguish “the model does not know the fact” from “the model knows it but accesses it unreliably.” A second key analysis decomposes frequency into entity-level aggregate frequency and surface-level frequency. If only surface frequency mattered, the phenomenon would look mostly string-local. If entity frequency still explained performance after controlling for surface frequency, that would indicate shared entity-level representations or cross-surface transfer. The paper finds the latter, but only partially.1a:T4fe,The study evaluates 13 LLMs. A strong early diagnostic comes from Pythia-12B on a redirect-augmented PopQA setup: 23.7% of canonical–redirect question pairs produce inconsistent predictions. That already shows how much canonical-only evaluation can miss. In the full RedirectQA experiments, inconsistency remains common across models and depends strongly on the type of surface variation. Models are relatively robust to small orthographic changes such as spelling, diacritics, and punctuation, but much less stable under larger lexical shifts such as aliases, alternative names, and abbreviations. The frequency analysis is the most informative part of the paper. Surface-form frequency correlates with accuracy, as expected, but entity-level aggregate frequency often retains explanatory power beyond the specific surface frequency. This suggests that factual access is not learned independently for each alias. A low-frequency alias can sometimes benefit from a high-frequency canonical form, yet the transfer is incomplete, which is why the same fact can still fail under a different name.
