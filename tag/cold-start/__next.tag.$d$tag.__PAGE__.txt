9:T660,Let Pθ = pθ(y* | x*) be the model’s total probability of producing the correct output y* for input x*. Since the reasoning trace z is latent, Pθ marginalizes over possible trajectories. The paper defines a Tsallis-loss continuum ℓq = -logq(Pθ) = (1 - Pθ^(1-q)) / (1-q). At q=0, ℓ0 = 1 - Pθ, which corresponds to expected-accuracy maximization and RLVR under exact-match rewards. At q=1, ℓ1 = -log Pθ, which is the log marginal likelihood over latent trajectories. The central identity is that all q values share the same per-example gradient direction and differ only by a scalar: ∇θℓq = Pθ^(-q) ∇θℓ0 = Pθ^(1-q) ∇θℓ1. Higher q amplifies low-Pθ examples more strongly, so the model commits faster to unfamiliar supervision. Lower q keeps the loss bounded and reduces sensitivity to corrupted labels. This reweighting cannot be reproduced by a single global learning rate, because a learning rate scales every example equally while q changes the relative weighting across examples. Since exact Pθ is intractable, the paper derives Monte Carlo estimators through two factorizations. The RL factorization uses all M sampled rationales with Rao-Blackwellized rewards pθ(y* | x*, z), improving statistical use of samples but potentially mixing in trajectories that do not semantically support the answer. The FT factorization approximately samples from the posterior over rationales compatible with the correct answer and then fine-tunes on them, giving more coherent gradients at the cost of sampling efficiency and coverage.
