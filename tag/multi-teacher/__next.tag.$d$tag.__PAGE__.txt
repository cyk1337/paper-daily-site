9:T60f,VG-OPD has two layers of selection: criterion-level expert eligibility and token-level supervision placement.

For the who question, each prompt comes with a rubric made of criteria, verifiers, and weights. Instead of collapsing the rubric into a single scalar reward and discarding structure, the method scores both the student rollout and each candidate expert answer with the same criterion-specific verifier. An expert is allowed to teach a criterion only if it shows a verified advantage over the student on that criterion for the current sample. This is a local, sample-specific notion of expertise, not a fixed domain prior.

For the which question, even an eligible expert does not supervise the whole sequence. The method identifies positions where the expert disagrees with the student and applies distillation only on those tokens through a sparse mask. The intuition is simple: if the student already matches the expert, there is little value in forcing additional imitation; the useful signal lies where the expert would actually change the student’s local decision.

Training remains inside an on-policy RL loop. The base policy update uses a rubric-based GRPO-style objective on the student’s own rollouts. The distillation term is a token-level reverse-KL estimate, but its weight is no longer uniform. It is gated by verifier-based eligibility and by the disagreement mask. In that sense, prompt-level multi-expert OPD methods such as MOPD appear as a special case with fixed teacher assignment and dense, uniform token weighting.a:T563,The setup uses four RL-trained specialist experts and evaluates 4B and 8B students on seven benchmarks spanning scientific reasoning, domain science, and general reasoning. The headline numbers reported in the paper are that, at 4B, VG-OPD improves average accuracy over rubric-GRPO by 2.0 points on scientific reasoning and 3.3 points on domain science, while consistently outperforming coarse-grained MOPD. The 8B student shows the same pattern and achieves the best overall performance across the evaluation suite.

The ablations are the most informative part. The paper claims that both components matter: verifier-based expert eligibility and selective token supervision. That is an important result because the two mechanisms solve different failure modes. Eligibility answers whether a teacher should be trusted at all on a given criterion. Token masking answers where that trusted signal should be applied. Removing either one should reintroduce noisy supervision.

When reading the experiments, it is worth checking a few details: sensitivity to the number and quality of criteria, sparsity of the supervision mask, and whether multiple experts are actually used within a single problem. If expert switching happens frequently inside one answer, that would support the paper’s central claim that capability integration needs finer routing than prompt-level assignment.b:T912,方法部分的关键不在于引入一个复杂新目标，而在于把 capability imbalance 对应到三个彼此独立的失真源，然后一一修正。

第一部分是 token-share balancing。作者观察到，按 prompt 均匀采样并不能带来按优化强度均匀训练，因为 OPD/RL 类目标是 token 级求和，长序列天然占据更多梯度份额。于是他们不再用固定 batch 内样本数控制 domain 配比，而是直接设定 weighted token-share target，让不同 domain 按目标 token 份额分配优化预算。这里的重点是“按 token share 控制”，而不是简单 oversample 短任务，因为 oversample 只在静态长度分布下近似有效，训练过程中 reward 强度变化后仍会漂移。

第二部分是 gap-aware allocation。即便初始 token 预算拉平，不同 domain 的 student-teacher gap 会以不同速度收敛。若仍固定配比，已经接近收敛的 domain 会继续消耗预算，而落后 domain 得不到补偿。作者因此用剩余 gap 来动态调度预算，把更多更新分给还没追上 teacher 的 domain。这里他们特别强调，不能只做 naive reward normalization，因为那会把预算错误地推向“看起来 reward 大但其实已接近收敛”的部分，文中给了一个在 step 74 直接训练崩掉的反例。

第三部分是 student reward refresh。标准多步 rollout reuse 会在多个 gradient step 中重复使用同一批 rollout 上基于旧 student 计算的 reward/advantage。对于依赖 student log-prob 的 OPD 信号，这会迅速变 stale，尤其在不同 domain 收敛速度不同的时候，staleness 会进一步放大预算失真。作者的做法是缓存 teacher states，但在每个 gradient step 前重新计算 student log-prob，从而刷新 student-dependent reward 组件。这样保留了 teacher 侧缓存带来的效率，又避免了 stale advantage。

全文还有一个重要的负结果：作者专门测了 token-level teacher disagreement，结论是它不像很多人预想的那样是主导瓶颈。也就是说，多 teacher 之间监督不一致并不是主要矛盾，真正的问题是训练信号如何被分配、何时失真、何时过时。这一点让方法更像“优化与预算控制”问题，而不是“多专家冲突消解”问题。c:T78a,实验设计很扎实，重点在于把 integration gap 定量化。基座是 SmolLM3-3B-Base，先做三域 mixed-SFT，再训练三个 domain RL teacher，然后用 oracle routing 做 M-OPD。这样 deployment 时的 RouteOPD/RouteRL 可以作为上界或参考，避免把路由错误混进结论里。

First, token-share balancing addresses the fact that prompt-balanced sampling does not imply optimization-balanced training. In OPD-style objectives, longer responses contribute more token-level loss and therefore more gradient mass. The authors replace implicit prompt-level balancing with an explicit weighted token-share target across domains. The point is not just to oversample short tasks. Oversampling is only a rough proxy under a fixed length distribution, while token-share control directly targets the quantity that drives optimization.

Second, gap-aware allocation handles the fact that even if raw token budgets are equalized at the start, they drift quickly because the student closes the teacher gap at different speeds in different domains. A fixed allocation then wastes updates on already-converged domains while under-training lagging ones. The proposed mechanism dynamically steers budget toward domains with the largest remaining student–teacher gap. The paper also shows why naive reward normalization is not enough and can even destabilize training by funneling budget into the wrong places.

Third, student reward refresh fixes staleness introduced by multi-step rollout reuse. In standard OPD/PPO-like training, the same rollout is reused for several gradient steps, while token-level rewards or advantages depend partly on student log-probabilities. Those student-dependent terms become stale quickly, especially when domains move at different rates. The authors cache teacher states for efficiency but recompute student log-probabilities before each gradient step, refreshing the student-side reward component at low overhead.

An important negative result is that token-level teacher disagreement does not appear to be the dominant bottleneck under their metric. That matters. It shifts the framing from “multi-teacher conflict resolution” to “optimization budget allocation and signal freshness.”f:T7c0,The experimental setup is carefully controlled. The appendix is worth checking if you care about reproducing the pipeline.11:T4c9,MOPD (Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation) is a 3-stage pipeline:
1) General SFT to obtain a shared initialization.
2) Domain-specialized RL in parallel, producing a set of frozen domain teachers (math via verifiable RL, SWE via executable sandbox agent RL, etc.).
3) On-policy distillation fusion: initialize the student from the SFT checkpoint; for each batch of prompts, the student generates its own trajectories (on-policy rollouts). Each trajectory is routed to the corresponding domain teacher, which runs a prefill on that exact trajectory to produce per-token distributions. The student is updated by minimizing per-token reverse KL (student || teacher) along the trajectory.
Key properties:
- On-policy training eliminates exposure bias by matching train-time and inference-time state distributions.
- Token-level distribution matching provides a dense, low-variance learning signal compared to trajectory-level rewards.
- Integration happens in policy space via prompt-to-teacher routing, avoiding weight-space arithmetic/averaging instabilities.
The paper also highlights practical knobs (top-k distillation, same-origin teachers for stability, multi-round refinement), which are worth checking in the ablations.12:T411,CoRD, or Collaborative Reasoning Decoding, treats each reasoning step as a high-level decoding unit. At each step, multiple teacher LRMs propose candidate next steps conditioned on the same current prefix. The system scores the resulting partial trajectories and extends promising ones. The method has three central components. First, prompt-guided step segmentation makes reasoning steps more consistent across teachers. Second, predictive perplexity scores a partial reasoning prefix by measuring how well the ground-truth answer is predicted given that prefix; this provides an early signal without waiting for a complete trace. Third, beam search maintains multiple candidate trajectories, which helps preserve paths that initially look weaker but later lead to useful self-correction or strategic shifts. Compared with S1- or LIMO-style curation, CoRD collaborates during generation rather than selecting after generation.
