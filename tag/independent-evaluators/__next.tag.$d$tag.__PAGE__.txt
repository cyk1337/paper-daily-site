9:T509,Anthropic describes a three-tier evaluator access model. Tier one supports narrowly scoped risk investigations, with Anthropic deciding what access to provide and approving publication. Tier two gives evaluators more information and allows publication without Anthropic approval, while still letting Anthropic require removal of predefined sensitive categories; evaluators can publicly state whether they think redactions were justified. Tier three is the METR arrangement. Six METR employees receive company hardware, office space, and access to internal tools and systems roughly comparable to Anthropic's own risk assessment teams. Apart from a small set of defined exclusions, such as customer data, attorney-client privileged information, and model architecture information, Anthropic employees are encouraged to share information with them as they would with an internal colleague working on alignment risk. METR can publish analyses of risk levels, incidents, and practices. Anthropic cannot veto publication, but can require redactions within predefined categories, and METR can characterize those redactions publicly without revealing the redacted content. The key design choice is the separation of access rights, publication rights, redaction rights, and organizational siloing.a:T4c4,There are no model experiments or benchmark results. Anthropic has onboarded six METR employees with company hardware, office space, and access to internal tools and systems close to those used by internal risk assessment teams. The embedded METR group is siloed from other METR employees, including those working with Anthropic on other projects. METR will publish its analysis of Anthropic's risk levels, incidents, or practices. Anthropic cannot veto publication, though it can require removal of predefined sensitive information, and METR can publicly state whether it views those removals as justified.
