9:T447,Xiaomi-Lens-0 is a 4B MLLM built on Qwen3.5. It reads spatial features from the vision encoder patch grid before the spatial merger. To specify a target region, the language model emits a special mask token. The contextual hidden state of this token is treated as a visual latent representing the region requested by the instruction or referenced in the response. A lightweight mask decoder combines this visual latent with shared visual patch features to predict a mask. Joint language and mask training updates both the shared visual features and the language states that select target regions. The paper also introduces thinking with visual latents: textual reasoning is interleaved with region-specific visual latents, each of which can be decoded into an explicit mask. Grounded reasoning traces are constructed to pair intermediate region references in reasoning chains with masks. This differs from LISA, Sa2VA, and Qwen3-VL-Seg-style designs that rely on extra segmentation or image encoders, and also differs from approaches that freeze the vision encoder and train only a mask decoder.a:T487,The evaluation spans six task families: REC, RES, multi-instance grounding, reasoning segmentation, embodied referring, and fine-grained perception. On the eight REC splits of RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, Xiaomi-Lens-0 improves over the Qwen3.5 backbone on every split, with an average gain of 5.6 points. For RES, it reports the highest cIoU among compared models on all splits. On COCO 2017 multi-instance grounding, it achieves the best F1 averaged over IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95. Beyond localization, it outperforms its backbone on all reported fine-grained perception benchmarks. The authors also introduce LensBench, covering explicit and implicit instructions, single and multiple targets, and general, aerial, driving, and panoramic imagery. Xiaomi-Lens-0 obtains higher cIoU in aerial, driving, and panoramic domains, while implicit instructions remain difficult, especially multi-target selection in panoramic scenes. The conditions are: image_only, object_named, part_named, localization_only, localization_shown, and mechanics_shown. The critical design choice is that from object_named, the benchmark branches into part_named and localization_only. This lets the evaluator measure the effect of target identity and target region separately. The two branches merge again at localization_shown, and mechanics_shown adds a bundled mechanics descriptor containing joint type and motion axis.

This factorization matters because localization_only is not just a weaker version of localization_shown. It is the control that asks whether knowing where the relevant region is, without knowing what that part is called, helps at all. Conversely, part_named asks whether the semantic identity of the part is already enough to drive the answer even without explicit localization.

The paper also adds a counterfactual re-ask setup: the model is given a real but noncanonical part name from the same image and object, and the evaluator checks whether the output follows the changed part request or defaults to the canonical action. The authors are careful not to overclaim here. They frame it as a prompt-compliance test, not proof of visual attention.

A less flashy but important contribution is the evaluator design. The benchmark explicitly guards against two measurement errors found in the companion evaluation: fixed thresholds that let trivial constant baselines score surprisingly high, and incorrect action-label rules. GroundBench derives thresholds and baselines from the scored data rather than fixing them in advance, which makes the reported differences more trustworthy.c:T55f,The experimental scale is modest but targeted: 1,068 predictions across three OpenAI models. The main findings are clear.

First, localization_only, which provides the target region while withholding the part identity, gives essentially no gain over a constant baseline. This suggests that merely telling the model where to look is not enough if the model does not know what semantic part category that region corresponds to.

Second, the gains appear mainly in conditions that provide target-part identity, such as part_named and localization_shown. The authors’ interpretation is that the improvement comes from supplying a category variable that already determines the answer within the curated object set, rather than from stronger grounding or mechanical reasoning.

Third, adding the mechanics descriptor yields limited additional benefit, implying that joint type and motion axis are not the dominant bottleneck in this benchmark. The larger bottleneck remains part identity and its association with action labels.

The paper repeatedly contrasts these findings with the companion evaluation, where naming the target part improved action accuracy by 0.32 to 0.63 across eight VLMs and nearly recovered push-action performance.
