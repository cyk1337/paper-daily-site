1b:T542,There are two main components. First, Poly-DPO extends Diffusion-DPO with an additional polynomial term that reweights samples according to model confidence. The motivation is that vanilla DPO keeps applying strong updates both to mislabeled or conflict-heavy pairs and to trivially separable pairs, which can amplify noise and induce overconfidence. Poly-DPO introduces a confidence-dependent correction, controlled by a hyperparameter alpha, so that samples contribute differently across confidence regimes. The intended effect is twofold: reduce the damage from contradictory preference labels and avoid wasting optimization on overly easy pairs. The idea is reminiscent of confidence-aware reweighting in classification, but here the target is the noise structure specific to pairwise preference learning. Second, the paper builds ViPO, a large-scale high-quality preference dataset: 1M image pairs at 1024px across five categories and 300K video pairs at 720p+ across three categories. The data are generated with newer models such as FLUX, Qwen-Image, and WanVideo, then curated with more systematic category design and balancing. A key claim throughout the paper is that robust optimization and better data are complementary rather than interchangeable: Poly-DPO is needed for imperfect data, while clean data make standard DPO sufficient.1c:T50d,The most important findings are trend-level rather than a single benchmark number. On noisy datasets such as Pick-a-Pic V2, Poly-DPO consistently outperforms standard Diffusion-DPO, supporting the claim that confidence-aware reweighting can extract more stable learning signals from conflicting preferences. The paper also shows that vanilla DPO saturates as more noisy preference data are added, whereas Poly-DPO delays that saturation. On the newly constructed ViPO data, the gains from data quality are larger than what prior open datasets provide. Training SD1.5 on ViPO yields results clearly beyond models trained on existing preference datasets, and SD3.5-Medium also improves broadly. The most informative ablation is on alpha: on ViPO-Image-1M, the best alpha moves toward zero, and performance remains stable in a neighborhood around zero. That means Poly-DPO effectively reduces to standard DPO on high-quality data, which is strong evidence that the algorithm is compensating for data defects rather than replacing good data. The paper also documents structural issues in prior datasets, including low resolution, limited prompt diversity, random-collection imbalance, and outdated generators; these observations are part of the causal story for why preference scaling had stalled.1d:T490,Start with Figure 1 and Table 1 in the introduction. They define the paper’s real claim: preference optimization does not fail because the paradigm is weak, but because current datasets do not provide stable preference supervision. Then read the method section with one question in mind: what exactly does the polynomial term change relative to Diffusion-DPO, and why should that help both conflict-heavy and trivially easy pairs? If time is limited, do not overinvest in the derivation details. The more useful part is understanding the role of alpha and how its optimal value changes with dataset quality. In the experiments, prioritize three pieces: the noisy-dataset comparison against vanilla DPO, the gains from ViPO over prior datasets, and the alpha ablation showing alpha approaching zero on clean data. This is a 20–30 minute skim if the goal is to extract the main idea.
