9:T90f,The paper proceeds in three steps. First is geometric approximation. Inputs are assumed to lie on a compact support with intrinsic dimension d. The target function is approximated by partitioning that support into K local cells and fitting simple local approximants. The model family is summarized by an effective partition budget K≈χN_e^γ, where χ captures partition efficiency and γ captures how expressivity grows with effective parameters. Combined with assumptions on smoothness, this yields a parameter-dependent approximation rate. A key point is that the parameter-scaling exponent is not primitive: it emerges from both the geometry of the support and the regularity of the target.

The second step is a spectral view of data scaling. Additional data reduce error only to the extent that they expose unresolved predictive mass. The relevant ingredients are the eigenvalue decay of the feature covariance, which directions are statistically visible at a given sample size, and how strongly the target aligns with those directions. If the target is concentrated in high-variance, easy-to-estimate modes, data scaling is favorable. If the remaining signal lives in the spectral tail, marginal returns from more data shrink quickly. This is close in spirit to spectral bias analyses in kernel or linearized settings, but the paper uses it as an explanatory lens for empirical data-scaling laws.

The third step is constrained optimization. With dense-training cost modeled as C≈κND, the paper combines parameter-limited and data-limited error terms and derives the optimal balance between N and D under a fixed training budget. It then extends the discussion to training-vs.-inference trade-offs. The treatment of test-time scaling is careful: gains from more samples are separated from gains due to improving the quality of each sample, which matters when interpreting pass@k-style improvements.

Two additional themes are worth noting. The paper repeatedly emphasizes that power laws only hold on connected positive-resource intervals; small-data plateaus and near-floor regimes should not be forced into a single slope. It also discusses extrapolation uncertainty explicitly: floor, amplitude, and slope are coupled, and the practical effect of slope error grows with log-distance from the pilot range.a:T5c7,The paper combines controlled experiments with a computational separation. In the experiments, a base model is trained on ground-truth demonstrations, then wrapped with a scaffold to form a teacher. A same-class, same-size student is trained on the teacher’s traces, either from scratch or by fine-tuning the base model. The tasks include Dyck languages, Sokoban, and modular arithmetic. The scaffolds include verifier-guided backtracking, A* search with model-pruned expansions, and a simpler fixed-token-deletion regeneration rule. The key experimental design separates local competence from compositional competence: the base model can perform the local steps, while the scaffold maintains candidates, search frontiers, visited sets, or verifier-controlled backtracking state. The theoretical part models search through directed s-t reachability. The base model answers local edge queries, which are computable by constant-depth Boolean circuits. The scaffold repeatedly uses these local queries to run BFS and decide reachability. Internalizing the scaffold requires the student to decide reachability from the graph representation. Under standard precision assumptions, fixed-depth polynomial-size Transformers can be simulated by TC0 circuits, even with logarithmic-length chain-of-thought in the cited setting. Building on prior queue-machine simulations of Turing machines, the authors fold L consecutive explicit CoT transitions into these L hidden-state iterations, so that one emitted token summarizes what previously required L generated reasoning tokens. The main technical obstacle is layer alignment. In ordinary CoT simulations, every past token is visible to every layer; in a looped transformer, iteration l can only read entries written into the iteration-l cache at earlier positions. The construction therefore reorganizes the queue-machine simulation so that the queue fronts needed at a given simulated step are stored exactly in the cache slice consumed by the corresponding loop iteration. This is implemented through per-iteration KV caches together with sliding-window attention.
