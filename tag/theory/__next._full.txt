19:T4bd,The authors model the space of decision-relevant situations as a topological space X, and a collection of contexts as an open cover {U_i}. Evidence on how a quantity contrasts between two contexts is represented as a real-valued 1-cochain ω on the nerve of the cover: an edge stores the contrast estimated on the overlapping population shared by two contexts, a triangle records the 3-way consistency condition. An agent transporting a claim across a chain of contexts is then performing path integration of this cochain. The core theorem states that conclusions are path-independent if and only if ω is exact (i.e., ω = δθ for some global assignment θ on vertices), and that disagreement between different paths is exactly the holonomy of the first Čech cohomology class [ω] ∈ H¹. Hodge decomposition splits evidence conflict into three orthogonal pieces: coboundary (fixable by recalibration), coexact (requires re-measurement), and harmonic (invisible to every local check; only remediable by refining the cover itself). First, it solves a minimal linear setting exactly. The setup uses two binary concepts, such as shape and color, giving four classes. The dataset kernel is assumed to have a structured block form called 2FS, which compresses the geometry into a small number of entry types and corresponding modes. In this basis, the representation can be decomposed into interpretable components: concept-aligned modes and an orthogonal interaction mode that reduces parallelism between concept vectors. This interaction mode acts as a noise direction for abstraction. With this decomposition, abstraction, measured as cosine similarity between counterfactual concept vectors, becomes an explicit function of mode amplitudes. The authors then derive exact gradient-descent trajectories for these amplitudes, yielding a closed-form training-time trajectory of abstraction rather than only a convergence statement.

Second, the theory is extended to arbitrary depth in deep linear networks. The key result is not that depth always helps abstraction. Instead, depth changes the relative learning dynamics of different spectral modes. Final abstraction and the maximum abstraction reached during training both depend on how depth reweights the competition between concept modes and interaction modes. This is a more nuanced statement than the common intuition that deeper representations are simply more abstract.

Third, the paper moves to infinite-width nonlinear networks. In this regime, it proves that nonlinearities weaken abstraction in features relative to preactivations. For ReLU in particular, the dynamics become more input-driven and less target-driven, which makes it easier for interaction modes to persist and therefore lowers abstraction. The paper also provides a theoretical explanation for prior empirical observations that tanh-like nonlinearities often produce more abstract representations than ReLU.

An especially useful distinction is between final abstraction and the maximum abstraction attained during training. Because the trajectory can be non-monotonic, the most abstract checkpoint may occur before convergence.1c:T692,The empirical section serves two purposes: validating the theory in controlled settings and checking whether the same qualitative laws appear in modern models.

In controlled experiments, the paper shows that abstraction trajectories in realistic deep networks can be non-monotonic and can plateau well below 1, matching the motivation in Figure 1C using a ConvNet on 3dshapes. Figure 1D is particularly important: abstraction tracks cross-context probe generalization. For example, a shape classifier trained only on red samples and tested on blue samples has an error curve that follows the abstraction measure and is well approximated by the theory. This makes abstraction more than a geometric curiosity; it is tied to out-of-distribution linear readout performance.

The more relevant modern-model evidence appears in Section 5. The authors report that ablating nonlinearities improves abstraction and linear-probe generalization in DINOv3 and Gemma 4. The supplied text does not include the full numerical tables, so it would be unsafe to invent exact gains, but the claim is presented as a consistent trend rather than a one-off layer effect. The paper also analyzes macaque ventral-stream data and finds increasing abstraction along the hierarchy, showing that the framework is intended to bridge machine learning and neuroscience.
