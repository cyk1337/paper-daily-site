9:T788,The method builds a proxy for the “visual direction” using a controlled intervention, then projects the teacher correction onto that direction. During training, the student generates prefixes on the full image x^0. A fixed teacher is queried on the same textual prefix under two training-only views: an evidence-present view x+ and an evidence-removed or degraded view x-. The difference between their centered log-probabilities defines u_t, a signed proxy for how revealing or removing the relevant evidence changes token odds. The sign matters: visual evidence can support some candidates and actively refute others.

VAD then avoids directly matching the privileged teacher distribution. Instead, it starts from the teacher correction relative to the student distribution and extracts the component aligned with u_t through a regularized projection. Intuitively, the teacher may want to move the student in many directions, but only the part consistent with the intervention response is treated as distillable visual correction. The remaining component is kept as an unexplained residual rather than being assumed to be useful supervision. A new target is reconstructed around the student’s current full-image distribution: tokens supported by the intervention are raised, tokens refuted by the intervention are suppressed, and teacher shifts not captured by the proxy are excluded.

Two stabilization choices matter. First, the method keeps a weak regularizer derived from the original teacher correction, so optimization does not rely entirely on a noisy counterfactual proxy. Second, u_t is split into support and refutation branches, allowing separate control over which candidates should be promoted and which should be pushed down. This is a meaningful departure from positive-only visual-advantage weighting. The paper’s central claim is that VAD changes what to distill, not merely how strongly to distill it.a:T65c,The evaluation uses 4B and 9B students on six fine-grained visual benchmarks. The most important empirical signal is not a single leaderboard number but a set of consistent diagnostics. First, the paper shows that in Vision-OPD only 23.2% of the strongest response-token corrections for the 4B model and 22.8% for the 9B model are strongly aligned with the evidence-conditioned teacher response. That diagnostic is the empirical basis for the source-mixing claim.

Second, VAD consistently outperforms direct privileged-view distillation and visual-advantage weighting across both model scales and all six benchmarks. The introduction does not expose the full table values, but the paper presents the gains as broad rather than concentrated in one task, which supports the target-reconstruction argument.

Third, the token-level semantic analyses are particularly useful. The intervention-derived proxy and the proxy-aligned correction are enriched in visually relevant, task-relevant, and answer-correct tokens, rather than generic language continuation tokens. This is one of the stronger pieces of evidence that the method is capturing visually attributable correction instead of introducing another heuristic bias.

Fourth, controlled offline studies indicate that the reconstructed targets preserve useful visual correction while filtering teacher shifts that the proxy cannot explain.
