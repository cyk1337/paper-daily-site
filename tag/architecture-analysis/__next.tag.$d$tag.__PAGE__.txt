The first uses released large-model checkpoints and performs an exact topology intervention without retraining. The authors instrument every normalization module in Huginn-3.5B, Ouro-1.4B, and Ouro-2.6B, counting calls under different loop counts to determine whether a given normalization acts inside the loop or only on the readout path. This audit shows that Ouro applies its model-level normalization once per loop step, creating a loop-boundary normalization, while Huginn applies the corresponding module only once before readout.

They then modify Ouro in a tightly controlled way: the model-level normalization is restricted to the final loop step only. This keeps the readout path bit-identical, adds no parameters, and is exactly the identity when r=1, which serves as an implementation check. That intervention isolates the effect on the state that re-enters the next loop iteration while minimizing confounds.

The second half is causal: small looped models are trained from scratch with the topology fixed in advance, with and without boundary normalization. The models range from 19M parameters at 6 layers to 76M at 24 layers, across two task families, with 16 independent seeds per configuration on the normalization axis and 50 models overall. The unit of analysis is the training seed, and the paper uses exact rank tests and Cliff’s delta rather than relying on single runs.
