19:T404,Starting from the established key-value memory interpretation of vanilla FFNs, the work first proves that static or per-pair query projections can be absorbed into the key matrix without increasing expressivity. To unlock additional capacity, dynamically computing per-pair queries and keys from input tokens is required, but this is parameter-prohibitive for large hidden dimensions. A rank-1 outer product approximation of each per-pair QK^T matrix recovers exactly the elementwise multiplicative form of gated MLPs. The work further proves that applying nonlinear activation to one branch (the query branch) before multiplication breaks two symmetries of the unmodified rank-1 formulation: (1) query-key exchange symmetry, broken by any nonlinear activation (only linear activations preserve it); (2) inverse-scaling symmetry, broken by non-homogeneous activations such as SiLU and GELU (ReLU preserves it for positive scaling factors due to positive homogeneity). A rank-R generalization of the formulation is also proposed.1a:T63b,This is a unified evaluation and controlled-analysis paper. The authors evaluate eight modern DLMs, covering both pure diffusion and block-diffusion families, and compare them against strong autoregressive baselines under a shared protocol. The key contribution is not a catalog of model internals, but a decomposition of the main degrees of freedom that govern DLM behavior. First, they run a unified downstream evaluation across eight benchmarks spanning general knowledge, reasoning, coding, and machine translation, instead of letting each model be judged on a different task mix. Second, they treat inference budget as a first-class variable: denoising steps, sequence length, block size, and unmasking ratio are explicitly analyzed rather than hidden as implementation details. Third, they run controlled small-scale scaling experiments under matched training conditions to compare perplexity and scaling behavior with fewer confounds from data and compute differences. Fourth, they add a systems view by reporting peak memory and FLOPs not only for a single forward pass but for full generation. The most informative comparison is between pure diffusion and block diffusion. Pure diffusion offers stronger global parallel refinement in principle, but often requires many denoising rounds. Block diffusion tries to recover some of that flexibility while reducing total generation cost through local blockwise generation and conditioning. The paper’s contribution is to place these design choices into one experimental framework and study how they affect quality, efficiency, and scaling.1b:T566,The experiments appear in three main groups. The first is the unified benchmark evaluation: eight DLMs are compared with autoregressive baselines on eight tasks covering knowledge, reasoning, coding, and translation. The authors emphasize comparable inference budgets rather than paper-specific best-case settings. The second group is hyperparameter and decoding ablations: they study how performance changes with more denoising steps, where diminishing returns begin, how sequence length and block size affect both quality and cost, and how parallel unmasking trades off throughput against output quality. The third group is computational analysis: peak memory and FLOPs are reported for both single forward passes and complete generation, making the deployment cost of pure diffusion and block diffusion directly comparable. The abstract and introduction do not expose all numeric results, but the paper is clearly organized around a few stable conclusions: DLM performance is highly sensitive to inference hyperparameters; more denoising steps usually improve quality but with non-linear returns; block-diffusion models often sit in a more practical efficiency regime, while pure diffusion better exposes the promise of global refinement but at higher end-to-end generation cost; and controlled perplexity/scaling experiments help separate modeling effects from decoding effects.1c:T4b0,Start with the introduction and setup sections to identify which eight DLMs are included, which are pure diffusion, which are block diffusion, and what autoregressive baselines are used.
