18:T711,The method is simple, but the framing is sharp. In standard multi-reward GRPO, the total reward is the sum of reward components, and the advantage is computed by centering and dividing by the within-group standard deviation of that total reward. The paper expands this variance term into the sum of all pairwise covariances among reward components. This reveals the issue: covariance mixes two effects, dependence and scale. If one reward has large variance, or if large-scale rewards are strongly positively correlated with others, they can inflate the denominator and shrink the advantage for the whole group.

CorrGRPO keeps the numerator unchanged: it still uses the centered total reward, so the relative weighting of reward components in the objective is preserved. The change is only in the denominator. Instead of covariance-based normalization, it uses Pearson-correlation-based normalization. In effect, each covariance term is divided by the corresponding component standard deviations, removing scale dependence. The diagonal terms of the resulting correlation matrix give equal footing to all reward components with nonzero within-group variance, while off-diagonal terms capture only the strength and direction of dependence. This lets the normalization remain sensitive to whether rewards reinforce or conflict with each other, without letting large numerical scales dominate.

An important practical point is that CorrGRPO does not redefine reward aggregation and does not require a new critic. It only changes advantage scaling, so it can be inserted into existing GRPO pipelines with low implementation cost. It is also conceptually different from manual reward reweighting or simple reward rescaling, because it modifies the normalization statistics rather than the reward objective itself.19:T526,The experiments cover three naturally multi-reward domains: code generation, tool calling, and agent security, using models from 0.5B to 8B parameters. The paper compares CorrGRPO against standard GRPO and related variants, and reports improvements in code accuracy and training efficiency, tool-call accuracy, and the utility–security tradeoff for agents. It also claims an expansion of the empirical Pareto frontier between competing reward objectives.

The most important empirical story is not a single benchmark number. First, in heterogeneous-reward settings, CorrGRPO is more robust because small-scale rewards are no longer drowned out by the covariance structure of large-scale rewards. Second, in tradeoff-heavy settings such as agent security, correlation-based normalization better preserves the distinction between conflicting rewards, which improves the balance between task utility and safety. Third, in code and tool-use tasks where rewards are partly positively correlated, standard GRPO can over-shrink updates when large-scale rewards dominate the denominator, while CorrGRPO avoids that failure mode.
