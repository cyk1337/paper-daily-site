17:T7a5,The method has two main components. The first is Quantile Token Regression. Instead of predicting all quantiles from a single pooled representation, the model inserts learnable quantile tokens such as <Q_tau1> ... <Q_tauQ> directly into the input sequence. These tokens participate in self-attention together with the text tokens. Each quantile token can attend to different parts of the input and accumulate quantile-specific evidence across layers. A small regression head on top of each final quantile-token representation outputs the corresponding quantile value. The key change is architectural: the path becomes input -> quantile token interaction, rather than input -> shared representation -> separate heads. This gives each quantile a more direct route to the relevant evidence while still allowing joint modeling through shared attention.

The second component is retrieval-augmented distribution estimation. For each query, the model retrieves semantically similar neighbors from a candidate pool and appends both their texts and their empirical quantile summaries to the context. This differs from prior retrieval-based regression setups that attach only a single scalar label to each neighbor. Here, each neighbor carries a full quantile grid, so the model can observe not only central tendency but also spread and tail behavior in nearby examples. The paper positions this as local grounding for uncertainty estimation.

The loss discussion is also notable. Beyond standard quantile losses such as the pinball loss, the authors analyze what different objectives optimize under empirical-quantile supervision. That part appears more clarifying than radically new, but it helps explain why some objectives favor calibration while others favor point accuracy. Overall, the main idea is to treat structured quantile prediction as a token-level modeling problem and to extend retrieval augmentation from point prediction to distribution prediction.18:T671,The experiments use two newly constructed text-to-distribution datasets. One is based on Inside Airbnb, where the task is to predict a price distribution from listing text. The other is StackSample, derived from Stack Overflow related text and paired with a continuous target distribution. Each example has multiple observations rather than a single label, enabling empirical quantile supervision. The backbone models are Qwen3 variants ranging from 1.7B to 14B parameters.

The reported findings are straightforward. First, retrieval augmentation consistently improves quantile regression across scales: average MAPE is reduced by 8% relatively on Airbnb and by 63% on StackSample. The much larger gain on StackSample suggests that the task relies more heavily on local analogies and that the query text alone is less informative. Second, the quantile-token architecture outperforms the shared-representation baseline, with a 14% relative reduction in average MAPE on StackSample and prediction intervals that are 6x narrower. If accuracy improves while intervals become much tighter, that indicates the baseline may have been compensating for weak uncertainty modeling by producing overly diffuse estimates. Third, combining quantile tokens with neighbor context gives the best overall results, suggesting that the architectural change and retrieval grounding are complementary.

The paper also includes ablations and loss-function analysis. The most important checks when reading the full version are whether quantile crossing is explicitly handled and how the retrieval pool is constructed to avoid leakage between train and evaluation
