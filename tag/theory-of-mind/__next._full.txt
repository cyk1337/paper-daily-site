18:T492,CARD has three steps. First, for each axis such as belief, intent, or knowledge, the authors train L2-regularized logistic probes on per-layer, per-head activations at the final input token. Each example contains a matched statement and its negation, and the learned unit vector points from the negated statement toward the matched one. Second, they select the top K=56 layer-head pairs by held-out probe accuracy, roughly 7% of all heads, and use forward hooks to add a scaled version of the probe direction only at the final-token position and only in those head slices. The steering scale is alpha=+10 or alpha=-10 in units of the activation standard deviation along the probe direction. Third, they measure a different prediction axis after steering, for example steering belief while evaluating action. The main statistics are the true-belief versus false-belief accuracy gap shift and a mean-accuracy shift. The accompanying Relay Chain benchmark is a cooperative grid-world setup with belief, knowledge, intent, and action questions, plus success and counter partitions that separate constant-prior behavior, world-state tracking, and belief-conditional behavior.19:T4dc,MindGames is built on TextArena, a text-only multi-agent environment with a gym-style interaction loop. A deterministic simulator maintains hidden state, renders an observation for the current player, receives a textual action, updates the state, and returns terminal rewards. The benchmark includes four games chosen to span different information and incentive structures: Colonel Blotto for hidden-information opponent modeling, Iterated Prisoner’s Dilemma for trust and betrayal under communication, Codenames for cooperative inference with constrained signaling, and Secret Mafia for deception and social deduction under partial observability. Evaluation is conducted through online matchmaking with TrueSkill ratings and full trajectory logging. Beyond the live arena, the paper introduces MG-Ref, a frozen reference pool of selected submissions plus a deterministic offline tournament protocol, so new agents can be rated offline with metrics compatible with the main benchmark. The main empirical contribution is not a model comparison table but an evaluation audit across environments. The paper finds that IPD and Colonel Blotto provide relatively interpretable signals, Codenames mixes strategic quality with error avoidance, and Secret Mafia suffers from a pronounced error-survival confound, where rankings can reflect robustness to failures or the ability to exploit opponent breakdowns as much as genuine strategic reasoning. 1) CoSToM framework: a two-stage pipeline “interpretation → intervention.”
- Stage A (Interpretation): use causal tracing to locate layers whose activations encode ToM/BDI (belief–desire–intention) information (RQ1). Given dialogue history x, extract layer-ℓ hidden state h^ℓ(x) and test whether ToM states can be decoded from it.
- Stage B (Intervention): perform activation steering on the identified ToM-critical layers to improve ToM reasoning and make it behaviorally stable in downstream dialogues (RQ2/RQ3). The method is designed to be lightweight: update only a small subset of parameters / a small set of layers, and operate plug-and-play.
2) Implementation detail visible in the excerpt: instantiate two copies/components—a context encoder producing activations and a probe decoder that queries those activations with ToM-focused questions to “read out” BDI content, enabling causal tests.
3) RQ-to-method mapping:
- RQ1: where does ToM information emerge/persist? → layerwise causal tracing + decodability.
- RQ2: can manipulating representations improve ToM? → targeted steering at ToM-sensitive layers.
- RQ3: does internal ToM alignment improve dialogue quality? → evaluate on negotiation/persuasion tasks.
