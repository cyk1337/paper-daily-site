9:T449,Multi-Block Editing adds a bounded revision phase to block diffusion. At inference time, after a new block is decoded, the method reopens a sliding window of already decoded blocks and performs joint forward passes over that window, allowing earlier tokens to be revised using later context. The design avoids token-level rollback logic and keeps the computation bounded by editing whole blocks through direct token-to-token revision. For windows beyond adjacent blocks, an entropy-based historical surprisal watermark is used to select the previously decoded block that is most likely to contain errors, rather than recomputing all history. The training component, Multi-Block Edit SFT, introduces bidirectional attention masks and a curriculum that expands the edit span from 2 to 4 blocks, so the model learns to exploit multi-block context instead of seeing it only at inference time. Its defining feature is that the corrector requires no additional training. Instead, it uses the structure of the already-trained concrete score function to construct the posterior quantities needed for Gibbs updates.

Operationally, GADD keeps a diffusion-style predictor that moves the sample from a high-noise state toward the target distribution. It then inserts a Gibbs-based corrector that updates discrete coordinates conditionally. The key observation is that the score estimator already contains enough local information to recover the relative posterior likelihoods needed for Gibbs sampling. This avoids the need for separately trained energy models, auxiliary correctors, or learned refinement networks.

A major part of the paper is the error-analysis framework. The total error is decomposed into initialization error, mixing error from the corrector, and score-estimation error. The argument is not simply that Gibbs mixes faster in isolation. Rather, the diffusion process provides a warm start, so the corrector does not need to mix from a poor initialization; at the same time, this warm start also reduces the impact of score-estimation error. Under this structure, the paper proves an overall sampling complexity of O(polylog(ε^{-1})). That is the central theoretical claim.

Relative to prior work, the differences are clear. Compared with exact simulation, τ-leaping, Euler, or Tweedie-style discretizations, GADD is not just a better numerical integrator for the reverse process; it explicitly introduces an MCMC corrector. Compared with fast samplers for masked diffusion, it does not rely on absorbing-state structure. Compared with methods that train additional correctors, it is training-free beyond the standard score model, which is an important practical property for large models.c:T681,The experiments cover synthetic distributions, zero-shot unconditional text sampling, and zero-shot conditional music generation.
