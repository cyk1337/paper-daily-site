9:T48e,The method centers on two diagnostic metrics. Structural Shortcut Reliance (SSR) uses attention heads to measure whether answer generation over-relies on a small set of salient shortcut cues—such as minimal query-to-answer paths in linearized graph triplets—while underusing the broader contextual evidence needed for verification. The input is partitioned into core structural cues and contextual cues, and SSR compares the attention mass assigned to each. Semantic Alignment Score (SAS) probes FFN representations to assess whether the model’s internal states stay aligned with the supporting evidence during generation. If semantic grounding weakens, the model is more likely to fall back to parametric memory and produce an answer that is plausible from training but unsupported by the current input. The paper studies these metrics on single-hop and multi-hop graph reasoning as well as tabular reasoning, and uses a quadrant-style analysis to show that attention bias and semantic drift do not always move together.
