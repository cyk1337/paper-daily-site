18:T4b4,The method has two layers. The first is High Entropy Phase (HEP), a variable-length segment of consecutive high-entropy tokens. Instead of averaging entropy over fixed windows, the paper detects bursts: a phase starts when token entropy crosses a high threshold and ends only after a specified number of consecutive tokens fall below a low threshold. This is meant to smooth token-level noise and better align the signal with a coherent reasoning unit under uncertainty. The second layer is the Entropy Centroid. Each HEP is assigned a mass equal to its token length and a position equal to its midpoint in the trajectory. The centroid is then the weighted average position of all HEPs in the trajectory. At selection time, among N sampled trajectories, the method simply chooses the one with the lowest centroid. The intuition is that good trajectories tend to explore early and settle later, whereas bad trajectories remain uncertain late into generation. Compared with prior intrinsic signals such as mean entropy, confidence, KL, or fixed-window aggregation, this method explicitly encodes temporal placement of uncertainty. Compared with reward-model-based selection, it adds no extra training cost.19:T43c,The evaluation spans models from 14B to 480B parameters and covers mathematics, code generation, logical reasoning, and agentic tasks. The analysis section appears to be more important than any single benchmark number. The paper shows that high-entropy tokens indeed occur in bursts rather than as isolated spikes, which justifies moving from tokens to phases. It also shows a separation between correct and incorrect trajectories in centroid space: correct trajectories concentrate uncertainty earlier, while incorrect ones exhibit more late-stage confusion. Relative to token-level entropy, confidence, KL-based signals, and fixed-window averages, the centroid better distinguishes useful exploration from unresolved uncertainty.
