9:Ta99,方法核心是 BALIGN，一种 balanced data selection 策略。它不是修改 DPO 目标，而是在训练前对 preference data 逐样本打分，过滤掉高风险样本，再用保留下来的子集做标准对齐训练。

作者先从 DPO 梯度出发分析参数漂移来源。虽然全文细节依赖具体推导，但结论可以概括为三类样本属性会显著影响 stability-plasticity trade-off。

第一是 reference model 的 length-normalized log-probability margin，也就是在初始参考模型下，chosen 相对 rejected 的对数概率优势，做长度归一化后得到的 margin。作者的观察是，margin 很大的样本通常意味着 base model 已经强烈偏向 chosen，这类样本再拿来做 DPO，提供的新信息少，但梯度仍会推动参数继续朝同一方向偏移，容易造成对已有分布的过度塑形。相反，margin 较小的样本更像“边界样本”，既能提供对齐信号，又不至于引发过大的无谓漂移。

第二是 chosen 和 rejected 的 token length difference。长度差大时，DPO 的偏好学习容易部分退化成长度偏置学习，因为对数概率是 token 级累积量，即便做了常见归一化，长短响应之间仍可能带来不对称梯度。作者把它视为稳定性风险的一部分：模型可能学到“更短”或“更长”的形式偏好，而不是更稳健的人类偏好。

第三是训练样本与通用 benchmark corpus 的 TF-IDF cosine similarity。这里作者把它作为 plasticity 相关特征来用：如果 preference data 和通用 SFT/benchmark 语料过于相似，那么它对“新偏好”的增量信息有限，反而可能在重复区域上过度更新；而与通用语料更不同的样本，更可能携带真正新的 alignment signal。这里的设计不是语义嵌入，而是 TF-IDF，原因大概是它更便宜、可解释，而且足以做粗粒度分布差异估计。

在这三个特征之上，BALIGN 为每个样本定义三个 risk score，并做归一化聚合成 composite risk score。高风险样本会被过滤，保留一个预设比例的低风险子集用于训练。论文强调这是一个 preprocessing step，因此可以直接套在 DPO 流程前面，不需要改模型结构，也不需要额外在线估计 Fisher 或重要性矩阵。

从方法定位看，它和以往 EWC、L2 anchoring、LoRA freezing、KL regularization 这类“训练时约束参数别动太多”的方法不同。BALIGN 的逻辑是：先减少那些最可能造成无效漂移的数据，再让标准优化去做剩下的事。这个角度更接近 pretraining 里常见的数据清洗、去重、课程学习，而不是传统对齐算法改造。a:T972,The method, BALIGN, is a balanced data-selection strategy applied before preference optimization. It does not modify DPO itself. Instead, it scores each preference pair, removes high-risk samples, and trains on a selected low-risk subset.

The analysis starts from the DPO gradient and identifies three sample-level features that govern the stability-plasticity trade-off.

The first is the length-normalized log-probability margin under the reference model, namely how much the initial model already prefers the chosen response over the rejected one. The paper argues that very large margins are risky: those samples add little new alignment information because the base model already strongly prefers the chosen answer, yet they still induce parameter updates that can further distort the model. Small-margin samples are more informative boundary cases and tend to produce less unnecessary drift.

The second is the token-length difference between chosen and rejected responses. Large length gaps can make preference learning partially collapse into learning a length bias, because sequence log-probabilities are token-accumulated quantities. Even with normalization, asymmetric gradients can remain. The paper treats this as a stability-related risk factor.

The third is the cosine similarity between TF-IDF representations of preference samples and those of a general benchmark or SFT-like corpus. This is used as a plasticity-oriented signal. If a preference sample is too similar to the general corpus, it may contribute little genuinely new alignment information while still causing repeated updates in already-covered regions. Samples that are more distinct are treated as more likely to carry useful alignment signal. The use of TF-IDF, rather than dense semantic embeddings, appears motivated by cost and interpretability.

BALIGN converts these three features into normalized risk scores and aggregates them into a composite risk score. High-risk samples are filtered out, and a predefined proportion of low-risk samples is retained for training. Conceptually, this differs from methods such as EWC, anchoring, KL regularization, or freezing-based strategies. Those methods constrain parameter movement during training. BALIGN instead tries to remove the data most likely to cause unproductive movement before training begins. In spirit, it is closer to data filtering and curriculum design in pretraining.b:T5cd,The experiments address two questions: whether the three proposed features are actually correlated with forgetting and alignment behavior, and whether training on the selected subset preserves alignment while reducing degradation of general capabilities.

From the available full-text content, the paper reports both theoretical and empirical evidence that lower reference margins, smaller chosen-rejected length differences, and lower TF-IDF similarity to a general corpus are associated with less catastrophic forgetting. This suggests that BALIGN is not just subsampling for efficiency; it is selecting examples that still provide alignment signal while causing less damage to previously learned capabilities.

The main outcome is that models trained on the filtered preference subset maintain alignment performance while preserving a broader set of general capabilities better than training on the full dataset. The paper emphasizes robustness across diverse capabilities rather than gains on a single benchmark.
