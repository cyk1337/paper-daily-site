18:T6dd,The method has three main pieces. First, a wavelet-domain representation. An 8-bit RGB image is converted to YCoCg with a reversible color transform, then decomposed by a four-level fixed 5/3 integer wavelet transform into 13 codec-defined subbands. This separates coarse low-frequency content from directional high-frequency details, turning one heterogeneous prediction stream into several statistically cleaner subproblems. Second, a numerical interface for the LLM. Instead of treating wavelet coefficients as ordinary discrete tokens, the paper introduces a coding-oriented numerical adaptation mechanism: coefficients are mapped into a compact local symbol space, represented with trainable value embeddings, and predicted through a dedicated value head constrained to valid coefficient ranges. The motivation is straightforward. Pretrained LLMs are built for discrete token vocabularies, while wavelet coefficients are numeric symbols whose ranges vary with subband and context. A naive global vocabulary would be sparse, inefficient, and poorly aligned with numeric structure. Third, a prompt-based adaptive entropy coding strategy. Since different subbands still have distinct distributions, training a separate model per subband would be expensive and impractical. The authors instead reuse one shared 3B backbone and condition it with task prompts carrying subband identity and decoded context, allowing the same model to adapt across subbands. The overall factorization remains causal and is paired with arithmetic coding for exact lossless compression.
