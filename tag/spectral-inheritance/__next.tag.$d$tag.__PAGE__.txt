a:T5ca,Each weight matrix is analyzed in its SVD basis W = U Σ V^⊤, with Σ the singular-value spectrum and (U, V) the output/input singular frames. The central empirical observation is that freely trained RLVR checkpoints stay near-isospectral to their base, and this proximity is not merely a high-dimensional artifact. Three interventions test functional necessity: (1) restoring base Σ while keeping learned U, V in RL checkpoints preserves most acquired gains; (2) training with Σ fixed throughout RL while updating only U and V still achieves strong RL learning; (3) the reverse—freezing frames and updating only Σ—yields only marginal improvement. This leads to spectral inheritance: the base spectrum can be reused, with new behavior carried by both frames. Follow-up ablations show both frames must remain adaptable; remixing only within input/output spans or holding one frame fixed leaves substantially more of the endpoint change unexplained. ISO (Isospectral Optimization) operationalizes this in two complementary forms. ISO-Merger (offline) consolidates multiple specialists trained from a shared base by reusing the shared base spectra and directly combining expert frame changes into a single fixed-spectrum checkpoint, without extra data, rollouts, or distillation.
