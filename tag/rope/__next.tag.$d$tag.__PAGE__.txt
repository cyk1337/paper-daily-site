First, the reduction rate is predictable: the paper derives a closed-form expression, so compute savings can be budgeted before deployment. High-frequency pairs have short wavelengths and can only discriminate relative position locally; low-frequency pairs have long wavelengths and remain informative at larger distances. Based on this, the paper defines a per-RoPE-wavelength distance window: for frequency pair r, keep its contribution only when the relative distance z is within w_r = min(k·λ_r, N); beyond that distance, drop that pair’s term from the score sum.

Three aspects are central.

First, the method prunes frequency terms, not tokens. It is frequency-level pruning rather than token-level pruning. As long as N < k·λ_max, the lowest-frequency components still span the full sequence, so every token remains reachable through at least those channels.

Second, it is designed to fit FlashAttention-style kernels. With the window disabled, the implementation is bit-identical to the stock kernel. That suggests a relatively clean deployment path rather than a full reimplementation of attention.

Third, the method has a single free parameter k, the number of retained periods. Larger k keeps more frequency terms and approaches full attention; smaller k gives more reduction.
