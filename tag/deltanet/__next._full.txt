19:T6b5,The paper is organized around three tightly connected components. First, it performs controlled empirical comparisons among xLSTM, Mamba-2, and Gated DeltaNet in matched hybrid settings. The authors compare them under from-scratch pretraining, Transformer-to-subquadratic distillation, and downstream code generation, while keeping parameter count, layer count, and the number of retained self-attention layers comparable. This is important because it reduces the chance that observed differences come from unrelated recipe choices. Second, the paper introduces a unified formulation of the three architectures. Instead of treating them as unrelated families—state-space models, linear attention, gated recurrence—it rewrites them in a common state-update language and compares how they write to state, forget state, overwrite state, and read from state. This yields the paper’s central hypothesis: the architectures should differ most on two primitive capabilities, accumulation and finite-state tracking. Third, the authors test that hypothesis directly using synthetic length-generalization tasks. Counting tasks isolate accumulation beyond the training horizon, while state-tracking tasks isolate ordered finite-state updates. xLSTM performs well on both, including beyond training length, whereas the alternatives are weaker on at least one side of this trade-off. A key interpretive point is that xLSTM is not a single mechanism but a combination of matrix-state linear-attention-style layers and recurrent layers. The paper argues that this combination gives it both stronger accumulation behavior and stronger state-machine-like tracking, which in turn explains its advantage on complex structured data.1a:T5cc,The strongest empirical section is the code-pretraining comparison. The authors train 400M-parameter inter-layer hybrid language models with 24 total layers, retaining only 3 standard self-attention layers and replacing the rest with the tested subquadratic operator. They compare Gated DeltaNet, Mamba-2, and xLSTM[7:1]. Training is run under three data settings: Nemotron-CC-Code-v1 for 20B tokens, the same corpus for 100B tokens, and a 20B-token mixture of Nemotron-CC-Code-v1 with FineWeb-Edu. The main evaluation is HumanEval pass@{2, 8, 16, 64}. The headline numbers reported in the excerpt are on pass@64: xLSTM beats the next-best backbone by 1.43 points at 20B code tokens, 0.90 points at 100B code tokens, and 1.81 points on the code-plus-FineWeb mixture. The paper also reports HellaSwag, PIQA, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, and WinoGrande, where the trend remains favorable but margins are smaller, reinforcing the claim that structured dependency tasks are where architectural differences become visible. Beyond from-scratch pretraining, the paper includes code-focused Transformer distillation and time-series foundation-model pretraining, showing that the pattern is not tied to one training regime or one data modality. The most useful follow-up questions are: how much of xLSTM’s advantage depends on retaining a few attention layers; whether the 400M-scale findings extrapolate to larger models; whether long-document or multimodal token sequences would show the same ordering; and whether the distillation results indicate a higher ceiling or simply better compatibility with Transformer teachers.1c:T48f,OSDN adds a diagonal preconditioner D_t = diag(d_t) on top of the usual scalar gate in the Delta Rule. Since the Delta update corresponds to gradient descent on 1/2 ||S k_t - v_t||^2, the new method right-preconditions the gradient. The key algebraic result is that this right-preconditioning is exactly equivalent to scaling the write-side key feature by feature: \tilde{k}_t = d_t ⊙ k_t. As a result, the implementation can keep the existing chunkwise WY pipeline and only replace the storage-side K with \tilde{K}. The preconditioner d_t is updated online using OSGM-style hypergradient feedback. Its update depends only on keys and scalar gates, not on the high-dimensional memory state, values, or residuals, so the full d_t trajectory can be computed as an O(K)-state affine scan before the write pass. The APF variant adds learned token-wise and head-wise forgetting for the preconditioner only, which helps under non-stationary contexts without decaying the main memory state. The theory gives a population-limit super-geometric rate against a right-Newton comparator and a token-local contraction bound for the residual-ratio surrogate used in diagnostics.1d:T5a8,The proposed model is Momentum DeltaNet. The paper starts from the interpretation of Delta Linear Attention as an online optimization process and replaces the plain SGD-style update with a stepwise momentum rule. Momentum introduces an auxiliary state that accumulates past gradient information, which can smooth updates and preserve longer-range signals.

The main technical device is a chunkwise parallel algorithm for this stepwise momentum recurrence. Instead of using blockwise momentum, which weakens within-block causality and creates a training-inference mismatch, the paper algebraically reorders the update coefficients so that the same causal stepwise rule can be evaluated in parallel within chunks. The derived equivalent coefficients include terms such as \(\widetilde{\alpha}_t=\alpha_t+\mu_t\beta_t/\beta_{t-1}\), \(\widetilde{\beta}_t=\beta_t\eta_t\), and \(\widetilde{\gamma}_t=\alpha_{t-1}\mu_t\beta_t/\beta_{t-1}\). Training uses the parallel chunk form, while recurrent decoding keeps the original stepwise update.

The paper also analyzes the momentum recurrence as a second-order dynamical system. Momentum changes the spectrum of the recurrence and can introduce complex eigenvalues. This gives the model richer temporal dynamics, but it also creates stability concerns. MDN therefore uses constrained gating mechanisms rather than unconstrained gates.
