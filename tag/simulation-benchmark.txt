The simulator advances hourly over 365 days, for 8,760 control steps, and the agent receives a decision window every 12 steps. The latent state includes demand trajectories, supplier conditions, store listings, finances, active orders, and pending events, but the agent only observes merchant-visible information. Decisions therefore depend on observation history and tool outputs rather than full state access.

The environment couples four decision components: product sourcing, listing and pricing control, cash-flow management, and adaptation to supplier and order outcomes. The key design choice is asymmetric feedback latency. Procurement and listing decisions affect cash and demand quickly, while fulfillment failures, after-sales issues, penalties, and reputation effects surface later. This forces the agent to connect delayed outcomes back to earlier actions and revise its policy when needed.

The benchmark is grounded in 98,843 real e-commerce product records, with demand converted into order-level lifecycle simulation.
