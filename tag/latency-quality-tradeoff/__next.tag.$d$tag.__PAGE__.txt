9:T7f7,The system is built on InfiniSST. A streaming speech encoder processes incoming chunks incrementally while reusing past KV states. An LLM translator then consumes interleaved speech features and previously generated text, and emits a partial translation for each chunk until it outputs an <EOT> token. For unbounded streams, the encoder uses a sliding window and the LLM uses Attention Sink to remain tractable over long contexts. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on synthesized interleaving trajectories, followed by the proposed Hierarchical Policy Optimization.

HPO is an adaptation of GRPO. For each long-form speech segment, the policy samples multiple candidate translation trajectories. Each trajectory is evaluated with two rewards: translation quality and latency. The main contribution is not simply applying GRPO, but restructuring the reward:
1. Quality and latency are computed separately rather than merged upfront.
2. A quality threshold is imposed. If a sampled trajectory does not exceed that threshold, its latency reward is forced to the minimum. This prevents the policy from collecting reward by translating too early.
3. Group normalization is applied separately to the quality and latency rewards before they are combined, which reduces instability from scale mismatch and prevents one reward from dominating the update.

This design matches the asymmetry of the task. Latency is monotonic and easy to optimize in the wrong way; quality is delayed, sparse, and depends on global consistency. The hierarchical reward turns a soft trade-off into a gated preference structure. The paper also makes clear that this is a correction mechanism on top of a usable SFT initialization, not a from-scratch policy-learning setup. The synthesized training data are built from long recordings ranging from minutes to hours, segmented into windows up to 67.2 seconds, with 60 chunks of 1.12 seconds each; ASR timestamps align speech to transcripts, and SimAlign is used for transcript-translation alignment.a:T588,Experiments are run on the ACL 60/60 development set and RealSI, focusing on translation quality at different average latency levels. The headline result reported in the paper is that at 1.5 seconds average latency, HPO improves over the strongest baseline by +7 COMET, +1.25 MetricX, and +4 BLEURT. That matters because the gain is not confined to a single metric.

From the full setup, the most important comparisons are:
1. HPO versus the SFT-only InfiniSST baseline, to test whether post-training can repair errors induced by noisy synthesized trajectories.
2. HPO versus simpler RL objectives that combine quality and latency with a flat weighting, to show why the hierarchical reward is necessary.
3. Performance across a range of latency operating points, to verify that the method improves the quality-latency frontier rather than merely shifting the model toward slower decoding.

The most informative ablations are on reward design. Removing the quality gate should make the model more eager to emit prematurely. Removing separate group normalization should make training less stable or let one reward dominate.
