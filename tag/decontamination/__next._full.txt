17:T6e4,The work has two technical pieces. First, it introduces a sample-level evaluation framework, mainly for multiple-choice benchmarks. Instead of relying only on accuracy, it compares output distributions on each sample. The intended criterion is not just whether the decontaminated model gets the same answer as a clean model, but whether its probability allocation over choices looks similar to the clean model. A contaminated model often over-concentrates mass on the gold answer; a successful decontamination method should reduce that distortion and recover a more natural distribution over alternatives.

Second, it proposes UBD, a family of uncertainty-based decontamination methods built on deep ensembles. The key idea is that loss is an unreliable proxy for contamination: both easy examples and memorized hard examples can have low loss after contamination. Ensemble-based predictive uncertainty is used to identify suspicious samples more selectively. On top of that signal, the paper develops two intervention styles: (1) debiasing, which adjusts the output distribution for likely contaminated samples to reduce contamination-induced overconfidence; and (2) unlearning, which applies a stronger forgetting-style update to move the model away from the contaminated behavior on those samples.

Compared with methods that minimize KL to a clean teacher, UBD does not require a clean reference model. Compared with paraphrasing or answer-permutation baselines, it does not try to evade memorization by changing the input surface form. It instead estimates contamination through uncertainty and then corrects the model behavior directly. That distinction is important: the former is closer to benchmark-side mitigation, while UBD aims at model-side debiasing.18:T4a3,From the available full-text excerpt, the experiments appear to support three claims. First, aggregate accuracy can make different decontamination methods look similar even when their sample-level behavior differs substantially. Distribution-distance metrics expose those differences. Second, UBD produces per-sample output distributions that are closer to those of an uncontaminated model than paraphrasing and choice-permutation baselines. Third, uncertainty is shown to separate hard-but-memorized samples from genuinely easy ones, even when both have low loss under the contaminated model. That is the most interesting empirical point, because it argues that loss alone is not a sufficient contamination indicator.
