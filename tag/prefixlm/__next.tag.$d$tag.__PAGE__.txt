It is relevant to pretraining research for three reasons. First, it revisits recurrent deep computation as a way to increase computation depth without increasing parameter count in the same way as stacking Transformer blocks. Second, it treats instruction-response training from scratch as a pretraining objective rather than as a post-training stage. A high-level state z_H is initialized from token embeddings and is meant to carry slower semantic and task-level context. A low-level state z_L is initialized in a fixed way and performs faster local refinement. The forward pass runs two high-level cycles. Each cycle applies three fast L-module updates followed by one slow H-module update. Token logits are produced from the final H state through a linear head. On the architecture side, HRM-Text uses dual-timescale recurrence: a slow H module maintains a relatively stable high-level semantic state, while a fast L module performs local iterative refinement. One forward pass consists of two high-level cycles; each cycle runs three L updates followed by one H update, and token logits are produced from the final H state. The intended trade-off is clear: use repeated computation instead of proportionally increasing parameter count, so the model can perform deeper integration under smaller budgets.

The hard part is optimization. Repeatedly applying the same transformation amplifies the classic normalization-placement problem already familiar from Transformers. The paper introduces MagicNorm and warmup deep credit assignment to make this viable. From the full text, MagicNorm is designed to preserve bounded forward activation variance while keeping backward optimization stable, effectively trying to capture the good parts of both PreNorm and PostNorm under recurrence. The surrounding design choices—parameterless RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, and sigmoid-gated self-attention—suggest a deliberate bias toward stability and reduced unnecessary flexibility. Warmup deep credit assignment is especially important: early in training, gradients are backpropagated only through the final two recurrent steps, and later expanded to the final five. This explicitly controls effective backward depth, allowing the model to first learn short-range credit assignment before exposing it to longer recurrent dependencies.

On the objective side, the paper abandons standard raw-text next-token pretraining. HRM-Text is trained from scratch on instruction-response pairs, with NLL computed only on the response tokens, i.e. optimizing -log P(x_a | x_q). This is paired with a PrefixLM mask: full bidirectional attention over the instruction prefix, causal generation over the response. The motivation is sharp. If the model is mostly used for conditional generation at inference time, then spending most of the budget predicting prompt-like text, web boilerplate, or task-irrelevant continuations may be wasteful. Compared with encoder-decoder training, PrefixLM preserves a single-stream generative interface; compared with standard causal LM, it allows richer integration over the conditioning context.
