1a:T44c,H-probes are a family of linear probes designed to recover hierarchical structure from hidden states. The controlled task is binary-tree traversal: nodes are randomly labeled, and the model must output the shortest path between two nodes. During generation, the authors collect activations at the tokens corresponding to nodes in the model’s final path answer. To reduce overfitting, they first project hidden states onto the top 10 PCA directions, which usually preserve about 90% of variance in the analyzed layers. The probes then predict two structural quantities: the depth of individual nodes and pairwise tree distance between nodes. This separates absolute position in the hierarchy from relational structure. The same identified subspaces are then used for interventions: removing or perturbing these directions tests whether task performance depends on them. Compared with classical syntactic structural probes, the target is not dependency syntax but hierarchy in the reasoning process.
