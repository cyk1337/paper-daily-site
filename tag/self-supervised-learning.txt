The forward theorem then specializes to z~N(0,I). The authors take five standard self-supervised vision tasks and reformulate them as verifiable visual puzzles that an MLLM can answer in a text interface, then optimize the model with GRPO. The five tasks cover different granularities of visual reasoning. (1) Rotation prediction: the image is rotated by a sampled angle from a discrete set, and the model predicts the angle. This probes global structure and orientation sensitivity. (2) Visual similarity: two crops from the same image form a reference and a positive, while crops from other images serve as negatives; the model selects the most similar candidate. This resembles instance discrimination and requires semantic and appearance-level matching. (3) Region inpainting: a region is masked and the model must infer the missing content or choose the correct completion-related option, forcing contextual completion. (4) Patch ordering: shuffled patches must be reordered, emphasizing spatial layout and part-whole structure. (5) Geometric correspondence: the model reasons about point or region correspondences under transformations, targeting finer spatial reasoning.

These tasks share the properties that matter for RLVR: labels are generated automatically from the corruption or transformation process, and correctness can be checked exactly. The RL algorithm is GRPO rather than PPO. That choice is practical: no critic is needed, and relative comparison among sampled responses is well suited to discrete, verifiable reward settings. The main novelty is not a new RL optimizer, but the alignment between visual SSL task design and reward verifiability. Another important detail is that the tasks are cast into the native MLLM interaction format, so the visual reinforcement signal updates the generative policy directly instead of training a separate vision head.
