CVQ instead treats each channel as the quantization unit (conceptually a 2D map of size h×w) and maps each channel to a discrete code, producing a length-c 1D token sequence. The paper’s mechanism argument is optimization-dynamics driven: patch embeddings are highly repetitive across images and within a batch, so early training concentrates updates on a small subset of codes, leading to dead codes and collapse; channel partitioning yields more separable embeddings across images and broader code coverage.

On top of CVQ, it proposes Channel-wise Auto-Regressive (CAR) generation via “next-channel prediction”: generate tokens sequentially over channels, yielding a coarse-to-fine progression where early channels capture global structure and later channels refine details.
