9:T4b8,Methodologically, this is an evaluation argument backed by a controlled case study. The paper defines an access path as any transformation or recovery mechanism that changes how the same targeted information is queried or extracted while keeping the underlying information approximately fixed. Cross-lingual querying is chosen because it is a clean instance of this idea: semantics are largely preserved while surface form changes substantially.

The experiments use cTOFU, a multilingual extension of TOFU, with parallel synthetic factual associations in English, Chinese, Hindi, Italian, and Spanish, plus romanized Hindi and Chinese. The original forget/retain partitions are preserved. The study focuses on one-to-one unlearning: unlearn in one language, evaluate in another. The contribution is therefore not a new training recipe but a reframing of what unlearning evaluation should claim. Standard metrics such as Truth Ratio, KS-test-based forget quality, and utility preservation can show distribution-specific behavioral change, but they do not by themselves support a privacy claim. The paper argues that privacy-oriented evaluation should ask whether forgetting survives alternative access paths.a:T41c,From the provided full text, the empirical study uses the 10% forget split in cTOFU and evaluates with TOFU-style forget-quality and normalized model-utility metrics. The main result is qualitative but important: conclusions about unlearning success change when the same information is queried through different languages or scripts. A model family may look successfully unlearned under one language-specific evaluation and still reveal the same information under another language or romanized script. That means monolingual evaluation can materially overestimate forgetting strength.

The paper also makes three broader empirical points. First, this is observed across multiple multilingual model families rather than a single isolated model. Second, the gap appears not only across languages but also across script variants, which is useful because it keeps semantics even more tightly controlled. Third, utility preservation is orthogonal to privacy: retaining general capability does not imply that the targeted information has become inaccessible.b:T4c8,The main contribution is a benchmark construction framework plus validation protocol. The paper first dissects failure modes in prior MIA evaluations. Temporal-cutoff benchmarks such as WikiMIA confound membership with time-dependent changes in writing style and content. Open-model train/validation splits are also problematic when only the training set is deduplicated, because duplicate structure itself becomes a membership cue. Even deduplicating validation against train is not fully sound, since it alters the non-member distribution. Based on this analysis, the authors build cleaner splits for two open LLM families, aiming to keep member and non-member examples matched in source distribution while differing only in whether they were seen before a fixed training point. They then run blind baselines—classifiers with no access to the target model, using only the raw text—to verify that the split is not trivially separable. Only after this sanity check do they benchmark standard MIA methods and report metrics such as AUC and TPR at low FPR.
