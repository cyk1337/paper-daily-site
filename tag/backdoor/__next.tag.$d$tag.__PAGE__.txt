5:T46e,The attack is BCBI, or Boundary-Conditioned Backdoor Injection. The adversary fine-tunes a victim model and deploys selected agents with the poisoned version. Poisoned agents emit plausible evidence cues; each cue is insufficient alone, while cues from distinct peers jointly indicate that the hidden activation threshold has been reached. BCBI constructs counterfactual boundary pairs where task, role, round, and surrounding context are matched, but peer evidence moves from just below to just above the threshold. The output objective prefers benign behavior before the boundary and the adversarial target after it. A latent-space constraint organizes intermediate evidence states according to progression toward activation, making the transition sharper. The defense, LATTE, uses only clean interactions. It models hidden-state changes between adjacent communication rounds, learns a low-rank subspace of normal latent transitions, and screens each update before the response is released to peers.
