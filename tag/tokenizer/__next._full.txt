19:T8cc,文章先做了两个诊断，再提出 SALT。第一个诊断是证明动作轨迹确实含有超出视觉结果的 verb 信息。作者把 verb grounding 拆成两部分：action goals，对应 episode 起止状态的视觉变化；motion dynamics，对应 7-DoF 连续动作轨迹。通过信息分析，他们发现两者各自都携带对方无法完全替代的 verb 信息，也就是说，单看图像前后状态不足以恢复动作语义，动作过程本身包含语言相关结构。

第二个诊断是现有离散动作表示会损伤这种结构。作者比较了 Bin tokenization、VQ-VAE tokenizer 和 FAST 等方案，考察 tokenized action representation 与 verb 之间的 mutual information。结论是：只按重建训练的 tokenizer 会系统性降低 verb 相关信息，而且压缩越强，损失越大。更重要的是，后续语言条件 policy training 不能完全把这部分结构补回来，说明瓶颈发生在 tokenizer 这一步，而不是 policy head 太弱。

在此基础上，作者提出 SALT，Semantically ALigned action Tokenizer。它以 VQ-VAE 风格 tokenizer 为基础，但在 tokenizer 训练阶段加入一个语言对齐辅助目标：把量化后的 action latent 输入一个冻结的预训练 VLM，让它生成对应 episode instruction。这样做的关键不是让 tokenizer 直接分类 verb，而是通过 instruction generation 这个更丰富的监督，把“被相似语言描述的动作”拉到更接近的离散表示，同时保留连续控制空间的重建能力。训练目标因此是 reconstruction / commitment / codebook loss 与 instruction-generation loss 的联合优化。这里选择冻结 VLM，而不是端到端共同训练，目的是把语言语义作为稳定教师信号注入 tokenizer 学习，避免 policy 训练阶段再去间接恢复。

从建模角度看，SALT 的重要区别在于：它把 action tokenizer 从单纯的压缩模块变成了跨模态语义瓶颈。相比 per-dimension binning，SALT 学的是组合式离散词表；相比 reconstruction-only VQ-VAE，它显式约束 codebook 保留语言可恢复性；相比 FAST 这类频域压缩+BPE，它不只追求序列压缩率，而是优先保留对 instruction 有用的结构。1a:T962,The paper first establishes two empirical diagnoses, then introduces SALT. The first diagnosis is that action trajectories contain verb information beyond visual state changes. The authors decompose verb grounding into action goals, represented by visual changes between episode endpoints, and motion dynamics, represented by the 7-DoF action trajectory. Their analysis shows that each carries verb-relevant information not fully captured by the other. This means that visual outcomes alone are insufficient; the action process itself contains language-relevant structure.

The second diagnosis is that existing discrete action representations erase this structure. The paper compares Bin tokenization, VQ-VAE tokenizers, and FAST, and measures the mutual information between verbs and tokenized action representations. Reconstruction-only tokenizers consistently reduce verb-related information, with stronger compression causing larger losses. Importantly, downstream language-conditioned policy training does not fully recover the missing structure, indicating that the bottleneck is created at the tokenizer interface rather than by an underpowered policy head.

SALT—Semantically ALigned action Tokenizer—is then built on top of a VQ-VAE-style tokenizer. During tokenizer training, the quantized action latents are fed into a frozen pretrained VLM, which is asked to generate the corresponding episode instruction. This adds a language-alignment objective to the usual reconstruction, commitment, and codebook losses. The point is not to classify verbs explicitly, but to force the codebook to preserve information that makes the original instruction recoverable. Actions described by similar language are therefore encouraged to occupy nearby or related discrete representations while maintaining control-space fidelity.

The key modeling difference is that SALT treats the tokenizer as a cross-modal bottleneck. Compared with per-dimension binning, it learns a compositional discrete vocabulary. Compared with reconstruction-only VQ-VAE, it explicitly constrains the codebook to retain language-recoverable structure. Compared with FAST-style frequency compression plus BPE, it prioritizes semantic preservation over compression alone. Using a frozen VLM as the teacher also stabilizes the language signal and avoids relying on downstream policy training to rediscover the missing semantics.1b:T6f8,The experiments have three parts. First, on BridgeV2, the paper analyzes where verb information comes from and shows that both visual endpoint changes and action trajectories contribute unique information. That supports the premise that action representations need their own language alignment rather than inheriting everything from vision-language pretraining.

Second, the paper studies information loss under tokenization. Across Bin, VQ-VAE, and FAST, mutual information between verbs and tokenized action representations drops, and the drop becomes larger under stronger compression. This is the core evidence that the discrete action interface is a semantic bottleneck.

Third, the paper evaluates downstream control in SimplerEnv. Policies trained with SALT reach 71.9% average task success, compared with 42.7% for a reconstruction-only VQ-VAE tokenizer and 31.2% for FAST. The gap is large enough to show that semantic alignment is not merely improving interpretability; it materially affects closed-loop control performance.

The abstract also notes that although the auxiliary objective does not explicitly label verbs, the learned action vocabulary becomes organized around verb-relevant distinctions. In the full paper, the most informative evidence here is likely to be codebook visualizations, nearest-neighbor analyses, instruction recoverability, or token-cluster semantics. Those qualitative analyses matter because they reveal whether SALT is truly learning language-aligned action primitives rather than simply benefiting from extra supervision. Start with UniTok. It is built on VQ-VAE, but the paper makes several modifications tailored to time series.

First, incremental tokenization. A standard autoencoding tokenizer often depends on the full sequence, which breaks compatibility with autoregressive next-token prediction. UniTok enforces that the tokenization of a prefix depends only on that prefix, not on future values. This is a central design constraint. Without it, the downstream LM would be trained on tokens that already leak future information.

Second, prefix normalization. Raw time series can vary wildly in scale, and naive quantization would let magnitude dominate the codebook and hurt cross-domain transfer. The paper decomposes each series into scale statistics and a normalized shape component. Scale is encoded separately into discrete tokens, while the normalized series is passed to the learned tokenizer. Importantly, the normalization is prefix-based rather than global, so it stabilizes scale without violating causality.

Third, a progressive-resolution causal autoencoder. Instead of compressing the series into a single granularity of tokens, the model allocates token resolution according to receptive field, so different tokens capture information at different temporal scales. The encoder and decoder are causal, which keeps the token stream aligned with autoregressive prediction. This borrows the spirit of hierarchical image tokenizers but avoids bidirectional leakage.

Pointwise reconstruction alone is not enough for time series because it can preserve local values while losing trend, periodicity, phase, or other temporal structure. The full formula is not visible in the provided excerpt, but the intent is clear: the tokenizer should preserve temporal shape, not just numeric proximity.
