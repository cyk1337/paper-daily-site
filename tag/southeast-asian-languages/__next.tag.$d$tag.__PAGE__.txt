The strength of the paper is the controlled setup.

Tokenizer training uses the same 1M-sentence, 3.5GB sample from mC4 across 11 Southeast Asian languages, sampled according to the original language proportions. Language-model training uses the same 100M-sentence, 203GB multilingual FineWeb2 sample, with temperature sampling (tau = 1.21) to upweight lower-resource languages. The authors report data size in sentences and bytes rather than tokens, which is the right choice because token count is itself the object of comparison.

Vocabulary size is matched where possible. MYTE is trained at 4,096 / 8,192 / 12,288 morphemes per language, corresponding to total inventories of roughly 45k / 90k / 135k. Byte-level BPE and parity-aware BPE are matched to those total sizes. BLT has no fixed vocabulary, so it is compared through average patch sizes of 4.5 / 6 / 8 bytes. For downstream LM training, the paper uses the 90k setting for the fixed-vocabulary tokenizers and the 4.5-byte setting for BLT.

The key methodological contribution is to connect intrinsic tokenizer analysis with downstream LM training. The paper does not stop at compression statistics; it trains 1.5B decoder-only models from scratch and compares what tokenization differences mean for actual multilingual modeling. It also reports preprocessing and tokenizer-training cost, which is important because some methods are much harder to deploy in practice: MYTE and BPE-style tokenizers can be trained on CPU, while BLT requires GPU-heavy entropy modeling and tokenization pipelines.a:T675,The study covers 11 Southeast Asian languages: English, Burmese, Chinese, Indonesian, Khmer, Lao, Malay, Tagalog, Tamil, Thai, and Vietnamese. Several concrete numbers are available from the full text provided. Tokenizer training uses 1M sentences (3.5GB). LM training uses 100M sentences (203GB). The main downstream comparison uses the 90k tokenizer setting, close to the 100k–128k range common in modern LLMs. Training runs on 4–8 H100/H200 GPUs, with runtime normalized to an 8×H200 equivalent. MYTE is explicitly reported as the most expensive option at 300 normalized hours because it produces far more training tokens—269B tokens after tokenization. Byte-level BPE is the most efficient baseline. BLT also carries substantial systems overhead because its entropy tokenizer and large-scale patching pipeline require multiple H100/H200 GPUs.

The likely takeaway is not a single winner but a set of trade-offs. More equitable tokenizers improve token parity for underrepresented and non-Latin languages, but they differ in where the cost lands. Parity-aware BPE appears to be a lower-friction compromise. MYTE may better respect morphological structure, but at the cost of token explosion and slower training. BLT avoids fixed-vocabulary bias more fundamentally, but with higher systems complexity.
