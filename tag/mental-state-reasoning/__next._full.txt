18:T541,The benchmark is built around three design choices. First, the authors structure latent mental variables with a BDI-P taxonomy: Perception, Belief, and Desire, expanded into 12 secondary aspects and 34 fine-grained subdimensions. This turns a vague notion of Theory of Mind into a controlled evaluation space. Second, they introduce Zero-Action Prompting. Prompts describe only internal state and never specify the action, so the model cannot rely on lexical action cues and must infer intention before behavior. Third, they use counterfactual prompt pairs under a fixed visual world state. Only one mental variable is changed, such as true belief versus false belief, or seeing versus not seeing an object. Success on both paired prompts is treated as stronger evidence that the model is tracking latent state rather than exploiting shortcuts.

The evaluation pipeline is also more careful than a standard video benchmark. Instead of only scoring visual quality, they use an automated large-vision-model judge to assess visual quality, commonsense plausibility, intention accuracy, and world-state maintenance. The judge is prompted with structured reasoning steps rather than a single scalar query. They also compare the automated pipeline against judgments aggregated from 10 human experts on 100 generated videos, reporting strong agreement.19:T49b,MindWorldBench contains 372 expert-designed images and 744 prompts arranged as counterfactual pairs. The paper evaluates 11 image-to-video models, including Veo 3.1, Kling V3, Wan 2.6, and CogVideoX. The main empirical pattern is consistent across models: visual realism and physical commonsense are noticeably stronger than mental-state consistency. In other words, models often produce plausible actions, but not the action that follows from the character’s subjective state.

The most informative experimental finding is the Omniscient Bias. In false-belief scenarios, a character should act on what they believe, even when that belief is wrong. Models frequently ignore that distinction and generate behavior aligned with objective truth. The paired counterfactual setup is important here because single-prompt success can be accidental; paired success under minimal mental-state changes is a stricter test.
