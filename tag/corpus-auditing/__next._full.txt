18:T689,The pipeline begins with outlet identification. The authors use State Media Monitor as the source of media organizations and their control labels. The original seven-level typology is collapsed into controlled versus independent for analysis, while the released dataset retains the finer labels. They then map media assets to web domains in two stages: an LLM is prompted to propose URLs in context, and humans manually verify or correct them. The resulting inventory contains 640 state media outlets, 1,910 assets, and 1,183 unique URLs spanning 173 countries.

Instead of crawling the web independently, the paper extracts pages directly from existing LLM pretraining corpora, namely English FineWeb and FineWeb-2. This design choice is important. It makes the dataset reflect content that models are plausibly trained on, rather than a generic snapshot of the web. It also enables year-by-year and language-by-language audits over a corpus that has already undergone quality filtering and deduplication.

The analysis has three layers. First, a corpus audit quantifies the prevalence of state media in English and multilingual web data, including temporal trends, language distributions, and the balance between controlled and independent outlets. Second, a content study uses Russian and Ukrainian state media as a case study, applying decontextualized fact extraction and information pursuit to compare coverage patterns and territorial narratives. Third, a system-level analysis places these sources into a RAG setup on the BorderLines territorial-dispute benchmark to test how retrieval over state media affects model responses across languages and target audiences.19:T605,Four findings stand out. First, in English web crawls, the share of state media pages coming from editorially controlled outlets rises sharply over time, from 39% in 2013 to 93% in 2025. This suggests that the English-language state media slice entering open-web corpora has become increasingly dominated by more tightly controlled sources.

Second, in several low-resource languages, state media accounts for a nontrivial share of all web data. The paper reports values up to 17.8% for Uyghur. For multilingual pretraining, this is important because when the total amount of web text is small, source concentration can have a much stronger effect on the learned distribution.

Third, the Russia-Ukraine case study shows that the issue is not merely ideological disagreement. Russian English-language state media covers international affairs broadly, while Ukrainian state media is more focused on Ukrainian affairs. On topics such as Crimea, the extracted factual claims and narrative framing diverge in systematic ways. This means that even when both sources look like ordinary news text, they provide very different world-model signals during training.

Fourth, in the RAG experiments, retrieval over foreign-targeted state media amplifies cross-lingual divergence in model responses on territorial disputes. The paper is less about a single benchmark score than about exposing a vulnerability pattern: the choice of retrieval source, language, and intended audience can systematically steer answers on geopolitically sensitive questions.1a:T499,If your main interest is pretraining data, start with the dataset construction section and the corpus-audit section. The most important methodological choice is extracting from FineWeb and FineWeb-2 rather than crawling outlets independently. That makes the dataset much more relevant for actual training-distribution analysis.

Then focus on the temporal and language-distribution figures, especially the year-by-year trend for controlled English state media and the prevalence numbers in low-resource languages. Those are the results most likely to inform data mixture design, filtering, and multilingual corpus governance.

If you care about model behavior, read the Russia-Ukraine content analysis and the RAG section next. The former clarifies how narrative structure differs even within the broad category of news text; the latter shows how those differences can be activated at inference time through retrieval.
