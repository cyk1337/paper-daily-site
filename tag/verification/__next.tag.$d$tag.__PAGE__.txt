9:T621,The paper introduces AgentPerturb, a two-dimensional perturbation framework. The first axis is perturbation target: user utterance (U), procedural specification (P), carried-over context (C), and tool contract (T). The second axis is perturbation type: Missing, Redundant, and Incorrect. This yields 12 perturbation families.

Two design choices are important. First, perturbations are single-target edits: only one channel is modified at a time while the others remain unchanged. This makes attribution cleaner and avoids confounding multiple failure sources. Second, perturbations are validated so that the original task goal is preserved, the environment remains executable, and a valid solution path still exists. So the benchmark is not broken; the agent is tested under controlled information corruption.

The framework is instantiated on APEX-Agents and Toolathlon, two long-horizon agent benchmarks with verifiable outcomes. The analysis is organized at three levels. Outcome-level analysis measures clean-to-perturbed performance drops. Factor-level analysis separates the effects of targets and types, and relates sensitivity to clean performance and task properties. Trajectory-level analysis audits reasoning traces, tool calls, observations, and state transitions to understand how perturbation-induced failures unfold. One of the most useful findings is that sensitivity clusters more strongly by perturbation target than by perturbation type. That suggests robustness interventions may need channel-specific treatment rather than generic noise injection.a:T46d,The evaluation covers 10 frontier models on APEX-Agents and Toolathlon. The headline result is consistent: all perturbations reduce performance, but the dominant vulnerability depends on the environment. In other words, the most damaging target is not universal across benchmarks. The paper also performs a focused analysis on Hunyuan-3-High and shows that task success alone can hide substantial losses in token efficiency. Some agents still solve the task, but only after much longer and less efficient trajectories.

For mechanism analysis, the authors manually audit 30 cases where DeepSeek-V4-Flash succeeds on the clean task but fails after perturbation. The recurring pattern is that agents rarely question altered inputs and often propagate corrupted information without verification. Once a bad assumption is accepted early, it contaminates later planning, tool use, and state updates. The full numeric tables are not available in the provided excerpt, so it is safer not to restate exact per-model drops. The more important experimental value lies in the target/type decomposition and the trajectory-level failure patterns.b:T732,The system has two main components. The first is 3DCodeVerse-2M. Instead of merely aggregating public code, the authors perform substantial representation unification and executabilization. They collect diverse 3D sources, including CAD construction sequences, Blender scripts, Shadertoy programs, Three.js / React Three Fiber projects, Thingiverse assets, and more. They then convert heterogeneous formats into runnable source code: for example, DeepCAD construction sequences are translated into CadQuery programs, and Articraft geometry routines are inlined while preserving URDF articulation specifications. Each sample is packaged as a standalone executable program with source files, assets, textual descriptions, provenance, and available verification records. The hard part here is not scale alone, but removing specialized or unavailable dependencies so that samples can actually run in the declared environment. The second component is the 3DCodeVerse Harness. It decomposes a complex 3D project into a DAG of coding and assembly tasks, lets multiple agents generate and integrate source files, executes and renders the result, and then uses an independent judge agent plus spatial consistency checks to evaluate it. The spatial checks are important because they prevent visually plausible but geometrically invalid outputs from scoring well. Feedback is converted into localized repair objectives, enabling repeated execution-evaluation-revision cycles. This differs from generic ReAct-style loops because the process is centered on executable artifacts, structured verification, and targeted repair rather than open-ended natural-language reflection. The harness is also designed as a data engine: it records successful programs, failed attempts, targeted repairs, and preference comparisons for later SFT and preference learning.c:T453,The headline results are straightforward. Fine-tuning on 3DCodeVerse-2M substantially improves executable 3D code generation: on 3DCodeBench, execution rates reach 84.9% for a 9B model and 92.9% for a 27B model. This suggests the dataset is effective at teaching models to produce runnable 3D programs. In addition, the Harness improves generation quality by 15.5 to 29.1 points over one-shot generation across coding agents, indicating that iterative execution-judging-repair is necessary for complex 3D projects rather than a marginal add-on. An independent evidence-based auditor builds a reference from the source and judges the saved labels and responses; unsupported cases are left unresolved rather than forced into a binary label. The metrics separate several effects: T_P is pseudo-label correctness, T_S is solver-response correctness, A is the in-loop match rate, F is false-agreement mass where the label and solver response match but are both wrong, and L counts correct solver answers denied credit by a wrong label. Multi-sample verification samples the same model three times with the source and three times without the source, admitting a proposal only when the majorities are compatible. This improves label quality but adds six generations per candidate and does not fully remove the failure. CrossFit is the main intervention. Source documents are split into two groups. Given T recorded denoising steps, they inspect a midpoint checkpoint and a recent window before it. If the extracted answer is valid and identical throughout that window, the example is labeled as an early lock-in. The locked answer is then compared with the gold answer to decide whether it is correct or wrong. A key part of the analysis is a surface-matched protocol: correct and wrong lock-ins are paired so that observable statistics such as confidence, entropy, margin, and answer stability are closely balanced. This isolates the contribution of hidden states.

Second, they propose LOCKR, a lightweight test-time planner. LOCKR uses the observed hidden-state trajectory as the planning state. A trajectory-based risk detector decides whether to continue the current denoising path or enter repair. If repair is triggered, targeted remasking creates a fixed bank of local counterfactual branches. These branches are continued, scored by a trajectory-aware verifier, and a final selector chooses the retained continuation. Compared with full restart or uniform extra computation, LOCKR spends compute only on high-risk cases.
