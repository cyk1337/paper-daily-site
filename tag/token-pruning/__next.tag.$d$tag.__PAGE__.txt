9:T574,The proposed method, STS (Structure-to-Semantics), is a training-free two-stage pruning framework. Stage one operates before visual tokens enter the LLM. Instead of ranking tokens only by attention, it formulates token selection with a potential-energy or repulsion-inspired objective: tokens are treated like charged particles in feature space, and semantically similar tokens repel each other more strongly. This encourages a selected subset that is spatially and semantically diverse, preserving global structure. Stage two operates inside an intermediate LLM layer and uses instruction-aware cross-attention to remove task-irrelevant visual tokens. The design is motivated by empirical analysis of representation dynamics. In deeper vision layers, feature-similar tokens receive increasingly similar attention scores, so attention loses discriminative power within local semantic neighborhoods. In the LLM decoder, earlier layers aggregate broad context while deeper layers become more task-focused. The paper also introduces a KNN Consistency Score to quantify how strongly feature similarity aligns with attention similarity, serving as a diagnostic for attention collapse. Compared with prior single-stage pruning methods, the novelty is not just adding another pruning step, but explicitly decoupling structural coverage from semantic relevance and using different signals for each stage.a:T42e,Experiments are conducted on multiple VLMs and benchmarks to evaluate the efficiency-performance trade-off. The method has three main steps. First, for each attention head, it computes an entropy-based selectivity score measuring how concentrated the head’s attention is over audio tokens; sharper attention implies stronger selectivity. Second, it uses the standard deviation of these selectivity scores across heads as a compact routing signal. Low variance indicates a more semantic-like regime, while high variance indicates a more acoustic-like regime, matching the authors’ empirical observation that acoustic tasks induce more heterogeneous head behavior. Third, instead of hard-switching between discrete modes, it performs Gaussian soft mixing over three pre-calibrated head-weight profiles—semantic, uniform, and acoustic—to obtain a continuous sample-specific head weighting. These weights are then used to aggregate head-level attention into token importance scores for pruning. A notable implementation detail is the use of no-RoPE selectivity statistics to reduce positional bias; prior methods tend to over-retain tail tokens.
