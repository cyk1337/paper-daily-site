18:T8ec,There are two central components. The first is the Asymmetric Planner-Executor architecture. A diffusion LM acts as the planner and generates K reasoning trajectories in parallel for the same query. A larger autoregressive model acts as the executor, evaluates the candidates, selects the best plan, and synthesizes the final answer. The asymmetry is not just about model size. It is about role specialization: the DLM handles parallel exploration, while the ARM handles conditional understanding, candidate discrimination, and fluent final synthesis. This division is motivated by the fact that bidirectional denoising is naturally suited to globally refining a whole reasoning trace, whereas AR models remain stronger at coherent conditional generation once candidate plans are available.

The second component is RDC, Reward-Guided Denoising for CoT. The paper interprets each reverse diffusion step as a thought step, and the full denoising trajectory as a stochastic chain of thought. Standard diffusion training optimizes reconstruction or likelihood-related objectives; RDC augments this with a terminal reward that reflects whether the trajectory leads to a correct answer. In the paper’s framing, this is an integrated denoising-guidance objective: instead of reranking trajectories only after generation, the reverse transition operator p_theta(x_{t-1} | x_t, q) is trained to prefer intermediate states that are more likely to end in correct solutions. That is the key distinction from best-of-N or post hoc reranking. The reward is meant to reshape the planner’s generation distribution, not just filter its outputs.

From a modeling perspective, the important move is to treat diffusion trajectories explicitly as reasoning trajectories and to inject outcome supervision into the denoising dynamics. This differs from AR process reward models, which usually score already generated steps. Here the reward influences the denoising transitions themselves. The paper also includes a theoretical motivation: under logarithmic resource constraints, parallel refinement is argued to be strictly more expressive than strictly sequential generation. That result is best read as architectural motivation rather than a decisive complexity-theoretic prescription for deployment.19:T4c3,From the visible full-text content, the headline result is that CoRAL outperforms prior diffusion-based methods on vision-language reasoning benchmarks and approaches strong AR baselines in the quality-efficiency tradeoff. However, the provided content does not include the full result tables, model sizes, training-token counts, planner/executor scales, benchmark names, or exact gains, so it would be unsafe to invent precise numbers.

That said, the experimental structure is fairly clear from the method. The critical comparisons should be: CoRAL versus pure DLM reasoning and pure ARM reasoning, to test whether the hybrid architecture actually improves the quality-latency tradeoff; RDC versus standard diffusion training, to verify that reward-guided denoising improves correctness rather than surface fluency; ablations over the number of parallel trajectories K, to study diversity versus executor selection gain; and efficiency analyses, ideally in wall-clock or decoding rounds, to support the claim that parallel planning mitigates the sequential bottleneck.
