18:T470,The study frames PPT as a two-stage autoregressive NLL training process. A model is first trained on a small synthetic or control corpus D_PPT, producing an initialization θ_PPT, and then trained on a standard PT corpus D_PT. This is compared against a PT-only model trained from random initialization. The experimental grid varies four axes: the PPT task, the PT mixture, model scale, and PT token budget. The five PPT tasks include two formal-language tasks, including k-Shuffle Dyck, two structured synthetic tasks without formal grammars, and an in-domain text control sampled from the PT corpus. The PT mixtures include C4, Marin, SmolLM3, and OLMo3, covering pure or web-heavy text, filtered mixtures with code and math, and a STEM-weighted mixture. Evaluation uses BLiMP for grammatical acceptability, verbatim retrieval for long-range retrieval, and ten general benchmarks spanning reading comprehension, science QA, commonsense reasoning, and language modeling. The strength of the work is the controlled scale-and-mixture matrix, which separates formal grammar transfer from retrieval improvement and data-mixture dependence.19:T469,LatentSift replaces the first-stage LLM-based execution-free verifier in a hybrid SWE verification pipeline. The standard pipeline scores all K trajectories with an EF verifier, keeps a subset, then applies regression tests, LLM-generated tests, and a final EF selection step. LatentSift instead collects hidden states while the policy is generating candidates, adding no extra LLM tokens. It represents each trajectory through three channels: reasoning states, environment-observation states, and function-call states. During training, states from successful and unsuccessful trajectories are stored in positive and negative banks. At inference time, candidate states are compared with these banks, step-level distance margins are aggregated, and channel-level ranks are fused conservatively so that a candidate must look good across the behavioral channels. A separate linear scorer trained on policy hidden states produces a learned trajectory score. The final filtering score combines normalized ranks from the distance branch and the learned scorer, then retains half of the candidates for the remaining verification stages.1a:T788,The core idea is Dense Reasoning Trace, or DRT. The goal is not just to make the model say less, but to make it reason in a different external format. DRT separates reasoning into two explicit channels: <visual> stores observed evidence from the image, while <think> stores compact logical derivations. Symbolic connectors and concise state transitions replace long natural-language sentences. This serves two purposes: reducing linguistic redundancy and disentangling observation from deduction so that assumptions are less likely to be written as if they were visual facts.

Training has two stages. The first is Dense Trace Initialization. High-quality multimodal instruction data are rewritten into the DRT format to create a DRT-SFT dataset, and supervised fine-tuning is used to internalize this reasoning mode. The key role of this stage is format adaptation rather than knowledge acquisition: the model learns to organize intermediate states in a compact structured space.

The second stage is Trace-Grounded Reinforcement Learning. The authors build a DRT-RL dataset using a tri-perspective verification pipeline, keeping only traces that satisfy three conditions simultaneously: correct final answer, logically consistent intermediate steps, and faithful visual grounding. They then optimize the model with a trace-grounded GRPO objective, augmented with process-based rewards and exploratory incentives. In effect, the policy is rewarded not only for being correct, but also for producing short, logically coherent, and visually grounded traces.

Compared with prior efficiency methods, this is not early stopping, token selection, or budget prediction within the same natural-language CoT space. It changes the reasoning expression space itself. Compared with standard CoT SFT/RL, it places much stronger emphasis on disentangling observation from deduction and on verifying grounding quality of intermediate traces.1b:T6a4,The model is evaluated on five challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks, including text tasks and out-of-distribution video tasks. The headline result is a 5.5x improvement in token efficiency together with a 1.3-point accuracy gain over Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct under official prompting. That is the main empirical claim: shorter traces do not merely save tokens at the cost of quality; the structured format can improve the efficiency–accuracy tradeoff.

The paper also emphasizes component ablations rather than only final benchmark numbers. Dense Trace Initialization appears to be necessary for moving the model into the new reasoning format; without it, simply asking for concise reasoning would not reliably produce high-density traces. Trace-Grounded RL further improves faithfulness, especially under tight token budgets, where models otherwise tend to sacrifice logical completeness or grounding for brevity. The tri-perspective verification pipeline is important because it raises the quality of RL reference traces and filters out trajectories that are answer-correct but process-poor, or process-plausible but visually ungrounded.

From a foundation-model perspective, there are clear caveats. The gains depend on a specific trace schema and a data rewriting pipeline, so transfer to other model families, modality mixes, or pure-text reasoning remains open. The 5.5x token-efficiency number should be read carefully: one needs to check whether it counts only output tokens or total tokens, and whether prompts are strictly matched. Also, some of the burden may shift from inference to schema design and data construction quality, which makes the dataset pipeline central to the result.1c:T403,For a quick pass, start with Figure 1 and the method overview to understand what DRT actually looks like; otherwise it is easy to misread it as ordinary concise CoT. Then read the two training stages carefully: how the SFT data are rewritten, and how verification and rewards are defined in RL. Finally, inspect the main tables and efficiency–accuracy plots to confirm that the gains come from changing the reasoning representation rather than from prompt engineering.

If time is limited, the introduction, the DRT representation section, Dense Trace Initialization, Trace-Grounded RL, and the main experiments are enough to capture the paper in roughly 25 minutes. For deeper reading, focus on three places: the rewriting rules for DRT-SFT, which determine whether key information survives compression; the criteria in the tri-perspective verification pipeline, which determine RL signal quality; and the ablations isolating the effects of observation/deduction disentanglement, process rewards, and exploration incentives.1d:T468,A controlled ablation study rather than a new algorithm:

First, train a standard byte-level BPE tokenizer as usual and keep the full learned merge tree. There is no modification to the merge-learning objective itself. Second, after training, compute each learned token’s final exposure, meaning how often it actually appears in the fully encoded corpus after all merges are applied. Tokens with low final exposure are marked as internal-only: they remain in the merge tree and can still serve as construction nodes for longer tokens, but they are removed from the model-visible vocabulary. Third, to preserve the target visible vocabulary size, the tokenizer training is resumed beyond the original vocabulary budget, producing additional candidate merges. Better-exposed candidates are then used to fill the visible slots vacated by the pruned tokens.

The encoding design is the key implementation detail. Encoding first follows the full merge rules exactly as in standard BPE. If a resulting token is internal-only, it is recursively expanded into visible child tokens until the returned token sequence contains only visible vocabulary items. This preserves the learned merge structure while changing the model interface. What changes is which learned tokens are exposed to the LM.
