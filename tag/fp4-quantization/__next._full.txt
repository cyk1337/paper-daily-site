19:T67a,The work has a diagnosis phase and a repair phase. In diagnosis, the authors build an end-to-end FP4 RL pipeline using HiFloat4 (HiF4), and compare against MXFP4 to check whether the conclusions are format-specific. They systematically vary precision assignments across rollout and training: low-precision rollout with higher-precision training, higher-precision rollout with low-precision training, and fully FP4 on both sides. A striking result is that restoring the training policy to higher precision while keeping rollout in FP4 performs worse than the full-FP4 baseline. This shows that the main issue is not training-side numerical noise but mismatch between the rollout distribution and the training distribution induced by quantized rollout. Activation analysis then reveals the mechanism: a small number of outliers expand the scale so much that the bulk of activation values underflow to zero in FP4. Keeping rollout activations in higher precision recovers much of the lost accuracy, but sacrifices the throughput benefit of true FP4 GEMMs. In response, the paper proposes Rollout-ResQ, a residual quantization scheme for rollout activations. Instead of using static PTQ-style calibration, smoothing, clamping, or rotation methods, which assume stationary activation statistics, Rollout-ResQ separates outlier residuals from the main activation tensor and represents them as a second FP4 tensor. This preserves low-precision matmul efficiency while retaining information that would otherwise be lost to underflow.
