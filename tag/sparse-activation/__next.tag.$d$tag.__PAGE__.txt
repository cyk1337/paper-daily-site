5:T4c9,NULLs partitions MLP neurons into shared backbone neurons and a pool of sparsely activated sinks. Each source identity is deterministically mapped to a sparse mask over the sink pool. During training, examples from that source activate the shared backbone plus the source’s masked sinks. The implementation is close to standard pretraining: the main extra operation is an elementwise mask over MLP activations. Unlike per-source modules, NULLs does not allocate a separate parameter block per source; sparse masks over a common sink pool provide combinatorial control, so the number of controllable sources can be much larger than the number of sink groups. The mechanism relies on training dynamics. A fact unique to one source produces gradients on both the backbone and that source’s sinks, but the backbone receives interfering updates from all other sources, while the relevant sinks see far less interference. The unique fact is therefore fitted by the sinks earlier. Once fitted there, the gradient pressure on the backbone decreases, reducing leakage into shared weights. Facts repeated across many sources keep reinforcing the backbone. At deployment, unlearning a source means disabling its corresponding sinks.6:T4ba,The paper presents two main case studies. In the Wikipedia setting, it trains an approximately 1B-parameter model on a corpus where about 6 million articles are treated as separate sources. The key test is single-article unlearning without broader topic erasure. Disabling the target article’s sinks sharply reduces recall of facts unique to that article, while preserving semantically related knowledge from other articles. Post-hoc unlearning baselines tend to remove related knowledge at a similar rate as the target knowledge. In the Harry Potter setting, the source is coarser and more topical. NULLs supports instantaneous removal by disabling the relevant sinks, and the removal is more robust to an adversarial relearning attack; the paper notes that standard gradient unlearning can be reversed in fewer than 10 gradient steps. On general natural-language benchmarks, NULLs matches a standard Transformer closely, suggesting that the sink mechanism does not impose a visible cost on broad capability. The most important experimental details to inspect are sink capacity, mask sparsity, source granularity, mask overlap, and the relationship between factual recall suppression and perplexity
