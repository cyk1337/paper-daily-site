Instead of taking a Euclidean gradient step, the method moves along the steepest descent direction normalized in the chosen geometry. Muon corresponds to using the spectral norm on matrices, whose dual stationarity measure is the Schatten-1, or nuclear, norm.

The technical difficulty is the stochastic term. In Hilbert spaces, martingale noise can be controlled with inner-product tools. In general Banach spaces, that route is unavailable, so prior work often falls back to norm equivalence, introducing a factor rho that can scale badly with dimension. This paper avoids that standard reduction.

The first key move is to formulate the heavy-tailed assumption directly in the native dual norm: stochastic gradient errors have bounded p-th central moments, rather than bounded variance. This is both more realistic and more geometry-aware.

The second contribution is a general convergence theorem for momentum normalized steepest descent when the dual norm is p-uniformly smooth. Under this condition, the method reaches an epsilon-stationary point in the dual norm with sample complexity O(Delta_1 L / epsilon^2 * (sigma/epsilon)^{p/(p-1)}). The result is notable because it holds for a stronger stationarity notion than the usual Euclidean one, and because the noise is handled directly in the native geometry. The analysis also extends to weak (L0, L1)-smoothness, not only standard global L-smoothness.

The third contribution is a Muon-specific analysis. The nuclear norm is not uniformly smooth, so the general theorem does not apply directly. The authors therefore derive a dedicated bound for Schatten-1 stationarity on m by n matrices, obtaining a complexity of the form O(min{m,n} * Delta_1 L / epsilon^2 * (sigma/epsilon)^{p/(p-1)}). They then compare this with Euclidean methods after correcting for the stronger stationarity metric. The message is that Euclidean algorithms can only match the same nuclear-norm stationarity by paying a worse, p-dependent dimension penalty, whereas Muon absorbs heavy-tailed noise much more naturally in its own geometry. The paper also proves a first-order lower bound for Schatten-1 stationarity, showing that Muon is first-order optimal up to the main factors and that a dimension-free guarantee cannot exist in this setting.

Relative to standard SGD or Adam theory, the novelty is not another adaptive-optimizer convergence proof. The paper jointly analyzes optimization geometry, stationarity metric, and tail behavior of stochastic gradients. Relative to earlier non-Euclidean analyses, it replaces the bounded-variance plus norm-equivalence template with functional-analytic tools that control noise directly under general norms.
