9:T912,方法分成“基础设施层”和“算法探针层”两部分。

基础设施层，作者在 VERL 上扩展出一个 mutual RL 框架。核心组件有三个。第一，SEE（Shared Experience Exchange）负责存储和交换带类型的经验记录，不只是文本 rollout，还包括 reward、advantage、可选 token-level trace 以及 provenance。第二，MWRA（Multi-Worker Resource Allocation）负责在多策略之间分配 worker 和预算，使并行训练可控。第三，THL（Tokenizer Heterogeneity Layer）解决异构 tokenizer 的互操作问题：把 peer rollout 重新分词到目标模型词表，并对 token-level trace 做对齐。作者强调 THL 不是简单文本转写，而是要让 token 级信号也能跨模型传递，并在附录里分析 alignment residual 对更新的影响。

在这个框架上，作者用同一个 GRPO 基座实现三种经验共享探针。

第一种是 PRP（Peer Rollout Pooling），属于数据级共享。做法是把 peer 产生的 rollout 当补充训练轨迹直接放进共享池，测试“直接复用别人的样本”是否值得。这个方案最接近直觉，但也最容易受到 off-policy density-ratio 偏移影响。

第二种是 XGRPO（Cross-Policy GRPO Advantage Sharing），属于价值级共享。这里 actor 仍严格使用本策略 on-policy 样本更新，但 advantage 的归一化统计引入跨策略奖励信息。也就是说，它共享的是 reward statistics，而不是完整轨迹，从而尽量保留 on-policy 支持集。

第三种是 SGT（Success-Gated Transfer），属于结果级共享。只有当 learner 在某个 prompt 上失败，而 peer 成功时，才把经过 verifier 确认的 peer trajectory 作为稀疏正样本注入训练。作者把它描述为 peer-conditioned、failure-triggered、interleaved with base RL objective。这个设计明显更保守：它不试图普遍复用 peer rollout，而是只在“自己没解出来、别人解出来”的 rescue subset 上借用成功轨迹。

从全文结构看，作者的主要判断是：共享层级越高，支持越丰富，但稳定性越差；共享层级越低，信息更稀疏，但更容易和现有 on-policy RL 兼容。附录中的 contextual-bandit 分析正是为这个稳定性—支持度取舍提供结构化解释。a:T7f0,The method has two layers: a framework layer and an algorithmic probe layer.

At the framework level, the authors extend VERL into a mutual RL substrate for heterogeneous policies. Shared Experience Exchange (SEE) stores typed records, including rollouts, rewards, advantages, optional token-level traces, and provenance. Multi-Worker Resource Allocation (MWRA) manages workers and budgets across policies. Tokenizer Heterogeneity Layer (THL) is the key interoperability component: it retokenizes peer outputs into the learner’s vocabulary and aligns token-level traces across incompatible tokenizers. This is more than plain text exchange, because the goal is to make token-level training signals usable across model families.

On top of this substrate, the paper instantiates three controlled probes using the same GRPO base update.

PRP (Peer Rollout Pooling) is data-level sharing. Peer-generated rollouts are inserted into a shared pool and reused as supplementary training trajectories. This is the most direct form of sharing, but also the one most exposed to off-policy density-ratio mismatch.

XGRPO (Cross-Policy GRPO Advantage Sharing) is value-level sharing. The actor remains strictly on-policy, but advantage normalization uses cross-policy reward statistics. In other words, it shares reward-derived statistics rather than full trajectories, aiming to preserve on-policy support while still transferring information.

SGT (Success-Gated Transfer) is outcome-level sharing. A peer trajectory is injected only when the learner fails on a prompt that a peer solves, and only after the peer output is verifier-approved. This makes the transfer sparse, failure-triggered, and tightly coupled to successful outcomes rather than general off-policy behavior.

The paper’s structural claim is that richer sharing gives more support but less stability, while weaker sharing gives less information but fits current on-policy RL more safely. The contextual-bandit analysis in the appendix is used to formalize this trade-off.b:T655,The experiments are presented as controlled comparisons under matched compute, matched hyperparameters, and deterministic validation decoding, all built on the same GRPO substrate. That setup matters: the point is not to maximize benchmark numbers with many moving parts, but to compare experience-sharing regimes cleanly.

From the main text, the qualitative findings appear to be the following. PRP, the data-level sharing method, pays the highest price in off-policy shift and is most sensitive to tokenizer-alignment residuals. XGRPO is structurally safer because the actor update remains on-policy and only reward statistics are shared across policies. SGT is the most selective and often the most natural for sparse-reward reasoning tasks, since it transfers only verifier-confirmed successful trajectories on prompts where the learner itself failed.

The theoretical characterization supports these observations. PRP is associated with density-ratio variance and THL residual sensitivity; XGRPO preserves on-policy support; SGT provides a positive gradient direction on the rescue subset. These are not substitutes for empirical results, but they explain why the three regimes should behave differently even before looking at benchmark scores.

So the safe takeaway is structural rather than numerical: the paper is a controlled study of how heterogeneous policies can exchange RL signals, not a standard single-model RL ablation.c:T4c1,Read the Introduction first to set the frame correctly. The paper is not mainly proposing a new RL objective; it is making heterogeneous experience sharing operational and then probing which sharing level is compatible with modern LLM RL.

Next, focus on the framework section, especially SEE, MWRA, and THL. THL is the part most worth inspecting because tokenizer mismatch is usually hand-waved away in multi-model training, yet it directly affects token-level credit assignment. If the paper includes alignment diagrams or residual analyses for THL, those are worth reading carefully.

Then move to the definitions and update forms of PRP, XGRPO, and SGT. The key reading question is not “which shares more information,” but “which level of sharing adds useful signal without breaking the stability assumptions of on-policy RL.”
