17:T8df,方法设计很克制。作者先构造一个 exactly labeled 的 syllogistic setting，保证 valid/invalid、satisfiable/unsatisfiable 的标签由形式逻辑直接给出，而不是人工标注。然后对冻结模型学习短 soft prefix，使其把某一类原本答对的样本推向指定目标答案。为了避免把结果误读成“prefix 学会了输出某个 token”，论文做了几层功能性解释。

第一层是区分 fixed-symbol forcing 与 semantic answer bias。作者通过随机化答案映射、统计 target-answer rate、分析对目标答案 logit/score 的直接加成，检查 prefix 是否只是强推某个表面符号。如果在答案映射变化后效果仍跟随语义而不是符号，说明更接近答案意义层面的偏置。

第二层是区分 task-specific bias 与 cross-task operation。作者分别在 validity 和 satisfiability 两个任务上训练/测试 prefix，再做跨任务迁移。如果一个 prefix 真学到更抽象的“逻辑操作”，它应在两个任务间较稳定转移；如果主要是任务内答案偏置，迁移会弱很多。结果支持后者。

第三层是看泛化边界。作者按 logical form 做 partition，在未见形式上测试；再换 wording、改 prompt interface，检查 prefix 是否只绑定训练模板。有效 prefix 在这些变化下仍能保持较高 flip，说明它不是简单记模板。

第四层是用 matched random controls 和 readable-text controls 做对照。随机前缀用于排除“任何额外连续向量都会扰动输出”的解释；文本控制用于比较可读上下文与不可读 prefix 的差异。作者还加入 reverse directions，检查这种干预是否具有明显方向性。

最后，论文用 score model 和 activation analysis 补充解释。score model 以 answer class 和 unprefixed margin 为输入，拟合加 prefix 后的 margin 变化，测试“广义答案偏置 + 原始决策边界脆弱性”能解释多少现象。activation 分析则看 learned prefix 与 norm-matched random prefix 在中间层/最终答案 margin 上造成的变化，并测试对 prefix 顺序的敏感性。整体上，这不是提出新训练法，而是把软前缀当作探针，系统拆解模型逻辑判断的脆弱来源。18:T73e,The method is carefully structured. The authors start from an exactly labeled syllogistic benchmark where validity and satisfiability are determined by formal logic rather than annotation. They then learn a short soft prefix on top of a frozen model, targeting examples that the unprefixed model originally gets correct. The goal is to redirect those judgments toward a chosen wrong answer.

The main contribution is the functional interpretation protocol for otherwise opaque prefixes. First, the paper distinguishes fixed-symbol forcing from semantic answer bias. It randomizes answer mappings and analyzes target-answer rates and score shifts to test whether the prefix is tied to a surface token or to the meaning of an answer class.

Second, it distinguishes task-specific bias from cross-task transfer. Prefixes are trained and tested across validity and satisfiability settings. If a prefix learned something like a shared logical operation, transfer should be strong across tasks. The observed transfer is limited, which supports the answer-bias interpretation.

Third, the paper probes generalization boundaries. It evaluates on unseen logical forms, new wordings, and rephrased prompt interfaces. Strong transfer under these changes argues against simple template memorization.

Fourth, it uses matched random-prefix controls and readable-text controls. Random prefixes test whether any extra continuous context can perturb the model. Reverse-direction experiments probe whether the effect is directional rather than symmetric.

Finally, the authors fit score models using answer class and the unprefixed margin to explain prefixed behavior, and add activation analyses to compare learned prefixes against norm-matched random ones. The overall picture is that soft prefixes are used as probes for model fragility, not as a new reasoning method.19:T5d1,The evaluation covers Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MoE, Qwen3-8B, and Gemma 4 31B. Learned prefixes flip a large fraction of previously correct judgments, and the effect survives transfer to unseen logical forms and interface changes.

On new-wording and rephrased-prompt conditions, flip rates are 72%–90% for Qwen3.6 MoE, 53%–72% for Qwen3-8B, and 40%–56% for Gemma 4 31B. Across four logical-form partitions, learned prefixes beat matched random controls in all 16 comparisons for Qwen3.6 and Gemma, with margins of 37 to 99 percentage points. One of the cleanest results is Gemma on validity: learned prefixes still achieve 54%–56% flip under wording and prompt changes, while matched random prefixes stay below 1%. The paper also strengthens the random baseline on Qwen3.6 by selecting the best prefixes from 1,000 random candidates; even then, transfer remains substantially weaker than with learned prefixes.

Mechanistically, the dominant effect is not fixed-symbol forcing but a broad preference for one answer meaning. That explains much of the aggregate steering, but not all instance-level variation. The score model fits Gemma’s prefixed margins much better than Qwen’s. For Qwen, it often predicts whether an example will cross the decision boundary, but not the final targeted margin.
