9:T71d,The method has two central pieces. The first is a local geometric view around the reference model. The reference model is treated as a common origin in behavioral space, and the allowed drift budget defines a local boundary. Within that neighborhood, drift magnitude determines distance from the origin, while direction determines how the limited behavioral change is allocated across capabilities. This gives a common coordinate system in which heterogeneous updates can be compared by directional efficiency: task improvement per unit of behavioral drift.

The second piece is a coarse but systematic layer-selective probe. Rather than introducing a complicated regularizer, the authors vary which layers are trainable while keeping the data, objective, and optimizer fixed as much as possible. This isolates accessible update directions as the main experimental variable. The result is that several layer subsets provide nearby directions that improve the QA-only target task while preserving reasoning and general capabilities. Full fine-tuning often fails because its large update space includes many easy loss-reducing directions that overwrite existing behaviors. LoRA also restricts directions, but its low-rank subspace does not consistently contain the efficient ones, so it helps in some settings and not in others.

Mechanistically, the paper argues that QA-only supervision specifies the answer but not the reasoning behavior. Optimization therefore tends to exploit whichever behavioral rewrite most easily reduces training loss. If the update space allows the model to bypass or compress its original reasoning style, standard SFT will often take that route. Layer selection changes the reachable subspace, nudging optimization toward adaptations that reuse rather than replace existing reasoning circuits.a:T68e,The experiments use Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B, with scientific reasoning and multilingual translation as the main tasks. The key empirical pattern is consistent: direct QA-only full fine-tuning increases target-task performance while steadily degrading downstream reasoning as functional drift grows. LoRA sometimes softens this trade-off, but not reliably. The layer-selective probe identifies multiple trainable-layer configurations that, at similar drift levels, improve the target task and preserve both reasoning and broader capabilities.

The translation results are the strongest practical validation. The resulting model improves over the reference model across more than 100 language directions and is reported to match or outperform dedicated multilingual systems such as Seed-X-PPO-7B and Tower-Plus-9B. The paper also claims that this SFT initialization beats a Qwen3-8B translation model trained with reinforcement learning across all evaluated directions, and that applying the same RL pipeline on top of the new initialization yields larger gains and the best final performance. That is important because it suggests the benefit is not a one-off SFT artifact; it changes the quality of the starting point for later RL.

The ablations are cleaner than usual. The authors try to hold data, objective, and optimization fixed, and vary only the trainable layer set. That makes the observed differences easier to attribute to accessible directions and directional efficiency rather than to confounded recipe changes. They also examine different drift regimes, showing that some directions are efficient under small budgets while others only become useful at larger drift.b:T484,For a quick pass, focus on three parts. Second, read the section that defines the behavioral geometry. The main point is not the formalism itself, but the claim that once drift is fixed, direction becomes the remaining degree of freedom for comparing fine-tuning methods. Third, inspect the matched-drift experiments for full FT, LoRA, and layer-selective tuning. Without matched drift, it is easy to confuse larger movement with better direction.
