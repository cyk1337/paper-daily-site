17:T836,The core method adapts activation patching to the vision encoder and measures how much a single layer-token activation restores the final image-text similarity score. The procedure has three runs. In the clean run, the model sees the original image and produces a similarity score s_clean. In the corrupted run, the image is perturbed with Gaussian noise and yields s_corr. In the patched run, the corrupted image is used again, but one intermediate activation at layer l and token t is replaced by its clean counterpart, producing s_patch. From these three scores, the paper defines a causal contribution Γ(l,t), interpreted as the fraction of the clean-corrupted gap recovered by restoring that activation. This is stronger than attention visualization or gradient saliency because it measures functional recovery, not just correlation.

The second key step is to connect causal importance to spatial grounding. Since ViT tokens correspond to fixed image patches, each token is mapped back to its patch region Pt, and the overlap with the ground-truth object box B is measured using IoU(Pt, B). For each layer, the paper computes the correlation between Γ and IoU, denoted Corr_l. If Corr_l is high, causally important tokens tend to fall on the target region. If it is low, the model may still perform well while relying on evidence outside the queried object. This layerwise analysis is important because it asks whether spatial grounding emerges progressively through the encoder or never becomes causally dominant.

The full text indicates that the analysis starts with CLIP as a controlled setting and then extends to larger VLMs, multiple corruption settings, and different generations of visual encoders, including ViT-based and SigLIP-based systems. The paper also probes whether models preserve visual structure when appearance cues are removed. The answer is nuanced: visual cues are indeed used for structural understanding, but that does not imply localized causal grounding. This is a meaningful distinction from prior work that relies mostly on attention maps or attribution heatmaps.18:T6d2,The clearest findings in the provided full content are threefold. First, on CLIP, the authors run token- and layer-level causal tracing over 100 samples and find only a weak relationship between causal contribution and target-box IoU. The qualitative conclusion is explicit: highly causal tokens often do not lie inside the target region. In other words, the activations that recover the image-text score are frequently outside the object referred to by the text.

Second, this is not presented as a CLIP-specific artifact. The same pattern is reported in larger VLMs from 2023 onward and under different corruption procedures. So increased model scale and stronger multimodal benchmark performance do not automatically produce better spatial causal alignment. The excerpt does not include the full numeric tables, but the textual claim is clear that the phenomenon persists across models.

Third, the paper studies structure understanding under reduced appearance cues and finds that models do use visual information to infer structure. That matters because it rules out the simplest interpretation that the models are ignoring the image altogether. The issue is subtler: they use visual evidence, but the evidence that is causally important is not necessarily localized where a human would expect it to be.

From an experimental-design perspective, the useful details are the layerwise Corr_l analysis, reporting mean and standard deviation across seeds, and comparisons across encoder families. When reading the full paper, it is worth extracting the actual correlation values, where the layerwise peaks occur, whether different corruption types change the conclusion, and whether SigLIP and ViT differ systematically in middle or late layers.19:T595,Start with the definition of Γ in the method section. That is the central quantity: it measures how much of the clean-to-corrupted performance gap is recovered by restoring one activation. Then read the part that maps tokens back to image patches and computes IoU and Corr_l. This is what makes the paper a causal grounding analysis rather than a standard saliency study.

In the results, focus on three kinds of figures. First, token heatmaps on individual examples, which make the off-target causal tokens visually obvious. Second, layerwise Corr_l curves, which show whether spatial grounding emerges anywhere in the encoder. Third, cross-model comparisons, which tell you whether the effect is specific to CLIP or more general. If time is limited, the introduction, method, the main CLIP experiment, and the cross-model generalization section are enough to form a solid view in about 25 to 35 minutes.

The most interesting follow-up questions are practical. Would the conclusion hold under occlusion, patch dropout, or frequency-domain corruption instead of Gaussian noise? Does the same mismatch appear when the target is a generative VLM next-token logit rather than an image-text similarity score? And can region-level alignment losses, object-centric crops, or stronger visual discretization constraints materially increase Corr_l during pretraining? Those are the places where this paper connects back to model design.1a:T643,The main contribution is a new evaluation metric, Synergistic Faithfulness (F_syn). The paper does not propose a new explainer. Instead, it changes the target of evaluation. Rather than asking whether removing important features from one modality reduces confidence, it asks whether visual and textual features jointly contribute something beyond the sum of their separate effects.

The theoretical grounding comes from cooperative game theory. The authors connect the problem to the Shapley Interaction Index and focus on the 2-way Harsanyi dividend between visual and textual features. This quantity isolates the true interaction term, not just the additive combination of marginal contributions. Exact computation is intractable for modern VLMs because it requires enumerating feature subsets with complexity O(2^(m+n)).

To make the metric practical, the paper introduces a scalable surrogate based on continuous perturbation trajectories. The reported result is a Spearman correlation of 0.92 with exact SII, together with a 24x empirical speedup. The conceptual shift matters more than the exact formula: an explainer that only highlights visually salient regions but fails to capture text-image grounding should not score highly under F_syn.

The evaluation protocol also uses a Linear Mixed-effects Model to separate explainer effects from dataset and model variance. This is an important design choice because explainability benchmarks are often dominated by the particular benchmark or architecture being used. The LMM lets the authors estimate the fixed effect of each explainer more cleanly.1b:T549,The benchmark covers 3 datasets, 3 VLM architectures, and 8 explainers. The tested models include LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL-3.5. The first key empirical result is the failure mode itself: when the same explainers are evaluated on the same data using visual-only versus text-only perturbation metrics, the rankings can strongly disagree. This is the paper’s central evidence that standard unimodal faithfulness metrics collapse in multimodal settings.

For metric validity, F_syn achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.92 with exact Shapley Interaction Index computation while being 24 times faster empirically. That supports its use as a scalable surrogate.

For explainer comparison, attention-based methods perform best. AttnLRP obtains the highest LMM coefficient, with beta = 0.029. The VLM-native explainers highlighted in the paper, including LLaVA-CAM and TAM, are reported around beta = 0.008. The authors interpret this as evidence that many VLM-specific explainers overemphasize visual salience and undercapture true cross-modal interaction.

When reading the full paper, the most important details are not the final rankings but the robustness checks: how the metric behaves under different masking baselines, perturbation schedules, and patch/token granularities. Those choices determine whether the benchmark is actually reliable.1c:T91a,The study combines architecture-aware probing with several mechanistic interventions. The authors analyze two representative VLM families. LLaVA-1.5 serves as a clean baseline because CLIP ViT-L/14 patch tokens are mapped one-to-one into the LLM space through a small MLP. InternVL-3.5 adds two complications: token compression via pixel shuffle and dynamic multi-view processing with a global thumbnail plus local high-resolution tiles. This contrast lets the paper test whether the same localization mechanism survives under compression and multi-view fusion.

The dataset is a curated subset of COCO val, not raw COCO. The authors first correct annotation issues using prior semi-automatic procedures, then filter out tiny or dominant objects, low-resolution images, and ambiguous cases. The final set contains 3,560 images and 6,403 object annotations. They also build auxiliary controls to reduce context-only hallucinated detections. This matters because mechanistic claims are fragile under noisy labels.

The main tools are:
1. Token ablations to identify which visual tokens causally support class prediction and box prediction.
2. Controlled perturbations that preserve object extent while scrambling internal semantic arrangement, or alter context outside the object, to test whether localization depends on internal semantic layout or mainly on token support boundaries.
3. Positional decoding from intermediate representations to see how a 1D token sequence still carries enough signal for the LLM to reconstruct an approximate 2D grid.
4. Attention knockout to rank heads by causal importance for classification and localization.
5. Causal mediation analysis to separate heads that directly affect localization from heads whose effect is mediated through classification.

The central claim is a containerization mechanism: object-aligned tokens define a spatial container, and the semantic arrangement inside that container matters surprisingly little for the predicted box. In InternVL, the global view carries most of the spatial signal, while local crops mainly refine semantics and help small-object classification. The paper also argues for a sequential dependency: localization-critical processing depends on classification-critical heads, even though the dominant heads for the two tasks are largely distinct.1d:T692,The most useful results are intervention-based rather than benchmark-based. The main empirical findings are:
1. Localization information is genuinely encoded in visual tokens. Ablating object-aligned tokens strongly disrupts box prediction, while ablating irrelevant regions has much smaller effect.
2. Scrambling semantics inside an object while preserving its token support leaves localization relatively stable. This is the key evidence for the containerization claim.
3. In InternVL, removing the global thumbnail hurts localization more than removing local high-resolution tiles, whereas local tiles contribute more to fine-grained recognition and small-object classification. This suggests a division of labor: global view for spatial frame, local view for semantic refinement.
4. Positional decoding shows that the LLM can recover an approximate 2D layout from the linearized token stream using residual positional structure and strong corner anchors, even without an explicit preserved 2D grid.
5. Only a very small number of attention heads carry strong causal effects. In LLaVA, these heads appear mostly in early-to-mid layers; in InternVL, more in mid-to-late layers.
