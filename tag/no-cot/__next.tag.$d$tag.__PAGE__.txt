a:T40d,Its main contribution is evaluation and trend estimation, but it matters for pretraining research as well. First, it turns latent reasoning from a qualitative concern into a quantitative object by mapping performance onto a 50% task-completion time horizon. That is a more interpretable capability scale than isolated benchmark scores. Second, it introduces a complementary reasoning-token horizon, defined via the minimum number of reasoning tokens an anchor model needs to solve a task. This gives a bridge between explicit reasoning budget and latent reasoning ability. Third, the paper finds that no-CoT horizons are improving substantially, but still much more slowly than CoT-enabled full-affordance agentic horizons. That suggests explicit scratchpads, tool use, and externalized reasoning remain major capability multipliers. For pretraining researchers, this raises a direct question: which training objectives and data regimes encourage internal compression of reasoning, and which make reasoning more legible and controllable?b:T6a2,The methodology has three layers. First, benchmark construction: the authors assemble 43 benchmarks with more than 30,000 questions spanning math, coding, puzzles, causality, theory of mind, strategic reasoning, and multi-turn tool-use settings. The key constraint is that models are prevented from emitting reasoning tokens before the answer, so the setup aims to measure latent reasoning rather than explicit CoT. Second, capability scaling via task-completion horizons: following and extending prior horizon work, they estimate the human time required for tasks that a model completes with 50% success rate. To make this credible, they explicitly model uncertainty in human solve times using a mix of real human timings, collected baselines, and calibrated model-based estimates, then propagate uncertainty through hierarchical bootstrap over benchmarks, problems, model stochasticity, and time estimates. Third, they define a reasoning-token horizon using o3-mini as an anchor reasoning model: task difficulty is measured by the minimum number of reasoning tokens the anchor needs to solve the task within k attempts. This provides a model-native difficulty scale that complements the human-time scale. For trend analysis, they fit no-CoT horizon growth over release dates for 14 frontier models, using the Pareto frontier of capability versus release date for the main trend. They also fit horizon against architecture scale for 35 open-weight models, relating 50% TH to total parameters and layer count. One practical trade-off is that the datasets are not publicly released to avoid future training contamination, which limits reproducibility but is understandable for long-term measurement.c:T69f,Several numbers are central. First, across 14 frontier models from GPT-2 to GPT-5.5, the no-CoT 50% task horizon doubles every 373 days, with a 95% confidence interval of 167 to 691 days and R² of 0.84. GPT-5.5 reaches an estimated no-CoT horizon above 3 minutes. Second, the median extrapolation projects horizons above 7 minutes by 2028 and 25 minutes by 2030, though the paper is careful to note large uncertainty. Third, the reasoning-token horizon doubles every 437 days, with a 95% CI of 341 to 571 days. The mapping between token horizon and human-time horizon is sublinear rather than linear, implying that token requirements grow faster than human solve times as tasks get longer. Fourth, compared with Kwa et al.’s 182-day doubling for CoT-enabled full-affordance agentic tasks, the no-CoT trend is roughly twice as slow. That is an important empirical sign that explicit CoT and tool use still materially extend the time horizon of tasks models can handle. Fifth, on 35 open-weight models, doubling the 50% TH is estimated to require about 4.2× more total parameters or 1.3× more layers. That is only a rough scaling fit, but it hints that depth may matter more than raw parameter count for latent reasoning horizons. Two experimental design choices are especially strong: the benchmark suite is broad rather than math-only, and uncertainty from human-time estimation and model stochasticity is explicitly propagated instead of hidden behind a single trend line. The main caveats are that TH depends on human-time calibration, which can vary by task and strategy, and that “no CoT” prompting may not perfectly eliminate compressed forms of hidden or quasi-explicit reasoning.d:T5d2,This is worth a close read.
