1a:T643,The main contribution is a new evaluation metric, Synergistic Faithfulness (F_syn). The paper does not propose a new explainer. Instead, it changes the target of evaluation. Rather than asking whether removing important features from one modality reduces confidence, it asks whether visual and textual features jointly contribute something beyond the sum of their separate effects.

The theoretical grounding comes from cooperative game theory. The authors connect the problem to the Shapley Interaction Index and focus on the 2-way Harsanyi dividend between visual and textual features. This quantity isolates the true interaction term, not just the additive combination of marginal contributions. Exact computation is intractable for modern VLMs because it requires enumerating feature subsets with complexity O(2^(m+n)).

To make the metric practical, the paper introduces a scalable surrogate based on continuous perturbation trajectories. The reported result is a Spearman correlation of 0.92 with exact SII, together with a 24x empirical speedup. The conceptual shift matters more than the exact formula: an explainer that only highlights visually salient regions but fails to capture text-image grounding should not score highly under F_syn.

The evaluation protocol also uses a Linear Mixed-effects Model to separate explainer effects from dataset and model variance. This is an important design choice because explainability benchmarks are often dominated by the particular benchmark or architecture being used. The LMM lets the authors estimate the fixed effect of each explainer more cleanly.1b:T549,The benchmark covers 3 datasets, 3 VLM architectures, and 8 explainers. The tested models include LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL-3.5. The first key empirical result is the failure mode itself: when the same explainers are evaluated on the same data using visual-only versus text-only perturbation metrics, the rankings can strongly disagree. This is the paper’s central evidence that standard unimodal faithfulness metrics collapse in multimodal settings.

For metric validity, F_syn achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.92 with exact Shapley Interaction Index computation while being 24 times faster empirically. That supports its use as a scalable surrogate.

For explainer comparison, attention-based methods perform best. AttnLRP obtains the highest LMM coefficient, with beta = 0.029. The VLM-native explainers highlighted in the paper, including LLaVA-CAM and TAM, are reported around beta = 0.008. The authors interpret this as evidence that many VLM-specific explainers overemphasize visual salience and undercapture true cross-modal interaction.
