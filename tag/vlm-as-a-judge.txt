17:T437,The method applies conformal prediction to VLM judges. Instead of using only the predicted score, it extracts the log-probabilities over all rating tokens at the scoring position and uses that K-dimensional distribution as the feature representation. A conformal calibration procedure then turns the point prediction into a prediction interval with target coverage guarantees. Because the labels are discrete Likert scores, the paper applies boundary adjustment to map continuous intervals back to valid integer score ranges. Beyond standard global conformal prediction, it also uses Mondrian conformal prediction, which calibrates separate quantiles for different task groups. The grouping is data-driven: after an initial standard CP run, the 14 datasets are partitioned into easy, medium, and hard groups based on interval width, and group-specific calibration is applied. The novelty is not a new CP algorithm, but the use of this framework to expose two structural findings: strong task-dependent uncertainty and a decoupling between ranking quality and scoring reliability.18:T4d1,The study covers 3 VLM judges and 14 visual task categories, mainly on MLLM-as-a-Judge style benchmarks. The main findings are clear. First, interval width is strongly task-dependent: natural-image and aesthetics tasks produce intervals covering roughly 40% of the score range, while chart understanding and mathematical reasoning can expand to around 70%, indicating weak absolute-score reliability on those tasks. Second, the paper shows a ranking-versus-scoring failure mode: some judges achieve decent correlation with human ratings yet still produce very wide conformal intervals, meaning they can order responses but cannot assign trustworthy absolute scores. Third, data quality matters a lot. On a cleaner multi-annotator captioning benchmark, the same judge and the same conformal method yield intervals about 4.5x narrower, suggesting that annotation noise and task ambiguity are major drivers of uncertainty. Fourth, Mondrian CP gives narrower intervals on easier tasks while improving coverage on harder ones, which is more practical than a single global calibration rule.
