a:T60d,The method centers on head Fisher alignment (similarity of output-head updates).
1) Non-identifiability (Theorem 3.2): there exist task pairs with identical probe activations (Zi = Zj) but orthogonal head updates, hence zero head Fisher alignment. The missing piece in representation-only metrics is the logit-space error vector e; head updates depend on the joint geometry of (a, e), not a alone. The paper provides concrete instantiations: disjoint label blocks under a shared-output embedding, and dense-vocabulary “verbalizer shift” where a fixed prefix keeps activations constant but changing the label token changes error geometry.
2) Key identity (Theorem 4.2): in a shared output coordinate system, head Fisher alignment equals a cosine similarity between kernel mean embeddings of the joint (a, e) distribution. This turns Fisher-matrix comparison into a distributional embedding problem and yields an explicit decomposition into activation geometry, error geometry, and their coupling.
3) FisherSketch: a one-pass streaming random-feature estimator (using SRHT-style projections) that directly estimates the product-kernel cosine without materializing K×K error moments or paying O(mK) per-sample projections. It outputs a portable task signature (~16KB at m=4096) plus a small streaming state (~192KB).
4) Scope: requires a shared output basis (shared vocabulary is the natural case). For fundamentally different output spaces, the paper recommends moving to full-network or internal-layer comparisons instead of head error geometry.b:T4ac,The core result is algebraic. For LayerNorm, mean subtraction introduces a projector structure; when combined with the affine scale γ, the inverse-scale direction γ^{-1}/||γ^{-1}|| becomes an exact kernel direction of the post-final-norm centered activation covariance, for any input distribution. In other words, the direction does not need to be estimated from data; it is determined analytically by the LayerNorm parameters. The paper then uses a KFAC-style Fisher factorization to map this residual-stream kernel direction into a corresponding dead direction in parameter space. A key contrast is provided by RMSNorm: because RMSNorm lacks the mean-subtraction projector, there is no analogous universal direction. This LN-versus-RMSNorm split is important because it turns the phenomenon into an architectural prediction rather than an empirical coincidence. To measure how training deepens degeneracy beyond the algebraic baseline, the authors define the Schur ratio R(h)=lambda_min(A_l)/(A_l)_{u,u} and its complement Δγ=1-R(h), where u is the predicted inverse-scale direction. This lets them distinguish the architectural baseline from additional training-induced spectral collapse.c:T47b,The empirical study covers 14 pretrained transformers: 9 with LayerNorm and 5 with RMSNorm. That is an extremely tight match. On all 5 RMSNorm models, the same alignment stays near the random-direction null, with centered-protocol |cos|≤0.10, matching the theoretical prediction that no universal direction should exist there.
