b:T454,COSE uses one shared backbone prompted into four roles: Proposer, Validator, Solver, and Judge. The Proposer generates candidate reasoning questions. The Validator assigns a quality score v(q) and a confidence score cV(q). The Solver answers accepted questions. The Judge assigns a correctness score p(q,a) and a confidence score cJ(q,a). Confidence is estimated from the entropy of the token-level output distribution when the Validator or Judge produces feedback. Low entropy is treated as higher confidence. Only Proposer and Solver trajectories receive optimization losses; Validator and Judge are used as inference-time feedback modules. COSE uses confidence in two places: confidence-weighted PPO, which downweights uncertain feedback during policy updates, and confidence-prioritized replay, which samples more from questions that are valid, confidently judged, and close to the Solver’s current capability boundary.
