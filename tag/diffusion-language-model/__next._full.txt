Intuitively, this makes sense: in an MDLM, deciding which positions to resolve early and which uncertain positions to defer changes the future conditional distributions and can affect final answer quality. 第一，架构层面，attention 层和 Mamba 层都按 block 边界缓存。对 attention 层，缓存对象仍是标准 KV；对 Mamba 层，缓存的是每层在 block 边界处的 forward recurrent boundary state。这样，已完成 block 不需要在每个 reverse diffusion step 重新跑一遍。

第二，作者没有采用全序列双向 Mamba，而是把 reverse-direction scan 限制在当前 block 内。原因很直接：如果反向扫描跨过 clean prefix，那么反向状态会依赖当前 block 的 noisy 内容和 diffusion timestep，prefix 就不再是稳定缓存。把反向扫描裁到 active block 后，prefix 只需要 forward scan，天然可复用；当前 block 仍然保留局部双向 mixing，避免退化成纯因果 denoiser。

第三，训练目标采用 all-block objective。一个训练窗口被切成多个 block，同一窗口内每个 block 都轮流作为“当前去噪目标”，其左侧 clean blocks 作为条件前缀。关键不只是 block-wise loss，而是训练时显式构造与推理一致的 prefix caches：attention KV 和 Mamba forward states 都在训练图里被真实建立，后续 block 的 loss 会直接反向训练这些缓存状态的构造过程。这样，模型学到的不是抽象的 prefix 表示，而是推理时真正要复用的状态。

第五，作为对照，作者系统比较了四类模型：full-sequence attention、full-sequence DiffuMamba-H、BDLM attention、BDLM Mamba-H。这里的价值在于把“block factorization”和“mixer 替换”两个变量拆开，而不是只和一个弱基线比较。22:T8b1,The proposed model is BDLM Mamba-H, a block diffusion Mamba-attention hybrid with partial bidirectionality. The key design choice is simple but consequential: bidirectional mixing is retained only inside the current active denoising block, while completed prefix blocks are represented through cacheable forward states.

At the architectural level, attention layers cache standard KV tensors at block boundaries, and Mamba layers cache forward recurrent boundary states. This lets completed blocks remain fixed conditioning context across reverse diffusion steps, instead of being recomputed every time.

The main departure from prior full-sequence DiffuMamba-style models is the restriction of the reverse-direction Mamba scan. A reverse scan over the whole denoising window would make prefix states depend on the current noisy block and on the diffusion timestep, which destroys cache reuse. By limiting the reverse scan to the active block, the model keeps local bidirectional context where denoising needs it, while preserving a stable forward-only prefix representation.

Training is built around an all-block objective. A training window is partitioned into blocks, and each block is trained as the current denoising target conditioned on the clean prefix blocks to its left. The important part is not merely summing block losses. During training, the model explicitly constructs the same cache objects used at inference: attention KV caches and Mamba forward boundary states. Losses on downstream blocks therefore directly train the mechanism that builds reusable prefix states.

The paper also introduces timestep-conditioning factorization so that diffusion-step modulation applies only to the active block. This avoids contaminating clean-prefix caches with timestep-dependent activations. The student starts from an ARLM checkpoint whose causal attention is replaced with bidirectional attention, enabling denoising over arbitrary masked positions. The teacher is the original frozen ARLM. The main challenge is that the teacher and student do not share the same state space: the teacher predicts the next token from a causal prefix, while the student denoises partially observed sequences.

The paper resolves this by letting the student generate its own reverse denoising trajectories. At each training step, the student rolls out a trajectory under its own diffusion decoding policy, and one partially denoised state x_t is sampled from that trajectory. Distillation is then applied only to the still-masked positions in x_t. For each masked position i, the student predicts a token distribution conditioned on the partially denoised sequence, while the teacher provides target logits conditioned on the causal prefix of the terminal sequence x_0 up to position i. In effect, the teacher says what an AR model would prefer at that position, and the student learns to match that preference on the states it will actually encounter during inference.

This design addresses both mismatches at once. Knowledge retention improves because supervision comes directly from the original AR teacher rather than from relearning everything through a diffusion NELBO. Train–test mismatch is reduced because training states are on-policy student states, not uniformly random masks. The paper also introduces a rollout-length curriculum: early in training, the student is supervised on shorter and easier trajectories, and the rollout horizon is gradually increased as the model stabilizes. That choice is important for optimization, since long on-policy trajectories from an immature student would otherwise create noisy and low-quality supervision.
