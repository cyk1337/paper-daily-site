18:Te4a,整体架构由三部分组成：一个基于 LLaDA 2.0 的冻结 dLLM-VLM 负责多模态理解，一个 connector 把理解侧 hidden states 投到生成条件空间，一个 6B 单流 Diffusion Transformer 负责图像合成。关键点不在“模块很多”，而在职责边界划得很清楚。

1. 先学视觉先验，再学语言对齐
作者反对从训练一开始就重度依赖图文对。原因有两个：一是 caption 贵且噪声大；二是在低分辨率训练阶段，caption 中描述的细节常因下采样而不可见，导致条件和目标不匹配。为此，他们先做 image-only pre-training 和 mid-training，让 DiT 先把自然图像分布学扎实。这里不是无条件生成，而是借助冻结 VLM 从“同一图像区域”提取条件表示，让条件和生成目标天然对齐，避免外部 caption 带来的语义漂移。这个设计很像在做一种自蒸馏式的视觉条件建模：监督信号主要来自真实图像本身，语言只在后期逐步接入。

2. 统一理解、生成、编辑，但不强行共用所有路径
文本生成和编辑共用同一条语义条件路径：文本、视觉上下文和结构化 reasoning trace 先进入 dLLM-VLM，再经 Residual Query Adapter 和轻量 Transformer connector 送入 DiT。编辑任务则额外引入 reference-image pathway，而且这个路径直接进 DiT，绕过 VLM。作者这样做的原因很实际：编辑时既需要高层语义指令，也需要像素级或局部外观证据；如果全部压进 VLM hidden states，参考图像细节容易损失。因此他们同时使用 SigLIP-VQ 特征和 clean VAE latent，分别提供语义级和像素级参考信号。这个设计说明作者没有把“统一”理解成单一路径，而是按信息类型分层接入。

3. progressive training pipeline
训练分阶段推进：先在 256 分辨率做 image-only pre-training；再做 aspect-ratio-bucketed mid-training，继续扩展视觉先验；之后在 512 和 1024 分辨率引入 paired language alignment；再对 text-rich 和 portrait 数据做 targeted refinement；最后做 generation-editing joint training。这里有两个关键取舍。其一，数据配比上始终保持 real-data-dominant，整条生成训练链约 2.2 亿样本，其中 98% 是真实图像，SFT 阶段真实图像占比也保持在 70% 以上。作者承认这种路线在早期 benchmark 收敛可能慢于 synthetic-heavy 配方，但长期 realism ceiling 更高。其二，训练目标不是一次性混训所有能力，而是按“先分布、后条件、再任务整合”的顺序推进，这和大模型训练里常见的 annealing / curriculum 思路一致。

4. 稳定性与优化
DiT 全面使用 parameter-free RMSNorm，而不是带可学习缩放参数的归一化。作者的意图是减少大模型 diffusion 训练中的尺度漂移和额外不稳定因素。优化器选 Muon，也是在强调大规模从头训练的稳定与效率。虽然从给出的片段里看不到完整优化细节和对比表，但论文把这两点列为 recipe 的核心组成，说明它们不是边角工程，而是支撑 6B DiT 训练成功的重要条件。

5. TwinFlow 蒸馏到 Turbo
部署侧使用 TwinFlow，把原始多步 diffusion 模型蒸馏成 2–4 步的 LLaDA-Image Turbo。方法上结合了 distribution-matching distillation 和 self-adversarial flow training。值得注意的是，作者把 Turbo 视为同一家族的部署变体，而不是另起一个轻量模型。这意味着训练时就考虑了 teacher 分布是否适合后续 few-step student 拟合，属于“为蒸馏而设计主模型”的思路。19:T65f,This is highly relevant to foundation-model pretraining. Its significance is not just that it trains a 6B DiT, but that it makes several training decisions explicit and backs them with a full recipe. Four contributions stand out.

First, it argues clearly for decoupling visual-prior learning from language alignment. The generator is trained to model image distribution first, and paired supervision is introduced later. That is closely analogous to the separation between broad pretraining and later alignment in LLMs, and it is a useful design pattern for multimodal foundation training.

Second, it shows a concrete role for a diffusion language model beyond understanding alone. The frozen LLaDA2.0-Mini-based VLM acts as a semantic conditioning hub that unifies text, visual context, and reasoning traces. This gives a practical answer to how an understanding model and a generation model can be separated without becoming disconnected.

Third, the paper contributes a serious large-scale training recipe: parameter-free RMSNorm throughout the DiT, the Muon optimizer, and a progressive curriculum over data type and resolution. For training large DiTs from scratch, these choices matter more than many cosmetic architectural tweaks.

Fourth, few-step distillation is treated as part of the main model family rather than an afterthought. In that sense, LLaDA-Image is not just a model but a full pipeline from prior learning to alignment, editing, and deployment. This should matter for open image-generation pretraining, and it also has direct relevance to unified multimodal pretraining and AR-plus-diffusion style systems.1a:T1037,The system has three main components: a frozen dLLM-based VLM built on LLaDA 2.0 for multimodal understanding, a connector that projects understanding-side hidden states into the generator conditioning space, and a 6B single-stream Diffusion Transformer for image synthesis. The key is not the number of modules, but the clarity of their roles.

1. Learn the visual prior first, then align to language.
The authors explicitly avoid heavy dependence on paired image-text data at the beginning of training. Their argument is practical: captions are expensive and noisy, and at low training resolutions many captioned details are no longer visible after downsampling, which creates condition-target mismatch. Instead, they begin with image-only pretraining and mid-training so that the DiT first learns the natural image distribution well. This is not purely unconditional generation. A frozen VLM extracts conditioning signals from the same image region that the DiT is asked to generate, which keeps condition and target tightly matched without relying on external captions. Conceptually, this resembles a form of self-distilled visual conditioning: the image itself provides most of the supervision signal, while language is introduced later.

2. Unify understanding, generation, and editing without forcing a single pathway for everything.
Text-to-image generation and editing share the same semantic conditioning route: text, visual context, and structured reasoning traces are encoded by the dLLM-VLM, then passed through a Residual Query Adapter and a lightweight Transformer connector into the DiT. Editing adds a separate reference-image pathway that goes directly into the DiT and bypasses the VLM. This is an important design choice. Editing needs both high-level semantic instructions and low-level appearance evidence; if everything is compressed into VLM hidden states, reference fidelity can be lost. To address this, the model uses both SigLIP-VQ features and clean VAE latents, providing semantic-level and pixel-level reference signals respectively. The paper’s notion of unification is therefore layered rather than monolithic.

3. Progressive training pipeline.
Training proceeds in stages: image-only pretraining at 256 resolution, aspect-ratio-bucketed mid-training to strengthen the visual prior, paired language alignment at 512 and 1024 resolution, targeted refinement on text-rich and portrait data, and finally joint generation-editing training. Two design choices matter here. First, the data mixture remains real-data-dominant throughout. The full generation pipeline processes about 220M samples, 98% of them real images, and the real-image share stays above 70% even during supervised fine-tuning. The authors explicitly acknowledge that this may converge more slowly on early benchmarks than synthetic-heavy mixtures, but they argue that it yields stronger realism at convergence. Second, capabilities are not mixed all at once. The curriculum follows a distribution-first, conditioning-next, task-integration-later order, which is very much in line with annealing and curriculum ideas in large-model training.

4. Stability and optimization.
The DiT uses parameter-free RMSNorm throughout rather than a learnable affine normalization. The motivation is to reduce scale drift and unnecessary instability in large-scale diffusion training. The optimizer is Muon, again chosen with training stability and scalability in mind. The provided excerpt does not include the full optimization ablations, but the paper presents these choices as central parts of the recipe rather than minor implementation details.

5. TwinFlow distillation to Turbo.
For deployment, the original multi-step model is distilled into 2–4-step LLaDA-Image Turbo variants using TwinFlow, which combines distribution-matching distillation with self-adversarial flow training. An important point is that Turbo is treated as a deployment variant within the same family, not as a separate lightweight model. This suggests that the teacher is trained with downstream few-step distillation in mind, which is a more integrated view of training and serving.1b:T8fd,The experimental results support several distinct claims.

First, on overall capability, LLaDA-Image performs strongly on Qwen-Image-Bench, LongText-Bench, CVTG-2K, and GEdit-Bench, covering photorealism, prompt alignment, bilingual Chinese-English text rendering, and instruction-guided editing. The emphasis is on balanced capability rather than optimizing a single aesthetic metric. That matters because many open models still lag badly on text rendering or reference-preserving editing.

Second, the data strategy is a central empirical result. The paper argues that image-only pretraining followed by progressive language alignment is not only viable, but preferable in the long run to synthetic-heavy paired training from the start. Synthetic mixtures may improve early benchmark numbers faster, but they also inject artifacts and caption bias that hurt realism later. The full generation pipeline uses about 220M samples, with more than 90% of training in image-only stages and 98% of samples coming from real images. Those numbers make the intended shift in supervision very concrete.

Third, the system scale and deployment profile are explicit. The main generator is a 6B DiT trained from scratch, while the understanding module is built on a frozen LLaDA2.0-Mini backbone. The release includes four checkpoints: Base and Turbo, plus FP8 variants of each. The Turbo models run with only 2–4 sampling steps, which substantially lowers inference cost.

Fourth, editing performance comes from the unified architecture rather than a separate editing backbone. Text-to-image and editing share the semantic path, while reference images are injected through an additional DiT-side pathway. This allows the model to balance instruction following and reference preservation on editing benchmarks such as GEdit-Bench. That is an important result for unified multimodal generation systems, where many models still trade off editability against fidelity.
