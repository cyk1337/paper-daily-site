The paper introduces three mechanisms. BLT-D trains the decoder with both the standard next-byte prediction loss and a masked-byte prediction loss over corrupted fixed-size byte blocks. These blocks are derived from dynamically segmented patches but may cross patch boundaries, forcing the decoder to model future bytes beyond the average patch length. At inference time, a block of byte positions is initialized as masked and multiple positions are iteratively revealed per decoder step, reducing calls to the decoder, encoder, and global model. BLT-S is a self-speculative variant for standard BLT: the lightweight local decoder drafts bytes past the usual patch boundary, and the full BLT computation verifies the draft. Because rejected drafts roll back to the first mismatch, it preserves the output of standard autoregressive BLT while reducing expensive global evaluations.
