Sensitivity is measured by Layer-Wise Representation Divergence (LRD), defined as cosine divergence between mean-pooled hidden states for clean and perturbed inputs at each layer. Two summary statistics, slope across depth and recovery rate from early to late layers, are used to classify models into spike-and-suppress versus late-accumulation regimes. Causality is measured with activation patching: at each layer, the clean hidden state is inserted into the noisy forward pass, and the authors record how often originally failed examples are recovered. To avoid over-interpreting weak patching signals, they introduce an identity-patch gate: only models with at least 80% recovery under all-layer clean patching are treated as confirmed for causal conclusions. Compensatory capacity is measured by training LoRA adapters in different layer windows and comparing repair gains against regressions on clean computation. The proposed explanation for the dissociation is cascade disruption: an adapter placed early may locally counteract perturbation damage, but because it changes the entire downstream computation, it can break parts of the clean-input trajectory that were otherwise intact.
