18:T7cb,The method inserts a recursive sparse reasoning module into diffusion transformers, with particular focus on the joint attention blocks of MMDiT-style architectures such as SD3. The key design choice is to spend extra computation where text and visual latents are structurally aligned, instead of uniformly deepening the whole network.

The first component is recursive latent refinement. Within a block, the model performs multiple latent recursion steps. At each step, it updates visual tokens using a small set of shared neural modules rather than a full new transformer block. The routing decision depends on the current visual tokens, the diffusion timestep, and the conditioning signal such as class labels or text embeddings. This gives the model a conditional internal computation path instead of a fixed one.

The second component is sparse expert routing. Rather than using a soft mixture over all modules, the paper uses Gumbel-Softmax to approximate discrete selection and allocates gradients in a winner-takes-all manner. The motivation is clear: soft routing often leads to averaged experts with weak specialization, while harder selection encourages distinct module roles to emerge.

The third component is a mixture-of-adapters design with recursive adapter updates. Instead of duplicating full attention or MLP layers for each recursion step, the recursive computation is carried by lightweight adapters whose outputs are accumulated across steps. This keeps parameter and compute overhead manageable and makes the method suitable for adaptation on top of existing DiT or SD3 backbones.

Conceptually, this differs from simply increasing denoising steps. More denoising steps spread compute across the whole sampling trajectory, while this method concentrates extra compute inside alignment-critical layers and allocates it sparsely.
