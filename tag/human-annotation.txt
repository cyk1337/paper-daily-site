18:T8cb,方法分两层。底层是 Active Statistical Inference（ASI），上层是作者提出的 Failure-Space Analysis（FSA）来设计抽样策略。ASI 的作用，是把“judge 对全量样本的预测”和“少量人工标签”结合起来，构造一个对 human-defined FAR 有效的估计器。直观上，它不是直接相信 judge，也不是只靠人工均匀抽样，而是用 judge 作为控制变量或辅助信号，再用带 inclusion probability 的人工抽样去校正残差。这样做的关键好处是：只要抽样概率已知，估计仍然锚定在人类标签定义的目标上，并且可以做方差控制。

真正有信息量的是 FSA。作者没有把 annotation policy 简化成“挑 judge 最不确定的样本”，而是先分析 factuality judge 的典型失配空间，再把这些失配风险转成可预测特征。全文里这套 pipeline 大致包括几类信号：
1. judge output features：例如 claim-level verdict 分布、解释文本、局部不一致、拒判或 unverifiable 模式；
2. evidence quality features：证据是否完整、是否存在时间失配、是否冲突、是否只提供弱支持；
3. task/input strata：不同 query 类型、回答长度、claim 密度、检索来源等；
4. answer-rubric alignment：回答是否需要释义、归纳、容忍轻微措辞偏差，或者是否触发 judge 过于字面对齐的问题。

这些特征不是直接拿来做最终分类，而是用来估计“judge-human residual risk”，也就是 judge 可能错到什么程度、错在哪些区域。然后作者把 residual risk 转成 ASI 的 sampling policy，让高风险区域有更高被人工标注的概率。这里的设计取舍很清楚：如果直接训练一个替代 judge 的复杂模型，容易把目标从 human-anchored inference 变成另一个黑盒预测器；而 FSA 只负责抽样，不改最终 estimand，因此更稳。

从全文表述看，这篇工作更像“统计估计 + 误差结构工程化建模”的结合，而不是单纯 active learning。active learning 通常追求提升分类器本身；这里追求的是在固定预算下更准确地估计总体 FAR，并给出有效不确定性区间。这一点和很多 LLM eval 论文不同。19:T77f,The method has two layers. The lower layer is Active Statistical Inference (ASI), and the upper layer is the paper’s Failure-Space Analysis (FSA) for annotation-policy design. ASI combines judge predictions on all examples with human labels on a sampled subset to estimate the human-defined factual accuracy rate (FAR). Intuitively, the judge acts as an auxiliary signal rather than a replacement target, and the sampled human labels correct the residual. Because the sampling probabilities are known, the estimator remains anchored to the human target and admits valid uncertainty estimates.

The more interesting contribution is FSA. Instead of reducing annotation policy to “sample low-confidence judge outputs,” the paper analyzes the structure of factuality-specific judge failures and turns that structure into predictive signals. The feature space includes at least four families: (1) judge-output features, such as claim-level verdict patterns and uncertainty cues; (2) evidence-quality features, such as incompleteness, temporal mismatch, conflict, or only indirect support; (3) task and input strata, such as query type and answer characteristics; and (4) answer-rubric alignment features, capturing cases where humans tolerate paraphrase or inference but the judge applies a more literal verification rule.

These features are used to estimate residual-risk regions, i.e., where judge-human disagreement is likely to be large. That residual-risk estimate is then converted into an ASI sampling policy, assigning higher inclusion probabilities to examples more likely to need human correction. This is an important design choice. The paper does not train a more complex replacement judge; it keeps the target fixed and improves the sampling design. That makes the method closer to statistically valid active inference than to standard active learning, whose goal is often to improve a classifier itself.1a:T631,The experiments are conducted on two settings: an internal AutoFA system for virtual-assistant factuality evaluation, and the public RAGTruth benchmark with human span-level hallucination annotations. The first empirical finding is that judge-only FAR substantially underestimates human-annotated FAR, confirming that automatic factuality estimates are biased in a systematic direction.

The main result is annotation efficiency. Aggregated across budgets, FSA-guided ASI yields an average effective sample size (ESS) gain of 40.3% on AutoFA and 27.1% on RAGTruth relative to the baselines. ESS is the right metric here because the goal is not just a better point estimate, but lower-variance human-anchored estimation under a fixed labeling budget.

The comparisons include uniform sampling, confidence-based sampling, and FSA-derived policies. Confidence-based sampling improves over uniform sampling, but still trails FSA because factuality residuals are not concentrated only in low-confidence cases. The paper also presents failure-mode analyses showing that confidently wrong judge decisions are common in cases involving incomplete evidence, temporal mismatch, indirect support, and rubric misalignment.

A useful aspect of the evaluation is that the paper cares about coverage and statistical validity, not only efficiency.
