9:T8d9,The authors build Multi-SFT, a 502,818-example instruction-response collection spanning five languages: English, Italian, French, Spanish, and German. It aggregates 13 heterogeneous datasets. The construction has two main pipelines. The translation pipeline is used when an English source can be meaningfully adapted into target languages. For each language, a native or near-native expert designs the translation prompt, chooses the model and decoding settings, and iterates with a separate QA reviewer until the outputs meet quality expectations. The regeneration pipeline is used when translation is insufficient, such as for conversational data, constrained instruction following, and safety behavior. In those cases, the target-language examples are regenerated from scratch using permissively licensed open-weight models.

The strongest part of the paper is its treatment of provenance. The authors start from 59 candidate datasets and exclude 45 due to licensing or content-origin violations. If a dataset card, repository, or paper indicates that entries were generated by restricted proprietary models such as GPT, Claude, or Gemini, the affected content is not accepted at face value even if the dataset itself carries a permissive label. When only part of a dataset is contaminated, they apply selective regeneration rather than discarding the entire source. The open models used for translation and regeneration include Mistral-Small, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct, and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, chosen for both capability and license compatibility.

There are also several construction details worth noting. In multi-smol_summarize, the authors do not merely translate summaries; they translate source documents, regenerate summaries, and expand the instruction variants to include structured output formats such as bullet lists and Markdown tables. For safety data, they keep harmful prompts from AdvBench but regenerate all responses as safe refusals, and they partition prompts across languages to avoid cross-lingual overlap. Aya red-teaming is repurposed into instruction-following data with safe targets rather than kept in its original adversarial form. Three aspects of the experiments matter most. The first is human evaluation. The paper reports inter-annotator agreement of at least 0.87 across all datasets and languages, which suggests that translation and regeneration were not treated as a loose automated pipeline but as an expert-reviewed process. The second is the effect of provenance-clean data on model quality. Removing closed-model-generated content does not appear to cause a major regression; instead, the resulting multilingual data often improves target-language performance. That supports the idea that language-aligned, high-quality, legally usable data can substitute for larger but noisier and legally problematic SFT mixtures. The third is robustness across model families.
