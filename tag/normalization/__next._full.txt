19:T582,The paper decomposes trainable state as Θ=(W, Φ, Ω): backbone weights W, normalization affine parameters Φ, and quantizer parameters Ω (scales/zero-points/rounding/rotations depending on the PTQ backend). It then runs controlled, matched-budget comparisons across these coordinate subspaces to identify where correction leverage concentrates.
SandwichQuant is a two-stage framework that only updates Φ:
1) Pre stage (SandwichQuant-Pre): train Φ_pre on the dense model to precondition response distributions presented to PTQ calibration/reconstruction. Then transfer only Φ_pre back to the original dense checkpoint and rerun the chosen PTQ backend from scratch.
2) Post stage (SandwichQuant-Post): after PTQ produces a fixed low-bit graph, freeze the quantized model and learn an independent Φ_post to correct residual response mismatch.
The separation matters: Φ_pre influences calibration/clipping/reconstruction by reshaping activations, while Φ_post targets remaining functional error after the quantized graph is fixed. The method adds no new inference modules/operators—only reuses existing norm affine parameters.
Mechanistically, the paper argues affine adaptation can fix structured response mismatch and error propagation, but cannot recover information irreversibly lost to severe clipping/rounding; gains depend on how much of the error is recoverable and aligned with the Φ subspace.1a:T416,The work reframes curriculum depth growing as a block-wise inherited initialization process, where normalization serves not only to stabilize gradient propagation across the full stack, but also to shape the forward distribution passed from trained prefix blocks to newly appended blocks. Pre-norm applies normalization only before each sub-layer and does not explicitly normalize residual stream outputs across block boundaries, while post-norm applies normalization after every residual update, delivering more stable representation scale at block boundaries. The study uses a frozen Qwen3-8B as the teacher, with a 9-layer expandable block-stacked student for controlled distillation, keeping parameter counts identical across normalization variants. It compares validation cross-entropy for pre-norm and post-norm under both joint training and curriculum depth growth, and isolates the mechanism via block-removal analysis, block-boundary activation scale diagnostics, and a matched-token post-joint control to rule out confounding variables.1b:T414,The analysis incorporates normalization-induced scale invariance into loss-landscape stability. For weights feeding into normalization layers such as BN or LN, rescaling the weight vector may leave the network function largely unchanged. The loss is therefore insensitive to radial scaling, but the curvature is not. When the norm of such a weight block becomes small, the relevant Hessian eigenvalues can be amplified, roughly following the inverse scale of the norm. Weight decay supplies a persistent shrinkage force, so increasing the decay coefficient drives these blocks toward a high-sharpness region. The paper then defines a weight-norm criticality indicator combining local Hessian information and the learning rate. A practical feature is the component-wise decomposition: one can evaluate the restricted Hessian for a specific scale-invariant block instead of the full parameter Hessian. This makes the criterion applicable to architectures with many normalization modules and allows attribution of instability to individual layers.1c:T528,The analysis is built around the effective rank of the Jacobian at initialization, examining how each feedforward block component modulates rank preservation: 1. Skip connections are reinterpreted as rank-preserving mechanisms: the full-rank identity path bypasses rank loss in the residual branch. The relative scale of the branch vs. skip path controls a tradeoff between rank collapse and ensemble-like behavior — when all gradient flows through the skip path, the network degenerates into an ensemble of independent layers and loses deep compositional capacity. 2. Normalization placement regulates this same branch-to-skip ratio: Post-Norm keeps the ratio constant, leading to monotonic rank collapse with depth; Pre-Norm lets the ratio decay with depth, so rank plateaus at a moderate level, but deeper layers shift toward the ensemble regime. 3. The two-matrix (up-projection + down-projection) feedforward structure applies nonlinear activation in the expanded hidden dimension, where enough directions survive to maintain full residual branch Jacobian rank despite activation-induced rank loss; the down-projection also decorrelates branch means to avoid representation collapse onto a single direction. 4. A lightweight NormSelector predicts mixture weights, trained end-to-end via a Gumbel–Softmax reparameterization to approximate discrete routing.

The paper’s technical core is the diagnosis of when this routing is learnable: on stationary datasets, the selector sees high-variance gradients early on while representations are still unstable, so the gating distribution does not collapse into meaningful preferences; on non-stationary language modeling/classification, sustained gating diversity persists long enough for the selector to discover useful layer-wise policies after representations become informative.

AutoNorm-S addresses this with a gate-freezing schedule (and related control of gating noise/temperature): reduce selector degrees of freedom early to let the backbone settle, then re-enable routing to allow specialization. The work also operationalizes “non-stationarity” using measurable signals such as batch-to-batch activation-statistics variability and the entropy-collapse rate of the gating distribution.1e:Ta5f,论文先做“理解”，再做“改造”。

第一部分是必要性与机制分析。作者先在 0.12B 级别 Llama 预训练中实证比较带 γ 与去掉 γ 的模型，发现即便 γ 参数量几乎可以忽略，去掉后训练明显变差。理论上，他们分析 Pre-Norm 结构中 Norm(x)⊙γ 后接线性映射 W 的组合，指出在 Pre-Norm 下，γ 的作用可以被吸收到后续线性层中，因此它不增加函数类表达能力；但在梯度流上，γ 会改变后续线性层不同方向的有效步长，形成 self-amplifying preconditioning。直观地说，某些坐标上的 γ 变大，会放大这些方向进入线性层的激活，也会相应改变这些方向的梯度更新，从而进一步强化有利方向的学习速度。这解释了为什么一个很小的向量会持续影响训练动态。

第二部分是 weight decay 的区分分析。作者把 Norm 层分成 Input-Norm 和 Output-Norm。所谓 Input-Norm，是 γ 后面立刻接线性层，典型就是 Pre-Norm；Output-Norm 则是 γ 更直接地决定输出表示本身。理论结论是两类 γ 的 wd 作用相反：对 Input-Norm γ，适度 wd 有利，因为它能诱导更平衡的动力学，控制 Hessian sharpness，提升稳定性和收敛速度；对 Output-Norm γ，wd 会压缩其表达自由度，因此有害。这个结论很实用，因为很多训练代码里对 norm 参数是否做 wd 一直没有统一共识，论文给出了结构相关而不是经验拍脑袋的答案。

第三部分是三类改进。
1）Heterogeneity：在标准 Transformer 里，一个共享的 Pre-Norm 输出会同时喂给 Q/K/V 以及其他分支，但这些分支的训练动态并不相同。作者为不同分支引入 branch-specific scale vectors，让每个分支拥有自己的预条件器，而不是共享一套 γ。
2）Placement：标准 Pre-Norm 的 γ 放在线性层输入侧，主要提供 row-wise 的预条件效果。作者提出新的放置方式，使其同时产生 row-wise 与 column-wise preconditioning，从而更充分地改善后续线性映射的优化几何。
3）Reparameterization：作者把 γ 做 magnitude-direction 形式的重参数化。理论上，这会诱导更强的各向异性预条件效果，让优化更快地沿有用方向推进，而不是被单一尺度约束。

The first stage establishes necessity and mechanism. Empirically, the authors compare standard Llama-style pretraining with and without scale vectors and show that removing gamma substantially hurts training, despite gamma accounting for a negligible parameter fraction. Theoretically, they analyze the composition Norm(x) ⊙ gamma followed by a linear map W in Pre-Norm architectures. Their key result is that gamma does not enlarge the function class in Pre-Norm settings, because its effect can be absorbed into the following linear layer. So the gain is not expressivity. Instead, gamma changes the effective optimization geometry of the subsequent linear map. It rescales coordinates before the linear transformation, which in turn changes gradient magnitudes and update speeds across directions. This creates a self-amplifying preconditioning effect: directions that become larger through gamma can receive stronger updates, which further reinforces useful learning directions.

The second stage studies weight decay. The paper distinguishes Input-Norm from Output-Norm layers depending on whether the scale vector is immediately followed by a linear map. This distinction is important. For Input-Norm scale vectors, including standard Pre-Norm layers, weight decay is beneficial because it promotes more balanced dynamics and controls Hessian sharpness, improving both speed and stability. For Output-Norm scale vectors, weight decay is harmful because those scale vectors directly affect expressivity and should not be unnecessarily constrained. This is a practical result with immediate recipe implications.

The redesign stage introduces three complementary modifications.
1) Heterogeneity: in standard Transformer blocks, one shared normalized representation feeds multiple projections such as Q, K, and V, even though these branches have different training dynamics. The paper assigns branch-specific scale vectors so each branch gets its own preconditioner.
2) Placement: in standard Pre-Norm, gamma sits on the input side of the next linear map and mainly induces row-wise preconditioning. The paper proposes alternative placements that provably induce both row-wise and column-wise preconditioning, improving optimization more fully.
3) Reparameterization: gamma is rewritten in magnitude-direction form. The analysis argues that this yields a more anisotropic preconditioner and can accelerate optimization further.

A strong aspect of the paper is the unified view. These variants are not presented as unrelated tricks. They are all interpreted as ways of reshaping the effective preconditioner seen by downstream linear layers.22:T665,The empirical section is broad and well aligned with the theory.

Second, the weight-decay ablations support the Input-Norm versus Output-Norm distinction. Applying weight decay to Input-Norm or Pre-Norm scale vectors helps, while applying it to Output-Norm scale vectors hurts.
