a:T5ed,The paper starts by testing whether the assumptions behind standard μP apply to nGPT. It uses the alignment-exponent framework of Everett et al. to measure how weight matrices and activations align during training. The key empirical observation is that nGPT does not match the alignment assumptions usually used in μP derivations. The authors therefore introduce a mid-alignment assumption: weights and activations are neither independent in the random-feature sense nor fully aligned, but partially aligned. Under this assumption, they derive a different width-scaling prescription for the learning rate and package it as a new nGPT parameterization called νGPT.

For depth transfer, the paper follows the line of work that studies residual networks with updates of the form h_{l+1}=h_l+n_layers^{-alpha_depth}F_l(h_l). The choice of alpha_depth controls the relative contribution of each residual block as depth grows. Prior work disagrees on whether alpha_depth=1/2 can provide reliable depth transfer in realistic blocks, while alpha_depth=1 is more consistently stable. νGPT adopts a depth-scaling heuristic aligned with the stable-transfer side of that literature. For token horizon, the paper finds that the optimal learning rate in nGPT scales approximately as the number of training tokens to the power -1/3, consistent with measurements reported for unnormalized Transformers.
