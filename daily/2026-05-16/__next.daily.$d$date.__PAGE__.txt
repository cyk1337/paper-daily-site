The two objectives are AR language modeling and masked diffusion modeling. To match raw information per step, byte models use an 8192-byte context and BPE models use a 1792-token context, rather than forcing the same sequence length. The MDM follows a continuous-time discrete diffusion formulation: a forward process independently replaces tokens with [MASK] according to a retention schedule, and the model learns to reconstruct the original sequence from partially masked states through iterative unmasking. The main technical value is the decomposition of the byte penalty. Part of the cost comes from longer sequences, but a separate cost comes from having to learn subword structure that BPE provides upfront. The mechanistic analysis shows emergent segmentation in AR byte models: predictive entropy aligns with BPE boundaries, meaning the model can often resolve the remaining bytes of a wordpiece once early bytes are observed. Random masking in MDM removes this reliable causal scaffold.
