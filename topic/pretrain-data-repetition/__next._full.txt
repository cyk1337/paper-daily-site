1a:T675,A common failure mode in web-pool dedup is “doc hashing and done.” Lee et al. [Lee2021Dedup] show that near-duplicates and long repeated substrings co-exist; the latter can create high-exposure fragments even when all documents are distinct. The minimum stable engineering stack is three pieces: doc-level exact hash (blocks exact duplicates), substring matching (blocks long boilerplate), and MinHash/LSH (blocks near-duplicates). The payoff is not only token efficiency, but also lower train-test leakage and verbatim reproduction risk [Lee2021Dedup].

Another failure mode is treating dedup as pure deletion. D4 is closer to budget reallocation: Tirumala et al. [Tirumala2023D4] cluster documents via embeddings to group near-duplicates, then diversify sampling within clusters to preserve coverage under fixed compute. This does not conflict with Lee et al. [Lee2021Dedup]: D4 handles redundancy from “information homogeneity,” while Lee handles the bad debt of “literal/fragment repetition.” Together they form a two-stage pipeline: first push obvious repetition down via substring/MinHash, then reallocate tokens via clustering/diversification toward broader topical coverage.

A separate ledger is required for cross-corpus repetition, which makes “per-pool dedup rates” misleading. Elazar et al. [Elazar2023WhatsInMyBigData] show overlap across public pools; if a recipe mixes multiple pools (e.g., C4 + RedPajama variants), per-pool dedup cannot give total exposure counts.
