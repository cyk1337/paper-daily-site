e:T5f6,A common illusion in long-context training is equating average sequence length with “long-dependency supervision strength.” With heavy packing, multiple samples are concatenated into long sequences; but if long samples are truncated or key supervision is fragmented, the model still sees “concatenated short dependencies,” not “coherent cross-span dependencies.” Fu et al. [Fu2024DE128K] reduce the engineering variable to an actionable knob: long-document ratio is the core variable for whether 128K learns stable behavior; when that ratio is insufficient, adding more long-sequence tokens is more likely to only raise NIAH. Xiong et al. [Xiong2023EffectiveLongContext] similarly show via long-text upsampling and continual pretraining that long samples must occupy a visible fraction of the training distribution to convert “can read long” into “can use long.” Gao et al. [Gao2024EffectiveLongCtx] further constrains the optimization target: perplexity drops may not yield long-task gains, so training changes must connect directly to task-evaluation loops. Never Lost in the Middle [NeverLostInTheMiddle2023] gives one training-signal direction: position-agnostic decompositional training to counter lost-in-the-middle, a signal closer to the “use” target than to a “fit” proxy. Operationally, a more reliable health metric set is: long-doc ratio (by domain), long-sample truncation rate, and regression curves across LV-Eval [LVEval2024] length buckets, rather than only average length and ppl.f:T691,At 1M+, cost curves and stability again dominate “which positional trick.” Xu et al. [Xu2025UltraLong] provide a reusable staged rollout: 128K→1M→2M→4M, validating short-context retention and long-task utility at each stage while injecting task signals through long-dependency SFT; mechanistically, this writes the “use” constraint into training instead of waiting for it to emerge under extrapolation. Alternatives, however, have not converged on a uniform replacement for dense long windows. Longformer [Longformer2020], Linformer [Linformer2020], LongNet [LongNet2023], Landmark Attention [LandmarkAttention2023], Compressive Transformer [CompressiveTransformer2019], and InfLLM [InfLLM2024] provide different sparsity/compression/memory tradeoffs, but Zoology [Zoology2023] warns that recall and fidelity are often the hard limits for efficient architectures, especially when tasks require cross-span composition rather than single-point retrieval. Retrieval-augmented routes (REALM [REALM2020], Atlas [Atlas2022]) are more cost-controlled for knowledge-intensive tasks; but Goldman et al.
