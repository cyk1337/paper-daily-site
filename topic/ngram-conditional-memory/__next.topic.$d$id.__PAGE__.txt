9:T886,External retrieval, trainable memory, and sparse experts modify different parts of a model. Comparing their computational benefits requires separating stored contents, addressing mechanisms, and prediction interfaces.

| Route | Address and contents | Interface and stage |
|---|---|---|
| N-gram hints | Corpus prefixes retrieve successor tokens and statistics | Trust learned during training; residual injection at inference [Brunborg2026] |
| OE | Input n-grams address trainable vectors through modulo lookup | Input-layer expansion during pretraining [OE2025] |
| Engram | Normalized tokens and multi-head hashes address vectors | Gated fusion at selected layers during pretraining [Engram2026] |
| N-Grammer | Discrete latent representations form latent n-grams | Architecture augmentation; training stage unspecified in the abstract [NGrammer2022] |
| Infini-gram | Suffix arrays retrieve corpus counts and probabilities | Statistical language modeling and neural-model augmentation [InfiniGram2024] |
| kNN-LM | Representation-space neighbors retrieve successor distributions | Inference-time probability interpolation for a pretrained model [KNN2020] |

No additional training does not eliminate indexing and search. Infini-gram’s abstract reports 5T tokens and millisecond queries: scale and query measurements, not pretraining savings.[InfiniGram2024]

Trainable memory also differs from expert computation. Memory Layers reports up to 128B memory parameters, 1T pretraining tokens, and bases up to 8B, outperforming dense models using over twice the compute and compute/parameter-matched MoEs; initialization is unspecified. PEER retrieves over a million tiny experts, but its abstract lacks complete scales and hardware protocols.[MemoryLayers2025][PEER2024]

BLT instead forms dynamic patches from next-byte entropy, changing computational granularity rather than introducing lookup memory. Its abstract reports a FLOP-controlled study up to 8B parameters and 4T training bytes. Bytes, words, and tokens are not interchangeable.[BLT2025]

PKM uses product-key lookup to add trainable memory capacity, providing an early example of sparse capacity expansion.[PKM2019]a:T812,Brunborg reports reducing time to the NanoGPT Speedrun target loss from 39.9 to 6.7 seconds on 8×H100. CPUs construct a 37B-token hint corpus while GPUs train. Successor-token embeddings for 6-, 10-, and 20-grams are combined with learned trust information associated with length, count, and purity, then injected at three residual-stream locations. The 39.9-second baseline already contains a large hashed n-gram table. This compares two memory-equipped systems rather than measuring the net benefit of adding memory to a memory-free model.[Brunborg2026]

For one fixed model trained on 70.8M tokens, expanding the evaluation hint corpus from 75M to 37B tokens lowers loss from 3.7219 to 3.2739, compared with 3.7454 without hints. This is an external-data-access curve, not a model-scaling curve. Unique corpora accessible to the backbone and index are not matched, and processed training tokens do not measure unique data.[Brunborg2026]

The 3.1-second variant uses different construction conditions: it reads validation prefixes before selectively counting their continuations in training text. Hint contents remain training-derived, but construction depends on evaluation inputs, and eligibility under the rules remains conditional. The author gives complexity of $O(N_{\mathrm{train}}N_{\mathrm{val}})$, versus $O(N_{\mathrm{train}}+N_{\mathrm{val}})$ for the main method, where the two quantities denote training and validation sizes. This variant cannot replace the 6.7-second main result.[Brunborg2026]

Twenty-gram matches cover 10.7% of validation tokens; the 97.7% successor accuracy is conditional on a match. After fuzzy document deduplication, the reported time remains roughly 10 seconds and coverage is 6.6%. Complete-document copying therefore does not explain all gains, but long-passage overlap remains relevant. Large hint corpora also induce dependence: removing hints after equal-step training leaves a slightly worse backbone. Assessing compute substitution consequently requires matched, unmatched, and memory-removal results.[Brunborg2026]b:T606,OE preserves the base tokenizer and output vocabulary. It maps hierarchical input n-grams to trainable tables and adds their representations through low-dimensional slices and projections. OD expands output supervision, while OT combines both approaches. The paper treats MTP as an approximation to OD, not OE. Different small-model behavior when expanding encoder and decoder vocabularies in CFG experiments supports studying input capacity separately from output supervision.[OE2025]

Natural-language experiments train 151M, 400M, and 1B dense backbones from scratch. The first two process 400B training tokens; the 1B model processes up to 1T. At the shared 400B-token point, the 400M OE-12.8M model approaches the 1B baseline’s loss. The 400M label excludes added embeddings. In 500B-token MoE experiments, OE embeddings contain 13.1B and 26.3B parameters. The reported 5.7× figure describes loss-convergence alignment, not guaranteed end-to-end wall-clock acceleration.[OE2025]

The log-linear trend in input capacity holds within the tested range. Row sharding and sparse communication keep throughput degradation below 5% in a particular implementation, whereas unoptimized FSDP loses 25% and encounters memory limits. The mechanistic advantage is expanded input representation with relatively little additional arithmetic; realized gains still depend on memory access and implementation. Rare-row training, collision effects, and rankings against the baseline over longer horizons require testing before further expansion.[OE2025]c:T879,Engram retrieves trainable vectors through normalized tokens and multi-head hashes, gates them with hidden states, and fuses them at selected layers through lightweight convolution while retaining standard input/output embeddings. “Static” does not mean untrained. Deterministic addressing supports prefetching, but gating, projections, and transfers still cost resources.[Engram2026]

At $2\times10^{20}$/$6\times10^{20}$ FLOPs, allocation experiments fix approximately 5.7B/568M and 9.9B/993M total/active parameters, with sparsity near 10; totals exclude vocabulary embeddings and the LM head. Allocating approximately 20%–25% of inactive capacity to memory performs best, while further allocation displaces conditional computation. This is a U-shaped relationship within two budgets, not a universal fraction.[Engram2026]

The larger pretraining comparison shares 262B tokens in identical order and 3.8B active parameters. Engram/MoE-27B both have 26.7B total parameters, including 5.7B memory parameters in Engram. BBH improves from 50.9 to 55.9 at 3-shot and HumanEval from 37.8 to 40.8 at 0-shot Pass@1; the latter falls to 38.4 for the 40B model. The abstract’s MMLU +3.4 conflicts with 57.4→60.4 in the v2 table and remains unresolved. Nonmonotonic gains do not establish cross-scale rank reversal; reduced early-layer reconstruction remains the authors’ interpretation.[Engram2026]

Tokenizer-Agnostic Engram uses polynomial hashing and shared embeddings across n. Its abstract reports comparable performance and hash equivalence for byte-equivalent sequences, not collision freedom or lossless backbone transfer; scale, stage, and budget remain unspecified.[TokenizerEngram2026]

TF-Engram is a train-free, post-hoc inference system using external phrase memory. Qwen3-0.6B’s average score rises from 57.6 to 59.4, outperforming the frozen backbone and parameter-matched LoRA; the abstract omits tasks, variance, corpus size, and complete budgets. GPU–DRAM–SSD storage and prefetching improve memory access, but parameter matching is not total-cost matching, and added content prevents attributing all gains to storage design.[TFEngram2026]d:T65b,OE and Engram research models explicitly implement their mechanisms; products require a distinction between explicit adoption, related mechanisms, and unknowns. Mentioning memory or n-grams is insufficient without disclosure of the target module’s contents, interface, and training stage. Three contrasts bound the conclusions.[OE2025][Engram2026]

First, DeepSeek V4’s MoE, MTP, and compressed attention do not establish OE or Engram deployment. ProphetNet/Engram citations and future-work discussion are not adoption evidence; research connections cannot substitute for implementation disclosure.[DeepSeekV4_2026]

Second, preprocessing n-grams differ from model lookup memory. Llama 3 uses them for repetition filtering, resampling, and evaluation; Nemotron 3.5 uses 8-/13-grams for SFT/RL decontamination. These identify processing or evaluation procedures, not pretraining memory modules. Nemotron’s Mamba-Transformer/MoE and MTP do not close that gap.[Llama3_2024][Nemotron35Report]

Third, Qwen3.8 Max discloses a Qwen3.5 architectural foundation and 2.4T total/95B active parameters, but the 27B card’s Gated DeltaNet/Gated Attention combination cannot be transferred into a complete Max layer specification. Neither confirms OE or Engram. TF-Engram’s post-hoc inference experiment on Qwen3-0.6B likewise does not establish native Qwen3.8 adoption.[Qwen38Blog][Qwen38Card][Qwen38Repo][TFEngram2026]

Other models remain in the separate inventory: new releases, shared laboratories, general data disclosures, and post-training improvements do not establish target-memory adoption; unknown does not mean absent.e:T8db,Judge compute substitution by total cost to target quality, not FLOPs alone. Measure training arithmetic, GPU/CPU hours, index construction and updates, storage, communication, energy, and serving latency separately. Parallelism shortens the critical path without eliminating consumption. Brunborg’s 352 GiB index and 377 GiB construction arena cannot be summed into peak memory without lifecycle measurements.[Brunborg2026] OE/Engram also require table weights, optimizer states, gradient communication, and host transfers. Reported offloading overhead below 3% for a 100B-parameter table does not generalize to arbitrary cold caches or concurrency.[OE2025][Engram2026]

The cleanest proposed primary comparison restricts the baseline and training-time memory to text already processed by the backbone. Measure reuse through deduplicated held-out loss and total cost to target loss, separately recording consumed tokens, unique data, repetition, and total/active parameters. Evaluate post-hoc inference systems separately with fixed backbone and content, including representation extraction, indexing, and serving, so additional information is not confused with architectural gains.

Develop with approximately 150M, 400M, and 1B active backbones, holding out approximately 3B and, resources permitting, another family; use three horizons per scale. Fix tokenizer, context, and data order, equalize tuning allowances, and report fixed-hyperparameter and fairly tuned results. Freeze configurations and predictions before testing held-out rankings. This is a prospective design; checkpoints from one run or family are not independent replications.

Separate backbone, index, and test data by document, domain, and time; exact decontamination can miss paraphrased leakage.[DeepSeekR1_2025] Measure matched/unmatched loss, tail performance, and novel compositions; probe dependence with misleading hints, stale corpora, and lookup failures. Separately report cold/warm caches, prefill/decode, concurrency, tail latency, and amortized serving volume. Match SFT/RL data, updates, and generation/verification budgets; fix agent scaffolds and include tool costs. Predefine loss of cost advantage, worse out-of-domain performance, or unmet serving constraints as failures.f:T5d3,Scalable memory offers concrete ways to reallocate pretraining resources: OE expands input capacity, Engram allocates budgets between memory and conditional computation, and external hints exploit reusable corpora to improve prediction.[OE2025][Engram2026][Brunborg2026] These findings suggest research priorities: test hints when recurring text can be reused, OE when local representations need more capacity, and Engram allocation in MoE systems that can accommodate sharding and transfers. This is an experimental ordering, not a general deployment recommendation.

The central judgment is whether reads are cheaper than the computation they replace under explicit data-access and serving constraints. Rankings by loss, downstream capability, and total cost require separate validation. There is no unified substitution rate, nor an established guarantee that gains persist across families or through post-training. Four open questions bear most directly on budget decisions:

- After matching unique-corpus access and isolating long-passage overlap, how much cost-to-target improvement remains for external hints?
- How do table capacity, training horizon, repetition, and sparsity jointly change OE/Engram rankings across scales?
- Under cold caches, small batches, and high concurrency, how much transfer can prefetching hide, and what serving volume amortizes construction?
