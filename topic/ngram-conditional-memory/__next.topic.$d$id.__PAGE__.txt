Tokenizer-Agnostic Engram treats n-grams as a way to sample byte sequences across tokens, replaces XOR hashing with general polynomial hashing, and shares an embedding space across n. It reports comparable performance and hash equivalence for byte-equivalent token sequences. This addresses tokenizer coupling, not collision freedom, lossless transfer between arbitrary backbones, or train-free embedding contents. Model scale, training stage and budget, and detailed evaluation are not established by the abstract.[TokenizerEngram2026]

TF-Engram instead constructs static phrase-semantic memory offline from external corpora, injects it into hidden states, and uses GPU–DRAM–SSD storage with Early-Exit Guided Predictive Prefetching to reduce external-access penalties. It is a train-free post-hoc memory/inference system, not from-scratch pretraining. On Qwen3-0.6B, its abstract reports average downstream scores rising from 57.6 to 59.4 and outperforming the frozen backbone and parameter-matched LoRA. The task suite, variance, corpus size, and complete budgets are not supplied. Parameter matching does not match total training, construction, or inference costs, and a 0.6B result cannot establish frontier-scale effectiveness.[TFEngram2026]

Relative to trainable-memory foundations, the former changes addressing conventions while retaining an embedding approach; the latter introduces offline-built static content into an existing model. TF-Engram reports reduced GPU memory demand with SSD storage and recovery of much of the throughput loss through prefetching. Train-free still entails corpus processing, construction, storage, and transfer costs. Because the intervention also introduces external content, a frozen-backbone comparison alone cannot attribute gains solely to better architecture.[TFEngram2026]

A proposed test should separate training and post-hoc tracks. The training track should compare memory layers, experts, and baselines across at least three scales or budgets, with separate matched-FLOP and matched-wall-clock/hardware-budget comparisons. Fix underlying corpora, unique data, repetition, and training horizon; record method-specific token/byte exposure and total/active parameters, and equalize tuning budgets. Hold out a larger scale or family for extrapolation; checkpoints from one run are not independent replicates. The post-hoc track should fix backbone, external corpus, and memory content while comparing GPU residency, hierarchical storage, and disabled prefetching. Separately control content access so that external-knowledge gains are not attributed to storage design.

Across tracks, measure pretraining loss, factual and long-tail tasks, and downstream performance under equal post-training budgets separately. Agent evaluations must fix scaffolds, context, decoding, and generation/verification budgets. Tokenizer-transfer tests should measure both address agreement and transferred quality; systems tests should report construction costs, GPU/DRAM/SSD occupancy, batch size, warm/cold cache states, latency, and throughput. Test address compatibility with quality degradation, SSD bandwidth limits, low rare-phrase hit rates, and ranking reversals on larger backbones or after post-training.
