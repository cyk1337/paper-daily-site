17:T5fb,An executable pricing rule: map each architecture change to (1) per-token inference bill (KV-cache capacity and bandwidth), (2) training-stability risk (loss-spike probability and debugging time), and (3) migration cost (kernel/serving/tooling work). For dense decoder-only ≤70B, GQA is often the low-risk default: KV-cache drops roughly linearly with KV heads, while quality degradation tends to accelerate mainly near the MQA endpoint (KV heads=1), making h_kv≈h/8 a sensible first sweep point [Ainslie2023GQA][Shazeer2019MQA]. For long context (≥128K), all-global attention hits memory and bandwidth before FLOPs; Gemma 3 reports ~4× lower inference memory at 128K with a 5:1 local/global interleaving recipe, and Mistral 7B shows SWA + rolling cache is deployable [Gemma3Report][Mistral2023]. At ≥200B or MoE scale, MLA reduces cache to ~1/7 with quality near GQA, but requires a latent reconstruction path and serving-stack migration, best treated as an option triggered by high-concurrency long-context + MoE constraints [DeepSeekV2]. On stability, Wortsman et al. mechanize loss spikes via attention-logit variance and output-norm growth, making QK-Norm a targeted component; evidence for sandwich norm remains more correlational than causal [Wortsman2023Instabilities][Gemma3Report]. Scaling is not only from-scratch: SOLAR and LLaMA Pro suggest depth-up-scaling / block expansion can be cheaper with a stable base, but public negative results and boundary conditions are still scarce [Kim2023Solar][LLaMAPro2024].18:T687,Architectural ``improvements'' in the LLM era have mostly followed one budget axis: KV-cache and bandwidth. Vanilla [Vaswani'17] / GPT-2 assigns independent K/V to every head, costing ~256 KB / token of KV and quickly dominating serving cost in long context; [Shazeer2019MQA] shares one KV head across all query heads to compress cache to 1/H; [Ainslie2023GQA] introduced GQA as a continuous interpolation between MHA and MQA (8 groups in practice), which became the default in Llama-2/3, Qwen2.5, and Gemma [LLaMA2023][Qwen2_5Report2024][Gemma2024]. From 2023, a parallel mainline emerged around ``cutting bandwidth'': [Mistral2023] uses sliding-window attention to confine each layer to a local ~4K window, and [AttentionSinks2023] showed that window + always-on sink tokens are required for stable extrapolation; [Gemma3Report] gives an engineerable compromise: 5 local layers + 1 global layer (5:1 interleaving), preserving long-range reasoning quality while compressing global bandwidth ~6x. Normalization and positional encoding show the same quiet convergence: RMSNorm has replaced LayerNorm [RMSNorm2019], RoPE has replaced absolute / sinusoidal [RoFormer2021], and both are now defaults in open-source stacks. 1d:T5ff,Evaluating attention changes under a “fixed KV-cache budget” better matches real deployment. GQA’s key property is not a new structure but continuous control: it reduces KV heads from h to a smaller h_kv and supports uptraining from MHA checkpoints, turning the quality–cache curve into a sweepable hyperparameter instead of a one-shot bet [Ainslie2023GQA]. MQA is the endpoint: KV heads=1 gives maximum compression and decode gains, but more often triggers quality regression, making it a useful lower-bound calibration point for the degradation curve [Shazeer2019MQA][Ainslie2023GQA].

MLA uses a different logic: it does not reduce head count; it stores K/V as low-rank latents and reconstructs per-head K/V on demand. DeepSeek-AI [DeepSeekV2] reports ~7× cache reduction with quality near GQA, indicating that for MoE or ≥200B serving bottlenecks, latent KV can be more cost-effective than further shrinking h_kv; the cost is a reconstruction path, kernel adaptation, and serving-stack complexity that public benchmarks often hide [DeepSeekV2]. QCQA further suggests that a uniform GQA grouping ratio is not necessarily optimal: under a fixed cache budget, allocating capacity by layer/head can preserve quality more reliably [QCQA2024].

The gap is equally clear: public evidence rarely reports head-to-head GQA vs MQA vs MHA under the same model, data, KV-cache size, and kernel/serving setup. Without that control, discussions often misread training-loss deltas as deployment-cost deltas [Ainslie2023GQA][DeepSeekV2].1e:T601,Long-context debates often reduce to “supports 128K/1M”, but deployment first has to price decode-time KV-cache traffic: how much KV is read/written per token, and whether HBM/PCIe/NVLink bandwidth is the limiter. Gemma 3 [Gemma3Report] provides a reproducible default with 5:1 local/global interleaving: a small number of global layers handle cross-segment routing, while most layers use local attention to bound per-step KV traffic within a window, reporting ~4× lower inference memory at 128K [Gemma3Report]. Mistral 7B [Mistral2023] engineers SWA with a rolling cache, showing that “windowing + cache rolling” can run in real serving.

Shared assumption: not every layer needs global routing; treating global-layer density as a budget variable is easier to control than making every layer all-global [Gemma3Report][Mistral2023]. In contrast, longer RoPE extrapolation alone does not reduce KV traffic; it is a prerequisite for preventing positional geometry from failing first [RoFormer2021]. Attention sinks [AttentionSinks2023] gives a behavioral prior for streaming chat: sink tokens stabilize attention patterns, making cache management and approximation strategies less likely to trigger catastrophic forgetting.

Evaluation must be stricter: Lost in the Middle [LostInTheMiddle2023] shows that long-context backbones should not be judged only by max length or perplexity; they must also reliably retrieve mid-context information in long sequences. Otherwise, local/global or SWA budgeting may erase usable information.1f:T727,Whether stability components should become defaults depends on whether they map to observable mechanisms and reproducible experiments.
