17:T488,Optimizer selection in 2026 is best framed as a 3-axis decision—scale (≤30B vs ≥70B), memory (state/communication budget), and tuning budget (how many sweeps you can afford)—rather than “who wins.” Under fixed hyperparameter-search budgets and aligned schedule families, many reported optimizer gaps shrink and rank flips are common; this is why AdamW remains a default in practice: reusable recipes, predictable failure modes, and μP-style learning-rate transfer that turns width scaling from “retune from scratch” into “light calibration.” Muon narrows near-second-order benefits to hidden 2D weights while routing embeddings/norms/heads back to AdamW, isolating risk via hybrid routing. SOAP follows the Shampoo Kronecker-preconditioning path and reduces stability/HP burden toward deployability, but public ≥7B head-to-heads and μP-style LR transfer for second-order methods are still missing. Under VRAM pressure, start with low-state methods that do not change the training loop (e.g., Apollo / Adam-mini-like), then consider gradient-representation methods like GaLore, evaluating quality-per-memory and loop complexity together.18:T5e3,Actionable recommendation: AdamW remains the 2026 default for “transferable tuning + mature ecosystem,” not pointwise peak performance. AlgoPerf [Dahl2023AlgoPerf] and Agarwal et al. [Agarwal2020LRConfound] show that without fixed HP-search budgets and aligned LR schedule families, optimizer gaps often shrink to about half of what is reported and rank flips are common—many “wins” are tuning-effort comparisons. For ≥70B dense production, get μP LR transfer working first, then spend ≤10% budget on calibration sweeps: Lingle [Lingle2024muPTransfer] and Noci et al. [Noci2024SuperConsistency] support widthwise LR transfer that directly saves sweep cost. For new ≤30B pretrains, the most deployable bet is Muon with hybrid routing: Newton–Schulz orthogonalized updates on hidden 2D weights, AdamW on everything else, isolating risk; public evidence is still skewed toward speedruns/smaller scales, and ≥7B head-to-heads remain a gap [Jordan2024Muon]. The second-order path is Shampoo→SOAP: SOAP runs Adam in Shampoo’s eigenbasis, reduces extra HPs to one, and reaches near-AdamW wall-clock while improving loss at 360M–1.3B; but ≥7B public evidence and μP-style LR transfer for second-order are still thin [Gupta2018Shampoo][Vyas2024SOAP]. Under VRAM pressure, start with low-state methods that don’t change the loop (APOLLO), then consider gradient-representation methods like GaLore and evaluate quality-per-memory and complexity together [Zhu2024Apollo][Zhao2024GaLore].19:T77a,LLM 训练的 optimizer 演进可以拆成三段：第一段是 first-order 自适应法的工程化 — [Kingma2014Adam] 的 Adam 把 RMSProp + 动量统一,[AdamW2017] 把 weight decay 与 LR 解耦解决了 wd↔LR 的混淆,这套到 2017 年定型,十年里仍是默认。第二段是二阶探索 — [Gupta2018Shampoo] 用 Kronecker 矩阵把曲率信息以 matmul 形式带进训练,但工程化代价是 ≥4 个新 HP 与昂贵的预条件运算,直到 [Shi2023DistShampoo] 把分布式实现做出来才算可生产化;Shampoo 的真正继承者是 [Vyas2024SOAP],把额外 HP 从 4 压到 1,在 360M-1.3B 的报告里 wall-clock 接近 AdamW、loss 更低,把二阶信号变成 ROI 可控的选项。

第三段是 2024 之后的“分维度优化”：[Jordan2024Muon] 不再追求“给所有参数都做二阶”,只对 hidden 的 2D 权重做 Newton-Schulz 正交化更新,embedding/norm/head 仍用 AdamW；这把“二阶 vs 一阶”的辩论转成“在哪些张量切面上做二阶”的工程问题。并行的另一支是显存约束：[Zhu2024Apollo] 用 per-tensor 标量替代 per-param 二阶矩,在 7B/13B 报告 SGD-like 显存 + AdamW-level 性能,[Zhao2024GaLore] 走低秩状态路径；二者都是“不改训练循环、只换 state 形态”的 drop-in。再外一层是协议层：[Lingle2024muPTransfer] 把 [Yang2023TensorProgramsIVb] 的 muP 公式工程化,通常可把 sweep cost 砍到原来 ~30%；[Dahl2023AlgoPerf] / [Nado2021RealityCheck] 让“谁更强”可被审计 — 在固定 HP 搜索预算 + 对齐 schedule 家族下,大量报告的优势会缩小到原差距的约一半,且存在 rank flip。今天选 optimizer 的真问题不是 “Muon 还是 SOAP”,而是 “规模 × 显存 × 调参预算”三轴上的可审计取舍 — 本节用 figure 0.2 把这条 decision flow 画出来,把后面 §1-§4 的取舍标准固化成 release-time checklist。1a:T78d,LLM optimizer evolution splits into three eras. The first engineered first-order adaptive methods: [Kingma2014Adam]'s Adam unified RMSProp + momentum; [AdamW2017] decoupled weight decay from LR, removing the wd<->LR confound; the recipe ossified by 2017 and remains the default a decade later. The second was second-order exploration: [Gupta2018Shampoo] brought curvature into training via Kronecker matmul, at the cost of >=4 new HPs and expensive preconditioning; [Shi2023DistShampoo] made the distributed implementation production-ready. Shampoo's true successor is [Vyas2024SOAP], which shrank the extra HPs from 4 to 1 and reported wall-clock close to AdamW with lower loss at 360M-1.3B, turning the second-order signal into an ROI-tunable option.

The third era, post-2024, is per-dimension optimization. [Jordan2024Muon] dropped the ambition of ``second order for everything'' and applied Newton-Schulz orthogonalization only to hidden 2D weights, leaving embedding/norm/head on AdamW; this reframed ``second vs first order'' as ``which tensor slice do we second-order''. A parallel line is memory: [Zhu2024Apollo] replaced per-parameter second moments with per-tensor scalars and reported SGD-like memory + AdamW-level performance at 7B/13B; [Zhao2024GaLore] used low-rank state. Both are drop-in changes that keep the training loop intact. On top, a protocol layer matured: [Lingle2024muPTransfer] productionized [Yang2023TensorProgramsIVb]'s muP, typically cutting sweep cost to ~30%; [Dahl2023AlgoPerf] / [Nado2021RealityCheck] made ``who is better'' auditable -- with HP search budget aligned and schedule family fixed, many reported advantages shrink to about half the original gap and rank flips are common. 1c:T498,The most common engineering error is reading “tuning effort differences” as “algorithm differences.” AlgoPerf makes fixed HP-search budgets a protocol requirement: within the same task, comparators must share trial count, tunable HP set, and the same early-stop/eval rules; otherwise gaps are not attributable [Dahl2023AlgoPerf]. 1e:T4e9,Treating AdamW as the default is often misread as AdamW being best in every setting. The more accurate mechanism is this: once scale reaches ≥70B, the opportunity cost of a sweep is high, and reusing historical tuning usually matters more than pointwise optimality. Lingle’s μP LR-transfer evidence shows that, under μP, learning rates transfer better across widths; this supports tuning LR on a small model and lightly calibrating on the large model instead of sweeping from scratch [Lingle2024muPTransfer]. Noci et al. provide compatible evidence from landscape consistency: when training dynamics are more consistent, LR transfer is more likely to hold [Noci2024SuperConsistency]. This reinforces AdamW’s engineering advantages: simple update rules and decoupled weight decay make regularization behavior more controllable, and recipes easier to reuse across model families [AdamW2017]. By contrast, any optimizer that adds large state, extra communication paths, or more complex numerical operators must answer two production questions even if it achieves lower loss at mid-scale: do LR/HPs transfer, and are failure modes predictable? If the answers are unclear, the gain is easily erased by 1–2 extra sweeps or a single instability rollback.1f:T4f4,Muon’s key move is not “replacing AdamW,” but casting second-/near-second-order updates as parameter partitioning: apply Newton–Schulz orthogonalized updates only to hidden 2D weights (typically linear-layer matrices), and route sensitive parameters (embeddings/norms/heads) explicitly back to AdamW [Jordan2024Muon]. This matches a testable engineering hypothesis: poorly conditioned, strongly coupled matrix parameters need better geometry most, while embeddings/norms/heads are more numerically sensitive and distribution-shift prone; keeping AdamW reduces the range of failure modes. Compared with moving the whole model to a new optimizer, hybrid routing isolates risk: if the Muon subset becomes unstable, rollback can route back or shrink coverage. Compared with SOAP/Shampoo, Muon trades fuller Kronecker structure for a cheaper approximation to preconditioning, prioritizing wall-clock and fewer tuning iterations. The gap is equally clear: public evidence skews toward speedruns/smaller scales; matched-schedule + matched-budget head-to-heads on dense 7B–70B LLM pretraining are still missing. Until that evidence exists, positioning Muon as a “low-risk candidate for new ≤30B pretrains” is more robust than calling it “the next default.”20:T59a,Whether second-order is worth it should be split into three questions: is the geometry better, can the system carry it, and is the tuning dimensionality controllable. Shampoo uses Kronecker-structured preconditioning across tensor dimensions to approximate more geometry-correct updates at tolerable O(d_in^2+d_out^2) costs [Gupta2018Shampoo]. In practice, the pain points concentrate in stability and grafting: how the preconditioned step scale combines with momentum/adaptivity determines whether many ad-hoc rules are needed. SOAP’s key move is to move updates into Shampoo’s eigenbasis and let Adam’s momentum/adaptivity “own” scale and stability, reducing extra HPs to one and reporting near-AdamW wall-clock with lower loss at 360M–1.3B [Vyas2024SOAP]. On the systems side, Shi et al. provide a distributed PyTorch Shampoo implementation, showing communication and decomposition frequency are engineering knobs rather than abstract objections [Shi2023DistShampoo]. Quantization (4-bit Shampoo) further lowers the state barrier, but introduces the tradeoff between quantization error and update frequency [Wang2024FourBitShampoo]. The hardest remaining gaps are (1) public ≥7B dense LLM head-to-heads under matched budgets and matched schedules, and (2) whether μP-style LR transfer holds for second-order; if it does not, production cost gets eaten by sweeps [Ishikawa2023SecondOrderParam][Lingle2024muPTransfer].21:T818,可执行清单（2026 选型与落地）：
