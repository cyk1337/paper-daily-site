4:T46b,A usable form of downstream scaling laws is now fairly clear: compute should not be the only x-axis, and jumps on exact-match should not be read as direct evidence that an ability suddenly appears. A safer workflow decomposes discrete acceptance metrics into continuous surrogates such as answer log-likelihood, token-level Brier, or BPB-of-correct-completion, then aligns different recipes by pretraining loss or a low-dimensional capability index. This does not remove every threshold task, but it turns “unpredictable” into an uncertainty-band problem. Two engineering routes are available. A model ladder trains a small set of internal models to fit task curves under the target recipe. Observational scaling regresses over public models to infer a capability manifold. The former fits architecture, mid-train, and pre-post-training acceptance decisions; the latter fits data-mix triage and competitor mapping. The fragile cases are code execution, multi-step math, and agentic tasks, where the final metric may contain a real threshold. Continuous surrogates are early signals there, not replacements for acceptance tests.5:T435,The core evidence in Wei et al. [Wei2022Emergent] comes from BIG-Bench-style discrete acceptance: small models have near-zero exact-match, then larger models receive non-zero scores. BIG-Bench [BIGBench2022] is not the problem; the reading of the plot is. The mechanism by which Schaeffer et al. [Schaeffer2023Mirage] challenges Wei et al. [Wei2022Emergent] is simple: the model’s probability on the correct answer can rise smoothly from 0.01 to 0.20, while sampled exact-match still reads as zero for a long time; after the first hit, the curve looks like a jump. Du et al. [Du2024LossPerspective] adds a second correction: compute misaligns architectures, token budgets, and sparse/dense settings, because equal FLOPs do not imply equal learning state. A safer workflow has two steps: replace the metric with answer log-likelihood, token-level Brier, or BPB-of-correct-completion; then replace the x-axis with pretraining loss.
