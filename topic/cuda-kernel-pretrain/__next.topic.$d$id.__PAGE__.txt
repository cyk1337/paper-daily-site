d:T786,FlashAttention’s core move is to make the attention IO ledger explicit: do not materialize the N×N attention matrix in HBM; stream tiles through SMEM instead, and use online softmax to preserve exactness [Dao2022FlashAttention]. FlashAttention-2 further shows that performance differences come mainly from work partitioning and parallelism reshaping, not “same compute graph, different implementation” [Dao2023FlashAttention2]. Together, they move attention optimization from the operator level to the dataflow level: tile sizes, register/SMEM pressure, and sync-point placement determine whether the bottleneck is moving HBM bytes or feeding tensor cores.

The cost of IO-awareness is a more complex numeric path: tiled reductions, reordered accumulation, and online softmax updates produce rounding behavior that differs from reference implementations. The point of Golden et al. [Golden2024FAStability] is not merely that “errors exist,” but that errors enter training state: optimizer momentum/second-moment stats, gradient clipping thresholds, and mixed-precision scale updates can amplify deviations across steps, eventually surfacing as loss spikes or training aborts. A steadier engineering boundary is: once fused attention enters the pretraining critical path, “numeric regression” must be gated at the same level as tokens/s—at minimum tracking loss-spike rates, gradient-norm distributions, and attention-output error proxies (e.g., sampled relative error vs an unfused reference) [Golden2024FAStability].

This also explains why Camp B’s “high-level kernel generation” path must supply train-time evidence: FlexAttention provides a generation/optimization space [Dong2024FlexAttention], but if generated kernels alter numeric paths, they still need stability regression and diagnostic hooks comparable to FlashAttention, not just speed curves [Dong2024FlexAttention][Golden2024FAStability].e:T768,Once decode KPIs are expressed as bytes/token, debates collapse onto quantities that can be settled: dense MHA’s KV-cache traffic grows linearly with head count, while decoding is often memory-bound; therefore “reduce KV traffic” is more direct than “make attention compute faster” [Shazeer2019MQA]. MQA’s mechanism is simple: multiple query heads share the same K/V heads, compressing the KV footprint from O(H) to O(1) relative to head count [Shazeer2019MQA]. GQA trades quality against bandwidth: share K/V within groups, reduce traffic, and avoid collapsing expressivity to a single KV head [Ainslie2023GQA].

MLA pushes the same line harder: KV is no longer an “explicit per-head cache,” but a low-rank latent representation projected back into per-head computation [DeepSeek2024V2]. The point of this parameterization is to write memory-hierarchy constraints directly into the model: when KV cache is the dominant HBM stream, latent dimension becomes a controllable bytes/token knob. DeepSeek-V3 further describes MoE (controls activated parameters per token) and MLA (controls decode bytes/token) under the same cost objective, so “architecture choice” and “system throughput target” no longer live in separate ledgers [DeepSeek2024V3].

A necessary counterexample is that long-context capability is not equivalent to “faster attention.” PI reaches 32K context with minimal finetuning [Chen2023PI], and YaRN plus continual pretraining also push long context through training strategy [Peng2023YaRN][Xiong2023LongContextScaling]. A steadier reading splits the problem into two parts: whether the model can use longer context (position/training strategy dominated), and whether the target batch/throughput can afford longer context (bytes/token and IO dataflow dominated).
