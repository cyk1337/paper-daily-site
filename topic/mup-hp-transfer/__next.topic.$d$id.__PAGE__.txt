9:T61f,The central question is not which method is universally correct, but which variables each method closes over and under which recipe changes it breaks. µP turns width-wise transfer into testable invariants of learning dynamics, Complete-P extends those rules to modern components such as QK-Norm, tied embeddings, and residual scaling, and u-µP adds precision and RMS constraints to the transfer object [Yang2022muP][Cerebras2024CompleteP][Blake2024UMUP]. A different line treats HP transfer as statistical fitting: under a fixed SP recipe, stable data distribution, and nearly unchanged scheduler, empirical formulas or joint scaling laws provide starting points for LR, batch size, and token:param, followed by small corrective sweeps [Dey2023CerebrasGPT][Bi2024DeepSeekLLM][Hoffmann2022Chinchilla]. A third line hands the residual error to cost-aware BO such as CARBS, using limited proxy budgets to search wd, β₂, dropout, and other variables outside µP’s closure [Fetterman2023TuneAsYouScale][Lee2020CostAwareBO]. A more reliable engineering decomposition is layered: parameterization defines invariants and is accepted only after coord check / pre-activation RMS overlap; formulas provide closed-form starts for a fixed recipe; local BO patches wd, β₂, and precision-stability blind spots. The common failure is not the absence of theory, but misattributing recipe drift, depth drift, fp8 numeric limits, or AdamW weight decay effects to “non-transferable LR” [Bordelon2023DepthwiseTransfer][Kosson2025WDMoreThanMuP][Wortsman2023ProxyInstability].a:T6a7,There is no single default for HP transfer in 2026. A more reliable setup has three layers. Layer 1 is parameterization: µP gives the conditions for zero-shot width transfer, Complete-P repairs modern Transformer components, and u-µP extends the transfer object to precision and RMS constraints; acceptance at this layer is not “the loss looks similar,” but whether coord checks or pre-activation RMS overlap across widths and learning rates [Yang2022muP][Cerebras2024CompleteP][Blake2024UMUP]. Layer 2 is empirical formulas and joint scaling laws: when the SP recipe, data distribution, and scheduler stay nearly fixed, Cerebras/DeepSeek/Chinchilla-style formulas provide good starts for LR, batch size, and token:param, often needing only 1–2 small corrective sweeps [Dey2023CerebrasGPT][Bi2024DeepSeekLLM][Hoffmann2022Chinchilla]. Layer 3 is local automation: wd, β₂, dropout, EMA, and similar variables are outside µP’s closure and are not always robustly extrapolated by formulas, so CARBS or cost-aware BO is a better fit, often within roughly 10–20 proxy runs [Fetterman2023TuneAsYouScale][Lee2020CostAwareBO]. The most common misread is to transfer LR alone: under AdamW, wd can dominate transfer error more than nominal LR, and β₂ together with batch size shifts the stability boundary [Kosson2025WDMoreThanMuP][Wang2024AdamWWeightDecayScaling][Cohen2022EdgeStability]. For greenfield stacks, start with Complete-P; for mature SP stacks, start with formulas plus small sweeps; in both cases, do not treat proxies as final arbiters because depth, fp8, and large-scale instabilities may not show up on small models [Bordelon2023DepthwiseTransfer][Wortsman2023ProxyInstability].b:T640,A more reliable reading is that µP is not a button for “automatically transferring all hyperparameters.” It first defines a network family in which the scales of key intermediate quantities remain comparable as width changes; only under that condition do zero-shot transfer of base LR and init scale make sense [Yang2022muP]. Cerebras Research [Cerebras2024CompleteP] decomposes the common mismatch points of original µP on modern Transformers: QK-Norm, tied embeddings, residual scaling, and norm placement all break the original scaling relations, so transfer failure is often a module-scaling error before it is a sweep-size problem. Dey et al. [Dey2025DontBeLazy] and Bordelon et al. [Bordelon2023DepthwiseTransfer] further show that depth needs explicit modeling; Jelassi et al. [Jelassi2023DepthDependence] counters the shortcut “if width works, depth follows,” because the µP learning-rate scale itself can depend on depth. Precision is not a mere implementation detail either. Blake et al. [Blake2024UMUP] and Blake et al. [Blake2024UMUPv2] merge unit scaling with µP and require activation, gradient, and weight RMS to stay in controlled ranges; Micikevicius et al. [Micikevicius2022FP8] and Narayan et al. [Narayan2025MunitScaling] show that fp8 numeric range can itself rewrite the stability boundary. The practical role of parameterization is therefore to define invariants and an acceptance protocol, not to replace all later HP choices. If coord checks or pre-activation RMS do not overlap, scaling up compute only amplifies the mistake [Yang2020TP3][Cerebras2024CompleteP].c:T66c,The shared assumption behind the empirical-formula route is actually quite explicit: the proxy and target scale share the same recipe, with limited changes in data distribution, optimizer, scheduler, and model shape, so the dependence of loss on model size, tokens, batch size, and LR can be fit locally by a low-dimensional function [Kaplan2020Scaling][Hoffmann2022Chinchilla][McCandlish2018LargeBatch]. Dey et al. [Dey2023CerebrasGPT] apply this idea to a stable open pretraining recipe; DeepSeek-AI [Bi2024DeepSeekLLM] goes further by fitting LR and batch size jointly and solving target-scale HPs directly from proxy runs. Hägele et al. [Hagele2024BeyondFixedDuration], Tissue et al. [Tissue2024LRAnnealingScaling], Zhang et al. [Zhang2024CriticalBatchSize], and Shen et al. [Shen2024PowerScheduler] all extend this line by explicitly modeling training duration, annealing, critical batch size, and scheduler structure. The problem is that the extrapolation radius of these formulas is usually smaller than it appears. Besiroglu et al. [Besiroglu2024ChinchillaReplication] show that the fitting procedure itself can move the coefficients; Pearce and Song [Pearce2024ReconcilingScaling] show that differences across scaling laws partly come from different setups; McLeish et al. [McLeish2025Gemstones] directly show that aspect-ratio and LR-schedule changes can break prior fits. The practical reading is therefore that formulas fit mature SP stacks when the recipe is stable; they provide an 80–90 point start, not a universal law across architectures, optimizers, or tokenizers [Hestness2017PredictableScaling][Tay2021ScaleEfficiently].d:T671,The reason “transfer LR only” is easy to misread is that under AdamW the optimum is inherently a coupled multivariable object. Loshchilov and Hutter [Loshchilov2017AdamW] first decouple wd from the equivalent L2 term in adaptive optimizers; this means wd is not a subordinate quantity of LR. van Laarhoven [vanLaarhoven2017L2Norm] further shows that in the presence of normalization, L2/weight decay can act through effective step size. Wang and Aitchison [Wang2024AdamWWeightDecayScaling] provide scaling behavior for wd with model and data size, and Kosson et al. [Kosson2025WDMoreThanMuP] state the engineering conclusion directly: in practice wd may dominate transfer error more than nominal LR. Bergsma et al. [Bergsma2025PowerLines] place wd and batch size into a joint scaling law, showing this is not an isolated effect. On the other side, Cohen et al. [Cohen2022EdgeStability] analyze Adam-like methods at the edge of stability, indicating that β₂ and batch size shift the stability boundary; Xie et al. [Xie2020WeightDecayPitfalls] also show that wd can create additional pitfalls through gradient-norm mechanisms. A more reliable order of operations is therefore: set wd first by scale rule or local sweep, then discuss LR; treat β₂, dropout, and EMA as variables requiring explicit validation rather than assuming parameterization will transfer them automatically [Kosson2025WDMoreThanMuP][Wang2024AdamWWeightDecayScaling][Cohen2022EdgeStability]. This also explains why some apparent “µP failures” improve after changing wd or β₂: the failure may not be in width scaling at all, but in an unfinished optimizer closure.e:T614,The value of automated search is not that transfer rules become unnecessary, but that variables outside the closure of those rules can be handed to budget-aware local search. In the CARBS / Tune-As-You-Scale framework of Fetterman et al. [Fetterman2023TuneAsYouScale], the core idea is not brute-force search but organizing experiments of different scales and costs into a compute-efficient path. Lee et al. [Lee2020CostAwareBO], Wu et al. [Wu2019MultiFidelityBO], Eriksson et al. [Eriksson2019LocalBO], Guinet et al. [Guinet2020ParetoBO], and Abdolshah et al. [Abdolshah2019CostAwareMOO] provide the associated toolbox for cost-aware, multi-fidelity, local, and Pareto-aware BO. The issue is that proxies are not automatically trustworthy. Wortsman et al. [Wortsman2023ProxyInstability] directly show that small-scale proxies can miss instability regions that only appear at larger scale; this constrains both “formulas + small sweeps” and “BO + proxy.” A more executable division of labor is therefore: for quantities with known scaling rules such as LR and init scale, start from parameterization or stable formulas; for out-of-closure variables such as wd, β₂, dropout, and EMA, use CARBS for roughly 10–20 proxy runs of local patching [Yang2022muP][Fetterman2023TuneAsYouScale]. If BO is used to rediscover already-known width scaling, it wastes budget and is more exposed to proxy bias [Wu2019MultiFidelityBO][Wortsman2023ProxyInstability].
