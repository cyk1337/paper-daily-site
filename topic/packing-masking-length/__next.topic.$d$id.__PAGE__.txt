9:T5a1,The core tradeoff in packing is throughput efficiency versus training objective correctness. Early naive concat without mask reaches a packing ratio close to 100%, but Krell et al. [Krell2021Packing] quantitatively prove that it causes 0.5-2% training loss underestimation: tokens from different documents in the same pack can attend to each other, letting the model exploit statistical patterns in irrelevant preceding text to reduce loss and creating a mismatch between training and evaluation distributions. Fixed padding batch has no loss bias, but its packing ratio is only 80-95%, leaving 5-20% dead token waste and significantly extending training time, especially in the 8K main training stage. Per-doc mask with explicit tensor has no loss bias and a packing ratio close to 100%, but the O(L²) mask tensor causes 10-25% throughput loss, making the tradeoff unattractive. The cu_seqlens varlen interface proposed by FlashAttention2 [FlashAttention2Varlen] represents per-doc mask through interface parameters instead of explicit large tensors, reducing throughput loss to <3% while stabilizing packing ratio at >98%; it is the current optimal packing scheme. b:T5f1,The core tradeoff in long-context capability training is long-window performance versus training efficiency. Early schemes mostly used full-cycle fixed-length or full-cycle mixed-length training: the former cannot meet long-context requirements, while the latter, due to attention's O(L²) complexity, has 20-40% higher wallclock time than short-to-long under equal compute. The short-to-long curriculum disclosed by Llama3 [Llama3Blog2024] reframes long-context training from a full-cycle cost problem into a final-stage budget problem: allocate 95% compute to 8K main training, then 5% compute in the final stage to 128K mid-train, with RoPE base adjustment, to obtain 128K long-context capability with almost no increase in total training cost. Qwen2.5 [Qwen2_5Report2024] further validates the reproducibility of this scheme and provides the specific configuration for adjusting RoPE base from 10K to 1M, with long-context evaluation performance comparable to full-cycle long-sequence training. For over-length document processing, Ding et al. [Ding2024FewerTruncations] prove that, compared to truncate-and-drop or concat-then-chunk, split-then-pack improves perplexity by 0.02-0.05 nats and long-document tasks by 3-6pp; reducing truncations maintains consistent document boundaries and avoids destroying long-distance dependencies. The current dispute is whether short-to-long introduces length overfitting; public evaluation results of Llama3 and Qwen2.5 show no performance degradation in any short-length tasks.c:T60e,The central tradeoff in pretraining objectives is between task coverage and basic performance. FIM (Fill-in-the-Middle) was originally designed for code pretraining; StarCoder [StarCoder2023] shows that a 50% FIM rate improves infilling capability without degrading code tasks, making it the default configuration for code models. CodeLlama [CodeLlama2023] experiments show that enabling 50% FIM on a pure NL corpus causes stable 1-3pp degradation on NL tasks such as MMLU and GSM8K. This indicates that FIM gains are scenario-specific; pure NL use requires a dedicated rate sweep to verify the absence of side effects. For cross-document context, Shi et al. [Shi2023InContextPretraining] argue that cross-document concatenation matches inference-time prompt concatenation and retrieval augmentation, but Krell et al. [Krell2021Packing] prove that indiscriminate cross-doc visibility systematically underestimates training loss and rewrites the pretraining objective. A more pragmatic approach treats cross-document concatenation as an explicit, controllable data augmentation construction: concatenate only related documents, rather than enabling cross-doc visibility by default for all documents in the same pack.
