9:T5b8,Looped models should first be classified by when the model is made compatible with recurrence, not by paper name. Training-native methods such as Huginn, Ouro, Parcae, and Hyperloop jointly optimize shared weights and recurrent dynamics from initialization, allowing the fullest joint learning, but paying for repeated forward computation, BPTD or truncated gradients, and variable-depth stability. Post-training retrofits such as RRT and LoopUS start from a dense checkpoint and use continued training to make a dense operator loopable; published experiments report 3B tokens for LoopUS and 15B short-run / 60B main uptraining for RRT, optionally with LoRA, selective gates, or confidence heads; they preserve much of the pretrained model but are not training-free. Training-free inference retrofits freeze all weights and add internal computation through damped-Euler or Runge–Kutta sub-stepping over a middle-layer window. They remove training cost, not inference cost: every extra stage adds FLOPs, published gains concentrate on knowledge-heavy multiple-choice tasks, and evidence on reasoning and latency remains weak. This three-way taxonomy is more useful than calling loop depth a third scaling axis. New foundation-model training should be judged by stable dynamics and isoFLOP curves; checkpoint conversion by adaptation tokens and capability retention; and zero-update inference by window selection, numerical integration, and task-level regressions.a:T419,The three regimes optimize different problems. Training-native methods spend expensive pretraining to obtain the fullest recurrent capability: Ouro trains on 7.7T tokens, while Parcae shows that stability depends on controlled state injection and joint scaling of recurrence and data. Post-training retrofits spend a smaller adaptation budget for checkpoint compatibility: LoopUS reports a 3B-token default experiment, while RRT reports a 15B short run and 60B main uptraining; both update parameters. Training-free methods remove weight updates, not inference computation. Training-Free Looped Transformers numerically sub-step a middle window of a frozen checkpoint; across 45 model–benchmark cells, their strongest gains are on knowledge-heavy multiple choice, while naive layer or block repetition usually degrades quality. Post-training retrofits preserve most of a dense checkpoint but use additional optimization to turn a single-pass operator into a loopable one. Training-free inference retrofits update no weights. Chen et al.
