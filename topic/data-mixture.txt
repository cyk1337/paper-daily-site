17:T6af,Data mixture is often encoded as a fixed ratio table, but a more reliable framing is: ratios are control variables in a training process, and must be accounted together with dedup, bucket definitions, search budget, and training phases—otherwise regressions are hard to diagnose and rollbacks are unclear. The first hard constraint is dedup before ratios: at minimum document-level dedup, and semantic dedup/diversification when feasible; otherwise web domains’ effective tokens are inflated by redundancy, optimization learns repetition rather than information density, and near-duplicates can contaminate evaluation into a “shadow of the training set”[D42023][SemDeDup2023][DetectPretrainingData2023]. Second, bucketize finely: 2–3 coarse buckets average out signals and make both regression and online reweighting uncontrollable; a practical default is 15–30 buckets, and all shares should be reported in post-dedup effective-count space[OrganizeWeb2025][Paloma2023]. Third, pick search by compute: for small/mid teams, the common optimum is heuristic warm starts plus 2–3 ablations, then offline calibration via RegMix/mixing laws; treat complex utility estimators and online methods with skepticism unless you can afford extra stages and stable accounting[RegMix2024][BiMix2024][Held2025UtilityMix][DoReMi2023]. Finally, ratios behave like a curriculum: public recipes show late-stage 3–5× upweighting of code/math/reasoning is closer to tail capability acquisition than a constant mixture[MetaLlama32024]. Quality filtering often captures the largest gains, but “cleaner” is not monotonic and does not replace explicit ratios/curricula for scarce domains[FineWeb2024][DataCompLM2024].18:T607,Recommendation-level takeaway: don’t encode data mixture as a one-shot ratio table. A more reliable approach treats ratios as control variables with auditable accounting and rollback, built around effective tokens, bucket definitions, search cost, and training phases.
(1) Dedup before ratios: at minimum document-level dedup; add semantic dedup/diversification when possible. Otherwise web domains’ effective counts are inflated and optimization learns redundancy rather than information density; near-duplicates can contaminate evaluation and inflate metrics[D42023][SemDeDup2023][DetectPretrainingData2023].
(2) Default to 15–30 buckets and report shares in post-dedup space: coarse buckets average out signals for regression and online reweighting, making tuning uncontrollable and hard to roll back[OrganizeWeb2025][Paloma2023].
(3) Pick search by compute: small/mid teams should start with heuristic warm starts plus 2–3 ablations, then calibrate offline with RegMix/mixing laws; treat complex utility estimators and online methods with skepticism unless you can afford extra stages and stable accounting[RegMix2024][BiMix2024][Held2025UtilityMix][DoReMi2023].
(4) Express ratios as a curriculum: public recipes suggest late-stage 3–5× upweighting of code/math/reasoning is closer to tail capability acquisition than a constant mixture[MetaLlama32024]. Quality filtering often captures the largest gains, but “cleaner” is not monotonic and does not replace ratios/curricula for scarce domains[FineWeb2024][DataCompLM2024].19:T6f5,The optimisation path for data mixture in LLMs traces a curve from hand-tuned ratios to a fit-able control variable. [CCNet2019] standardised web filtering but left mixture hand-tuned; [Pile2020] introduced explicit domain buckets, giving later ``bucket + weight'' work a coordinate system, but still left ratio choice to team intuition. From 2023, mixture first became learnable: [DoReMi2023] used Group-DRO on a small proxy model to learn domain weights, cutting 8B training steps ~2.6x; [SemDeDup2023] and [D42023] separated ``effective tokens'' from raw tokens, so the mixture denominator was no longer polluted by near-duplicates. By 2024, mixture had become the third scaling axis: [RegMix2024] used a regression surrogate over a 1M-scale sweep to predict the optimal mixture; [BiMix2024] expressed mixture-vector -> loss in a closed analytic form that nests with the N/D power laws, enabling large-scale loss prediction without training; [DataCompLM2024] quantified ``filtered web >= noisy diverse'' as a >=7 pp downstream gap via controlled experiments.

The 2025 trend is to upgrade mixture from a vector to a trajectory: [MetaLlama32024] publicly documented the phase migration from web-heavy to math / anneal-heavy mixtures; [Held2025UtilityMix] gave a negative counterpoint on utility-mixing -- without stronger effective-token accounting, complex estimators degrade the ratio; [OrganizeWeb2025] moved domain construction earlier in the pipeline, stressing interpretability and rollback: until the buckets are right, more elaborate searchers only amplify noise. 1d:T4fa,Many mixture failures come from overly coarse buckets, not “bad algorithms”: treating web/books/code as buckets averages over quality tiers, topics, and languages, so regression or online reweighting can only learn a blurred average effect. Organize the Web’s key move is to front-load domain construction: organize web into interpretable domains before filtering and mixing, lifting diagnostic granularity from sources to domains[OrganizeWeb2025]. Once domains are first-class, evaluation must align at the same granularity; Paloma provides per-domain perplexity slices so “bucket definition → training mix → evaluation signal” closes the loop instead of relying on a single global loss[Paloma2023]. Another path is to let buckets emerge from data: unsupervised domain discovery learns latent buckets that often better match gradient-statistical groupings, reducing the arbitrariness of hand taxonomies[UnsupervisedDomainDiscovery2023]. In practice, a stable default is 15–30 buckets: below ~10, signals get averaged out; above ~50, accounting and sampler complexity slows iteration, and per-bucket sparsity raises estimator noise. This range is not a theorem, but it puts controllability and iteration speed on the same ledger[OrganizeWeb2025][Paloma2023].1e:T68f,Treating domain weights as first-order variables yields three routes: offline search, online/adaptive reweighting, and heuristics plus a few ablations. RegMix compresses search into an affordable offline phase: train many small models on random mixtures, fit a regression response surface from mixture→loss, then extrapolate to larger scale[RegMix2024]. Its upside is reproducibility and interpretability for budget planning; its cost is the need for stable bucket definitions and versioned accounting, otherwise the regression target drifts with pipeline changes[RegMix2024][OrganizeWeb2025]. DoReMi casts weight learning as Group DRO: use proxy training to estimate per-domain losses and perform minimax updates, aiming to improve worst domains and speed convergence[DoReMi2023]. It better matches training-time non-stationarity, but adds an online signal channel and implementation complexity; with unstable accounting, failures are harder to localize. The third route leaves complexity to engineering: warm-start from public recipes, then run 2–3 ablations to calibrate stage boundaries and upweighting factors. Held et al. give a practical constraint: complex LLM-estimated-utility mixing can be less stable than simple token-count / heuristic rules in several settings[Held2025UtilityMix]. The pragmatic division of labor is therefore: small/mid teams should first stabilize bucketization and accounting, then use heuristics plus a few controlled ablations to find rollbackable knobs; introduce offline regression or online reweighting into main training only when you can afford extra stages and guarantee version stability[Held2025UtilityMix][RegMix2024][DoReMi2023].1f:T618,“Fix quality before ratios” is usually most cost-effective when low-quality web dominates: DataComp-LM quantifies this in controlled experiments—under dominant noise, filtering/selection gives more stable gains, while the marginal benefit of fine-grained mixture tuning is swallowed by noise[DataCompLM2024]. FineWeb operationalizes web decanting, making “raise web quality until it stops dominating gradients” an executable default[FineWeb2024]. But treating ratios as a secondary knob hides a structural consequence: filtering usually cuts volume first, and scarce domains (code/math/multilingual/science) become scarcer after filtering; the remaining degrees of freedom shift to ratios, repetition caps, and curriculum.
